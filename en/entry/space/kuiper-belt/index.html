Kuiper Belt

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The Kuiper Belt is a flat ice zone outside Neptune orbit. The NASA layout gives a range of about 30-55 au, and the page takes 50 au as a round comparison.

What is it?

The Kuiper Belt, which is a flat ring outside Neptune's orbit, consists of small bodies of ice and remains of the stars after the formation of the solar system. [1][2] Short-lived planets, such as Pluto, Saturn and Bird Star, are among them, and the short-term comets are mostly derived from dispersors here or outside. [1] Most of the members are mixed with ice and rock and keep volatile substances because they are far from the Sun.

How?

Since 1992, when astronomers discovered the first Kuiper Belt object, 1992 QB1, thousands of ground telescopes and Hubble have been catalogued. [1] In 2015, the New Horizon flew over Pluto, and in 2019 it flew over Aroxis, providing near-range shape and surface data. [1] The distance of the celestial body is estimated mainly in combination with photometric, infrared and infrared measurements, the greater the distance the object is. [1] The New Horizon also provides background dust and universe rays to help estimate the mass density of the belt.

Value and Measurement condition

NASA's Copt version of the Kuiper Belt is about 30-55 au, outside baseary about 55 au. [1] Britannica states that the average distance of the celestial body is more than 30 au on Neptune, that the outer edge is not clear, and that objects with a point of the nearest day always greater than 47.2 au are excluded from the belt. [2] This article takes 50 au as a round reference for ease of comparison and falls within the above range and does not represent a solid measurement. The NASA layout also reminds that there is a dispersor outside the belt, which is often referred to as the outer solar system.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that the Coyber belt is a narrow ring or 50 au as the outer edge of the entire solar system. In fact, it has a significant thickness, with different orbital inclinations and eccentricity of its members, and a dispersor. Another misunderstanding is to mix it with Ortyun: the latter is much more distant and scale is about a thousand times the former. [1]

Relationship with other scale

50 au is approximately 7.48×10¹² metres, 1.7 times the half-axis of Neptune's orbit, but only about 40 per cent of the ceiling of 121.6 au. It's about 100,000 au, 2,000 times more than it is. [1] It can be seen that the Kuiper Belt is only a circle outside the planetary area, not the end point of the solar system.
