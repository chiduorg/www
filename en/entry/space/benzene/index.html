Benzene

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Benzene is a six-dollar cyclic aromatic molecule with a total carbon-carbon key length of about 1.40 E; this page selects the symmetric carbon atom range of about 2.80 E as the molecular ring diameter, excluding van der Waals.

What is it?

Benzene is a flat aromatic aromatic hydrocarbons, molecular C6H6, six carbonatoms consisting of a hexagonal ring, each carbon being connected with a hydrogenatom. [2] The ring carbon-carbon key is not a single and double key interchangeable, but aromatic key of equal length and electron out of domain. [1] This page uses approximately 2.80 E. as the molecular ring diameter, which refers to the distance between the two opposite carbonatom cores rather than the outer edge of the entire molecule Electron cloud.

How?

The structure of the benzene is derived from a combination of spectral, distillation and chemical evidence. Britannica notes that benzene is a plane molecule with six carbon-carbon keys equal in length, about 1.40 E, with an angle of 120 degrees; this type of key length between a single and a double key supports Electron leaving the field model. [1] The NIST CCCBDB experimental data page lists the molecular, D6h dots and flat configurations of benzene, consistent with its rule hexagonal skeleton. [2] This information gives key length and symmetry and is not an estimate of the " diameter " of a molecular image.

Value and Measurement condition

1.40 Egypt equals 0.140 nanometres. In a hexagon, two two-bit carbon atoms in a projection, twice the length of the key, i.e. about 2.80 E, or 0.280 nanometer. [1] This number only describes the ring diameter of the carbon skeleton; if the hydrogenatom and Electron clouds are included, the molecular shape will be larger and van der Waals ' diameter will usually be different. [1][2] This page therefore labels Quantity as an approximation and expressly states “Specific carbon spacing”.

Common misunderstandings

One of the common misconceptions is that 0.28 nanometer is the van der Waals diameter of a benzene molecule; it's just the opposite nucleus of the carbon ring, without hydrogenatom and the outer Electron cloud. [1] Another misunderstanding is the drawing of benzene into a single two-key fixed structure; experiments have shown all carbon-carbon keys to be long, electron leaving the ring. [1][2] There is also a confusion between the carbon skeleton size of the benzene ring and the accumulation distance between molecules in liquid benzene, which is affected by the temperature and concentration state.

Relationship with other scale

Put 0.28 nanos back on the scale axis, and the benzene ring is much larger than the nucleus of the water molecule, but still much smaller than protein and cell. [1] It shares ssp2 carbon skeletons with graphite, but benzene is a small individual molecule and graphite is an extended single layer carbon network. [2] Continue to read water molecules, graphite and crystal cells at different intervals than the smaller molecules, 2D materials and crystals “atom spacing”.
