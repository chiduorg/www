Himalayas

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The Himalayas are between the Tibetan plateau and the South Asian plain. The volume of this article is from the length of the south of Gargarbat to the south of Garbava, using a narrow mountain system of approximately 2,500 kilometres.

What is it?

The Himalayas, located in southern Asia, are between the Tibetan plateau and the Indus-Range plain, a huge arc mountain system. This article measures its length from the west to the east, from near the south of Gargarbat to near the south of Gargabava; it is not the north-south width or the length of the entire Asian mountain belt. [1][2]

How?

The length of the mountain range is usually measured along the main ridge or the outer edge of the mountain system, with peaks and path determinations. The painting of the outer edge of the mountain system by different maps will also change the length, especially in the west end, where the Kalakunlen is connected, and the east end, where the mountains are adjacent. [1] Britannica defines Himalayan itself as approximately 1,550 miles (2,500 kilometres) and gives a North-South width of about 200 to 400 kilometres and an area of about 595,000 square kilometres. [1] NASAclimate page says that Himalayan is composed of three parallel mountain ranges, which extend to a total of over 2,900 kilometres. [2] The latter is broader and may include parallel and adjacent mountains.

Value and Measurement condition

This article uses 2,500 km, as it corresponds to the narrow “Himalayan” West-East main ridge, with clear starting and ending points; 2,900 km is a wider measration convance with three parallel mountain ranges. The difference between the two is about 16 per cent, which is different from the target group. [1][2] Everest is 8,848.86 m high vertically, and mountain ranges are horizontally long and cannot be interchangeable.

Common misunderstandings

The "Himalayan length equals Everest height multiplied by multiples" is only of mountain size. Combining Himalayan with Karakunram, Hindu Kush or the whole Tibetan plateau would also yield longer figures. [1][2] Another misconception is that the north-south width is 200 to 400 kilometres in reverse length; the width and length are in two different directions.

Relationship with other scale

The 2,500 km represents 39 per cent of the Earth ' s average radius of 6,371 km and about 38 per cent of the Nile ' s length of 6,650 km. Everest's height of 8,848.86 metres is only about three thousand kilometres; the height of the mountain is far below its length. [1][2]

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
