Pencil

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

According to the Japanese stationery brand of Tombow, JSS prescribed a pencil length of 172 mm or more and referred to the historical history of 7 inches (17.78 cm); Britannica also used about 7 inches of wooden board when describing the manufacture of pencils. This page takes 17.78 cm as a common reference for pencil length.

What is it?

The pencil is a writing tool with graphite and clay blends and a wooden pole outside. The so-called “lead” is not metal lead, but a historical term; the depth and hardness of the pen core are regulated by the ratio of graphite to clay. [2] This article compares the length of a common wooden pencil with the length of a pencil, not the length of the pen core, the diameter of the pencil or the length of the available length after the sharpening.

How?

Tombow's stationery knowledge page states that JIS provides a pencil length of 172 mm or more, with a maximum diameter of not more than 8 mm; it also describes a proposal to set the length at 7 inches (17.78 cm) around 1840, based on a distance similar to that between adult hands and the middle finger. [1] Britannica’s student pencil settry described the wood plate as about 7 inches (18 cm) long in the manufacturing process and indicated that the cores were made of materials such as graphite and clay. [2] Together, they support the common order of 7 inches.

Value and Measurement condition

17.78 cm equals 177.8 mm and 0.778 m, multiplied by 2.54 cm conversion by 7 inches. [1][2] This number refers to the total length of uncut pens; 172 mm of JI is the lower limit and the actual product may be slightly longer. If the amount is “so long as it can be written”, sharp, short or rubber-coated pencils will give different results, so the comparison will indicate whether the volume is long or can use a pen core.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “the pencil is exactly 17.78 cm”. Seven inches is a historical and common specification, and JIS only sets a lower limit of 172 mm or more, with different products. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is the length of the penbar as the length of the pen core: the core is enclosed in the wooden pole and the point is sharpened in a short part; the writing consumes the core rather than the whole wooden pole.

Relationship with other scale

17.78 centimetres of pencils are slightly shorter than 20 centimetres of hands, nearly eight times longer than 22.25 mm of coins and more than 30 times longer than 5 mm of grain of rice. [1] It is a good reference for daily objects of a "metre class": a pencil equals about 15 centimetres and a little bit more. Hands up, height and room scale, coins down, grains of rice, sand and cell.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
