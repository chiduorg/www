Hydrogen Atom

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Hydrotom shows the scale concept of moving from a “marginal ball” to a quantum probability distribution. This page compares the length with twice the Bolt radius, helping to understand the difference between the size of atom and the Electron cloud, the atom nuclear.

From the hydrogenatom tomscale.

Hydrogen is an important starting point for understanding the structure of atom: there is only one proton atom at normal hydrogenatom and one Electron outside. Polmodel can explain some spectrum patterns of hydrogen, but cannot copy the same simple set of orbit rules to all atoms. Quantum theory further describes atom in the electron state rather than looking at electron as a small ball running along the orbit of a microplanet. [2]

What does the electron cloud describe?

An orbital corresponding wave function in quantum mechanics, the square density of the wave function model gives the position. The answer was where Electron was more likely to be present at the time of the recurrence, rather than what Electron had left behind in the event of an observation. A common spherical, dumb bell orbital map depicts a space distribution in different states; the colour baseary on the map is a representation rather than antom shell. [2]

Why does this page use a double Bolt radius?

NIST lists the Bolsian radius a0 about 5.29177210544 × 10⁻¹¹ m. This page explicitly takes 2 a0 or about 1.058 × 10⁻¹⁰ metres as the relative length of hydrogen atom. This is a choice for comparison, and it is not possible to reverse the fact that all hydrogenatoms have this only hardball diameter from the outside of the model circle. The status and size of the atom need to be explained together. [1][2]

Spectra and location are different evidence.

The electron energy level has a dispersive value and changes between the energy levels are associated with absorption, launching light. Spectra can thus test the energy structure of atom; orbital maps help to understand space probability distributions. Even if a pattern is very similar to the solar system, it does not mean that Electron can be interpreted in terms of the classic position and trajectory of the planet, with different descriptions on the two scale.
