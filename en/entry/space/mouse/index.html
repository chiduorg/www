House mouse

Rats are small mammals in rodents. The long finger size of this article, i.e., the tip of the nose to the end, does not contain the tail; the tail of the rat is usually close to the length, as compared to 8 cm.

What is it?

Mus musculus is a small mammal of rodents and one of the most widely distributed rodents with the closest connection to human habitation. This article refers to the length of the head, i.e. the length of the nose to the anus or the root, without the tail; the tail of the rat is long and usually close to the head. [1][2]

How?

Animal Diversity Web records a head length of 65-95 mm. [1] House Mouse entry of Britannica gives a body length of about 6-11 cm and indicates that the tail length is approximately the same as the body. [2] Both submissions are consistent and point to tail-free head length; the difference is only from scope alignment and individual variations. They all describe the tail separately, indicating that head length is the more common comparison.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 8 centimetres, which are used as a medium reference for the head length of the rat, and falls within 65-95 mm of ADW and 6-11 centimetres of Britannica. [1][2] It is not the exact value of a single rat, nor does it represent a juvenile mouse or a large individual. Measurement usually takes its nose to the end, and its tail is not included; if it contains a full tail, the reading may be close to or even greater than 16 cm. There are also differences in the readings of specimens, living organisms and individuals of different ages.

Common misunderstandings

There is a common misconception that “how long a rat” is understood to be a long tail. Rats often have tails that are about twice as long as their bodies, with tails and no tails. [1][2] There is also a misunderstanding that rats are mixed with mice, rats or mice; different types of head length are not the same and 8 cm is a reference for rats.

Relationship with other scale

8 The head size of a cm rat is approximately equal to that of a frog, about six to seven times that of a bee, much smaller than that of a cat, about half the length of a human hand. [1][2] On the scale axis, it is located in a small mammal belt “a few centimetres to ten centimetres”: cats, dogs and humans up and frogs, bees and fruit flies down. The length of the head rather than the length of the tail is used to compare the rat with the other animal intestine.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
