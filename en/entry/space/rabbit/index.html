Domestic Rabbit Head-Body Length

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The rabbit originates from European rabbits; this article measures the head length of the adult individual from the end of the kiss to the end of the root, without long ears and short tails.

What is it?

The rabbits originate from the European rabbit (Oryctolagus cuniculus) and are common domestic animals in rabbits. The article is “head length”, from the end of a kiss to the end of a root, without ears and tails; rabbits' ears are long and stand up to make the whole body look much higher but not long. [1][2] It's different from shoulder heights and ear lengths. The back limbs of the rabbit are clearly longer than the front limbs, jumps well, and the head lengths describe only the torso and do not include the breadth of the limbs after the stretch.

How?

Animal Diversity Web ' s European hareentry records: 38-50 cm long, 1.5-2.5 kg body weight, and indicates that the individual is likely to be larger. [1] Britannica's rabbit and rabbit-shaped intestry gives: The smallest rabbit is about 20 centimetres long, the largest can be about 50 centimetres. [2] Most rabbits fall between 38-50 centimeters. The sample ADW is from the wild European rabbit, Britannica, which covers many species in rabbits; rabbits are artificially selected and can be shaped beyond the wild.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 0.40 m, or 40 cm, as the representative value of adult rabbit head length. It is in the 38-50 centimetre range of ADW, indicating that it is a common medium-sized rabbit rather than the largest species. [1][2] The true range of rabbits is approximately 0.35 to 0.50 m, with large varieties approaching 0.5 m. Measurement does not count both ears and tails from the length of the length of the kiss to the end root; readings are alternative if they are high or stand up. The variety of rabbits varies widely, with small varieties of about 30 centimetres and large varieties approaching half a metre; this article takes the median.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings is to take up the length of an ear, which leads to an additional 10 centimetres. [1] Misunderstanding two is the mixing of rabbits and mice; 0.08 m is much smaller than 0.40 m is a rabbit. [2] Others are as big as all rabbits, ignoring differences between varieties and ages. The length of the rabbit ' s ear alone as long as it is long, or the straightness of the back arc while crouching, will cause readings to deviate.

Relationship with other scale

0.40 Mets are about five times the size of 0.08 Mets, slightly less than 0.48 Mets, about one third of the height of 1.1 Mets and much less than 1.2 Mets. [1][2] In meiofauna, rabbits are at the “thousand centimetres” level: cats, dogs, rats, bats and insects up and down.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
