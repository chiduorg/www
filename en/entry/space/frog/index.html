Frog

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Frogs are amphibians. The length of the article refers to the distance from the excretion cavity to the end of the leg; it refers to the European forest frog 8 cm.

What is it?

Frogs are amphibians, have no tails, have developed limbs and are good at jumping. Frog skin is exposed and often respirated with skin aid, and the length thus becomes the first size to be recorded when the species are identified. The length of the article is the distance from the kiss to the excretion cavity, which is often written in English, snout-vent length; it does not contain the length of the extension of the rear leg or the upper limb. [1][2]

How?

Frig entry of Britannica gives an endless kiss to the excretion cavity of about 9.8 mm to 30 cm, with a wide range. [1] Animal Diversity Web records a length of 7.5-8 cm for Rana temporaria. [2] The former is a class-wide range, while the latter is a common species; both indicate that frogs are small species of less than 1 cm and large species of near 30 cm.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 8 centimetres as a long reference for the formation of European forest frogs and falls within the range of 7.5-8 centimetres given by ADW. [2] It is not the average of all frogs, nor does it represent a large frog or a small frog. [1] Measurement usually allows the frog to fall naturally, measuring the straight distance from the mouth of the excretion cavity; post-legs folding or stretching and whether or not the body pulls straight changes the reading. If the mass is “full” and the rear leg is included, the number is significantly larger than the size and cannot be directly compared to the size.

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is to mix frog length with long jump length or leg length. The length is only a kiss to the excretion cavity, and the back leg can be longer than the body. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to use one number to represent all frogs: the tailless is from less than 1 cm to 30 cm, and the European forest frog is only the medium-sized species common in temperate zones.

Relationship with other scale

8 The European forest frog is approximately equal to the size of the mouse's head, several times larger than the bee, much smaller than the cat's body, and less than the breadth of the hand open. [1][2] On the scale axis, it is located in the biological belt “a few centimetres to ten centimetres”: cats, dogs and books up and bees, butterflies and spiders down. The length measure condition allows comparisons between the different frog species and avoids misadventure of leg length as a body size.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
