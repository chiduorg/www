What's Quack?

quarker is the basic mass particle in standard model, with six “smellings”: upper, lower, lower, lower, odd, top and bottom, divided into three generations, each with one upper and one lower quart. They have a colorload, combined by force; Proton and Neutron are made of quarks. [1][3]

How to study them?

quarkers cannot exist alone, and experiments can only be made from the direction of high-energy impact products, from energy and from within the opposite thrusts. Standard model requires that quarke appear only in non-coloured combinations, so detectors often record impurities and injections rather than individual free quarkes. [1] The “discretion of smaller structures” is two things: the former is a probe resolution, and the latter requires a clear shape. [1][3]

PDG 2025 gives the mass associated with the program: u, d, s for MS mass of 2 GeV, approximately 2.16, 4.70, 93.5 MeV; c, b 1.273 and 4.183 GeV, respectively; quarkers give 17256 GeV from event sports. [2] These values cannot be directly converted to a " radius " because mass is not of the same length. [2] [3]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings is that the difference between mass in different quarks is considered to be a difference in size; it is about 80,000 times greater than the quác, but there is no evidence that its “diameter” is also 80,000 times larger. [2][3] Misunderstanding two is that the maximum resolution given by the experiment is considered to be measured in diameter; the upper limit only indicates that “is less than a scale can still see structure”. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to consider “colour charge” as a true colour; it is only a name of a powerful effect, not related to visible light, and cannot be used to judge what Quack “looks like”. [1][3]
