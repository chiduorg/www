Great White Shark

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The big white shark is a big cartilage. This article takes 4 m as a full-length reference, measuring the distance from the end of the kiss to the end of the tail, not the length of the fork, weight or width of the mouth; 4 m is the whole value of a young or submerge and is not the largest record.

What is it?

Carcharodon carcharias are large carcasses and one of the top predators in the oceans. [1][2] This article compares “all length” with a straight distance from the end of the kiss to the end of the tail. It is not a common fork length for fishermen, is not a weight, is not a high body size or a wide mouth.

How?

The White Shark page of NOAA Fisheries gives two end points: about 4 feet (1.2 metres) at birth and 21 feet (6.4 metres) in size. [1] Britannica ' s white shark intestry similarly indicates that the largest fully growing individuals do not exceed 6.4 metres, with the majority body weight between 680 and 1800 kg. [2] 4 m equals approximately 13 ft, falling between birth and upper extremity and being taken as a full-length reference for young or submersibles; it is not the largest record and there is no information to determine it as the average total length of the species.

Value and Measurement condition

4m equals 400 cm. The full length ranges from the end of the kiss to the end of the tail; the length of the fork ends to the gap of the upper leaf of the tail, usually over the full length, and the two cannot be mixed. body weight increases rapidly with full length, but four metres is not directly conversant to a certain weight because of nutritional, gender and age implications. [1][2] 4 m is a whole value, which does not mean that the measurement is accurate below centimetres or that all extremities are parked at this length.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “the great white shark is four metres long”. Both NOAA and Britannica have a ceiling of about 6.4 m and 4 m is only a reference point in the middle. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to treat the whole length and the length of the fork as the same or to exchange the length and weight directly. The great white shark is a fish, not a whale; its “big white” refers to the abdomen colour, not a body classification. Nor can all sharks be described with 4 metres, with different types of adult size ranging from less than 1 to 10 metres.

Relationship with other scale

The 4 m is approximately 2.4 m high, the first end of a diameter of approximately 40 0.1 m cover and about one seventh of the total length of the 30 m blue whale. [1][2] On the scale axis, the great white shark is at the upper end of the “metre-scale animals”: upward blue whales and giant sprouts, downward octopus, gills and mushrooms. The full-length measure convention is used to distinguish it from the octopus described with the arm and the golden eagle described with the wing.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
