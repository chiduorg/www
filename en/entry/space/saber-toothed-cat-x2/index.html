Sabre-toothed cat body length × 2

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Based on 2 m of "Swordtooth length", x 2 conversion gets 4 m; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is 4 m, obtained from the base reading of "Swordtooth length" 2 m times 2. The baseline is the front-to-tail length of the skull, with no tail; conversion doesn't change the physical object, it just changes on which scale we put it. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review: 2 m x 2 = 4 m. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the "Swordtooth Long" to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller?", it can be directly related to this article without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with the mammoth shoulder height (3.5 m); the elephant (African elephant reference) (3.35 m) to see whether the unit is uniform, then whether the reference is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement convention. The benchmark entry's summary provides a readable starting point: the Swordtooth is a modern-day, prehistoric large cat. This article takes 2 m as a long reference, measuring the front end of the skull to the end, without tail; 2 m is the upper limit for the extraction of large individuals, which is usually Smilodon Fatalis shorter. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

♪ Bang, bang, bang ♪

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]
