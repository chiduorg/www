Amino Acid

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Amino acid is attached to the same alpha-carbon chain by an amino, a gill and a side chain; this page uses the linear extension of acrylic acid for approximately 5.5 E as a representation of the molecular length from which smaller or larger amino acids deviate.

What is it?

Amino acid is an organic molecule whose common feature is that the same centre of carbon is associated with ammonia, aluminum and a unique side chain, known as alpha-carbon. [2] The difference between amino acids is mainly in the side chain, so their overall size varies. This page uses about 0.55 nanometers as the representative length, derived from the estimate of the linear stretch of acrylic acid, rather than claiming that all amino acids are so large.

How?

These lengths are derived from geometric estimates of the length and angle of the keys. Table 2 from BioNumbers, calculated as linear stretch model, is 5.5 E, 3.3 E; glycerine is 3.9 E; pyrochlor is 10.9 E; lysine can reach 11.3 E. [1] The original reminder is that this estimate does not take into account scrolls and is only suitable for the provision of general order of magnitude. [1] Britannica describes the common structure of amino acids: amino, molybdenum and side chains are linked to alpha-carbon. [2] The combination of the two may explain why the "amino acid size" must be carried.

Value and Measurement condition

5.5 E equals 0.55 nanometer. It describes the maximum stretch of acrylic acid from one end to the other rather than the spacing of the adjacent residues in the protein chain. [1] Glycerine is smaller, colourine and lysine are larger; if all common amino acid medians are taken, another number is obtained, but that is not a page-based measration condition. [1] Hydraulic, electrical state and side-chain images will also change the effective dimensions, so this page will mark Quantity as approximate.

Common misunderstandings

One of the common misconceptions is that all amino acids are as large; the median length in Table 2 varies from 3.9 to 11.3 E, with significant differences. [1] Another misunderstanding is the repetition of the length of amino acid extension as a protein residual; after the chain has folded, the space relationship between the remains is determined by the main chain image, not by the simple molecular length. [2] There are also people who mix amino acids with pelicans; amino acids are monomers, and pelicans are connected by several amino acids through pelicans.

Relationship with other scale

0.55 Nanometers leave amino acids in small molecular zones: larger than water molecules, up and down to one nanometer with glucose, nucleotide, but much smaller than folding proteins. [1][2] When many amino acids are condensed into polyazine, the molecular volume increases linearly, but the three-dimensional dimensions are folded and cannot be easily multiplied by a single length. The reading of proteins, glucose and nucleic acid continues, comparing the size of the biological monomer with the scale after aggregation.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
