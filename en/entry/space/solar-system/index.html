Solar System

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The solar system has no single outer baseary. This page represents the width of the major planetary orbit region by approximately 9 billion kilometres of the average daily distance of Neptune.

What is it?

The solar system is a system of celestial bodies, central to the Sun and bound by its gravity, including eight planets, dwarfs, satellites, asteroids, comets and interplanetary media. [1] It does not have a single external baseary: different questions are subject to different definitions, such as the planetary orbit region, the heliosphere top or the outer edge of the Alt cloud. The Sun, which accounts for more than 99 per cent of total solar system mass, is the dominant source of gravity and energy.

How?

Planetary orbits are determined by long-term celestial body Measurement and spacecraft tracking. The NASA Neptune fact sheet gives a semi-long axis of orbit 4,514.953×10⁶ km, about 30.18 au, about 29.89 au, and about 30.47 au. [1] The wider Kuiper Belt, the Dispersion Dispersion Drive and the Alt cloud are catalogued by small objects, comet orbit reverse and model estimations, rather than direct imaging. [2][3] For more distant celestial bodies, the orbital cycle and near-day point distribution is more binding on their attribution than a single imaging.

Value and Measurement condition

This article indicates the width of the major planetary orbital region by twice the average orbital distance of Neptune: approximately 4.515 billion kilometres x 2.930 billion kilometres, totalling9×10¹² metres. [1] This is Planetary Area, not the diameter of the entire solar system. The planetary orbit region appears to be based on Neptune, but Neptune's orbit itself is elliptical. The heliosphere peaks at about 121.6 au and the outer edge of the Ortt cloud reaches 100,000 au, both of which are well above this width. [3]

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that the solar system has a recognized spherical shell or uses Neptune orbit as the end point of the solar system. Beyond Neptune is the Kuiper Belt, the dispersor and Oort Cloud. [2][3] Another misunderstanding is that the size and spacing of the planet on the orbital map are understood at the same rate; the graph usually magnifies the object and compresses the distance.

Relationship with other scale

9×10¹² is approximately 60 au, about 60 times the radius of Earth orbit. It's half as small as the heliosphere top 121.6 au, and about 1,700 times smaller than the 100,000 au on the outer edge of the Altte cloud. [3] Therefore, the “size of the solar system” must indicate which layer to use first. If the Altair cloud is included, the solar system scale can expand by about three more times of magnitude.

There are several types of solar system.

The planetary area is based on Neptune's orbit, about 30 au; the heliosphere tops about 121.6 au; and the outer edge of the Orté cloud is about 100,000 au. [2][3] Three types of boundary answer different questions: where the line galaxy goes, where the solar wind ends, how far the comet reservoir extends. Combining them into a “sun system diameter” would make a difference of three order of magnitude.

Orbital maps are not proportional maps.

Textbook maps often magnify planets and compress space, otherwise planets would be too small to see on the page. Neptune orbits about 30 au, while Earth orbit 1 au; if painted at the same scale, the planet's diameter is only micro-scale. Read the chart first, not from the image.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, the planetary area is approximately 60 au wide; on the mass axis, the Sun accounts for more than 99 per cent of the total mass of the solar system; on thetime axis, the solar system was formed about 4.6 billion years ago; and on the temperature axis, the surface of the Sun is very different from the planetary atmosphere. The "size" of the solar system depends on the basearydefinition, not a single number. [1][3]

The solar system has three versions.

The solar system baseary can mean the range of planetary orbits, the outer edge of the Koiber belt, or the ultuous cloud dominated by solar gravity. The scale of the three is from dozens of stronomical unit to tens of thousands of stronomical unit, three times of magnitude. If you write “the diameter of the solar system”, you have to explain which type of beamary is used; the system is much larger than the normal planetary plate by including the Oort Cloud. [1][S1]

The length on the space axis also distinguishes the radius, diameter, distance from the projection range; the same object gives different values in different contexts.
