Jupiter

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Jupiter ' s volume equivalent average diameter is 139,822 km on this page, twice the average radius of 69,911 km in the NASA fact sheet. Its equator is different from the polar radius, and the comparison must be made in a way that preserves the use of the measurement convention.

What is it?

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system, volume, and mass is approximately 318 times the Earth, consisting mainly of hydrogen and helium. The average diameter of the volume equivalent in this volume is the doubling of the ball equivalent by an average radius of 69,911 km. [1] It does not have a solid surface, and the radius usually refers to a bar-pressure layer, and the clouds are only visible upper layers. Jupiter rotates very quickly, about 9.9 hours a week, and the equator is clearly ablaze, resulting in measurable flat rates. It also has thin rings, but this does not count in diameter.

How?

The NASA Factual List shows an average radius of 69,911 km and a separate 1-bar layer equatorial radius of 71,492 km and a polar radius of 66,854 km; the three are from the same set of observation and internal models. [1][2] Measurement combines flying images, octopus, radio occult and orbital dynamics; different definitions correspond to different pressure layers or rotations. Britannica summarizes the equatorial diameter at approximately 143,000 kilometres, which is not inconsistent with the average diameter of this article, but is different from the measration convention.

Value and Measurement condition

139,822 km is the average diameter; the equatorial diameter is approximately 142,984 km and the polar diameter is about 133,708 km. Jupiter flattens 0.06487 and cannot summarize all directions in one diameter. [1] Volume Equivalent Radius is appropriate for comparing the overall size of the planet; if the cloud belt movement or ring system is studied, the equatorial radius, extreme radius or specific pressure layer is used. Mass, volume and density are also independent.

Common misunderstandings

Jupiter's diameter is 143,000 km; the average diameter is about 139,822 km. Nor can the average diameter of volume be confused with the visual diameter seen from Earth. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is that Jupiter is called a “failedstar”; its mass is far below what is required to ignite hydrogen fusion, although there is an internal heat source but not a Star. It is also seen as a solid terrain, which is actually an active atmospheric vortex.

Relationship with other scale

139,822 kilometres are approximately 11 times the Earth ' s diameter of 12,742 kilometres and 1.2 times the average diameter of Saturn of 116,464 kilometres. Compared to the solar diameter of approximately 1.39 million kilometres, Jupiter is about one tenth of that. It's still a planet, not a Star. [1][2]

One is not the same surface as the clouds.

Jupiter does not have a solid surface, NASA has a 1 bar-pressure definiation radius; visible clouds are at higher and colder levels. [1] Equator radius 71,492 km, polar radius 66,854 km, average radius 69,911 km. Different layers of pressure give different radiuss and cannot consider the edge of a cloud top photograph as the only baseary.

The flat rate is from fast rotation.

Jupiter is about 9.9 hours of rotation for one week, and the equatorial centrifugal effect is causing the equator to pick up and flatten at the polar level at about 0.0649. [1] So the equatorial diameter is about 9,000 km in diameter. Painting Jupiter into a perfect sphere would throw away this measurable shape information; its ring system is not within this diameter.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, Jupiter has an average diameter of about 139,822 km; on the mass axis, it is about 1.898×10²⁷ kg, which is 318 times the Earth ' s size; on thetime axis, Jupiter formed in the early stages of the solar system; and on the temperature axis, the cloudtop is about 165 K, which is far hotter internally. The diameter, mass and internal temperature cannot be replaced. [1][2]

Cloud Top Radius & Flat Rate

Jupiter's radius usually takes about 71,500 km of the equatorial radius, but it is a clear flat ball: the equatorial radius is about 6,700 km of the polar radius. There is no single height on the top of the clouds, and ammonia clouds, water clouds and deeper gas layers give different layers of reference. If the Jupiter diameter of the body text is based on the equatorial radius, it cannot be made of a polar radius at the same time. [1][S1]
