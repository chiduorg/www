Human

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The height of a person is a vertical distance from standing flat to the top of the head. The 2021 Human Measurement Procedures Manual of the National Centre for Health Statistics of the United States of America has a chapter dedicated to standing heights; the page uses 1.7 metres as a reference to heights rather than the statistical average of a population.

What is it?

Height is the vertical distance between the standing plane and the top of the head. It is determined by the length of the lower limb, the length of the spinal column, the height of the skull and the position, and also changes with age, nutrition and health status. [1][2] This article compares the height of an adult standing up, not the length of sitting up, arm stretching, weight or part of the body.

How?

The CDC National Centre for Health Statistics ' 2021 Human Measurement Procedures Manual includes standing heights as an official item of human measurement and includes in its catalogue a specific section on the need for standardized equipment and operating procedures for measurement. [1] The human body of Britannica explains that the human body acquires comparable body sizes by selecting reliable physical markers and standardizing measurement techniques. [2] The two support “how heights are met” and “why humans need uniformity”.

Value and Measurement condition

1.7 m equals 170 cm. It is the chosen reference to human height, is not the global average of the population, and is not the result of a country or age group. [1][2] Regulation usually requires that the person under examination be barefooted, standing, head on Frankfurt plane, measured to the maximum vertical distance to the top of the head, and that position be leaning forward, knees, hair or measurementtime affecting readings.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that 1.7 m is considered to be “human standard height”. The heights of different population groups, genders and ages are very wide, and averages vary according to region and gerogical age; 1.7 metres are only for comparison purposes. Another misconception is to ignore the condition of measurement: wearing shoes, stomping feet, bowing heads or different timemeasurement in one day can lead to a centimetre difference; height is not a fixed attribute on the identity card.

Relationship with other scale

The 1.7 m population is about 8.5 times larger than the 20 cm hand and is much smaller than the 30 m blue whale, and about seven times the diameter of the Earth (about 7.5 million times). [1][2] On the scale axis, people happen to be at the “metre level” of daily scale: buildings, mountains and planets up, hand, pencils, insects, cells and molecules down. Using human heights as anchors, other intestines are most easily placed in the same scale.

The error book of this value.

1.7 m is a round example of the adult population; globally, the average height of adult males is different from that of females, with changes in regions, nutrition and age. 1.7 metres is not a “human standard”, much less the height of a child or ancient human being. When comparing furniture, door frames or spacecraft with this number, it should be considered a convenient scale rather than an accurate demographic value.

Another scale to look at it.

1.7 About 200,000 times the diameter of the red blood cell and about 1/5,200 at Everest height. If a person is reduced to 1 millimetre, the Earth is about 7.5 kilometres in diameter and Moon about 2 kilometres in diameter. The human body is in the “m” scale, connected up to the city and planet, and down to the cell and molecules, one of the most common references for this station.

The average population is not a standard person.

1.7 Mets are a round reference for easy comparison, but the height of adults is widely distributed globally; regional, gender, nutrition, global age and measurement methods change averages. [1][2] Treat 1.7 m as a “standard person” that would deprive human scale designs for door frames, seats, clothing and spacecraft of distribution information. The body scale usually uses a percentage instead of a fixed number.

The height will change with the day and position.

Standing up may differ by about 1 centimetre in the morning and night, and intervertebrae pressure, position, barefoot or not affect readings. [1] Regulation requires bare feet, straight feet and a head on Frankfurt plane. Using the height of the identity card as a precise physical constant, these measurement conditions are ignored.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, people are approximately 1.7 metres; on the mass axis, adults are approximately 70 kg; on the temperature axis, body temperature is about 37 °C; and on thetime axis, the body scale of modern people is different from the ancient population. The "size" of a person is both length, mass and physical state. [1][2]
