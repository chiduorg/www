Mount Everest

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

In 2020, China and Nepal jointly released Mount Everest at 8848.86 metres. This article uses the elevation of the peak snow surface, not the elevation of the rock surface or the height of the mountain from a certain foothold.

What is it?

Mount Everest, the main peak of the Himalayas, is located in China and Nepal, where the peaks are constantly snowed. The height of the snow surface at the peak of this article is the vertical height relative to the global elevation benchmark. [1][2] It is not the relative height of the mountain from foot to summit, nor is it the height of the rock; it is also the snow layer between the snow and the rock. When putting Everest in the top ranking of the world, it is important to indicate which layer of surface is used.

How?

The combined use of GNSS, standards, PV range, snow depth radar and gravity in 2020 was co-published on 8 December with 8848.86 metres. [1][2] Measurement first established a GNSS control network in the Everest area, obtained three-dimensional coordinates of hundreds of sites and then passed the Yellow Sea height benchmark to the bottom. The snow depth radar is used to detect the height of the peak snow layer, and gravity and geometric data are used to harmonize the high-range reference. This is the height of snow passed along the benchmark, not from the foot of the mountain.

Value and Measurement condition

8848.86 meters is snow surface height; China released 8844.43 meters above rock surface in 2005 as another measration condition. Snow depth, gravity baseline and data processing all affect the results and cannot be treated as the same level of metal. [2] [3] The difference of several metres between the rock surface and the snow surface depends on the snow thickness and density of the peak; the same mountain can thus have two reasonably “highs”. The relative height of the mountain body, the climbing of the mountain and the rise of the terrain are additional quantities.

Common misunderstandings

It is not true that Everest's altitude starts with the foot count: its altitude is based on a global elevation and is not a rock surface height. Combining the snow with the rock surface gives different “world highest” values. [1][2][3] Another misunderstanding is that Everest is considered to be the remotest point from the Earth ' s surface; it is not the same concept that the altitude is influenced by the Earth ' s shape. At the height of Citiation Everest, both the year and the surface type should be indicated.

Relationship with other scale

The 8848.86 metre is approximately 10.7 times the 828 metre of Khalifa and 3.2 times the 2737 metre of the Golden Gate Bridge. Compared to the 3474.8 km diameter of Moon, it represents about 0.25 per cent of the latter. But in front of the Earth's radius of 6371 km, it is only the highest point in the thin layer of the surface. [3]

Altitude is not "from foot to foot."

8848.86 Mets are the height of the peak snow surface relative to the global elevation benchmark and are not the relative height calculated from base or mountain feet. [1][2] The southern slope of Everest and the northern slope are additional volumes of climbing, mountain rises and terrain cutting. Altitude is considered to be “the height of the mountain” and the base surface is confused with the height of the mountain.

The snow is a few meters away from the rock.

China published 8844.43 metres of high surface in 2005 and the United Measurement announced 8848.86 metres of high snow in 2020, a difference of about 4.43 metres. [2] This difference is from peak snow thickness and measuremeasurement convention, not twice who is wrong. At the height of Citation Everest, the year and surface type must be written simultaneously.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, Everest is about 8.85 kilometres; on the mass axis, the mountain mass is much smaller than the Earth; on the Time axis, Himalayan is still lifting and eroding; and on the temperature axis, the hypothermia and oxygen deficiency limit human activity. High, mass, lift rate and temperature are four books. [1][3]

Altitude doesn't mean it's the furthest from the Earth's core.

Mount Everest is 8,848.86 metres above sea level; the Earth ' s equator is soaring that the top of Mount Chimborazo is far from the core. The height of peak ice and snow changes with season, and the height of the surface is not the same as that of the snow. Body text 8,848.86 meters is above the surface of the snow, and the baseline must be specified. [1][S1]

Read-ment convention and conversion

The volume used in this article is 8,848.86 m (high snow in 2020) and measurement convention is the height of snow above Everest, which was published in mid-2020.
