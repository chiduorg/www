Brown Dwarf × 10

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

1.398×10^09m, based on 1.398×10^08 m of the Brown Dwarf Star, obtained by x10 conversion; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is1.398×10^09m, obtained from the base reading of the Brown Dwarf Star 1.398×10^08 times 10. The base entry is in diameter (similar to Jupiter); conversion does not change the physical object, it changes only on which scale we put it. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review:1.398×10^08m x 10 =1.398×10^09m. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the "brown dwarf" to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks, "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller?", the article can be applied directly without recalculating. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with Jupiter (13982,000 m); the Red Dwarf (2097000 m) to see whether the unit is unified, then whether the reference is in diameter, radius or or orbit distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The base entry sumary provides a readable starting point: the brown dwarf is an ass of less than 0.075 sunmass that cannot maintain a stable astar object of hydrogen fusion. They are similar in size to Jupiter, typically about1.4×10⁸metres in diameter, but mass can be dozens of times greater than Jupiter. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]
