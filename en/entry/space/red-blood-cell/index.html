What is it?

Red blood cell is the largest number of cell in mammalian blood, and the mature man red blood cell has no cell core and most cell devices and is filled with haemoglobin. Its outer shape is a dimpled disk: it is dented in the centre of the plate and has thicker edges, a shape that increases the area of gas exchange and allows the cell to pass through microvasculars. [1][2] Hemoglobin is responsible for the transport of oxygen from the lung to tissue and is also involved in bringing CO2 back to the lung.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is to treat red blue cell as a rule sphere, with a diameter representing both long, wide and high. The thickness of the dimple does not equal the diameter, nor does the central dimple mean that the cell is empty.
