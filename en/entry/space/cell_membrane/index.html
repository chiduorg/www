Cell Membrane Thickness

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Cell membrane is a thin membrane of phosphophosphate double-molecular layers and proteins with a thickness of about 3-5 nm; a comparison of 5 nm is taken from this page to illustrate its relationship with the cell diameter, membrane protein and mobile models.

What's cell membrane?

Cell membrane is a thin membrane coated outside the cell, consisting mainly of phosphorous resin double-molecular layers and embedded proteins, which may also contain sugar resin and sugar protein. Phosphorus is pro-watered and dilated, forming a double layer of approximately nanometre thick; proteins can be transected, attached to the surface or partially embedded. [1][2] It both separates the content of the cell from the outside world and allows for selective material passage.

Thickness and measurement

NIST's neutron spinback study summarized the thickness of the model biomembrane in the range of about 3-5 nm; this page takes 5 nm as a comparative reference. [1] The distance between the interfaces of the "heavy" finger membrane here is not the diameter of the cell as a whole, nor the overall height of the outside part of the membrane protein. Different lipid compositions, temperature, hydrate state and measurementdefinition change readings; the "well" given by refrigerated mirrors, X-rays and neutron scatter may also correspond to different interfaces.

Why not a wall?

Cell membrane is mobile: lipid molecules can move within membranes, proteins can diffusion or aggregate, which is the basic scene of "mobile models". [2] Membrane selection takes place through hydrophobic barriers from lipid layers, channel proteins, carrier proteins and pumps rather than a hard shell. The membranes are also bent, sprouted, integrated, involved in transport and signal transmission.

Common misunderstandings

The “cell membrane thickness of 5 nm” is not equal to all membranes and all positions are equally thick; it is an overview value. Membrane is lipid, too: the composition of proteins, sugar chains and cholesterol can affect the mechanics and function of the membrane. In addition, the thickness of the film is not the same as the area of the film: the membrane itself is thicker than the size of the membrane.

Relationship with other scale

5 nm is only one in four thousand of the typical cell diameter (about 20 μm) and is also much smaller than the linear particle (about 2 μm) and bacterium (about 1 μm). [1] This scale reminds us that cell membrane is a layer of molecular structure, but it has three roles on cellscale: soundary, transport and signal.

Membrane thickness is not the same as membrane area.

Cell membrane is about 3-5 nanometres thick, but a cell can have a large membrane area: microflows, wrinkles and bumps that allow the same membrane to cover more surfaces. [1] Thickness describes the horizontal scale of the lipid double layer and the size of the area interface; the two cannot converse each other. Membrane proteins also reach several nanometers from the side of the membrane, changing the "visible thickness".

Mobile mosaics are not static sandwiches.

The lipid molecule is capable of diffusion within the membrane plane, and the protein can also move, aggregate or internalize; the whole shape of the membrane changes with the cell state. [2] Refrigerated fractures, X-rays and neutron scatters see a "wellness" that relies on sample preparation and interfaces. Draw membrane into a wall of fixed thickness, which leaves out its mobility and selectivity.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, the membrane is about 5 nanometres thick; on the mass axis, the lipids and proteins on the membrane are only a fraction of the cellmass; on the tymperature axis, the memperaturity of the membrane changes and phase change; and on the time axis, the membrane structure evolves and develops with the cell. The thickness is only one parameter of the film, not the size of a cell. [1][2]

Is it dry or active?

Cell membrane is about 4-5 nanometres thick, most of which come from cross-sections of electroscopes: samples are fixed, dehydrated, dyed and membrane protein and lipid hydrates are removed. Livecell membrane contains water and bends, and thickness changes with tension, temperature and cholesterol content. Treating the "dry film thickness" under the mirror as the actual thickness of the active cell, would ignore several euphemisms and dynamic rises. [1][S2]

Read-ment convention and conversion

This article uses a volume of about 5 nm (film thickness), which is a reference of 5 nm in the overview of biofilm thickness. The length on the space axis also distinguishes the radius, diameter, distance from the projection range; the same object gives different values in different contexts. When comparing this reading to the adjacententry, replace it with the same unit and then take the margin; do not reduce it directly with a different value. If there is only one thing to remember: the answer is “the object probably falls on which order of magnitude”, it is not a constant that can replace all samples.
