Ant types are different from individuals and cannot share a precise length. This page selects pharaoh ant as a small ant reference: 1/16 inches given by the Texas Agroindustrial University and Utah University, and 1.6 mm in conversion.

What is it?

Ant is a social insect with a membrane wing, with a head, chest, abdomen, and a clearly constricted " waist" between the chest and abdomen. The pharaoh (Monomorium phaaonis) is a small, globally distributed indoor pest, with a working ant colour ranging from yellow to brown, with twelve tentacles and three upper sections. [1][2] This article compares the length of the main axle of the worker ' s body with the size of the whole group of ants.

The city entomology page of the Texas Agro-industrial University lists the length of pharaoh ants as 1/12-1/16 inches and indicates that the worker ant is of a single type, i.e. similar in size within the same group. [1] The identification guide for Utah State University similarly gives 1/16/1/12 inches and lists identification features such as antagon, two abdominal, etc. [2] The two sources independently gave the same order of magnitude, indicating that this scope is not an isolated example of a single web page.

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that “ants are small, so they can only use one number”. Ants range from approximately 1.6 mm pharaoh to more than 10 mm carpenter ants, which may vary from species to class. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is that the length of the tentacle or foot is calculated to be long; this would make the reading clearly biased.
