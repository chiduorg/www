Glacier

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

This article takes the example of the Eastern Antarctic Lambert Glacier, using approximately 400 kilometres of length. It is measured from the direction of the Ameri ice shelf line upstream to the end of the visible stream line, not by width, size or ice thickness.

What is it?

Glaciers are ice bodies that accumulate for a long period of time and that can flow with gravity. This article takes the example of the Eastern Antarctic Lambert Glacier, measuring the length of the line from the Ameri ice shelf upstream to the end of the visible stream, approximately 400 kilometres. [1][2] It is not a glacier width, area, ice thickness, nor is it a process in which the entire flow of ice starts from a watershed. The glaciers flow and the baseary changes with accumulation and digestion, so the “long” needs to describe the time and end points of the measurement.

How?

The Australian Antarctic Plan describes the Lambert Glacier as the world ' s largest glacier, approximately 40 kilometres wide and 400 kilometres long, while the paper Journal of Gender clearly gives 400 kilometres of surface-to-up visible current lines. [1] [2] These values are derived from topographic maps, satellite images and ice-heated radars, etc., and are not on foot along the coast. In different studies, the length of the ameri ice shelf or the whole drainage area is extended.

Value and Measurement condition

400 km equals 400,000 m. It corresponds to the length of the main stem of the ice stream rather than the area covered by the glacier; the Lambert glacier system also contains several tributaries and ice shelves. [1][2] The thickness of the ice can reach thousands of metres, which is a vertical amount and cannot be interchangeable with length. The flow of ice is hundreds of metres per year and cannot be used to calculate length.

Common misunderstandings

“Glacial length is the distance from the edge of the ice sheet to the top of the mountain” is not accuracy: glaciers are mobile ice bodies whose endpoints are to be pressed by the line, current line or ice shelf blockarydefinition. [1][2] Another misconception is to treat the length as area or volume; a doubling of the length does not equal a doubling of the size or amount of ice. The comparison of glaciers should be accompanied by an indication of endpoints, year and whether ice shelves are included.

Relationship with other scale

Four hundred kilometres are approximately 45 times the height of Everest at 8848.86 metres, which is equivalent to about 394 kilometres north and south of Taiwan. [1][2] It is about 3 per cent of the Earth ' s 12,742 km in diameter. It belongs to a regional scale that is both larger than city and much smaller than the continental ice sheet as a whole.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
