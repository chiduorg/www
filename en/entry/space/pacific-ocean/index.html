What is it?

The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean basin on Earth, stretching from 60 degrees south to the Arctic, bound between Asia, Australia and the Americas. This article seeks to compare the horizontal span of the basin rather than the size, average depth or length of a particular route. [1][2] It contains more than half of the world's free water and is the subject of the “peripheral zone of the Pacific Ocean”. Unlike politics, ocean basins are divided by history and regulations.

How?

NOAA states that the Pacific Ocean covers about 63 million square miles, with more than half of the world ' s free water, and notes that the planet has only one connected global ocean, named areas of history, culture and geography. [1][2] Britannica gives a slightly different area of approximately 62.5 million square miles and explicitly excludes the South China Sea; its student version further states that the Pacific Ocean is about 12,000 miles east-west, or about 19,300 kilometres wide. [3] [4]

Common misunderstandings

“There is a uniform width of the Pacific Ocean” is not correct: the basin is irregular, and the span of east and west changes with latitude and endpoints, and the name baseary is not a water wall. An area squared as a diameter is also impersonated as a 2-dimensional dimension. [1][4] Another misconception is that the Pacific Ocean is directly compared to the land area; the global ocean covers approximately 71 per cent of the Earth ' s surface, and the ocean is not simply added to the land baseary. The comparison indicates the width convulsion and whether or not to contain the edge sea.

Relationship with other scale

It covers about one third of the Earth ' s surface. [3] [4] The Pacific Ocean is still less than half its width compared to the Earth ' s perimeter of 40075 km. Its scope is described as a scale area on the planet ' s surface and cannot be considered a planetary radius or perimeter.

Size is not the same as width.

The Pacific Ocean covers an area of approximately 163 million square kilometres, with an east-west width of about 19,300 kilometres; the “equivalent edges” obtained from squared areas are neither width nor range. [1][3] The shape of the basin is irregular and the width varies with latitude and endpoint. Size, width, average depth and volume are four different volumes.

The Pacific is not an isolated water.

NOAA points out that there is only one interconnected global ocean on Earth and that the Pacific has a history, culture and geography. [1][2] Seawater, heat and organisms are exchanged between the ocean basins; treating it as a closed pool would leave open the connectivity of currents to the climate system.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, the Pacific Ocean is approximately 19,300 km wide; on the mass axis, the total amount of seawater corresponds to the large mass; on the temperature axis, the surface and the deep sea temperature differential drive the circulation; and on the Time axis, the ocean basin is open and closed with plate motion. Area, width and heat content are not interchangeable. [1][3]

Area, volume and baseary

The area of the Pacific Ocean is about 165 million square kilometres, with an average depth of about 4,000 metres and a volume of about 660 million cubic kilometres; the three numbers correspond to the surface, the water column and the basin baseary. The boundaries of the Pacific Ocean and the surrounding seas vary from one institution to another, and the area changes. The “maximum ocean” of body text is the area of mass protection, not the length of the volume or coastline. [1][S1]
