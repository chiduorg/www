Honeybee

Bees are social insects in membranes. This article is based on the Western bee-form 12 mm long; the workers, bees kings and male bees are different in size and the species are different.

What is it?

Bees are social insects in membrane bees, most commonly Apis mellifera, etc. This article refers to the length of the adult insect from the front end of the head to the end of the abdomen, without the span of the wings and without the tentacles. There are bees, kings and males in the bees, and they are not the same size. [1][2]

How?

Britannica's Honeybee entry recorded the bee's length as about 1.2 centimetres. [1] Animal Diversity Web gives a long range of 10-20 mm to bees in the west, indicating differences between individuals and types. [2] One to a typical value, one to a range; the lower limit of the range is close to 1 cm and the upper limit is up to 2 cm, indicating that the bees' length will vary with the type and the object.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 12 mm as a reference to the length of the Western bee-form, which corresponds to about 1.2 cm in Britannica. [1] It falls within the range of 10-20 mm of ADW, but it is not the midpoint of that range, nor does it refer exclusively to bee king or male. [2] Measurement is usually measured to the end of the abdomen without wings and tentacles; when the specimen is dry, it shrinks and the activity may differ from the reading of the specimen. The bee king is usually older than the worker and the male is in different shapes and proportions, so 12 mm should be understood as a reference to the general form.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is to take 12 mm as the fixed size of all bees. The worker bees, bee kings and male bees have different shapes and different bees; the AAW range is up to 20 mm. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to mix the length with the size of the wings show or the nest: the width of the wings spread, the space occupied by the whole group of bees, and not the size of a single bee.

Relationship with other scale

Twelve millimeter bees are approximately two or three times the size of a mosquito, larger than a fruit fly, smaller than a spider, and about one fifth the width of a human hand. [1][2] On the scale axis, it is located in the “cm” insect belt: butterflies, frogs and mice up, mosquitoes, dust mites and pollen down. It can be fairly compared with the same kind of insectentry by positioning it with long, rather than wings.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
