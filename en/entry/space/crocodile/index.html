Nile Crocodile Total Length

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The Nile crocodiles are the largest crocodiles in Africa. This article takes 4 m as a full-length reference, measuring the straight distance from the end to the end, not the weight, height or length of the head; 4 m falls on the lower line between the averages of the adult and is not the largest record.

What is it?

Crocodylus niloticus is one of Africa ' s largest crocodiles in freshwater and brackish water, and one of the largest existing crocodiles. [1][2] The quantity of this article is a “full length”, that is, a straight line distance from the end to the end. It is not weight, it is not height, it is not only head length; crocodiles have long tails, leaving their tails clearly low.

How?

The Nile alligator in Britannica states that the body averages 4 to 4.5 metres (13.1 to 14.8 feet) and weighs approximately 410 kg, with a maximum of 6 metres and more than 680 kg. [1] SeaWorld ' s species page gives an average of 5 m and a maximum of 6 m in form and indicates that approximately 2 m in female form is ready for sexual maturity. [2] The averages given by the two families varied slightly; 4 metres fell below the average area of Britannica, approximately 13 feet. It's the whole reference, not the biggest record.

Value and Measurement condition

4m equals 400 cm. The measurement shall be straight along the axle, from the end of the kiss to the end of the end, and not the head length or standing height. Weight cannot be directly converted from the whole length: the same four metres can be significantly different from wild, male and female weights. [1][2] Crocodiles do not have fork lengths like fish, and do not transpose the fork length concept. A 4 metre also means that the crocodile is well above the sexual maturity of approximately 2 metres of females. [2]

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “the Nile crocodiles have six meters”. 6 m is the upper limit for a very small number of large individuals, with an average size of 4 to 4.5 m. [1] Another misconception is that the whole length is considered an alternative indicator of weight or height; a 4-metre crocodile can be heavy, but “4-metre” only indicates length. Nor should it be confused with the Bay Crawl: both can grow large, but they are distributed differently from the outside, and this article deals only with the Nile Crawl.

Relationship with other scale

Four metres of approximately 2.4 metres of the 1.7-metre-high tails of the population and the total length of the 4-metre white shark are equal to the order of magnitude, 1 metre longer than the 3-metre Komodo iguana, and about one seventh of the total length of the 30-metre blue whale. [1][2] On the scale axis, it is in the middle of the “mite class”: up, blue whales and cortex, down, pyres, turtles and hummingbirds. A uniform, full length, allows comparison to be made on the same axis with an animal with a wingspan and a standing high description.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
