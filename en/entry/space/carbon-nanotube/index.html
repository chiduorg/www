Carbon Nanotube

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Carbon nanopipes are carbon networks that are rolled into cylinders; single-wall carbon nanopipes are typically about 1 nanometer in diameter, with a literature coverage of about 0.6-2.0 nanometres, and this page takes 1 nanometre as a representation of the pipe and does not represent a particular tube.

What is it?

The carbon nanopipe is an empty cylinder made up of a carbonatom six ring network belonging to the Fuller Family. [1] It can have only one wall or more than one wall; a single tube is the type most frequently identified. This page uses approximately 1 nanometer as a representation of the pipe diameter, describing the horizontal diameter of the cylinder rather than its length; the carbon nanopipe has a long diameter greater than usual, order of size of diameter. [1]

How?

The tube path is determined by the structure model in conjunction with micro- and spectral experiments. Britannica indicates that the typical diameter of the monolithic carbon nanopipe is approximately 1 nanometer and emphasizes its high longitude ratio. [1] The synthesis reproduced by the physics of the Central College of Sciences indicates that the diameter of the monolithic carbon nanopipe is usually 0.6 to 2.0 nanometres, ranging from several nanometres to centimetres. [2] The data are derived from statistics on samples of different manual and prepared conditions and are therefore given range and typical values rather than the exact size of a tube.

Value and Measurement condition

1 Nanomy equals 1×10⁻⁹ m2. It is the whole representation of the diameter of the one-way tube, which falls within 0.6 to 2.0 nanometers. [1][2] Pipelines are associated with manual indices: The same carbon network is curled in different directions and gives different diameters and electron properties. [2] The diameter of multi-wall carbon nanopipes is usually larger, as is often the case in the literature, where 5-20 nanometers are not suitable. [1][2] Quantity is thus marked as approximate.

Common misunderstandings

One of the common misconceptions is that 1 nanometer is considered to be the length of a carbon nanopipe; it's only a pipe, and the tube can be much longer. [1] Another misconception is that all carbon nanopipes are treated as monolithic tubes; the outer diameter of multiple tubes is significantly larger and of a different nature. [1][2] There is also a confusion between carbon nanopipes and graphite tablets; graphite is a two-dimensional tablet and carbon nanopipes are a one-dimensional cylinder of scrolls, which are geometrically different but associated with carbon networks.

Relationship with other scale

1 Nanolines leave carbon nanopipes in the “nanomaterials” zone: bigger than benzene, slightly smaller or closer than quantum points, but much smaller than cell. [1][2] It shares ssp2 carbon skeletons with graphite, which are part of the carbon nanostructure family.
