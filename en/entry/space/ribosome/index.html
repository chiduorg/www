What's a rib sugar?

Nutrient sugar is the molecular machine of synthetic proteins in the cell: it reads the password of the courier RNA and connects amino acids sequentially to a polypeline chain. The nucleus consists of two small and large Archipelagos, each containing the nucleo-RNA and various proteins. [1][2] It does not have a membrane structure and therefore does not belong to a membrane cell; both the original nucleus quell and the genuine nucleus quell have a nucleus, but the composition and deposition factors vary. [2]

Dimensions and Measurement

This page uses approximately 100 x 200 e scale, as shown by the Scripps Research Lecture, in a longer direction of approximately 20 nm. [1] This is not a unified path, but an overview of the overall shape of the lecture. RCSB PDB describes the different structure of the nucleus in different life groups; S in 70S, 80S indicates the deposition factor, not the nanolength, and cannot simply add the values of the size of the Archipelago. [2] When precise dimensions are required, species, structural intestry, assembly state and direction should be specified.

Why the machine, not the parts?

Nutrient sugar changes in protein synthesis, catalyzing the platinum; it connects genetic information, amino acid activation and the extension of the pelican chain. [1][2] Nuclear sugar is not a one-time consuming enzyme, but a compound that can be reused; multiple nuclear sugars can also be combined with the same mRNA to form polyplastic sugar. The structural differences between the nucleus and the nuclei of cucumbers are also the basis for the selective effects of some antibiotics. [2]

Common misunderstandings

The term “nuclear sugar is protein” is not correct: it is made up of RNA and protein, and the catalytic core relies mainly on RNA. [2] “20 nm is the diameter of all nucleus” is also incorrect: this page is just a long-term orientation of the overview of the lecture; different species, different Archipelagics and different images give different shapes. [1][2] In addition, the deposition factor S is not a unit of length and 70S nuclear sugar is not “10 nm short” than 80S.

Relationship with other scale

Twenty nanometers of nuclei are about 50 times smaller than the baterum (about 1 μm) and about 1,000 times smaller than the typical cell (20 μm); it contains dozens of proteins and long chains of RNA. [1] The nuclear sugar, tRNA and protein molecules together form the molecular machine layer within the cell, located between the cell and the atom.
