White Dwarf

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The white dwarf is the dense core of low-massstar spent nuclear fuel, and mass is close to the Sun, but the radius is similar to the Earth. This page takes the Earth's diameter as the typical white dwarf diameter, about 1.27×10⁷ metres.

What is it?

The white dwarf is the dense core of low-massstar spent nuclear fuel, and mass is close to the Sun, but the radius is similar to the Earth. [1] The "diameter" of this page refers to the light ball scale of a typical white dwarf, not its residual atmospheric, suction or bigalaxy mass structure.

How?

White dwarfs cannot "quantify" directly with normal Star structures. Astronomers use spectral, photometric and gravitational red-red migrations, combined with atmospheric models, or use shelter and orbit to decipher the radius. [1] Britannica summarizes the typical radius of the Earth, which can be as dense as a million times water. [1] The average radius of the Earth is 6,371 km according to the NASA fact sheet, and thus approximately 12,742 km in diameter. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

Take "the radius is comparable to Earth" and the diameter is about 1.27×10⁷ metres. [1][2] The white dwarf radius is not fixed: the larger the mass, the smaller the radius, which is the result of Electron ' s simplicity and pressure; Chandraseka ' s limit is about 1.4 solar mass. [1] Therefore,1.27×10⁷ is only a typical order of magnitude, and individual white dwarfs may be significantly smaller or slightly larger, and mass and model should be identified at the same time in the report.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that the white dwarf is considered a “small sun”: it no longer undergos stable hydrogen fusion and is cooled by residual heat. Another misunderstanding is to mix the white dwarf diameter with the neutron diameter; the neutron radius is about 10 kilometres order of magnitude, about 1,000 times smaller than the white dwarf. The white dwarf was also confused with the planet: its mass was near the sun, but the volume was compressed to the size of the planet.

Relationship with other scale

1.27×10⁷ metres corresponds to the Earth ' s diameter order of magnitude, about one per cent of the Sun ' s diameter (1.39×10⁹ metres). It's about four times less than the diameter of the red giant star, but about three times the diameter of the neutron star. [1] By grouping these three endpoints together, one can look directly at the three dense remains left by the evolution of Star: the white dwarf, the neutron star and the black hole, with smaller dimensions and higher density.
