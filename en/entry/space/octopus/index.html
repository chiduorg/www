Octopus

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The octopus is an oscillator. This article takes 1 m as a medium-sized octopus arm stretch to measure the maximum span between the right and the right wrists, not the length of the coating film, the size or weight of a single wrist.

What is it?

The octopus is an octopus mollusc with a soft body with eight suction wrists that move by spray or crawl. [1] This article compares “arms stretches”: the maximum span between the right and the right wrists when the eight wrists extend completely to both sides. It is not the length of the coat membrane, nor is it the length of the wrist measured along a wrist, much less its weight.

How?

The octopusentry of Britannica indicates that the size of octopus varies widely: the smallest is about 5 cm long, the largest Pacific octopus can grow up to 5.4 m, and the arms extend close to 9 m. [1] The regular octopus page of National Geographic gives a size of 12 to 36 inches and states that it can grow to approximately 4.3 feet. [2] 1 metre falls within the range of medium octopus and is used as an arm stretch reference; it is not an average of all octopus or a record of large species.

Value and Measurement condition

1 metre equals 100 centimeters. The arms must be stretched naturally and stretched to the right and left, measuring the distance between the tip of the wrists; the wrists can bend and shrink, and the octopus can measure a much smaller range under the contraction. [2] Ordinary octopus coated membranes are approximately 25 cm long, which is a body length, not an arm stretch; another metrology convance is obtained from one wrist to the tip of the wrist. [1] 1 metre is the full value and does not mean that the measurement is accurate below centimetres.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that the arm is shown as long. The eight wrists of the octopus can be extended or concretized, with the arms stretch describing the lateral span after expansion, and the length usually refers to the length of the coating film or the length along the single wrist. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is that “octopus are only 1 metre”: giant Pacific octopus have arms stretching nearly 9 metres, while small octopus are only a few centimetres long. There are also direct inferences of weight by arm stretch, which are in fact influenced by the water content, type and age of the muscles. 1 metre is only suitable as a medium reference for medium octopus.

Relationship with other scale

One metre of approximately 0.59 people at a height of 1.7 metres, the first end of a diameter of about 10 0.1 metre caps, and about a quarter of the total length of four metres of large white sharks. [1][2] On the scale axis, the octopus is at the lower end of the “migrade”: up sharks, blue whales and giant sprouts, down plankton, bacterium and viruses. This amassion convention separates the octopus from the great white shark, also described horizontally.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
