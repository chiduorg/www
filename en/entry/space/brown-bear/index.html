Brown Bear Head-Body Length

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Brown bears are large bears across Eurasia and North America; this measure of adult males from the end of a kiss to the end of a root, excluding short tails.

What is it?

Ursus arccos is a bear animal spread across Eurasia and North America, where inland populations are often referred to as grizzly bears. The volume of this article is “head long”, ranging from the end of the kiss to the end root (button) and does not contain the tail; the tail of a brown bear is short and is about 6.5-21 cm. [1][2] This is not the same as the “stand altitude”, which is the distance from the bottom to the top of the head when the back leg is standing straight.

How?

The brown bear intestry of Animal Diversity Web states that the length from head to hip is 1 - 2.8 metres, tail length 65 - 210 mm, shoulder height 90 - 150 cm and body weight 80 - 600 kg or more. [1] The Brown Bear page of the National Parks Administration of the United States gives a more common range: 5-7 feet (approximately 1.5-2.1 metres) in size, 3-5 feet in shoulder height, and up to 9 feet for individuals who are particularly large on stand. [2] The two cap differences are derived from sub-species and individuals, in particular large male bears along the Alaska coast. NPS figures come from North American national parks, and ADW aggregates records of several sub-species in Eurasia and North America, so they cannot be considered the same samples.

Value and Measurement condition

This Article takes 2.2 m, or 220 cm, as the representative value of adult male brown bear head length. It falls within the range of 1-2.8 metres of ADW, but is higher than the common range of 1.5-2.1 metres given by NPS, indicating that 2.2 metres are closer to the large male than the population average. [1][2] Adult males are usually between 1.7 and 2.8 metres, and female and southern populations are shorter. The length is measured from the end of the kiss to the end of the root, with the tail being counted; the number obtained is greater if the measure stands at the height.

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is to consider the "stand close to 9 feet" as a long body, which is, in fact, the height of a straight leg, not the head length. [2] Another misunderstanding is that 2.8 metres are considered to be the mean length; 2.8 metres are the upper limit for large individuals, with a common adult male population of about 1.7 to 2.2 metres. [1] Weight is also used to infer length, but weighty and seasonal fats cause significantly less weight for the same individuals. The tail of a brown bear is short, and the size of the size does not affect readings at all, but it should still indicate whether it is to the end or to the end.
