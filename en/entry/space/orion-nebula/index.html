What is it?

Orion's great nebula, one of the closest star formations to the Earth in the galaxy, is located in Orion, with a small spot of fuzzy light. [1][2] It consists of incandescent youngstars, ionizing gases, dust and the original planetary disk, centred on a quadrilateral constellation. It belongs to the large massstar complex in the arm of Orion in the galaxy.

How?

Multi-band imaging reveals its structure: visible light shows ionizing gases and dust, and infrared penetrating dust shows still forming stars and discs. [1] Visible and infrared composite maps of APOD indicate a range of approximately 40light-year and distance of approximately 1,500light-year. [2] The light from the ionizing hydrogen zone and the infrared radiation from the dust are combined to create a three-dimensional structure.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is to mix the span of Orion's great nebula with its 1,500 light-years distance, or to treat the dust bands in the nebula as empty. Another misconception is that Stars in the nebula are young; in fact, they are both emerging and young Stars in the mainstream. [2] It is also incorrect to consider nebula brightness as a number of stars: the brightness is mainly contributed by ionizing gases and dispersing light.
