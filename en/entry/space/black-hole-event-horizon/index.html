Stellar-Mass Black Hole Event Horizon

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The event radius of the star class black hole is given by the Swazi radius, which is positive for mass; 10 times the solar mass corresponds to approximately 2.95×10⁴ metres, or about 30 kilometres. This number is a radius, not a diameter.

What is it?

The sight of the event is a “go-and-go” bombary around a black hole: through it, even light cannot escape outside. [1] For a non-rotation ball symmetrical black hole, the event scene is a sphere with a radius called the Swazi radius. [2] This page selects a 10-fold solar mass star class black hole as a sample that places approximately 2.95×10⁴ metres on the scale axis; this number is the radius, not the diameter.

How?

Measurement does not come from the "see" view. Astronomers assume a black hole mass by means of X-rays from the ingestion of gas from the companion stars, and Star orbits around invisible objects, and use the gravitational theory to halve mass conversion. [1][2] NASA ' s Cop note states that by pressing the sun mass into a radius of about 3 km, flight speed will reach light speed; Britannica gives 10 times the Sun mass equivalent of about 30 km. [1][2] The Swazi radius formula Rs=2GM/c2 links mass, gravitational constants to light speed, so the radius is proportional to mass.

Value and Measurement condition

Based on 10 times solar mass, Rs is about 2.95×10⁴ metres, or 29.5 km; Britannica takes 30 km for writing. [1][2] The corresponding visual diameter is approximately 5.9×10⁴ metres. The event horizon here refers to theory, but not to shadow contours seen in telescope images; the latter are influenced by a strong gravitational lens, which is larger than the vision itself. [1] If the black hole turns around, the vision shape will deviate from the perfect sphere, and the Swazi formula is just approximation.

Common misunderstandings

The most common confusion is that the Swazi radius is used as a diameter, and then 30 kilometres is described as “the black hole has a width of 30 kilometres”, with an actual width of about 60 kilometres. Another misconception is that the event vision is a physical surface; it does not have a material shell, it is just a mathematical shell. [2] There is also a direct view of the dark dimensions of the EHT images; the rings in the images and the structure of the shadows, which contain light bends and suction flows, cannot be equated with Rs.

Relationship with other scale

2.95×10⁴ is only about 30 kilometres of metres, less than one per cent of the Earth ' s diameter (about ZXXQPH1QX metres), close to a city or an asteroid scale. Magnify mass to 4 million times the solar mass, increase the radius to about 1.2×10¹⁰ metres, order of magnitude in the heart of the galaxy; then zoom in to 6.5 billion times the solar mass into the scale of M87*. Thus, the "size" of the black hole is almost entirely determined by mass, not by the star radius that forms it.

The sight and shadow are not the same circle

The event vision is theoretical, and the radius is determined by mass; the “shading” observed by the telescope is a dark zone formed by the bending of light in a strong gravity field, which is usually larger than the view. [1][2] EHT images derive their bright rings from suction streams and the edge of the shadow is not the exact contours of the vision. Treating both as the same circle will overestimate the "visible size" of the black hole.

Swap change view shape

The Swazi radius only applies to non-rotation, charge-free black holes. Real black holes have many self-rotations, the shape of the horizon is detached from the surface, and drags with reference can also change the light around them. [2] Mass is the same and rotates different black holes, and shadows can be different from siphon flow structures. The event scene still exists, but cannot be summed up by only one Rs.

Five-axis Contrast

On the space axis, the visual radius of the 10-fold solar mass black hole is about 30 km; on the mass axis, the radius is proportional to the mass; on thetime axis, the black hole grows by suction and by combining; and on the temperature axis, the Hawking temperature decreases with the mass. Vision, shadow, mass and temperature are four different concepts. [1][2]

The sight isn't glowing.

The event vision is a zero-curve “no return” and does not glow in itself; the telescope captures the shadows of the ingestion of flow and photoring around the sight. The shadow diameter is slightly greater than the diameter of the vision, as the light bends in the field of strong gravity. Body text overestimates or misconstrues the "size" of a black hole if it looks at the shadow as a view. [1][S1]
