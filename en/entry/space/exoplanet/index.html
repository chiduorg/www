Exoplanet

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Extraplanetary planets are planets that orbit other Stars outside the solar system. Thermal Jupiter is sampled with a diameter comparable to Jupiter, about 1.4×10⁸ metres; the sizes of extraterrestrial planets vary considerably from one system to another.

What is it?

Extraplanetary planets are planets that orbit other Stars outside the solar system. [1] This page is based on the sample “Heat Jupiter”, which is measured in diameter, approximately 1.4×10⁸ metres, comparable to Jupiter; it is not the orbital radius of the planet, nor the diameter of the motherstar.

How?

The outer planets themselves are dark and are usually detected indirectly through the algae or visual speed. In the Lingsat method, the passage of a planet from the front to the star causes the cyclical dilution of the star, the depth of which is associated with the ratio of the planet's diameter to the Star's diameter; combined with the Star radius, the planet's diameter can be estimated. [1] NASA, introducing young hot Jupiter HIP 67522b, noted that it was about 10 times the diameter of the Earth, close to Jupiter. [1] Jupiter ' s volume average diameter is given by the NASA fact sheet as1.398×10⁸ metres, which can serve as a benchmark for hot Jupiter scale. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

Take Jupiter’s diameter1.398×10⁸ metres as a model value for Hot Jupiter. [2] The hot Jupiter radius is usually between 1.0 and 1.5 Jupiter radiuss, with a small number of hotly-heated planets expanding to a greater extent; the rocky planets are much smaller and may be only 1-2 times the diameter of the Earth. [1] Therefore, the term “integrity diameter” cannot be summed up by a number, and this article represents only the type of hot Jupiter in the gaseous giants and is clearly a diameter rather than a radius.

Common misunderstandings

A common misunderstanding is to consider “extraplanets” as a specific type; it is simply a generic term for “extraplanets of the solar system”, both rocky and gaseous giants. Another misconception is to interpret “10 times the Earth's diameter” as “10 times the Earth's diameter”; the diameter is not linear with mass, and the heat Jupiter's mass can be several times that of Jupiter, but the diameter is still on Jupiter. There is also a misperception of Hot Jupiter as Star, which does not fusion itself.

Relationship with other scale

1.4×10⁸ is about 11 times the diameter of the Earth and one tenth of the diameter of the Sun. [2] It is slightly larger or more similar than a brown dwarf, but much smaller than a red dwarf; it also indicates that it is difficult to distinguish between large planets and brown dwarfs in diameter alone and that mass and formation history are required. Thermal Jupiter is placed in the original planetary disk, which is only a small part of the plate but can significantly influence the dynamics of the plate in close orbit.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
