City does not have a natural unified diameter. This article, which is based on a sample of the Shanghai City District of China, uses a maximum span of about 100 km from east to west as a comparable value to reflect the differences between administrative areas, population concentration areas and the contact areas of Commuting.

What is it?

“city” does not have natural uniformity. The article is intended to compare the physical scope of the city, which may refer to an administrative city, a densely populated core of the city, or a commotional urban area. [1][2] Administrative boundaries are often drawn along roads, rivers or historical boundaries and may not necessarily coincide with a continuous build-up area; the same place name may vary in size under different statistics. There are also open areas, waters and agricultural land inside city, so the total area within baseary is not equal to the construction area.

How?

The OEDD identifies the city with a continuous build-up area, and integrates the surrounding areas that are closely linked to the centre into the central urban area; Eurostat sets the core of the city at at least 50,000 inhabitants and requires at least 15 per cent of the employed people in the Commuring area to work in the city. [1][2] Specifically in the sample, the Shanghai Statistical Yearbook of the Shanghai City Bureau of Statistics states on its Natural Status page that the city has an area of 6340.5 square kilometres, approximately 120 kilometres north and south and about 100 kilometres east and west. [3] The Shanghai Profile page of Xinhua gives the same order of magnitude. [4]

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes as sample values approximately 100 km from east to west of Shanghai City District (1×10⁵ m) and about 120 km from north to south. [3] [4] Administrative regulation cannot be interchangeable with the city core, financial urban area: the same city may vary several times depending on the size of the city. To reproduce this number, it is necessary to fix the city, baseary version, year and direction; the old 10 km indicative value is not available and is no longer used. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

“city size is a diameter” is not the right: it has an irregular shape, and it can be a narrow and condensed area; population is not equal to span. Nor can the area be directly squared as a true width. [1][2] Another misconception is that all land in the administrative baseary is counted as bilt-up area; often mountains, waters and agricultural lands are included in the baseary. When comparing city, indicate first whether it's an administrative city, the core of the city, or a nonactional urban area, before talking about size.
