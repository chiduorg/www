Grain is the largest object in the main asteroid belt, with an average diameter of approximately 940 km. It has an equatorial radius of about 490 kilometres and a polar radius of about 455 kilometres, and is therefore not a rigid sphere.

What is it?

Grain is the largest object in the main asteroid belt and the only dwarf in the asteroid belt. It consists of rock and ice, with a large number of Meteorite pits on its surface and famous bright spots. The amount of this article is its volume equivalent mean diameter, i.e., a slightly flat ellipse is converted to the same volume sphere. [1] The equator of the Vale Star is out of the diode, so the “diameter” must indicate which direction.

How?

Britannicantry gave an equatorial radius of 490 km and a polar radius of 455 km, based on the observation of the Dawn detector, and specified its volume equivalent diameter of 940 km. [1] The NASA Asteroid Fact Sheet shows a three-axis diameter of 965 x 961 x 891 km, with an average of about 939 km and an equivalent of only about 1 km from Britannica. [2] The Dawn orbits the Vale Star from 2015 to 2018 and binds shapes together with image and gravity data; ground-based observation provides long-term orbital parameters.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 940 kilometres, corresponding to 9.4×10⁸ metres. It is neither the maximum axle 965 km, nor the shortest axle 891 km, but rather the diameter of the volume sphere. [1][2] This number does not contain bright spots or local ups and downs on the edges of the crater, nor does it include thin water vapour exterior layers. If the equatorial width is discussed, 980 km of equatorial diameter should be used; if the discussions are extreme to height, 910 km of polar diameter should be used.

Common misunderstandings

A common misunderstanding is the view of the Goliath as “a big stone”; the Dawn found that it had the rock core, the ice magma and a possible ancient ocean, which had been polarized internally. [1] Another misconception is that bright spots are formed by direct exposure to aqueous ice; observation is more supportive of salt deposition, which is a high-reflective material left by the evaporation of salty water bodies. [2] There is also a confusion between the Goliath and the Goliath: the Goliath is bigger, darker, and the Goliath is brighter but smaller.

Relationship with other scale

940 km is about 0.27 times the diameter of Moon 3,474 km and 1.8 times the diameter of the 526 km of Katosaki, but only 0.40 times the diameter of Pluto 2,376 km. [1][2] It's much smaller than Mars 6,779 km. The 50-long orbital axis of Goliath is approximately 2.77 au, located between Mars and Jupiter, and is an internal member of the main asteroid belt. [1]
