Tectonic Plate

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The tectonic plate is a huge rigid block that has been cut open by the circle. This article refers to 5,000 km as the typical width of large plates and does not represent the measured width of any plate.

What is it?

The tectonic plate is a massive rigid block formed by the round circle cut open by baseary, encompassing both the central crust and the top of the oceanic crust and the upper mantle beneath it. This is the typical width of a large plate, taking 5,000 kilometres as a reference for order of mass; it is not the exact size of a particular plate or the thickness of the plate. [1][2]

How?

The plate baseary is illustrated by the location of the seismic zone, the volcanic zone, the mid-ocean ridge and the transect, while the plate movement is based on GPS, very long baseline interference and magnetic anomaly bands. [1][2] USGS indicates that the size of the plate ranges from hundreds to thousands of kilometres, with the Pacific and Antarctic plates being the largest; the thickness ranges from less than 15 kilometres in the young ocean ' s rock circle to about 200 kilometres in the old continent ' s rock circle. [1] Therefore, the terms "width" and "thickness" must be separated.

Value and Measurement condition

This article uses 5,000 km, which is the integer reference taken from USGS “hundreds to thousands of kilometres” order of magnitude, and is used to place the plate in a number of axes; it is not the measured width of the Pacific plate or of any plate, and the specific plate may be significantly larger or smaller than it. [1] There is another group of plate thickness: Oceanic rock circles can be less than 15 km and ancient continents can be more than 200 km. [1][2] The width is horizontal, the thickness is vertical and cannot be interchangeable.

Common misunderstandings

“The plate is the continent” is wrong: many contain both the continent and the ocean, and the Pacific is almost full of ocean rock circles. It is also wrong to treat board as a fixed line, and it moves, collages, cracks and changes the position of board. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to provide for the same “standard width” for all plates; microclates are only hundreds of kilometres and large plates can reach thousands of kilometres.

Relationship with other scale

The 5,000 km represents 78 per cent of the Earth ' s average radius of 6,371 km, 167 times the average thickness of 30 km of the earth ' s continent crust and 565 times the height of Everest ' s 8,848.86 m. It describes the horizontal extension of surface plates, not the inner radius of the Earth. [1][2]

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
