Elephant (African elephant reference)

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Elephants are large mammals in African elephants. The shoulder height of this article is a vertical distance from the ground to the highest point of the shoulder, not a height above the head; it is referred to as approximately 3.35 m of the male African elephant.

What is it?

Elephants are large mammals in African elephants, and there are mainly African elephants and Asian elephants, which differ in shoulder height, ear shape and shape. The shoulder height referred to in this article is the vertical distance from the top of the ground to the shoulder, not the height of the head, nor the overall height of the nose lifting with the elephant. [1][2]

How?

The WWF Africa elephants page recorded the shoulder height of African elephants as about 11 feet, or 3.35 metres. [1] Animal Diversity Web has a much smaller record of elephants in Africa: male shoulder height 3.2-4 m, female 2.2-2.6 m. [2] One to typical values and one to male and female ranges; both indicate that shoulder heights vary according to sex and individuals and cannot be summarized in a single number for all elephants.

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes 3.35 metres, which is a high shoulder reference for African pictoid males, and falls within the range of 3.2-4 metres given by ADW. [1][2] It's not a typical female value, nor does it represent an elephant or an Asian elephant. Measurement shoulder heights typically allow natural standing to measure the vertical distance from the ground to the top of the shoulder; headtop, ears and raised ivory noses are not included. Post positions, ground leveling and individual age influence readings. Females are clearly short and overestimated if 3.35 meters were applied to females. [2]

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that shoulder height is considered to be “the height of the head of an elephant”. The shoulder of an elephant is often higher than the top of the head, and shoulder height is the ruler of the four-footed animals; the top of the head is given different numbers. [1][2] Another misconception is the neglect of gender and type: Africa is about 2.2-2.6 metres for females, Asia is usually smaller, and 3.35 metres is just a reference for African males.

Relationship with other scale

3.35 The shoulder height is approximately twice as high as that of an adult of 1.7 metres, slightly higher than that of a giraffe of 3.3 metres, but well below the total giraffe height of 5.5 metres or more, and well below the length of a blue whale of 30 metres. [1][2] On the scale axis, it's like standing at the upper end of the “migrade large terrestrial animal”: building and redwood up, dogs, cats and people down. Shoulder heights allow elephants to be placed on the same ruler as giraffes, human beings and intestines without additional interference from ears or noses.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
