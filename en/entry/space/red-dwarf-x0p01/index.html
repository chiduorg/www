Red Dwarf × 0.01

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

2.097×10^06m based on 2.097×10^08 m, x 0.01 conversion; used to link adjacent scale anchors, not new independent.

What's the value?

The value of this article is2.097×10^06m, obtained from the base reading of the Red Dwarf Star 2.097×10^08 times 0.01. The base intestry is about 1.5 times the diameter of the neighboring star; conversion doesn't change the physical object, it just changes what level we put it on scale. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review:2.097×10^08m x 0.01 =2.097×10^06m. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the Red Dwarf to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks “What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller than it” it is possible to directly report this article without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with neighbouring stars (40302711813194210 m); the Sun (1391.14 million m), depending on whether the unit is unified or whether the comparator is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The base entry summary provides a readable starting point: the red dwarf is mass around 0.08-0.6 sunmass, low massstars luminous by hydrogen fusion, and the most common star type of universe. This page, sampled by a neighbouring star, is about 1.5 times the diameter of Jupiter, i.e. about2.1×10⁸metres. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]
