Neutron Star

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The neutron star is the wreckage of Star's core after its collapse. The NASA NICER description describes its compact scale in a diameter of about 20 kilometres, which is used for this introductory reference.

What is it?

The neutron star is a dense remains of the collapse of the large massstar core, consisting mainly of neutron, with a radius of only a dozen kilometres and capable of accommodating one or two solar mass. [1][2] It's not a black hole, it's not a white dwarf after normal Star has burned out. Typical neutron stars are simple and compacted, with strong magnetic fields and thin atmospheres on the surface.

How?

The existence of neutron was initially extrapolated from the pulsating star ' s rules for radio pulses and was later tested by X-ray observation, double-star orbit and gravitational wave event multi-channel paths. [1][2] NASA's NICER task is to reverse the radius and mass by combining gravitational lenses with changes in the X-ray brightness of the pulsed star. [1] Britannica summarizes its diameter of about 20 kilometres and mass of about 1.18-1.97 solar mass. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

This article uses an introductory reference of “about 20 kilometres in diameter”, i.e. 20,000 metres. [1][2] This is an overview of the order of magnitude, which is not the latest radius of a neutron; the true radius depends on the mass and the internal physical equation, and the measurement remains model dependent. [1] The NICER ' s observation is intended to reduce this uncertainty. The pulsating stars are calculated in relation to the X-ray pulsing contours to give a mass-radio relationship, but it is still difficult to distinguish between the equations of different material states.

Common misunderstandings

Common misconceptions are that all neutron stars happen to be 20 km or that density analogies such as “a spoon of mass” are used as accurate data. Twenty kilometres are a common overview. [1] 20 km diameter corresponds to about 10 km radius and about 1 in 70,000 solar radiuss. Another misunderstanding is to confuse the neutron with the black hole: the former with the surface of observation and the latter with an event horizon. [1]

Relationship with other scale

About 1 in 70,000 parts of the solar diameter of 1.39×10⁶ m, 20 kilometres of which are equipped with one or two solarmass, a density much higher than the white dwarf. [2] When compared to the Earth ' s diameter of about 12,742 kilometres, the neutron diameter is only about one per cent of the Earth. Similarly, the white dwarf radius of mass can be thousands of kilometres, while the neutron is even smaller.
