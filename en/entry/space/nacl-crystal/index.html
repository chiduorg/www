Sodium Chloride Crystal

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

NaCl is a rock salt structure ion crystal, Na-Cl's recent neighbour's distance is about 0.282 nm; a crystal beam is about 0.564 nm, twice the nearest neighbour's distance.

What is it?

Sodium chloride is salt and is arranged by sodium and chlorine ion in three-dimensional crystals by rock salt structure. [1] Each ion is surrounded by six alien ions, forming an octopus-based position. [2] This page indicates approximately 0.282 nanometres of Na-Cl ' s nearest proximity, i.e. the distance from a sodium ion to the nearest chlorine ion centre, rather than the length of the whole crystal.

How?

The electron distillation experiment gives Na-Cl's recent proximity range r0 = 2.82 E at constant temperature pressure; crystal density and structural model give an independent second estimate, consistent with the results. [2] In the rock salt structure, the ion rotates in the direction of a cubic axis, so that the length of the crystal beam a is equal to twice the nearest adjacent distance, i.e. about 5.64 E, i.e. 0.564 nanometer. [1] This multiple is geometry, not another measration.

Value and Measurement condition

2.82 E is equal to 0.282 nanometres, describing the nuclear spacing of the alien ion; 5.64 E is equal to 0.564 nanometres, describing the length of the crystals. [1][2] The recent proximity distance is twice as long as that of the crystal, and the two cannot be interchangeable. If 0.282 nanometers are called "NaCl molecule diameter", they are also not available: there are no separate NaCl molecules in the ion crystal, only a ion network that repeats over time.

Common misunderstandings

One of the common misconceptions is that the crystal constant 0.564 nanometer is considered to be a Na-Cl distance; the nearest neighbour is only half its distance. [2] Another misunderstanding is that 0.282 nanometers are considered to be the ion radius of Na+ or Cl-; the sum of the radius is approximately that distance, but the individual ion baseary depends on definition. [1] There are also those who mix the nearest proximity distance in the crystal with the watery ion spacing in the solution and the hydrate layer significantly alters the effective distance.

[1][2] It connects the "ion key length" to the "crystal cycle": the same 0.282 nanometer repeats twice to form 0.564 nanometer-long crystals.
