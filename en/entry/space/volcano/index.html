Volcano

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

This article uses, for example, Hawaii's guillotine Narcaia shield volcano, a peak height of 4205 metres relative to sea level. It is not the total height of the cone from the seafloor base, nor is it the diameter of the volcanic vent.

What is it?

Volcanic formation is the terrain of magma, ash and gas that accumulates from the surface. This article takes the example of Hawaii's guacayan shielded volcano, the height of which is not the total height of the cone from the seafloor base, nor the width of the vent. [1][2] Nakaya is a shielded volcano with a flat slope, which is made up of multiple periods of lava flows.

How?

The USGS indicates an altitude of 4205 metres at the peak of Nacaya, while noting that it extends about 6,000 metres below sea level, to a total altitude close to 10211 metres. [1] The volcanic page of USGS shows a peak height of 4207 m in “Quick Facts”. [2] The difference of 2 metres between the two is the difference between the baseline and the whole; the article uses 4205 metres and retains another value in uncertainty.

Value and Measurement condition

4205 m is the elevation of the sea, i.e. the vertical height relative to the sea level; the total altitude is about 10211 m if the seafloor base is taken into account. [1][2] These two numbers cannot be used intermingled: one describes “how much above the sea surface” and the other describes “how high from the ocean floor to the peak”. Volcanic cone height may also indicate the relative highness of the point of fire to the surrounding ground, which is the third type of surface. Relatively high levels of volcanic cones are also affected by the surrounding surface altitude, so that the same volcano can be reported at different heights.

Common misunderstandings

“The height of the volcano is the altitude of the summit” is incomplete: the altitude begins only at the sea level, and most volumes of the submarine volcano are below sea level. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to mix volcanic height with volcanic width; shielded volcanoes can be wide and relatively low. The peaks of submarine volcanoes may still be underwater, with negative elevations, at which point the altitude measured from the base should be used. At the height of the Citation Volcano, the baseline and endpoint shall be indicated.

Relationship with other scale

The 4205 metre is approximately 48 per cent of the snow surface of Everest at an altitude of 8848.86 metres, but above Everest altitude when measured at 10211 metres from the seafloor base to the summit. [1][2] It is less than 0.04 per cent of the Earth ' s 12,742 km in diameter. Volcanoes belong to surface topography scale, both above city and far smaller than planets.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]

Based on BIPM definition's vacuum light speeds of 299,792,458 m/s, it takes about 14 microseconds for light to pass through this length, order of magnitude on the mime axis. [3]
