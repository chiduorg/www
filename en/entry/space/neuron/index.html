What is it?

Neuron is the basic function of the nervous system, the cyll, which consists of cysts, trees and axes. The diameter of the cytogenesis is mostly measurement on fixed, dyed tissue slices, with common neanderthal or immuno-chromosomal chromosomal choreography[1]. The thickness of the slices, whether the sliced face passes through the centre of the cell, and the fixed contraction affects readings; the same cell may have different diameters on the different sides. A more sophisticated study uses a focus microscope or electron microscope to re-establish a three-dimensional contour and separate measurements of the size of the cyll-type cylles [2].

Value and Measurement condition

The reference to the “typical neuron” cylindrical diameter is about 20 μm[2]; organizational information gives ranges ranging from cell approximately 6-8 μm for small brain particles to cell approximately 60-80 μm for Pucanno and for the front corner cell[1]. Benentry uses 20 μm as an introductory reference, Measurement convention is a cylindrical diameter and does not contain tree and axles. It is a representative value, not a fixed value for all neuron; perception, motion, meso-neuron and the cells of different brain regions vary considerably.

Common misunderstandings

“neuron is large” is a common misunderstanding: small brain pellets are just a few μm smaller than red blue cells; the real “big” is Pucanno cell or front corner cells, and their suddenness is more alarming [1]. Another area of error is the size of the cell as a whole; a cyst of 20 μm can reach the axle of a metre. It was also assumed that the size of the neuron remained static and that, in practice, growth, learning and pathology could be accompanied by changes in cytology and sudden onset.
