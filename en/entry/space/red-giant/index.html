Red Giant

What is it?

The red giant star is the stage where the outer layer swells and the surface cools when sunstars leave the main sequence. [1] This page contains an overview of the diameter at this stage, not the exact ball of a single red giant, nor the size of a red superstar or a white dwarf.

How?

Star-moderized samples of red giant stars combined with observation give a radius. In introducing an ageing Star swallowing planet, NASA pointed out that some 5 billion years later the Sun would undergo a similar process, possibly swelling 100 times the current diameter and swallowing Mercury, Venus and possibly even the Earth. [1] The average radius of the volume of the Sun is 695,700 km from the NASA fact sheet, approximately 1.39×10⁹ metres in diameter. [2] To swallow the Venus orbit, the radius must be close to 0.7 historical unit, which is a greater model value than "100 times the diameter".

Value and Measurement condition

This article takes approximately 3×10¹¹ metres as the diameter of the solar class red giant star phase order of magnitude, equivalent to approximately 1 radius unit, covering the inner planetary orbit. [1][2] This is not the exact value given by a single model: the red star continues to expand on its branches, with a diameter that can rise from dozens to hundreds of times the diameter of the sun; and Britannica also points out that the volume of the giant star can differ from the superstar by several order of magnitude. [3] Reporting on the size of the red giant star must therefore be accompanied by a description of the evolution phase, mass and the model used.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that the Red Star is considered to be “the larger sun that is burning”; its core is still in processes such as helium burning, but the outer layer has swelled and cooled and the surface is reduced. [1] Another misconception is to mix the Red Star with the Red Superstar: the mass and radius of red superstars such as Cebu Four are larger and cannot be applied to the same values as those of sun-like red giants. Also, “100 times the diameter” was used as a fixed limit for all the red giant stars; it was simply a general science.

Relationship with other scale

3×10¹¹ Mi approximately 2 original unit, more than 200 times the diameter of the Sun and more than 20,000 times the diameter of the Earth. [2] If the sun is placed in this diameter, it has a radius of about 1 astromical unit sufficient to swallow Mercury, Venus and approach Earth orbit. It is about four order of magnitude in diameter, but it is still much smaller than a red superstar such as Cebu; it is placed in the solar system and can see how the fate of the planet's orbit can be rewrited in the late years of star expansion.
