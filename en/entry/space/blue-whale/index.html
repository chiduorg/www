Blue Whale

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The blue whale is the largest animal available. NOAA gives a maximum length of 110 feet (approximately 33.5 metres) and distinguishes between approximately 90 feet and 110 feet of records in different areas; 30 metres is used as an easy-to-comparison reference on this page.

What is it?

The blue whale is a type of whale that uses krill as its main food, filtering from seawater. It is the largest existing animal, blue and gray, with wrinkles in the abdomen and jet holes on top of its head. [1][2] The article compares the total length of the adult body from the end of the kiss to the end of the tail, not the width of the body weight, the height of the water spray or the breadth of the fin leg.

How?

The NOAA Blue Whale page in Quick Facts lists a maximum length of 110 feet and indicates that the larger individuals in different areas are about 90 feet; it also gives a maximum weight limit of 330,000 pounds and a life expectancy of approximately 80-90 years. [1] Britannica ' s blue whale entry describes the blue whale as the largest animal on Earth, with an upper limit of about 98 feet (30 metres) and an indication of its distribution, feeding and endangered condition. [2] Both sources support “30 morder of magnitude”, but the specific ceiling relates to samples of the sea area.

Value and Measurement condition

30 meters equals 3,000 centimeters. It corrects the length of the record of the blue whale to an easily comparable scale rather than an individual ' s precise measration value. [1][2] The total length usually ranges from the end of the kiss to the end of the tail; a different number is obtained if the attitude of the water column, the chest fin spread or the body bend is included. The 90-foot NOAA is about 27.4 metres and 110-foot is about 33.5 metres, indicating that the same blue whale also exists between different seas and between different individuals.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “blue whales are all 30 meters”. Thirty metres are easy-to-remunerated references, with actual individuals likely to be about 27 metres or more than 33 metres; record values are not equal to averages. [1][2] There is also a misunderstanding that the weight is directly proportional to the weight of the body: the weight of the body is approximately increased by the cube relationship, and a longer blue whale is much heavier and cannot be inferred from the length alone.

Relationship with other scale

The 30-metre equivalent of 17 1.7-metre-high tails and the equivalent of three 10-metre buses. [1][2] It is still small compared to Earth, mountains and mountains; but it is at the level of a giant creature compared to the animals we know daily. Up are mountains and Earth scale and down are human beings, hands, insects and bells. The blue whale makes the scale tens of metres visual.

This article uses SI metre-based length units, following the BIPM definition of the metre. [3]
