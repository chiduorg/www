Earth's Crust

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The crust is the rigid rock layer of the outermost layer of the Earth. This article measures the average thickness of the continent crust, using the USGS for approximately 30 kilometres of measration convance, not the overall rock circle thickness or the crust thickness below a given mountain.

What is it?

The crust is the rigid rock layer of the Earth ' s outermost layer, divided into the central crust and oceanic crust. The volume of this article is the average thickness of the continent crust, which takes about 30 kilometres; it is not the thickness of the entire rock circle or the thickness of the crust below a given mountain. [1][2]

How?

The thickness of the earth ' s crust is mainly measured by seismic refraction and reflection: the wave speed of artificial or natural earthquarkes mutates near the Mojo surface and can reverse the depth of the interface. Drilling can reach only a very shallow layer and the deepest scientific boreholes are just over 10 kilometres away, so deep thickness depends on geophysical inversion and rock samples. [1] [2] Gravity, magnetism and satellite altitude will also provide constraints.

Value and Measurement condition

USGS states that the ocean crust is generally only about 5 km thick, averages about 30 km and can be as deep as 100 km below the mountains. [1] Britannica writes about 40 km of typical crust thickness. [2] This article uses 30 kilometres, since the question is “average”, where USGS clearly gives an average value; 40 kilometres are more typical of the general continental zone, and the difference is from the sample area and round. The thickness of the crust also changes slowly with erosion, sedimentation and tectonic squeeze, so the average is only available for large-scale statistics. Either way, the continental and marine crust cannot be combined into a single thickness.

Common misunderstandings

“The Earth's crust is the soil” is wrong: the soil is only a thin layer of the surface, and the crust is a thick layer of rock that includes granite, basalt, etc. There is also a mistake in mixing the crust with the Rock Circle, which also includes upper mantle tops, usually 80 to 100 kilometres thick. [1][2] There is also a misunderstanding about the height of Everest as the thickness of the earth ' s crust; the roots of the mountain extend down, but the height of the mountain is different from the thickness of the earth ' s crust.

Relationship with other scale

The 30 km is about 0.47 per cent of the Earth ' s average radius of 6,371 km, 3.4 times the peak range of 8,848.86 m and 2.7 times the maximum depth of the Mariana Trench of 10,935 m. It looks thick, but it's just a thin layer in the Earth's radius. [1][2]
