Diamond Unit Cell

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Diamonds are carbon-based cube structures with conventional crystals bordering approximately 0.357 nm; C-C keys measuring approximately 0.154 nm by a√3/4 conversion.

What is it?

Diamonds are a homologue of carbon, and carbon atom is organized according to the diamond cube structure: each carbon and four carbon keys form a three-dimensional network. [2] The page uses approximately 0.357 nanometres to indicate the length of the edges of the conventional cube crystals, i.e. the elasticity of the basic box describing the cyclical repetition of crystals, rather than the length of a carbon-carbon key.

How?

The structure of diamonds is determined by X-ray distillation and crystalline model. The literature gives a crystal-scale constant of room-temperature diamonds a around 3.567 E, space group Fd3m, carbon-carbon keys about 1.54 E. [1] The teaching materials list carbon as a crystalline crystal of diamonds in 3.566 E, which corresponds to three signatures. [2] Crystal cyst lengths are derived from the location and indexation of distillation peaks and are not estimated from the shape of the crystal.

Value and Measurement condition

3.567 E equals 0.3567 nm and approximately 0.357 nm. [1] In the structure of diamonds, the nearest neighbour of carbon atom is located at a quarter of the diagonal line with a √3/4, or about 1.544 E, or 0.154 nanometer. [1][2] So the crystal is about 2.3 times the length of the key; the two are not the same. The originals are smaller, and the conventional crystals contain eight carbonatoms.

Common misunderstandings

One of the common misconceptions is to consider 0.357 nanometers as C-C long; the key length is only about 0.154 nanometres. [1] Another misunderstanding is the distance between the crystal lobes as the two closest adjacent carbonatoms; the nearest adjacent distance along the diagonal line is multiplied by √3/4. [2] There are also people who mix the distance between the crystal cells and the graphite layer: the graphite layer is approximately 0.335 nanometer, with different structural dimensions and key sizes.

Relationship with other scale

The nearest neighboring distance of Nacl 0.357 is 0.282 nanosized and slightly greater than the benzene ring ' s carbon skeleton space, but it is still much smaller than protein and cell. [1][2] It returns the cyclical unit "Crystals" to the scale axis: Cystolic border lengths describe repeat cycles, and long keys describe inter-tom connections, which together determine the distillation and mechanic properties of the crystal. It contains eight carbonatoms, from which the crystal volume and density can be verified.
