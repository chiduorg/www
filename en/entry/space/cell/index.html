Human Cell

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Cell is the basic structural unit of life; this page uses 20 micrometres as an introductory reference to the nuclei cell, describing an overview of teaching materials from 10 to 100 micrometres, the size limits of the cell and the measurement condition.

What's a cell?

Cell is the basic structure of life, which is wrapped out by the cell membrane, and which contains both the cell mass and the genetic material. The real kernel also has the membrane bell core, as well as the nematodes, the internal network, the Gorkid, the green leaf etc.; the original bell does not have these membrane structures. [1][2] The “human cell” is a scale reference on this page and does not mean that the human body has only one cell.

Common

OpenStax summarizes the diameter of the real nucleus cell at about 10 to 100 micrometres and the original nucleus at about 0.1 to 5.0 micrometres. [1] This range is an overview at the level of teaching materials and not a uniform constant; the cell of different organizations, species and stages of development can fall outside its reach. This page takes 20 micrometres in order to give an easy-to-read midpoint on the axis and to allow readers to compare it directly with a 1 microbacterium reference; Britannica also sums up the typical scale of human cell in about 20 micrometres. [2]

What are the size limits?

Cell can't grow up indefinitely. In near-spherical terms, volume grows by radius, surface area increases by square; when volume becomes larger, the membrane area is relatively inadequate and the transport efficiency of diffusion falls. [1] Cell increases effective surfaces by dividing, flattening, extending or developing membrane systems. Shapes are function-related: the same volume, with long or folded cells covering a larger exchange.

Common misunderstandings

“All quells are 20 μm” is not valid: 20 μm is only a presentation value selected for this page; 10 to 100 μm of teaching materials is just a common range. Cell size, internal structure and lifestyle are different dimensions and cannot be judged by size and complexity alone. [1][2] In addition, the distinction between the original nucleus and the genuine nucleus quell is in the quell core and membrane quell, not just in size.

Relationship with other scale

On the axis of this station, the human cell lies between the bacterium (about 1 μm) and the hair (about 100 μm).
