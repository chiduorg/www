Neptune

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The volume equivalent of Neptune is 49,244 km in mean diameter and is doubled by an average radius of 24,622 km on a bar layer. The equatorial diameter is approximately 49,528 kilometres and the polar diameter is about 48,682 kilometres.

What is it?

Neptune is the eighth planet near the sun and the most external of the eight planets. It belongs to the ice giant planet, with the atmosphere dominated by hydrogen, helium and methane, which absorbs red light to make the planet dark blue. The amount of this article is the mean diameter of its volume equivalent in a bar of pressure. [1] Neptune has a dark and weak ring, but this article does not count.

How?

The NASA fact sheet lists 24,764 km of the 1-bar layer equatorial radius, 24,341 km of the polar radius and 24,622 km of the volume average radius, mainly from Traveller 2 ' s 1989 ejection, ground occultation and subsequent orbital tracking. [1] Neptune does not have a solid surface, with a radius of 1 bar air pressure as a reference, as in the case of Uranus. Owing to the distance, ground access relies mainly on spectroscopy, light detection and star shelter, the details of which are largely supplemented by the short flight of Traveler 2. Britannicantry gave about 30.07 au a semi-long orbital axis and 16 known satellites and recorded the dark spots and wind speeds that had arrived in 1989. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

Based on 2x24,622 km, 49,244 km is taken from this article. The equatorial diameter is approximately 49,528 kilometres and the polar diameter is about 48,682 kilometres, or 846 kilometres. [1] This number does not contain rings and does not include the bright spots of the clouds. Neptune's rotation cycle is about 16.1 hours, and rapid rotation is the main source of its flat rate.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that Neptune is larger than Uranus; it is indeed larger by mass, but it is slightly smaller by volume average diameter, 49,244 km to 50,724 km. Another misconception is that “dark spots” are used as a fixed terrain; it is actually a vortex in the atmosphere that moves, deforms and even disappears. [2] There are also people who mix Neptune ' s distance with its diameter: 30.07 au is orbit scale, not planetary width.

Relationship with other scale

49,244 kilometres are approximately 3.86 times the Earth ' s diameter of 12,742 kilometres and 0.35 times Jupiter ' s 139,822 kilometres. It's about 3% smaller than Uranus, but more than half smaller than Saturn's 116,464 km. Neptune's semi-long orbit is about 30.07 au, the largest of the eight planets, about 30 times the orbit radius of the Earth. [2]
