Shielding and effective nuclear charge

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Increases in nuclear charge do not amount to an increase in the attractiveness that electron feels. The interior Electron shield allows only a fraction of the nuclear charge to be seen in the outermost layer, a margin that explains three trends in ionizing energy, tom radius and negative power.

What's the active nuclear charge?

The attraction of atom to check one electron is partially offset by the exclusion of others. The net charge actually felt by electron is called a valid nuclear charge, which can be roughly written as a nuclear charge minus shield constant. [1] This amount is not a simple discount for nuclear charge: the inner layer electron is shielded more thoroughly, the same layer electron is shielded less well, and the penetration capacity of different sublevels is different. Many of the “trends” on the element axis are essentially the result of changes in effective nuclear charge cycles or along ethnic lines.

Why does the same cycle rise from left to right?

In the same cycle, each additional proton, nuclear charge plus one; the new electron enters the same valence shell, which is weak in shielding itself and its peers. As a result, the effective nuclear charge has risen steadily, the valence of the electron has been tightened, the atom radius has contracted, the first ionizing power has risen and the negative power has risen. [2] The element of thorium, thorium and kryptonite shells is at the height of this trend, so that they have the highest ionizing capacity in their respective cycles. That's the "closed shell stability" quantitative source.

Why does the upper to lower ionizing drop?

Main quantum numbers have increased along ethnic lines, valence electrons are on average further away from the core, and the number of Electrons in the inner layer has increased significantly, and the total number of shields has risen. Although the nuclear charge has also increased, most of the additional nuclear charge has been offset by the addition of an inner layer Electron, which has experienced limited growth in the effective nuclear charge at a valence. [1] The result is increased radiuss, decreased ionizing energy and increased metal properties. The cleanest demonstration of this pattern is the ability of alkaline metals to ionize from lithium to caesium.

How it changed the way you read the element page.

With the concept of shielding, “one more proton” is no longer automatically equivalent to “more difficult to lose Electron”. When you are relatively close to element on the element page, you have to ask which layer is added to the new electron: entering the same valence shell, increasing the effective nuclear charge and increasing the ionizing power; entering the new shell layer with a sharp increase in shielding and decreasing the ionizing power. The difference between sodium to sodium, sodium to potassium, krypton to calcium is explained in this way. Without this layer, the periodic table is only a neatly organized table.

Common Misunderstanding and Boundary

The first misperception was to treat the shield as “the inner layer of Electron in front of the core” as if Electron had a fixed orbit; it was actually the average Electron cloud exclusion. The second misconception is to estimate the effective nuclear charge using a simple subtraction method and treat it as an accurate value; the shield constant given by different approximation methods is different and cross-methods are meaningless. [3] The third misunderstanding is to ignore the relativity effect: for gravity, the inner Electron speed is near the speed of light, orbital contraction changes the shield, and the unusual properties of mercury, gold and platinum come from here.
