Solar-System Elemental Abundance

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The abundance of the solar system is based on silicon, with helium in absolute majority, iron peaks visible, and heavy eliment is double-strangling with s/r processes. It's the heritage of the interstellar cloud that was mixed 4.6 billion years ago, not the universe average.

What is it?

The abundance of the solar system is described in the interplanetary cloud that formed the solar system 4.6 billion years ago. [1] Common measure convention is the equivalent of a million copies of silicon and gives the relative number of other elements. Hydrogen and helium make up the bulk of the mass, followed by oxygen, carbon, americium, nitrogen, magnesium, silicon and iron; there is a significant build-up near the iron peak, and heavy element displays strange alternations.

How?

The solar spectra gives volatile element abundance, and the CI ball particle meteorite gives the improbable element and iisotopepe benchmarks, which are combined into a set of “sun system abundance scales”. [2] Sotope anomaly in the meteorite also distinguishes between nucleo-synthesis components of different sources. By comparing the abundance curve to the mass of the s process, the r process, it is possible to determine which path the main contribution of an element is.

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This abundance is on the formation nodes of the solar system, a snapshot of that generation of interplanetary clouds, not today ' s universe average, or the abundance of the Earth ' s crust. [2] Distinction occurs in the formation of the Earth: iron nickel sank into the core and volatile element escapes, resulting in significant differences between crust abundance and solar abundance. When comparing the content of element, it is important to indicate whether it is the Sun, the Meteorite or the Earth's crust.

Common misunderstandings

The abundance of the solar system is not “universe abundance”, but different galaxy and different times of chemical evolution. [1] It is not the product of a single process: helium comes from the original nuclear synthesis, carbon dioxide from Star, iron peaks from supernovas, heavy element from s and r processes. It is not directly deduced from the high abundance of a given element that it is “easy to form” and that it depends on the amount and stability of the fusion product.
