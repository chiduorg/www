Fantasia and extra stability

According to nucleus model, when the number of protons or neutrons is equal to 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126, nuclear restraints are significantly increased, and these numbers are known as illusions. The number of protons and neutrons for helium-4 is 2 and therefore extremely stable; the number of neutrons for lead-208 is 126, one of the most heavy double-fiction nuclei. [1] Fantasies explain the peaks of many element abundances in universe and why certain nuclei, far from the stabilization line, still have half-lifes.

How do you choose the decay way?

Α decay releases helium, suitable for renuclear reduction mass; β decay converts neutron and proton, suitable for adjusting the neutron proton ratio; gamma decay releases only the stimulation and does not change the type of nuclide; and a very heavy nuclide can also be spontaneous. [2] A nuclide often has multiple branches, which vary with the nuclide. When labeling “radioactiveity” on the element page, it is important to indicate which type of decay is dominant, otherwise the reader cannot judge its impact on abundance and chemical behaviour.

Common Misunderstanding and Boundary

The first misconception is that “stabilization” is considered to be “permanent”; stability is simply half-life much older than the age of universe, and in theory it may still be decay. The second misconception is to link atommass to nuclear stability, which is considered to be more unstable; in fact, the heavy nucleus near the line of stability can remain stable, the key being the neutronproton-hull effect. The third misconception is to treat predictions as proven: the super-heavy “stabilized island” currently has only theoretical support and a small amount of experimental leads, and the element page must separate predictions from reality.
