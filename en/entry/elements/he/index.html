Where did the 2nd Proton put Electron?

Helium on the right side of hydrogen. The second proton enters the atom core, the second electron still enters 1s instead of a new sublayer; the configuration changes from 1s1 to 1s2. [1] The key to this step is pairing: two Electrons occupy the same 1s orbit and, contrary to the rotation, the cologne between Electron and the increase in the nuclear charge coincides. The effective nuclear charge is higher than hydrogen, but it has not doubled because another 1s electron is shielding the core. The result was a rise in the first ionizing energy from 13.598 eV to 24.587 eV, an increase of 10.989 eV and a reduction in the radius from 53 pm to 31 pm. [3] 1s capacity is exactly two, and helium fills the position; the next electron can only wait for lithium 2s. There's no more space for the same sublevel here.

The main reason for these six is that 1s is filled and paired, and it directly determines the jump in ionizing energy and the contraction of the radius. The vacancy in the negative electrical element is not a missing data, but a result of a closed shell: there is no space in the valence shell, and helium does not need Pauling scale to describe electron tendencies. The difference between nuclear source and N/Z is nuclear: two neutrons, from hydrogen to helium, make 4hes an exceptionally tight core, which is two things that cannot be mixed into a chain of cause and effect.

Why does it matter?

1s2 The immediate consequence of a closed shell is chemical inertity: the valence shell is full and the first ion energy is 24.587 eV, which is neither easy to lose Electron nor low-energy space orbits to receive Electron, so helium is hardly formed under conventional conditions. [2] It has two stable isotope 3 He with 4 He; 4 He with 2 protons with 2 neutrons, which is why alpha particles always appear in the form of helium. Chemically closed shells are the same name as nuclear physics here, but they correspond to two different energy sets: the former explains why helium does not react, and the latter explains why helium cores are stable. Where did it come from?

The helium nuclear source is marked as the original nucleo-synthesis and star hydrogen combustion, with the first half hanging at time.big-bang node on thetime axis and the second half hanging at star hydrogen combustion. [1] During the initial synthesis phase, proton and neutron were combined into a cylindrium, followed by 4he; because of the high combined power of the 4he, it became the end point of this short chain, with the vast majority of the neutrons being sealed in the helium core. The Star phase continued to increase helium stocks by placing four protons in helium through hydrogen combustion. The helium abundance is high not because it is particularly easy to form, but because the chain of original nucleo-synthesis is stopped at 4he and Star is constantly replenishing. [2]

How do we keep going?

One step forward is hydrogen, one step forward is lithium; hydrogen provides raw materials and lithium displays 1s and Electron is forced into the next step of 2s after full capacity.
