Lead-208

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Lead-208 atomic number Z = 82, mass number A = 208, stable; lead-208 is the stable end point of the thorium-232 decay chain and isotope, the highest proportion of natural lead; thorium-lead systems provide a clock independent of uranium-lead.

Nuclidesdefinition

Lead 208 is a type of sotope: atom core containing 82 protons and 126 neutrons, mass A = 208. The chemical properties of isotope are largely determined by the proton numbers, but the neutron numbers change the nuclear stability and the decay approach and therefore cannot be treated as the same nuclide. [1]

Why does it matter?

Lead 208 is the stable end point of the thorium-232 decay chain and the highest proportion of sotope in natural lead; the thorium-lead system provides a clock independent of uranium-lead. This nature gives it an independent position in the thorium-lead system and in the isotope study; the highest proportion of natural lead means that it is usually not a pure substance “if there is” in the laboratory, but is separated or enriched from other sotope with element. [1][2]

How about measurement?

On measurement, the mass spectra is separated by mass difference and isotope ratio is read; radioactiveisotope is also determined by decay count, scintillation or accelerator mass spectrometry. The endpoint of the thorium-232 decay chain provides a physical basis for timing or trace, but sample preparation and background deductions tend to influence results more than instrument readings. [1][3]

Put back the periodic table

Put Z = 82, A = 208 back on the elemental table, and you can see it's on the sotope position of Pb; when you compare it with the adjacent nuclides, look at the difference between the numbers of neutrons and the half-live and decay products. half-life is not a “life guarantee”, but a timescale of a large number of atoms. [2] [3]

This article separates the words “element” and “isotope” so as not to confuse atomic number Z with mass number A; all values apply only to the nuclides and conditions indicated in this article. Measurement with Error

Isotope is usually measured by mass margin; radioactivity nuclei can also be measured by decay, scintillation or accelerator mass spectrometry. Sample preparation, background deduction and standard matter deductions affect ultimately uncertainty more than instrument readings, so half-live or abundance is reported together with methodology, sample type and error range. [1][2]

Compare with adjacent nuclides

The comparison is made by fixing the number of protons Z, then by looking at the number of mass A, which is different from the number of neutrons; the different sotope chemical behaviour of the same element is close, but nuclear stability, decay approach and natural abundance can be completely different. Mixing “element” with “nuclides” is the most common error on this page. [2] [3]

Finally, the Z, A, half-lives of this article are copied in the same line of natural abundance to check whether they point to the same nuclide; if one does not match, then go back to the source table and do not fill out the data from the adjacent nuclides.
