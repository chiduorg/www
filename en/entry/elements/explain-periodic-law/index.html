Why the periodic table has this shape

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Mendeleev forms element by nature, leaves spaces and predicts unknown element; quantum mechanics later proved that those spaces correspond to the fixed capacity of the electron crust. Periodicity has thus evolved from a pattern of experience to a structural inference.

There's a watch, then an explanation.

In the nineteenth century, Mendeleev ranked known element according to its atomic weight and chemical properties, and found that the nature was cyclical. On this basis, he left spaces and predicted the nature of unknown elements, such as thorium, which were later confirmed. This table was the rule of experience at the time: why there is a cycle, nobody knows. The element axis is separate from this history because the “cycle is so long” is not self-evident and it was a phenomenon that needed to be explained.

Tomic number replace atomic night

At the beginning of the twentieth century, atom nuclear and Proton were discovered, and it was realized that it was the nuclear charge that determined the identity of element, not the atomic weight. After sorting by atomic number, the original positions of “atomic weight upside down” naturally reverted, with the rows and columns of the periodic tables having a physical meaning for the first time. [2] This is a crucial step: it transformed “element” from a chemical classification to “the same class of proton atom” and paved the way for later electron fragmentation theory.

Quantum mechanics gives capacity.

Pol and the subsequent quantum mechanics placed Electron on discrete energy levels and substrates with a capacity of 2, 6, 10 and 14, respectively. Filling in these capacities in energy order gives them a cycle length of 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32. [3] The space left by Mendeleev corresponds to the position of the sublayer that has not yet been filled; the inert gas is “not responding” and the valence shell is just filled. The pattern of experience has become structural inference.

How it changed the way you read the element page.

Once you understand the cycle, the location information on the element page is no longer decorative. The cycle tells you which floor you're filling, the family tells you what the valence shell is, the block tells you whether it's s, p, d or f. Seeing that a certain element “should” appear in the table to be of a certain nature, you will ask: what is its valence shell, whether there are any anomalies in the inner layer and whether the nuclear level is stable. The periodic table thus changes from a map requiring memory to a set of denotable coordinates.

Common Misunderstanding and Boundary

The first misconception is to consider the periodic table as a complete list; it lists confirmed or named element, which may structurally extend beyond 8s, but whether or not it can be synthesized is another matter. The second misconception is that the number of the ethnic group is simply matched to the valence electron; the dominant group is largely established, and the transition is not the same as the transition. The third misconception is that the cyclical rule has been fully explained; the relativity effect of overweight element, stabilizing the position of the island, still depends on calculations and a small number of experiments. [1] Each page of the element axis shall retain this “known sense of self”.
