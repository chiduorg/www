Electron configuration and the Aufbau order

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The nature of the element is not flatted by proton numbers, but by electron. This page explains the Aufbau order, the staggering of 4s with 3d, and why chromium, copper, platinum and platinum are exceptions.

The rules themselves

The basic agreement for electron's bilges is that neutral aom enters orbits by energy from low to high, each orbit containing two reverse electrons. This rule, usually known as the Aufbau doctrine, corresponds to the capacity of each sublayer (s2, p6, d10, f 14), and gives the basic configuration of most element. [1] The order was then 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s, 5f, 6d, 7p. The length of each line of the periodic table is the result of the order being cut off.

4s, why is it in front of 3d?

If only the number of primary quantum, 4s should be higher than 3d. But the real energy is determined by the number of primary and angular quantum, and 4s electron is more capable of penetrating the inner layer, is less shielded and is slightly less than 3d. So the nineteenth Electron entered 4s instead of 3d. [2] This staggering is only apparent when the adjacent energy level is approaching; to the heavier element, 5s and 4d, 6s and 4f/5d are similar, resulting in the length structure of the periodic table.

It's not the exception, it's the result of competition.

Chromium should be 3d44s2, measured as 3d54s1, and copper as 3d94s2, measured as 3d104s1. The reason is that half full d substrates are more advantageous in exchange, and the energy gap is enough to move one electron of 4s into 3d. [2] Similarly, 4f75d1, 5d96s1 of platinum. These are not “rules wrong”, but the premise of the rule — that the order can be fixed — is no longer strictly established in these elements. The element page must be written in a physical configuration and cannot be rewritten for completeness.

How it changed the way you read the element page.

When the order of filling is known, the " same cycle from left to right" is no longer a parallel of nineteen cells, but a process of " one Electron in the same layer ". See the sodium, you'll think of 3s just starting; see the thorium, you'll think of 3p full and 3d empty; see the iron, you'll think of 3d being filled and 4s already occupied. The column on the element page, “What is more than Z-1”, essentially asks: which sublevel is this Electron in, and whether it changes the valence or the inner layer.

Common Misunderstanding and Boundary

The most common misperception is that the order of the placement is considered to be absolute and capable. 4s and 3d have changed with the atomic number, and 3d is below 4s in the transition period, which explains why the transition metal was lost first. The second misunderstanding is that rules are used as a formula for predicting everything: the configuration of overweight element is based on relativity and experimental data are extremely limited. [3] The third misunderstanding is to treat the same configurations as being of the same nature - the same valence shells are similar and different internal fillings change the effective nuclear charge by shielding.
