Hydrogen

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Hydrogen has only one proton and one 1s Electron, no Z-1 comparison; 13.598 eV ion energy and 53 pm radius, providing the first ruler for helium, lithium and the whole element axis.

Where did the first proton put Electron?

Hydrogen is the first grid on the element axis: a proton, an electron, and 1s1. [1] This Electron enters the 1s orbit of the smallest primary quantum, without the inner layer Electron shielding the nuclear charge, so it feels the effective nuclear charge close to +1. 1s has a capacity of two Electrons, half of which is hydrogen; this half-full state is both the starting point for hydrogen and the prerequisite for helium to continue to fill the same substrate. Helium to the right: The second proton still puts Electron in 1s, with two Electrons swirling the opposite; the increased nuclear charge is accompanied by increased exclusion between Electrons, which together push first ion. Hydrogen does not have a 4s/3d staggered problem, and structural changes take place only in the innermost layer, but are sufficient to determine the order of filling of all of the later elements. [3] In one sentence, 1s1 of hydrogen is both the lowest point of energy and the point of discussion of shielding and effective nuclear charge.

Relative Z-1: Six Differences

  • First ionizing energy: 13.598 eV for hydrogen; no starting point Z-1. Forward helium, 24.587 eV, 10.989 eV higher than hydrogen. [3]
  • Atom radius: 53 pm for hydrogen (calculated value medical condition); no starting point Z-1. Look forward to helium, helium is 31 pm, hydrogen is 22 pm larger than helium. [1]
  • Electro-negative: Hydrogen is 2.20 (Pauling scale); no starting point Z-1. Looking forward to helium, helium is unrecognized Pauling power negative, unable to give travel value.
  • Valence electron configuration: 1s1 for hydrogen; no starting point Z-1. Looking forward to helium, helium 1s2, more electron is still 1s, just paired with the original electron.
  • N/Z: Number of mass of hydrogen A = 1 at N = A-Z = 0, N/Z = 0; no starting point Z-1. Forward helium A = 4, N = 2, N/Z = 1.00, 2 neutrons more than hydrogen.
  • Nuclear source: Hydrogen labelled original synthesis; no starting point Z-1. The helium, helium, is the original nucleo-synthesis and star hydrogen combustion, which are both raw materials for the original nucleo-synthesis and fuel for star hydrogen combustion.

The main reason for these six is the fact that the starting point is no Z-1 and the capacity of 1s is two structures: it determines that hydrogen's radius and ionizing energy can only be used as an absolute ruler, not as a relative margin. The high ionizing energy and small radius are the accompanying result of the same 1s Electron being held tight by the +1 nuclear charge; the negative power of 2.20 indicates that hydrogen can get Electron in some keys and lose Electron in others. A forward comparison with helium suggests that the second proton does not result in simple doubling, but a non-linear rise after pairing and shielding.

Why does it matter?

1s1 of hydrogen has only one valence electron, which concentrates its chemical behaviour on getting an Electron and losing an Electron: the former gives hydrogen ion, the latter gives proton, and the two hydrogenatoms share a pair of Electrons give hydrogen molecules. [2] The electronegativity of 2.20 are in the middle, so hydrogen changes roles in the face of different partners, forming both ion-type hydrogen and covalent hydrogen. Its two stable isotope are 1H with 2H, 3H with 12.32 half-life; mass is sufficient to change the vibration frequency of the hydrogen-containing molecule without changing the 1s1 valence shell skeleton. [2] The first ion energy 13.598 eV also gives one of the cleanest atom spectroscopy: there is only one Electron, with minimal correction, which is the reference for subsequent ionation. [3]

Where did it come from?

Hydrogen's nuclear source is marked as original nucleination, a clue that is directly attached to the time.big-bang node of the time axis. [1] When the universe swells to temperature not enough to sustain the fusion of proton and neutron, the nuclear reaction is frozen and the vast majority of protons remain in the form of hydrogen cores without finding the neutron pairs; the americ and helium have only taken part of them. So the abundance of hydrogen is not the product quota of a Star, but the remaining amount after the universe cooling rate versus the nuclear response rate. Star did burn hydrogen later, but that was the consumption of hydrogen into helium, not the remaking of hydrogen; the original nucleination was the first and largest distribution of hydrogen stocks. That's why the lightest element is the most.

How do we keep going?

Hydrogen does not have Z-1 and can only go right: the helium grid then fills 1s, and the lithium grid shows 1s when Electron is forced to enter 2s. Another scale to look at it.

Hydrogen is the wealthiest element in the UNiverse, accounting for about 75 per cent of the mass of ordinary matter. Its 21 centimetre spectra is used for radio astronomicals, proton is the fuel for fusion of the Star. The nuclear combination of hydrogen is low compared to iron, and fusion to iron releases energy; the electron structure of hydrogen is the simplest when compared to helium.
