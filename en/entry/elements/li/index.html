Why does it matter?

The valence shell for lithium is only 2s electron, first ion energy 5.392 eV, negative electricity 0.98, which points to the same thing: it tends to lose this electron and to form +1 ion, rather than share it with another atom. [2] 2s electron is far away from the nucleus and is shielded by 1s, which is the structural cause of this tendency to defeat electron; this is also why lithium ' s chemical main line is an ion-type compound rather than a covalent network like carbon. Lithium has two stable isotope 6Li and 7Li; isotopesmass affects vibration and diffusion behavior, but does not change the 2s1 valence shell.
