Lead: the 82nd proton adds 6p2 at the end of the decay chains

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The 6p of thorium adds an Electron and lead becomes 6s2 6p2; 6s2 forms inert Electron pairs, making +2 vs. +4 common and four stable isotopes make lead the end point of the Decay chain.

Where did the 82nd Proton put Electron?

The valence shell for thorium is 6s2 6p1. Upon the arrival of the 82nd proton, the new Electron entered 6p and the lead base became [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p2. [1] In terms of configuration, 6p is filled to two, four times less than the full shell; but it is not the "under" of 6p2 that really determines lead chemistry, but the "too stable" of 6s2. The relativity effect is low on 6s, and two 6s electrons are reluctant to participate in the key formation, creating what is called inert electron; lead is therefore often found in +2 instead of +4.

This is also the end point for nuclear stability. Lead consists of 204 Pb, 206 Pb, 207 Pb, 208 Pb and four stable iisotope, with the last stop of the natural radioactivity decay chain falling on lead: uranium, thallium and thorium alpha, and beta decay down, and eventually iisotope of lead. [2] First ionizing energy rises from 6.109 eV to 7.417 eV, increasing 1.308 eV; radius from 156 pm to 154 pm, reducing by 2 pm; electronegativity from 1.62 to 1.87, increasing by 0.25. [3]

Relative Z-1: Six Differences

  • First ionizing energy: 7.417 eV; thallium 6.109 eV, increase 1.308 eV. [3]
  • Atom radius: 154 pm (calculated); thallium 156 pm, minus 2 pm. [1]
  • Negative electricity: 1.87; thallium 1.62, increase 0.25.
  • Valence electron: Lead 6s2 6p2 and thallium 6s2 6p1 with new Electron entering 6p.
  • N/Z: mass 208, N = 208-82 = 126; N = 204-81 = 123, new number 3
  • Nuclear source: Lead is the end point of the s process and the r process; the origin of the thirium is not recorded and cannot be compared with the measurement condition.

The main reason is that 6p adds an electron, while 6s2 inert electron “press” to the valence shell: 6p electron feels a higher effective nuclear charge, so the ionizing energy rises 1.308 eV; the radius is only 2 pm less, indicating that 6s2 and 4f14 have locked their dimensions. Electro-negative rise 0.25, which is the result of 6p filling. The number of neutrons increased by 3, bringing the 208Pb to 126 neutrons, which, together with 82 protons, makes lead nuclearly stable; the nuclear source's “s process end” explains why the heavy elementdecay chain will eventually run out here.

Why does it matter?

Inert Electron effects are central to lead chemistry. 6s2 after stabilization of the relativity effect, the loss of these two Electrons takes a high valence, and lead therefore prefers +2: Pbo, Pb2+ salt is much more common than +4; +4 is pulled out only in combination with high-power negative or in a specific level environment. [1] This is not “lead is not lively”, but rather it concentrates reaction in a narrow window - 6p2 electron can be involved and 6s2 electron basically bystands.

The larger consequences are on the nuclear side. Of the four stable iisotopes of lead, 206 Pb, 207 Pb and 208 Pb are endpoints of uranium, thorium and thorium, respectively, and 204 Pb have a source; thus, the lead iisotope ratio is a tool for measuring ore age and tracking lead source. [2] It is no coincidence that an element is the end point of the chemical inert Electron to the classics and the nuclear descay chain: 82 protons and 126 neutrons combined, bringing it close to closing down at both the nuclear and electron levels. Lead is thus often used as a binding point for heavy eliment axes rather than another card of a nature.

Where did it come from?

Lead nuclear source is recorded as s process endpoint and r process. s The process slowly increases the nuclides in the giant branch, and when it reaches the lead, the competition between the neutron capture and the alpha decay makes it difficult for the substance to circulate near the lead and the americium, meaning the “end point”; r The process directly throws heavier nuutron cores from the combination of the neutron and falls back to lead through the decay chain. Both routes contribute to lead, but the "endpoint" identity of the s process is more specific.

The complementarity of lead with uranium can be seen by bringing it to the heavy eliment node of the time axis: uranium is produced in the r process, after the long half-liftecay of 238 U, and eventually leads are found; lead is therefore both a product of the nuclei synthesis and a product of radioactivitydecay. Lead is higher than gold and platinum in abundance, partly because of this “endpoint pile” - many of the heavier cores eventually remitted to lead's isotope. The uranium half-lifeentry on the time axis is supplemented by the length of this waiting period.

How do we keep going?

It was previously mercury, 6s2, closed, 6p empty, a pre-lead comparison; then back to uranium, 5f filled in, and nuclear stability gave way to radioactivity.
