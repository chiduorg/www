Star core burns hydrogen into helium, and the pp chain with the CNO cycle is two main paths. CNO recycling uses carbon, nitrogen and oxygen as catalysts to dominate hydrogen combustion in the more hot core.

What is it?

There are two main paths to hydrogen combustion at the core of the star. pp. The chain gradually combines two protons into americium, helium-3, which eventually produces helium-4; the CFO cycle uses carbon, nitrogen, oxygen as catalysts, and captures the proton with beta decay, turning the four protons into a helium. [1] CNO cycle is more sensitive to temperaturee and dominates the larger, more hot corestar of mass.

How?

The Sun's neutrinos are the most direct evidence: the ppp chain and the CNO cycle produce neutrons with different energy, and detectors can count separately. [2] Star spectra gives surface carbon dioxide abundance, and constellation time lines give age and mass relationships, which are mutually supported by the calculations of the nuclear response network. The catalytic nature of the CNO cycle also explains why carbon nitrogen is “recycled” within the Star rather than being net consumed.
