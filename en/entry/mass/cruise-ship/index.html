Queen Mary 2

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Queen Mary II has about 79,000 t, or approximately 7.9×10⁷ kg. 1QX, often used by the Citation, is a volume gross tonnage (GT), not mass; mixing GT with drainage will almost double the ship's weight.

What's this reading?

Queen Mary II has about 7.9×10⁷ kg, or 79,000 t. [1] It was placed on a transatlantic route in 2003 with a total length of 345 m, 41 m wide, capable of carrying approximately 2,700 passengers and more than 1,200 crew members. Drainage follows the Akimid principle definition: seawater mass that is drained from the hull is equal to the total mass under which the ship eats.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

Three volumes are most likely to be used in ship data: gross tonnage, heavy tonnage and drainage. The total volume of the vessel is approximately 1.49×10⁵, which is the volume of one ton per 100 cubic feet and the schematic is volume, not kg. [1] When it's mass, you get 1.49×10⁸ kg, this widely spread error reading. Heavy tons are loaded only for goods, fuel, fresh water and supplies, and equally not for ships themselves. This article uses only full-load discharges and indicates that it is the engineering value of the standard seawater density conversion.

How?

During the design phase, water-eating volumes are derived from the type of wiring, and drainage is obtained by the water density; the water is followed by an oscillation test, a static graft and a water reading correction at a test voyage. [2] The classification societies have written this curve into a steadyness manual so that crew members can find current discharges by reading the water scale. So this number is the result of calculations and tests, not the suspension of the ship.

Order of magnitude

7.9×10⁷ kg equals approximately 60,000 domestic cars, or 530 blue whales, or 7.8 times the Eiffel Tower structure mass. [1] It is just below the10⁸ kg threshold: it is also called the “100,000-tonne class” and the carrier has crossed the line with the large freighter, which is still a little short. The same ship could be more than 20,000 tons less empty than full, so we have to talk about mass first.
