GW150914 merger

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

1.23×10³² kg, 62 solar mass. The values were derived from a gravitational wave-form inverse, relative to uncertainty, about 4 per cent, which was the first direct human detection of a double black hole combined.

What's this reading?

GW150914 is a gravitational wave incident recorded by two LIGO detectors on September 14, 2015, from a combination of two black holes outside of approximately 1.3 billion light-year. [1] The combined black hole mass is about 1.23×10³² kg or 62 solar mass, of which about 3 solar mass energy is radiationed in the form of gravity waves. This is the first time that humans have directly detected gravity waves. The whole event lasted about 0.2 seconds, peaking to vary1×10⁻²¹.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

Measurement convention is a combination of post-black hole mass, a time and space parameter for gravitational wave-shaped inverses and does not contain dark matter. [2] It describes the gravitational field of the conjoined product, rather than the “massing of the substance at the scales”. Relative to uncertainty, approximately 4%, classified as level A, most of which comes from wave model and detector calibration. This 4% is already high-precision in gravitational wave astronomy, and the detector is about half of uncertainty.

How?

The wave shape of two black holes in close proximity to three stages of radiation combined with the bell, the frequency and amplitude are coded as mass, spin and distance as the time changes. [2] Matching detected response signals with large numerically relativistic waveform templates to Beyers allows backshow of parameters and subsequent distribution. [3] Convergence of results from multiple independent templates is key to the credibility of this event. The whole convergence process is completed in less than a second.

Order of magnitude

1.23×10³² kg is located above the top of the B13 belt and is the transition mass between the Star class black hole and the medium mass black hole. The combination of two black holes, about 36 and 29 solar mass, shows that dozens of times the solar mass can actually pass and continue to grow up. This is also the first time that a gravitational wave has confirmed and combined to create a heavier black hole.
