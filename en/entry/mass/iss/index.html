International Space Station

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The International Space Station is in orbit ass. 419,725 kg, which contains cabin sections, pallets, solar wings, radiators and insider equipment and does not contain docking spacecraft. The combination mass increases temporarily by 7-20 t each time a cargo or manned spacecraft docks.

What's this reading?

The operation of the international space station is approximately 419,725 kg. This is the officially published space station home body mass: the cabin segment, the frame, the solar wing, the radiator plate, the mechanical arm and the inside equipment, excluding the docked manned and cargo spacecraft. [1] It runs in orbits around 400 km from the ground at about 7.66 km/s.

This reading describes a "station" and not a "assembly". When reading any space station parameters, confirm whether to include a visiting spacecraft, otherwise the difference is more than 10%.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

On-orbit mass will vary according to the mission: the combined mass will temporarily increase each time a cargo ship is sent for resupply, and the new equipment installed by astronauts will change the number. [1] 419,725 kg is a baseline value that is frequently used in the civilisation, grade A, from official statistics rather than estimates.

To distinguish a station from a combination: a manned dragon ship is about 12 t, a cargo dragon ship is about 20 t, and Progress is about 7 t. [2] These visitors docked for weeks to months, during which the answer to the “multiple space station” was indeed different. Writes it as a fixed value and ignores the dynamic nature of the in-orbit assembly.

How?

Mass isn't called on the ground. Engineers add up item by segment, plant by unit of equipment, and then use orbital forces to determine the orbital decay rate due to atmospheric resistance in conjunction with solar activity to determine ballistic coefficients, which contain mass and wind-precedented area, which can reverse mass. [2] The propellant budget required for attitude control also relies on the same set of massmode.

Participating agencies are responsible for the mass accounts in their own segments, which are then consolidated by multilateral coordinating bodies. [3] This “list-and-orbit verification” approach is the only viable mass identification method for large-scale on-orbit structures.

Order of magnitude

4.2×10⁵ kg equals approximately 320 Carola, or 2.8 blue whales. It's at the top of the B8 belt, the same ten-digit weight as 747, but it's almost twice the size of the latter. Mass here determines the propellant needed to maintain the orbit and how much altitude it falls each day.
