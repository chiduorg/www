What's this reading?

PSR J0348+0432 is a millisecond pulse star, close to two stars with a white dwarf consisting of approximately 2.46 hours of orbital cycle, approximately 2100 light-years. [1] Its mass is approximately 4.00×10³⁰ kg, or 2.01 solar mass, one of the most heavily measured neutron stars. It's rotational about 39 milliseconds. It belongs to a bigalaxy of pulsed stars and white dwarfs.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

"Measurement convention is a neutron stellar mass, belonging to stellar mass, which refers to all of the material that makes a dense star and does not contain dark matter; mass here is both gravity and weight mass. [2] Values are obtained from pulsating stars, relative to uncertainty approximately 0.5 per cent, and set to level A. Such a high degree of precision is derived from accumulations of years at pulse arrival. The nature of what is inside the neutron is so far uncertain and mass is the main constraint.

How?

When pulsed star signals pass near the companion star, the transmission path is curved by the companion's gravitational force, resulting in a millisecond delay in reaching the time. [2] The magnitude and shape of the delay depend both on the companion mass and on the orbital inclination, combined with the mass and radius given by the companion optical spectrum, and can release the pulse mass. [3] This method measured the neutron star mass at 0.5 per cent and also made the pulse star a target for the test of strong field gravity.

Order of magnitude

4.00×10³⁰ kg, located in the B13 band, is about twice the size of the Sun, but is condensed into a ball about 12 km in diameter and has reached nuclear material density. [1] Its presence pushes the upper limit of the neutron stellar mass to more than twice that of the solar mass and directly binds the state equation of the dense substances. This makes it representative evidence of the “twice solar mass” threshold.
