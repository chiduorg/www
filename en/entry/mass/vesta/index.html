Vesta

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The superstar is the second largest asteroid in the main belt. Radio-tracking by the Dawn detector when it surrounds itself forces relative uncertainty to about 1 per cent, a small number of asteroids measured by spacecraft at close range.

What's this reading?

As the second largest asteroid in the main belt, Kibo Star recorded its total mass of approximately 2.590×10²⁰ kg. [1] This number is the sum of mass of the body and contains rock, metal and surface weather layers and does not contain dark matter. It's about 11 times heavier than the Star of Spirit, but it's still only about 3500 percent of Moon.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

Measurement convention is total mass, containing all internal substances and holes. The Dawn detector tracks in orbit giving a relative value of approximately 1 per cent, so this article is set at level A, and body text retains four signatures. [2] Mass is independent of the diameter, volume: the volume from the shape model can only be used to calculate the average density of mass and cannot be used as a substitute for mass. The average density of the SOS is approximately 3.4 g/cm3 and is close to the undivided or only partially differentiated rock object.

How?

Dawn, from 2011 to 2012, orbiting the Measurement probe through Doppler, followed by a combination of gravitational field coefficients, J2 and orbital retrospect resonance mass. [2] This method is essentially a gravitational field, not a “heaviness”. The closer the spacecraft is, the longer the tracking arc, the easier it is to decipher the relevance of mass to low-level gravity field coefficients. The JPL small object orbital database also provides compatible gravitational parameters that can be cross-checked. [3]

Order of magnitude

2.590×10²⁰ kg is located in the lower part of the B11 strip, approximately 3.6 times lighter than the Vale Star, and about 2.8×10² times lighter than Moon. [1] It accounts for approximately 9 per cent of the total asteroid belt mass and is one of the few objects in the main belt that have been measured by spacecraft at close range.
