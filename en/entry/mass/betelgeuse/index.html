What's this reading?

Cebu IV is Orion Alpha, a red superstar about 550 light-years, about 700 times the diameter of the sun. [1] Its mass is about 3.28×10³¹ kg, roughly between 16.5 and 19 sunmass by model. It is one of the most visible Stars in the night sky, and it is at an advanced stage of evolution.

Measurement convention is Starmass, which contains all the substance of this Star and does not contain dark matter. This object can only be given to order of magnitude and should not be considered an exact reading of 16.5 or 19. If the distance or temperaturee changes by a few percentage points, mass moves several sunmass.

How?

The model method is the only viable path: first angular diameter, effective temperaturee and photometry are determined by interferometry and spectra, then they are placed on a star evolutionary trajectory to find matching initial mass and age. [2] Surface gravity is estimated by spectral shape as the third constraint, but it itself is not small. [3] None of the three constraints are sharp enough and the result is naturally only for the order of magnitude. There are often overlaps between areas given by different studies, but the central value is a few solarmass.
