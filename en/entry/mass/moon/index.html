Why is Mommass so accurate?

Moon mass is mainly not estimated by “size-looking” but by orbital dynamics: lasers measure the distance between the moonmeasurement Earth and the Moon reflector, orbital trackers give gravitational field coefficients, and the common moon mass motion constraint mass ratio. How does the laser reflector work?

Apollo 11, 14, 15 and Moon left an agular reflector array on the surface of the moon. The ground station fired lasers at these reflectors, and the measurement travels to and from the time and conversions of the ground at a monthly distance; multiple rangings can also produce minor changes in the Moon orbit. [2][3] Angular reflectors return the input light in its original direction, so that the echo strength is sufficient for detection. It is from such long-term ranging and orbital tracking that Mommass is bound by the distance from the ground to the moon.

Mass is not the same as gravity.

Moon mass is about 7.346×10²² kg, with only about one sixth of the Earth ' s gravity on the surface, as gravity also depends on the radius. If only mass is known and the radius is not known, surface gravity cannot be introduced; in turn, gravity alone cannot be the only certainty. [1][2] This is why planetary science always reports mass, radius and orbital parameters together.
