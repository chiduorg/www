What's this reading?

The Hoover dam, built in 1936, is 221 m high and the dam tops 379 m long. It is a concrete arch on the Colorado River. [1] The U.S. Reclamation Agency has published dam concrete volume of 3,250,335 cubic yards and recorded as 6,600,000 tons, or approximately 6.6×10⁹ kg. This reading is the mass of concrete in the dam itself and does not contain plant, floodways and metal structures.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

The Department of Reclamation separates the dam concrete from that of the whole project: the dam is 3,250,335 cubic yards, and the subsidiary buildings, including the plant and the water entrance, are up to 4.36×10⁶ cubes. [1] The “6,600,000 tonnes” in the same information is based on short United States tonnes and is converted to a public constraint 6.0×10⁹ kg; this article follows the official tonnage with order of magnitude and explains this difference in body text. Another bombary is steel and gates: they make up a small percentage of the entire project, but it cannot be said that “the dam is only concrete”.

How?

At the time of construction, concrete was produced by a mix of buildings, each plate of cement, gravel and water was recorded, and the squares multiplied by the wet density were massed, which was then reconciled with the design formula. [2] Dams are built in separate blocks, each of which is covered with cooling pipes to avoid thermal fragmentation of water. The concrete is siloed with 8 cubic-code canisters, pouring thousands of cans per day during peak periods, and the volume is accumulated daily. This record allows “3250,335 cubic yards” to become a retroactive amount of work, not an estimate after completion.
