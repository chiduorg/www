US nickel

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

In the United States, the statutory mass of 5 nickel centimetres is 5.000 g, i.e.5.000×10⁻³ kg; diameter 21.21 mm, thick 1.95 mm and composition 75% copper, 25% nickel. This is a single net weight, which is specified by the United States foundry.

What's this reading?

The United States statutory mass of 5 cents nickel is 5.000 g, or 5.000×10⁻³ kg. [1] It is made of 75% copper and 25% nickel alloys, 21.21 mm in diameter, 1.95 mm thick. This number is the net weight of a single coin and does not contain paper, currency boxes or transport packages, nor is it the total weight of a thousand boxes.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

This is a specification value, not an average sample value: while the Castery Authority controls production on a public average basis, the coins from the plant fluctuate slightly around 5.000 g and the wear and tear in circulation is reduced a little, but the specifications are recorded by law. [1] The distinction needs to be made with the “face value”: 5 points is a face value, 5 grams is mass, and there is no relationship between the two; nor is it to be misinterpreted as pure nickel, which is only one quarter. 25 cents of coins in the same series are 5.670 g and cannot be used intermingled; the alloy ratio has also changed in different years, and this article takes only the current copper nickel material.

How?

Statutory mass from alloy density and cake size in the design phase: Target volume and mass by diameter, thickness and alloy density, followed by stamped sampling. [1] Heavy equipment at currency mills will remove cakes that exceed the official rate by item or sample, while measurement will be placed on the mass scale of national standard agencies. [2] Following the revision of the international unit system in 2019, kg was converted from the Planck constant definition,[3] the statutory mass of coins was still expressed in grams, but the trachea was replaced by the base constant. The process is mature, so that 5.000 g can be written to three decimals and not to a false precision.

Order of magnitude

5.000×10⁻³ kg lies at the top of the B6 belt: about 10 times the size of a rectangular needle, about eight times the size of a 500 mg pill and 30,000 times the size of a 0.5 mm grain. [2] It is also a step between “small objects” and “kg”: 1,000 such coins are about 5 kg.
