What's this reading?

The Swan X-1 is an X-ray twin star, made up of an O-type superstar and an invisible, dense object, approximately 7,200 light-years. [1] The kinetic mass of the incisive body4.22×10³¹ kg, or 21.2 Sunmass, far above the mass ceiling of the neutron star, is considered a black hole. It was also the first widely accepted candidate for a black hole. It's located in Swan, one of the most important sources in the history of X-ray astronomy, and it's the starting point for black holes.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

Measurement convention is kinetic mass, which refers to gravitational mass, which is reverse by the companion orbit and does not contain dark matter. [2] The mass here is a time and space parameter that describes the strength and weakness of the gravity field, rather than the weight of the substance in the scales. This is also the reason why allentry does not write “how many tons” and mass only appears as gravitational field parameters. Relative to uncertainty, about 1%, set to level A.

How?

The spectral cyclical change of an O-type star gives an orbital velocity and a combination of an orbital inclination and a mass function, which can decipher the mass of the body. [2] The determination of the inclination is based on photometric and spectroscopy modelling of ellipse-like companion stars, a major source of error. [3] Multi-group independent analysis constricted to 21 near Sunmass to reach 1% accuracy. Once the inclination is small, mass is underestimated and vice versa overestimated.
