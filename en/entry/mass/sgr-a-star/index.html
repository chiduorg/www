What's this reading?

A* is the megamass black hole at the center of the galaxy, mass around 8.55×10³⁶ kg, which corresponds to 4.297×10⁶ solar mass. It's one of the most well-known megamass black holes. It's about 26,000 light-years, the nearest, and most easily followed by a Star-by-Star black hole.

Order of magnitude

8.55×10³⁶ kg is located in the middle of the B14 band, approximately 3,000 times heavier than the GCIRS 13E and about 1,500 times lighter than M87*. [1] It corresponds to an event radius of approximately 12 million km, much smaller than the Mercury orbit. For the Milky Way, the black hole, which accounts for only a very small share, determines the star movement of dozens of bright-years. Using the solar mass as a unit, it is equivalent to more than four million suns, but still represents only a small fraction of the total of stellar mass in the Milky Way.
