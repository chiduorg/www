Labrador retriever: 30 kg, mean adult body mass

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The adult body mass of the Labrador dog is approximately 30 kg, expressed as living body weight (total wet weight). The majority of dogs are 29–36 kg and the majority of dogs are 25–32 kg, and differences in health make uncertainty about 20%.

What's this reading?

The mass of the Labrador dog takes 30 kg, in terms of the average adult individual, wet weight (i.e., living body weight). Labrador is one of the most common dog varieties in the world, with adult dogs usually between 29 and 36 kg, and female dogs between 25 and 32 kg, 30 kg, a reasonable central value for this variety. [1] This reading clearly points to a named species rather than “a typical dog”.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

The living body weight is the wet weight of the whole animal, but it is stated that the measure conditions are: empty abdomen, fresh water, collars and towed rope. Anentry takes 20 per cent, covering gender, blood and physical differences; working dogs and race dogs are thin and pet dogs are obese, and the usual Veterinary Health Ratings set “overweight” at 10 to 15 per cent above the desired weight. [2] There are no decimals, because the same dog has several hundred grams of weight fluctuations in one day.

How?

The platform scales of veterinary clinics are standard tools, with large dogs allowing dogs to stand on their feet and read directly; in households, the difference in dog hugs is often greater. The average variety is derived from the body standard of the canine club, the medical history of the pet hospital and the sample size of the nutritional study. [3] The selection deviations for these samples are obvious: the dog who goes to the hospital is obese and the dog who goes to the competition is thin, so 30 kg can only be used as the central value of the species and not as the actual mass of a dog.

Order of magnitude

30 kg is about 43% of the adult 70 kg, about six and a half times heavier than a cat 4.5 kg and about nine times more than a newborn 3.3 kg. [1] It's ten times different from 300 kg of a triangle piano, and about six and a half times different from 200 kg of a motorcycle. Also known as the “average adult individual value”, the difference between dog and cat indicates that the variety and body size affect mass much more than the error.
