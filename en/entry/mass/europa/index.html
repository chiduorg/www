What's this reading?

Io II, the fourth Galileo satellite of Jupiter, records its total mass of approximately 4.800×10²² kg. [1] Measurement convention is the total mass of the celestial body, containing the rock inner core, the global ice shell and the liquid water layer beneath it, and does not contain dark matter. It's a little lighter than Moon, about 65% of Momass.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

General massmeasurement convention contains ice shells, oceans and rock components. The Galileo probe tracks in orbit giving about 1 per cent relative to uncertainty, class A, and body text retains four signatures. [2] The average density of Io II is approximately 3.0 g/cm3, indicating that it has both a rock and a large amount of water; but density and volume cannot substitute for massmeasurement, and mass still depends on gravity fields.

How?

In the years 1995-2003, Galileo was able to fly a number of times, and the ground-controlled measurement probe changed its speed before and after the raid, and was then replayed by an orbital accelerator and gravity field coefficient J2. [2] The closer the flight distance, the easier it is to separate the correlation of mass to the low-level gravity field coefficient. [3] Future Europa Clipper will give a higher accuracy gravitational field if he enters orbit.

Order of magnitude

4.800×10²² kg is located in the middle of the B11 zone, about one third lighter than Moon, about three times lighter than Io, and about 124 times lighter than the Earth. [1] Together with Io Io, Io and Io, it constitutes the Galileo satellite constellation, and the mass of the four together represents more than 99 per cent of the total satellite mass of Mogalaxy. Its mass, unlike Moon, is one of the most important targets of astrobiology because of the liquid water under the ice crust.
