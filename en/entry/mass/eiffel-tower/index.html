What's this reading?

The structure of the Eiffel Tower is approximately 1.01×10⁷ kg. This is the official structure of mass, with an iron exercise framework of approximately 7,300 t and the rest being floors, stairs, elevators, equipment and coatings. [1] The tower is 300 m high and contains the top antenna approximately 330 m, the highest building in the world when it was built in 1889.

This reading describes “the structure on the ground” and does not contain foundations or stone bases. It is not an estimate in the sense of “total weight”, but is the number given by the building block account maintained by the operator.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

The metal frame has to be distinguished from the entire structure: 7,300 t is 18,038 exercise iron components and 2.5 million nails of mass, 10,100 t to include platforms, elevators and ancillary facilities. [1][2] Bentry takes the official corporate structure value, mark A because it is published by the operator and is not estimated as volume.

Another often neglected part is the foundation. The four legs were sitting on each other ' s stone and the foundations and foundations were excluded from the above-mentioned mass. [2] Thus, 10,100 t is not " the weight of an entire mountain ", but the weight of steel and equipment. The coating appears to be insignificant, re-painting every seven years and using about 60 t paint.

How?

In the nineteenth century, when there were no computers, engineers were able to compute and add to the list of components manually; today the operator maintains the complete building block account, which is continuously reviewed through the material inventory and replacement records, rather than decapitating the tower. [1] The seven-year re-painting works provide both rust and replacement records that can be used to correct the accounts of the mass.

As part of the world heritage, the maintenance and alteration of the towers are subject to protected planning and any structural replacement is registered. [3] These files make “10,100 t” a retroactive engineering figure rather than the approximate number in the travel case.
