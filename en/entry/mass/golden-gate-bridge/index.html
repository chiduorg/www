Golden Gate Bridge

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The total number of bridges for official statistics in 1986 was 887,000 tons, or 804,700,000 kg; the bridge, which does not contain anchors and lead bridges, is approximately 3.82×10⁸ kg. The difference between two is twice, and the mix will turn the bridge in half.

What's this reading?

The total number of bridges for official statistics on the Golden Gate Bridge is 887,000 tons, or 8.047×10⁸ kg. [1] This number includes the bridge, two concrete anchors and a north-south pilot bridge. The original total weight at the time of transit in 1937 was 894,500 tons, which was reduced to 887,000 tons after conversion in 1986. The bridge is a full length of 2,737 m, with a main extension of 1,280 m and two bridge towers high of 227 m.

Measureconvention with uncertainty

The authorities gave two simultaneous measures: approximately 840 million pounds, i.e.3.82×10⁸ kg, at the time the bridge was counted without anchors; 887,000 tons were counted for anchors and lead bridges. [1] The anchor is going to carry the pull of the main cable into the ground, and the two concrete blocks are so large that the two numbers are nearly double. The article takes the sum of mass with anchor and lead bridge and specifies that it is a United States short-ton conversion value. When it is not stated that the bridge contains no anchor, the reader will assume that there is a difference between the two bridges.

How?

The design phase is based on a list of building blocks and steel density, with separate accounts for the main cable, booster beams, bridge towers and clamps; the operator recalculated the total weight in 1986 following the replacement of the bridge and the boosting structure. [2] This “list addition” is the normal practice for bridge works, as no one will weigh the entire bridge. Masses of main cables, clamps and cords are included in the list, and concrete and steel components in Sota are included separately to avoid duplication, which is why the list method gives hundreds of millions of tonnes of grade results. Its accuracy depends on the completeness of the list rather than on the accuracy of the measurement.
