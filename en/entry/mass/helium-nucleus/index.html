Helium nucleus

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Helium-4 atom nuclear (alpha particles) mass approximately 6.644657×10⁻²⁷ kg, or 4.001506 u. It is about 28.3 MeV/c2, a loss that is the source of fusion energy.

What's this reading?

Helium-4 atom is made up of two protons and two neutrons, or alpha particles. Its mass is approximately 6.644657×10⁻²⁷ kg, i.e. 4.001506 atomic mass unit. [1] This is nudity mass, excluding Electron; if helium atom mass is neutral, two electron masses will be required and the corresponding atom combinations will be deducted.

Where's the mass loss?

The sum of the mass of two protons plus two neutrons is about 6.644657×10⁻²⁷ kg plus 28.3 MeV/c2, i.e. about 5.0×10⁻²⁹ kg. [2] This segment of mass is released in gamma ray and neutron form in fusion. Helium-4 is about 7.07 MeV, one of the highest in the light nucleus, so it is both a product of fusion and one of the end points of the fusion chain.

How?

Helium mass cannot be measured directly, but is obtained by the mass spectrometry of neutral helium atom minus electronmas and atom. [1] This chain connects nuclear mass, atomic mass and electron mass; every step of the chain spreads to final values. entry uses atomic mass unit conversion to avoid multiple steps and decreases in body text.

CODA’s electron mass and atomic mass’s unit conversion point to the other end of the same chain, with two sources within the relative precision of 10⁻¹⁰. [3] If helium mass were combined into four nuclears, 28.3 MeV combined energy would be lost. [2]

Order of magnitude

6.645×10⁻²⁷ kg is the upper end of the B2 belt, about 3.97 times the hydrogen atom and 3.97 times the proton. It is close to the integer of the “four nuclears”, suggesting that the nuclear mass scale is very stable in the light nuclear; what really crosses the mass axis is atom, molecules and macro-objects, not the intra-nuclear differences.

The peak of a conic

The combination of helium-4 per nucleus is about 7.07 MeV, close to peak in light nucleus, and around 8.8 MeV near iron-56, followed by a gradual decrease in heavy nucleus. This curve determines how star fusion releases energy until iron, and how heavy fission releases energy. Helium cores are just a local height on the curve. [1][S2]

Alpha particles are helium-4 cores, excluding electron; neutral heliumatom mass add two electrons and deduct toom combined energy. When fusion products write “helium cores”, mass losses are only a nuclear reaction, not a subsequent Electron compound.

Helium mass losses are about 0.030 u, equivalent to 28.3 MeV; this energy is released in solar fusion with gamma rays and neutrinos, and is one of the sources of the Star energy.

Why is it so important to combine peaks?

The combination of helium-4 per nucleotide can be around 7.07 MeV, nearing peak in the light core; this makes it a particularly stable product in the fusion chain. [2] Star releases this energy when helium is concentrated; energy returns become smaller when further fusion becomes more heavy. Combining the curve determines Star's energy books.

Mass, combined with temperature, can decide on a synthesis. [1][2]

Read-ment convention and conversion

The quantity used in this article is 6.645×10⁻²⁷ kg, Measurement condition is 4 He atom nuclear mass, with two Electrons and atoms reduced by neutral helium tomms. Mass is wide and readings depend on wet/dry weight, blood/free of blood, package/full load, etc.; sample and measration conditions must be together. When comparing this reading to the adjacententry, replace it with the same unit and then take the margin; do not reduce it directly with a different value. If there is only one thing to remember: the answer is “the object probably falls on which order of magnitude”, it is not a constant that can replace all samples.
