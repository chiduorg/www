Early Mammalian Dental Evidence

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

About 225 million years ago, Brasilodon dental material was used by research teams to discuss early mammalian tooth exchange and classification. The Fossil records relate to the classification range used by “mammalians” and cannot be used to assign a common birthday to all modern mammals.

What is it?

And here's the late triple mammal, Brasilodon. It is a canine animal of small size and has been replaced with mammalian-like double-set teeth: a set of permanent teeth. [1] This does not mean that it is the direct ancestor of modern mammals, but rather that the dental characteristics associated with mammals are already present in the late triad. Fossil is mainly from the late triad basin in Brazil, with materials including several lower jaws and teeth, providing a rare sequence for comparing dental development. [1]

How?

The evidence came from three substrates at different stages of development. Researchers judge by the order of the tissue slices and the carving and replacement of the teeth that they have undergone an orderly replacement of the breast teeth - constant teeth - and compare this characteristic with current mammals and reptiles. [1] The branch system studies place Brasilodon in a specific position for the dog and rodents, indicating that “mammalian” is not in the same range as “capacitating mammals”. [2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

The original study set the Fossil layer at the Noli period at 225.42 ± 0.37 Ma; this was the result of a combination of radioactiveiisotope and biostitigraphy. [1] According to the International Stratigphy Commission 2024/12 edition, the trignoly period began around 227.3 Ma, consistent with this. [3] The time axis uses round 225 Ma as a reference and does not consider the number after decimal points as the new measurement precision.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings was to consider “the earliest mammals” as an absolute record without a classification range. Unlike the different studies using clusters, Brasilodon is a mammalian rather than a recent co-grain of all current mammals. [1][2] There is also a misunderstanding that the replacement of teeth is directly equivalent to the complete mammalian physiology; double teeth are only one of many characteristics, and other evidence is required for breast, hair and mid-ear structures. [2] There is also a misperception that double teeth are equivalent to all mammals; the number and pattern of tooth replacements in different branches are still changing. [1][2]

Relationship to other phases

Brasilodon, along with the first dinosaurs, suggests that mammals have coexisted with dinosaurs in the late triad. [1][2] They kept small and nightly eclogy positions throughout the middle generation until K-Pg was extinct. [2]
