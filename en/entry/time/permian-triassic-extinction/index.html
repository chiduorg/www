End-Permian Mass Extinction

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Some 251.9 million years ago, oceans and terrestrial ecology experienced an extremely serious biological crisis. Large-scale volcanic activity in Siberia is the main trigger hypothesis for this extinction, but the process of extinction is bound by multiple evidentiary chains and cannot be summed up in a single instant.

What is it?

The end-of-the-century extinction was the largest biological crisis of any Zionist size, about 251.9 Ma. The three-leaf, four-embracing corals, bed-bed corals and almonds in the oceans have all disappeared, most of the wrist-footing, mossworms and pyres have also suffered severe damage, as has the terrestrial ecology system. [1]

How?

Proof of this is a significant decline in diversity in the Fossil records: the United States National Parks Authority (NPA) gives about 96 per cent of the species, 56 per cent of the loss and 57 per cent of the loss, both marine and terrestrial. [1] On sratigraphy, this line can be tracked in multi-terrestrial sea phase with carboniisotope deflections and sediment variations near the line. [2] As to the reasons, Siberia ' s massive flood-flow basalt is considered the main trigger mechanism: CO2 and sulphur dioxide from volcanoes can lead to warming, acidification and oxygen deficiency. [1] This is a mainstream hypothesis, not a single cause that has been fully proven.

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

The International Stratigphy Commission, in its 2024/12 edition, sets the diode-triple line at 251.902 ± 0.024 Ma, which is bound by the year of radioactiveiisotope in conjunction with biotigigraphy. [3] About 251.9 Ma is a rounded representation; extinction is not instantaneous, and ocean and land records in different regions show a pulse or tens of thousands of years of process. [1]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings was that the Siberian volcano was the sole cause. Volcanoes, methane releases, seawater oxygen scarcity, acidification and warming may be magnified, and research needs to explain multiple evidentiary chains. [1][2] Another misconception is that “96 per cent of species disappears” is understood to be the simultaneous death of all organisms; survival groups and ecology recover for a long time, with sampling deviations also recorded by Fossil. [1]

Relationship to other phases

The crisis ended in ancient times, releasing eclogyspace for reptiles and dinosaurs. [2] The difference between it and the K-Pg extinction is that it is larger in scale and that the ocean and the land suffer simultaneously, but both indicate that the event of extinction will reshape the direction of subsequent evolution. [1][3]

Why is the ocean the hardest hit?

The loss of marine species at the end of the colossal period was approximately 96 per cent, much higher than the level of loss on land; ocean warming, acidification and oxygen deficiency can simultaneously hit calcified organisms, reef ecolory systems and benthic fauna. [1][2] Carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide releases from Siberian flood current basalts may be magnified by weather and seawater chemistry. The connectivity of the oceans allows environmental pressures to take place globally.

How long did it take to recover?

After extinction, the complete restoration of the oceanic ecology system took millions of years; the early trimesters experienced repeated environmental fluctuations and the recovery of reef-building organisms and large predators was slower. [1][2] “Emergency” is not an end point, but a process of economic collapse and reconstruction. Painting the recovery to zero would underestimate the subsequent effects of the crisis.

Five-axis Contrast

At the end of the time axis, the extinction was about 251.9 million years ago; at the space axis, the Siberian volcano was far away from the Tettis sea but was affected simultaneously; at the temperature axis, warming and oxygen scarcity were core environmental pressures; and at the mass axis, biomass and carbon pools changed dramatically. The extinction mechanism must be interpreted across axes. [1][3]

Species Loss Rate and Time Resolution

At the end of the colossal extinction, it is often written “about 90% of marine species disappear”, but species statistics rely on the Fossil database, sampling density and classification; estimates for different studies vary from 80% to 95%. Extermination can last tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of years, limited by the resolution of statigraphy. Body text ratio is a statistical estimate and is not a species-by-species death certificate. [1][S1]
