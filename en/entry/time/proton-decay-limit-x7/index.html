5.302×10^42s, based on the 7.574×10^41 s of the "protondecay lower limit", obtained by ×7 conversion; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is5.302×10^42s, obtained from the base reading of "protondecay floor" 7.574×10^41 s times 7. The baseline is the lower limit of the protondecay experiment, not the measured lifetime; conversion doesn't change the physical object, it changes the level of the scale on which we put it. [1]

How's that?

The algorithm is subject to gradual review:7.574×10^41s x 7 =5.302×10^42s. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the "protondecay lower limit" to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks “What it feels like to be bigger/smaller”, the article can be directly provided without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with black hole evaporationtime (6.6e+77 s); heat deathtime (3.1576e+107 s) to see if units are harmonized, then whether the comparator is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under different measurement conditions. The baseline entry sumary provides a readable starting point: the protondecay lower limit point experiment excludes the conclusion that “proton life is less than that” under the given confidence. The analysis published by Super-Kamokande in 2020 pushed the lower limit of p→e+0 to approximately 2.4×10^34; this is the lower limit, not the measured lifetime. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple.
