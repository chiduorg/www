The Great Oxidation Event

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The big oxidation event describes a long-term shift in the oxygen-containing state of the Earth ' s atmosphere. The NASA information places this phase between 2.4 billion and 2.1 billion years ago; the earlier short-lived oxygen trail and continuous atmospheric oxidation are not the same records.

What is it?

The large oxidation event refers to a long-term shift in the Earth ' s atmosphere from a largely aerobic to a low but persistent level of free oxygen. [1][2] It changes surface oxidation conditions and affects which microbes can flourish; it is not the moment when oxygen reaches 21% today. [2]

How?

Key evidence comes from Rock ' s geochemical records: the mass of sulphuriisotope, which is not dependent on fractions, disappears around 2.4 billion years ago, indicating the presence of UV-proof ozone and oxygen in the atmosphere; the emergence of iron oxide, red layer and uranium minerals also indicates oxidation conditions. [2] NASA's overview places the big oxidation event between 2.4 and 2.1 billion years ago and discusses earlier evidence of the “short-lived presence of oxygen”. [1] The sequence of the construction of strips of iron and the appearance of the red layer on the floor also provides independent, but not equivalent, evidence of oxygen rises. These are sediments and isotope signals, not direct samples of the ancient atmosphere. [1][2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

Benentry uses the distance of about 2.4 billion years today as a navigation point, corresponding to approximately 359.7×10¹⁵ seconds from Big Bang; the range was retained between 2.4 billion and 2.1 billion years ago. [1][2] This span is a summary of the geological phase, which is revised with the profile and year; the internal seconds are only used for sorting and do not represent the accuracy of the geologic approach. [2]

Common misunderstandings

It is common confusion to directly equate the older “shorter appearance” of oxygen with a large oxidation event; short signals may be limited to local water bodies and may not necessarily represent the global atmosphere. [1] Another misconception is that large oxidation events are treated as a single moment or cause; oxygen generation competes with consumption processes such as volcanoes, weather and ocean mixing. [2]

Relationship to other phases

It occurs after oxygen-producing photosynthesis has taken place, earlier than the expansion of the life of the real nuclear organism and the Docell. [1][2] Atmospheric oxygen rises to create conditions for the aerobic metabolism of complex cells and post-living animals, but there is still independent time evidence of the true nuclear origin, which cannot be determined directly by the large oxidation event. [2]
