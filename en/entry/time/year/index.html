What is it?

One year corresponds to a continuoustime of about 365.24 days for a week around the Sun. It measures how long it lasts, not the year in which it happened in the history of universe. In daily life, from one birthday to the next, from the seed season to the next, the cycle is roughly one year; however, there are a number of different years of astronomy.

How do you know or why?

Astronomy retrogression years, also known as solar years, are the times of two consecutive spring spots of the Sun, approximately 365 days, 5 hours, 48 minutes and 46 seconds, or 365.2422 days. Treating a year as a 365-day fix would ignore a year; treating an average calendar year as the exact value of a year of regression would also mix two kinds of meetings. Star year is about 365 days, 6 hours, 9 minutes, 10 seconds, and, unlike the year of return, cannot be replaced at will. [1]
