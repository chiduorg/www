Mammalian Recovery After the K–Pg Extinction

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Corral Bluffs' Fossil records show that K-Pg recovered about 1 million years after extinction. Mammals are gradually changing in size and diet; they are located in a specific research area and do not assign a single global radiation start date.

What is it?

This is about the recovery of the first 1 million years of mammal after K-Pg's extinction at Corral Bluffs, Colorado, USA. Fossil records show that surviving small mammals are gradually changing in size and diet, and that the herbs are shifting to more diverse forests. [1] Fossil includes teeth, skulls and phytoleaf blades, and the researchers form relatively continuous sequences by layer. [1]

How?

Corral Bluffs' lake sediment wraps Fossil in nodules, allowing for a more complete preservation of the skull and teeth. The research team collected layers by statigraphy, measurement reduced jaws and teeth, reconstructed body weight changes and compared plant Fossil with mammalian Fossil in the same profile. [1] This type of regional record shows the rhythm of recovery, but does not directly represent all the global subsystems. [1]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

About 66 million to 65 million years ago was this research window. According to the International Stratigphy Committee, version 2024/12, the K-Pg line is 66.00 Ma, and the ancient New Danny chain continues to 61.66 Ma, so the window is in the early days of the ancient Newities. [3] The time axis takes the front end for reference; the age of the Fossil layer is bound by the magnetic statigraphy and radioactive measurements, with an error at a specific level smaller than a million years scale. These layers span about 1 million years above the K-Pg boundary, and magnetic statigraphy provides a layered time framework. [1][3]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misconceptions is that mammalian radiation is considered an “emergence” immediately after extinction. Corral Bluffs records show that body size increases and dietary differentiation take hundreds of thousands of years and are accompanied by changes in herds. Another misconception is that the recovery curve of an area is globalized; the surviving clusters of different continents differ significantly from ecology conditions. [1][2]

Relationship to other phases

The K-Pg extinction emptied the large land eclogy position occupied by non-bird dinosaurs, and mammals expanded. [2] This recovery laid the foundation for later diversification of primates, cetaceans and herbivorous mammals, but the divisions of the times vary. [1] While recovery has also been recorded in other continents during the same period, the composition of the clusters, unlike the ecology conditions, cannot be lumped directly into a global curve. [1][2]
