What is it?

Pluto Zeus was the first time passage in the history of the Earth, usually from before and after its formation to about 4 billion years ago. It is not a rock or a location, but a time zone used to organize early Earth history. At that time, the solar system had just been formed by a concentration of gas and dust, with the Earth ' s surface experiencing frequent impacts and magma activity and the atmosphere and oceans still forming. The term “students” used to describe the circumstances as being different from those of today, but did not mean that the whole paragraph was in the same state. [1]

How?

Pluto Zeus has almost no record of a consecutive rock. Researchers rely mainly on meteorite, Moon samples, small amounts of ancient minerals and radioactivity to extrapolate early events over the years. Minerals such as zircon, like microtime capsules, preserve information on isotope at the time of formation; in combination with impact simulations and planetary formation models, it is possible to estimate the early time framework of the Earth. This evidence is bound by process and range, and does not amount to a record of each year. [1][2]
