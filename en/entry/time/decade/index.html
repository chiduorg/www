A Decade

What is it?

Ten years is the ten average calendar year continuoustime, about 3.1556952 × 10⁸ seconds. It describes the duration of the process, not the location of the event in which it occurred. In life, a decade is often used to sum up a person from childhood to adolescence, from entry to maturity, as well as to refer to a cultural period, such as “twenty-geologic age”, but the latter is a historical label that does not amount to an arbitrary decade.

How do you know or why?

Ten years without an independent SI unit, it's multiplied by ten years. Based on an average of 365.2425 days per calendar year, 10 years is 3,652.425 days; multiplied by 86,400 seconds per day, it receives 315,569,520 seconds, or approximately 3.1556952 × 10⁸ seconds. [1][2] This is the average calendar year, not the exact value of the regression year, nor the sum of the days of a specific calendar year.

Value and Measurement condition

Ten years equals about 3,652.425 days, 120 months or 521.775 weeks. Since the actual calendar rotates between 365 days and 366 days, an arbitrary period of 10 consecutive years may consist of one to three years and the total number of days may be one or two more or less than the average. [2] The3.1556952 × 10⁸ sec is therefore a recalculated average measurement condition and should not be considered to be the exact same measurements every 10 years.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings was that the name “twenty-first century” was used as a decade from an absolute starting point. Those names are historical year labels, and ten years are only ten years of duration. Another misconception is that the decade corresponds to a fixed astronomical cycle; it is not a solar activity cycle, nor is it a public rotation cycle for Moon or planets. It is also important to remember that the discussion here is about the duration, not the first decade after Big Bang.

Relationship with other scale

Ten decades make up a century, 100 constitute a thousand years; ten years connect the century and the millennium upwards, and one year, month, week, day. [1][2] It is best suited for comparing human history and life, and the description of universe should be changed to a larger scale of years, millions, etc. The figures for the different scale are presented together to confirm whether they all use the average calendar year.
