An Early Accreting Black Hole

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

A Web study of CEERS 1019 showed that some 570 million years after Big Bang had been in existence as a stowage black hole. This provides clues to the early growth of the black hole, but it is not the date when it began to emerge from its existence.

What is it?

It's about the supermass black hole that was siphoning in the early UNiverse, not the general formation of stellar mass black hole. CEERS 1019, an active black hole system identified by Web during the approximately 570 million-year era, is classified in the paper as a super-mass black hole [1] being accumulated. It is located at the centre of an early Galaxy and falls into the category of active Galaxy nuclear phenomena.

How?

The evidence is not a picture of a black hole, but a sign of its activity: the paper lists CEOS 1019 as a super-mass black hole, which is being accumulated and which, combined with light and model, can be estimated as a black hole mass and asphyxiation [1][2]. We do not see the event vision directly; its identification depends on near-infrared observation and host galaxy[1]. How, and when, black hole seeds are produced is still subject to model testing with more samples and cannot be directly determined by a single detection. The event vision itself is not luminescence and can be seen to come from substances outside the sight[2].

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This article draws on the post-Big Bang approximately 570 million years of observation, paper title from CEOS 1019 and analysis of observation [1]. It means that “it was there”, not that it was born on that day. If a black hole is formed from an early star collapse, the time of formation should be earlier; depending on the seed model and the stowing history, it may also be combined.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings is that the era of observation is the date of formation. Another misconception is that a black hole is seen; what is actually seen is the radiation from the ingestion of gases and the event scene itself does not glow [2]. Neither can CEOS 1019 be the first black hole in the UNiverse, which is an early traceable sample. The black hole mass estimates that it relies on suction and light model, with different scenarios giving different results.

Relationship to other phases

Early black holes may affect host galaxy ' s star formation and provide a growth starting point for later quasars [1][2]. It was formed in parallel with Galaxy, after the first generation of Stars. Compared to the quasarentry, the emphasis here is on the “existing” observation clue, rather than the brightest or most distant ranking. The separation of seed formation, siphoning and growth from the activities of Observation is key to understanding this history.
