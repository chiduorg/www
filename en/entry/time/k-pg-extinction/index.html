Cretaceous–Paleogene Extinction

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Some 66 million years ago, the Earth experienced a large-scale ecolory crisis, and groups such as non-bird dinosaurs disappeared. The Hiksulub impact crater, the baseary layer and Fossil record together to limit the extinction and its environmental impact.

What is it?

White Screen - The extinction of K-Pg occurred in about 66 Ma, with about 76 per cent of species disappearing, and non-bird dinosaurs, Wing Dragons, large sea reptiles disappear along with many acetals and double-hulls. [1][2] Birds, mammals, parts of plants and marine life survive and expand rapidly thereafter.

How?

Proven evidence includes argon abnormalities in the clay layer of the global multi-frontier boundary, pebbles and particulates, and the Hikesulub impact crater in the Yucatán Peninsula, Mexico. [1][2] These leads link impact to biological crisis; isotope year sets the boundary near 66.00 Ma. [3] Volcanic activity, sea level changes, etc. may also magnify environmental pressures, but impacts are currently the most powerful cause of interpretation rather than the only proven detail. [1] The impacting quartz and microglass ball particles in the boundary are found in multiple global profiles, indicating that the impact ejection has been distant. [2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

The International Stratigphy Commission, version 2024/12, has fixed the ancient nearness boundary (i.e. the K-Pg boundary) at 66.00 Ma, with no symmetrical error, since the boundary is mainly magnetic statigraphy and astronomical carication. [3] About 66 million years is a round expression; extinction and ecology recover across the timescale and cannot be summed up in one day. There is a slight difference between the sediment thickness of different profiles and the position of biological statigraphy, and absolute age requires astronomical rotation and magnetic statigraphy cross-calibration. [3]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings is that all dinosaurs are described in K-Pg extinction. Non-bird dinosaurs have disappeared, but birds have continued to this day as dinosaurs. [2] Another misunderstanding is that impact is the sole cause of impact; the changes in German-dry volcanic activity, climate and sea level may work together and research is still comparing the weight of the factors. [1]

Relationship to other phases

The K-Pg extinction ended with the transfer of space for mammalian radiation and the emerging ecolory system. [2] It is the same mass crisis as extinction at the end of the Era, but it is different in size, in the group of victims and at the rate of recovery, and both contribute to an understanding of extinction mechanisms. [1][3] Mammals and bird radiation after K-Pg are not synchronized and recovery rates vary widely among the different subsystems. [2]

What happened after the impact?

The dust and sulphate aerosols thrown from the Hiksuluber impact shielded the sunlight and the global temperature dropped sharply; the impact could also trigger wildfires, acid rain and tsunamis. [1][2] These environmental effects last for months to years and are sufficient to break the photocooperative and food chains. The iridium anomaly and impacting quartz are common signals at the global boundary, suggesting that the projectile diffusion is far away.

Why do birds and mammals survive?

Small, food-intensive, cave-dwelling or water-diving animals are more likely to find shelter in a post-disaster environment; birds continue as dinosaurs, while mammals use the empty ecolory position for rapid radiation. [2] Survivors are not “higher”, but they happen to have certain ecology and physiological characteristics. Thinking of the world after extinction as a wasteland would ignore the remaining food chains and refuges.

Five-axis Contrast

On the time axis, the K-Pg boundary was about 66 million years ago; on the space axis, the impact crater was located in Yucatán, Mexico; on the temperature axis, the impact of winter and subsequent warming coincided; on the mass axis, the impactor was about 10 km in scale, but changed global ecology. The event is very different from the consequences. [1][3]

The impact evidence is more than an anomaly.

The impact evidence for the K-Pg extinctions included global aridium anomalies, impact quartz, particulates, the Hiksurubmeteorite pit and tsunami deposition. Iridium anomalies may also be caused by volcanoes or crustal sources and therefore require multiple evidence lock-in. Body text's “impact” is the conclusion of the composite chain of evidence and is not a single element anomaly. [1][S1]
