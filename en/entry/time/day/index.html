One Day

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The day usually refers to the continuation of the Earth ' s time relative to the solar rotation for a week, which is 86,400 seconds by SI conversion. It measures how long a process lasts, not the first day after Big Bang.

What is it?

A day usually refers to the continuation of the time that the Earth corresponds to a week of solar rotation, that is, 24 hours. It answers “how long this process lasts” rather than “when this happened”. A sleep and awakening cycle, a calendar date, is used as the basic unit; but a day is not the same as a time with sunlight.

How do you know or why?

The International Bureau of Metrology lists the days as 1 d = 86,400 s, and the seconds of which are leaped from caesium to definition. [1] There are also several days to be distinguished in astronomy: the Sun Day is referred to by the Sun for an average of 24 hours; the Star Day by reference to the background Star is about 23 hours 56 minutes 4 seconds, approximately four minutes shorter than the Sun Day. [2] Differences arise from the fact that the Earth rotates around the Sun while at the same time turning around.

Value and Measurement condition

The modern unit converts the exact date of 86,400 seconds, i.e. 1,440 minutes or 24 hours. [1] However, the length of real Sun Days will vary slightly in a year with the rate of the Earth ' s public turn, and flat Sun Days are the average of such changes. [2] Thus, the term "one day equals 86,400 seconds" is a time-based regulation and is not the same as the measured interval from sunrise to the next.

Common misunderstandings

It is the most common confusion to equate a day with a day; “a day” in the legal and temporal sense usually refers to 24 hours between midnight and midnight. [2] Another confusion is the use of one day as an absolute fixed cycle for the rotation of the Earth: there are small irregularities in the rotation of the Earth, and Star Day is different from Sun Day. [2] It is also important to remember that a day lasts for a long time, not the moment after Big Bang.

Relationship with other scale

Seven days comprise one week and twelve months one year; the exact monthly and annual composition is determined by the astronomical cycle and the calendar, respectively. [1][2] Sky links up the calendar, divided down by hours, minutes and seconds. It is a small number of units with both astronomical references and precision, and should be read to confirm which one.
