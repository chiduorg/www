The first generation of Stars formed in the early gas cloud of UNiverse. Webcop placed the first star formation about 100 to 400 million years after Big Bang; the time axis took 200 million years to anchor the conference, rather than treating it as the exact birthday of the first star.

What is it?

The first generation of Stars (Star III), which is made of primary hydrogen and helium gas, has almost no element than helium. They appear at the end of the Dark Age and are the first star sources of light in the UNiverse; concrete mass, brightness and grouping remain objects of study. They're not the metal-richstars in the galaxy today.

How?

No single first generation Star is directly visible. Evidence to support their existence includes: the need for light sources in the dark period after compounding and before the earliest Galaxy appeared; the need for UV radiation for re-ion; and the simulation of cooling collapse[1][3] of zero gas clouds. Weeb has identified early Galaxy[3] less than 400 million years after Big Bang, but seeing the light of Galaxy as a whole does not mean that the single star group IIIstar[1] is identified. Star evolution model also suggests that zero metalstar may be large and have a short life.

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This article covers the period from 200 to 400 million years after Big Bang and 200 million years of navigation anchor. Webcop placed the earliest star formation between about 100 million and 400 million years, emphasizing that it was impossible to establish a precise starting point [1]; ESA summarized it “in hundreds of millions of years”. These figures are time bound by model and observation, not the birthday of a single star. The earlier 100 million years were only the minimum allowed by model, and direct identification was not yet available.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misconceptions is that the first generation of Stars is used as a set of light bulbs that are lit at the same time; they are sequenced and may occur in groups many times. Another misperception is that Webb has taken a single star from the Stars III; most of what is actually seen is a combined light in the Galaxy or Galaxy regiment[1][3]. Nor can they be judged to be non-existent because they have not been found; they may have already erupted or collapsed.

The first generation of UV radiation ended the dark age and promoted reionation [1][2]; their supernovas spilled carbon, oxygen, etc. into the gas and prepared raw materials for subsequent star and galaxy. Why would a single first-generation Star have to prove it?

The first generation of Stars is virtually metal-free and theoretically may be mass, short-lived and long dead; what we can see is their heavy element, UV radiation and subsequent galaxy light. [1][3] Most of the highly migratory objects seen by Web are Galaxy as a whole, not a single single metalstar; it is confirmed that a star group IIIstar needs very low metal abundance, with a specific spectrum established with age evidence. This is much more difficult than seeing a light.

Indirect chain of evidence

The clues left by the first generation of Stars were combined: the microwave background showed that composite gases were almost neutral and that re-ionation required ultraviolet light sources; the oldest poor metalstar retained low metal abundance; and the hyperred galaxy spectra showed a build-up of gravity. [1][3] These evidence point, respectively, to “there are the first stars” “they formed early” “they created heavy element”, but none of them singled out the coordinates of a single star. Using the chain of evidence as a single-point photograph is a common misinterpretation. And that's why the time range is written in 100-400 million years, not an exact date.

Five-axis Contrast

At the time axis, 200-400 million years is a period of reference; at the element axis, the first generation of Star is a plant that transforms the original helium into heavy element, such as carbon oxygen; at the temperature axis, the fusion temperature inside Star determines which element can be built; at the space axis, they are located in dark matter scale far less than today ' s galaxy. The combination of the four axes explains why “first generationstar” is both a celestial body and a starting point for chemical evolution. [1][3]

They are short-lived and bright, and the first metals are injected into universe by ultraviolet radiation and supernovaes.
