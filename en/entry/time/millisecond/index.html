One Millisecond

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Milliseconds are 1 in 1,000, i.e.10⁻³ seconds, equal to 1,000 microseconds, which is a 10-point score for SI seconds. It measures how long a process lasts, not at some point on the absolute time axis.

What is it?

A millisecond is a millisecond, i.e. 1 ms =10⁻³, equal to 1,000 μs. It measures the duration of a process, which is long and not on the absolute time axis. Many of the “speech moments” in daily life are described in milliseconds as: the discharge of camera flashlights, a vibration of a beeper, the interval of displays to refresh the frame, and the delay of Internet traffic.

How do you know or why?

Milliseconds are si-seconds of decimals, not independent benchmarks. The BIPM prefix list records milli as10⁻³, micro as ZXXQPH1QZX, nano as ZXXQPH2QZX; added to the second is given in milliseconds, microseconds, nanoseconds. [1] The second definition is still the 9,192,631,770 cycles of two ultra-precision energy-grade leaps to radiation in the caesium-133 atom matrix, and NIST indicates that the atom clock produces the time interval by counting the frequency of wave cycles. So the millisecond length is entirely from the second.

Value and Measurement condition

Pressing definition, a millisecond is precisely equal to 0.001 seconds, i.e. a thousand microseconds or a million nanoseconds; a second contains a thousand milliseconds. This conversion has no approximation. In actual measurement, millisecond timetips may be affected by the movement of operating systems, network transmissions and equipment clocks, which may be much more accurate than the unit itself. [2] Thus, the "msec level" sometimes refers to resolution and sometimes to the error range, which needs to be described separately.

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that the "mixer-second delay" is treated as a fixed value; it is usually just a description of the order of magnitude, which may actually range from zero to tens of milliseconds. Another misunderstanding is to confuse milliseconds with absolute moments: milliseconds are continuous length, and there must be another starting point for saying “in a millisecond”. It was also assumed that milliseconds, microseconds and nanoseconds were three unrelated units, all of which were graded by 1,000 times the size of the second. [1]

Relationship with other scale

One second equals 1,000 milliseconds, one millisecond equals 1,000 microseconds, one microsecond equals 1,000 nanoseconds, forming a continuous 1,000-step ladder. [1] milliseconds uplink seconds and minutes, downlink microseconds and nanoseconds; it is exactly scale at the intersection of human senses with Electron devices. The daily “blink-up” is usually much longer than a millisecond, and the chip clock cycle is much shorter than a millisecond; when comparing in milliseconds, it should be accompanied by a description of the process measured.

The time unit of this article and the conversion is based on BIPM for second definiation. [3]

Based on the same vacuum speed, the light travels about 300 km within the time and approaches the "tsunami wavelength" on the space axis. [3]
