What is it?

This article discusses the nature of the transformation of the quark substance to a strong substance: whether it is a violent one-step phase change or a smooth cross. [1] Stronger nuclei are linked to high temperature quacks, glues and substances that are confined to strongs at low temperature. [1][2] It is not a single event that encodes a few free quack machines as proton, but a change in the collective heat. [1][3]

How do you know or why?

QCD calculates hand crosses in the vicinity of zero chemistry, and Bazavov et al. give a false threshold typitude Tc(0) = 156.5 ± 1.5 MeV. This is theoretical calculation, not direct measurement in the history of universe. [1] Thermal history review of Ramelski et al. placed the original QGP phase in dozens of microseconds order of magnitude, and described a powerful transformation around about 150 MeV. [2] The heavy ion impact experiment studies the properties of substances under similar conditions, but does not allow direct re-envelvence expansion. [3]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

Stronging occurs near the temperature of about 150-160 MeV, conversion is about dozens of microseconds order of magnitude; specific seconds depend on state equations, particle freedom and expansion history and are therefore not real readings. [2] Different heat history calculations will give a similar but different moment, and one value cannot be considered the only baseary. [1][2] The separation of thermometers from historical moments is key to understanding this article; otherwise, a value of temperature would be misread as a UNiverse clock reading. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings is that pseudocritical is considered to be an “exact reading” of what happens in change; it is calculated under certain low-chemical conditions. [1] Another misconception is that all powerful manization is painted as a first-stage explosion of a bubble collision; the standard model dot results support smooth cross-cutting, and bubble scene requires additional conditions. [1][2] The plume in the figure is only an illustration of the concept of strong sonization, not a snapshot of actual events. [1]

Relationship to other phases

It took place between the times of quarke and John and was the point of convergence described in both times. [1][3] The transformational nature will also affect the formation of hypotheses such as strange quarts: if it is actually smooth cross, the old formula of relying on a first-order scenario to create large groups is challenged. [1][2] It can be read as a bridge between the “era coordinates” and the “physical transformation mechanisms”, without mixing the temperature, time and phase change types into a conclusion. [1][2]
