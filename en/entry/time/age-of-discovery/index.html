The Age of Discovery

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The age of great navigation usually refers to the period of 15 to 17th century European expansion into the Atlantic, Africa, Asia and the Americas; 1450 was only the starting point for the expansion of Portuguese navigation, and 1492 was often used as a symbol.

What is it?

The age of great navigation usually refers to the long-term process of expanding the knowledge of oceanic navigation, trade, colonization and maps in European countries from the fifteenth to the seventeenth centuries. It is not a single voyage by a navigator, but an expansion involving the royal family, businessmen, fleets, ports and cartographers. [1][2] The links between Europe and other continents are deepened by the fact that Portugal sails south along the African coast and Spain finances transatlantic navigation.

How?

The logbooks, royal charters, maps, vessel remnants and trade records provide evidence. Britannica recorded that Vasco da Gamma had bypassed the Cape of Good Hope during his voyage from 1497 to 1499, opening up maritime routes from Western Europe to India; his fleet consisted of three dhows, light and fast sails and supply ships. [1] Columbus completed four transatlantic voyages under the auspices of Spain (1492-93, 1493-96, 1498-1500, 1502-04), mainly to the Caribbean Sea and the American coast; however, the Vikings had travelled to North America several centuries ago and the Native Americans had already lived there. [2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This entry took the year 1450 A.D. for navigation, corresponding to the early stages of continued Portuguese expansion along the African coast. As agreed in the project, the period from 1450 to 2026 is approximately 576 years; the upper seconds of the axis are used only for sorting. Another common practice, marked by the first transatlantic voyage in Columbus in 1492, shows that there are different options for “the beginning of the era” itself. [1][2] The name of the beginning of navigation, the occurrence of a particular voyage and the “era of great navigation” was later used and is three different types of communication.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that Columbus is called the “discovery” of the Americas. Britannica made it clear that Vikings, like Lev Eriksen, had been to North America centuries ago and that Native Americans had lived there long ago; the arrival of Europeans had brought about colonization, slavery and disease, and could not be written only as heroic adventures. Another misperception is that the era of great navigation is an achievement for a single nation; Portugal, Spain, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, etc. are involved, with different motives and routes. [1][2]

Relationship to other phases

Trade and the development of city before the age of great navigation overlapped with classical interest in Renaissance and the spread of printing, followed by the Enlightenment Movement and the Industrial Revolution. [1] [2] Ocean navigation relies on shipbuilding, mapping, astronomical navigation and resupply organizations, which in turn alter Europe ' s world view. When comparing it with the space age, care should be taken that both have expanded the scope of their activities, but that power and violence brought about by colonial expansion cannot be eliminated by the term “exploration”.
