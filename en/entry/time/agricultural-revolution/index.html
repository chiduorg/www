Origins of Agriculture

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Agricultural origins include long-term plant, animal management and domestication processes. The Near East study found that such activities had begun before at least 115,000 cal BPs; agricultural transformations in different regions were not synchronized and could not be summarized in one year.

What is it?

Agricultural origin refers to the long-term process of human beings moving from collecting wild resources to managing, planting and domesticating plants and raising animals, not an “innovation”. New Crescent Land in the Near East is one of the areas most well studied, but China, New Guinea and Central America also have their own domestication centres. According to Zeder, the management of future crops and livestock that are still morphologically wild can be traced at least to 11,500 cal BPs, or even earlier. [1]

How?

The evidence included charcoal seeds, pollen, animal bones, instruments and residential residues. The researchers used the radioactivity carbon year to establish the cal BPtime ruler, comparing morphological changes such as seed size, an axis break. Zeder emphasizes that archaeologically visible morphological changes are often later than they begin to be managed; therefore, “the earliest domestication” is not equivalent to “the first human intervention”. Britannica, for its part, outlined the transformation of neo-rock agriculture around 10,000 B.C. and listed domesticated targets such as wheat, barley and sheep and goats. [1][2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This station took some 12,000 years ago for navigation, close to 11,500 cal BP; this is a round time anchor, not a precise year for a village. Cal BP uses 1950 as the benchmark, corrected by radioactivity carbon, different from calendar year, historical year. There are thousands of years of difference between regions, not one year to represent the globe. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that “agriculture spreads throughout the world after an invention somewhere in the Near East”. Zeder stressed that there were also many centres and many domestications within the New Moon and that the same species could be domesticated several times; independent development in different regions was also common. Another misconception is that agriculture is equated with settlement: some groups manage resources for a long period of time, while others remain predominantly collecting. [2]

Relationship to other phases

The agricultural transformation took place some 12,000 years ago in the early hours of Holocene, followed by the emergence of villages, population growth and surplus products, providing conditions for city, copper technology and documentation. [1][2] It is approximately 11,000 years away from the industrial revolution, and two “revolutions” cannot be considered a continuous acceleration of the same speed.

It's not a decision to tame.

From collection to cultivation, there is a long period of “management of wild resources” in which people can burn, sow, weed and conserve target species without changing their patterns. [1] Visible signs of domestication — larger seed particles, non-breakable axes — often appear hundreds to thousands of years later. Treating agricultural origin as a one-day invention would ignore this gradual process.

Cost and population

Agriculture has increased food production per unit of land, but has reduced dietary diversity and increased labour intensity and the risk of infectious diseases; settlement and population density have complicated social stratification and resource conflicts. [2] Agriculture does not move forward in a single direction, but rather in exchange for greater carrying capacity and new vulnerabilities. Compare collection with agriculture, not just production.

Five-axis Contrast

On the time axis, the agricultural origin was about 12,000 years ago; on the space axis, centres such as New Moon, China and Central America were independent; on the témperature axis, the heating of Holoclimate provided conditions; and on the mass axis, population and crop biomass increased. It is time, geography and climate that shape this transformation. [1][2]

Agricultural origin is a multi-centre process.

Agriculture is not a single-point diffusion from a “New Moon” land; China, Mexico, the Andes, Africa, etc. have independent domestication. The transition from collection to cultivation has lasted thousands of years and the tamponization has gradually stabilized. The “agriculture revolution” of body text is an overview of future generations, not an option that occurs in a given year. [1][S1]
