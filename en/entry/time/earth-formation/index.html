Formation of Earth

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

About 4.5 billion years ago, gases and dust in the solar system gathered in gravitational force and the Earth evolved. Planetary formation consists of suction and internal evolution, and cannot be understood as the completion of all structures at the same moment.

Planets are formed in small measure.

The formation of the Earth takes place in the young solar system. NASA's overview shows that gravitational force has led to a gradual accumulation of gas and dust distributed around the disk, forming rock planets like the Earth. The “formation” here is a process of material convergence and evolution, and not a blue planet that already has the oceans, crust and atmosphere of today suddenly emerges at one second. [1]

What kind of reference is 4.5 billion years?

This paper does not mix more detailed estimates with round integers into precise dates, nor does it equate the age of a mineral with the time when all the Earth ' s composition and structure are completed simultaneously. Then what happened?

NASA describes the Earth today as a planet with core, rocked mantle and solid crust. Understanding its history requires separate discussion of material accumulation, internal evolution and subsequent surface environments.
