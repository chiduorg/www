The Renaissance

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Renaissance usually refers to the transformation of the arts and culture in Italy and later in Europe between 1,400 and 1600, characterized by the revival of classical science and humanism; 1400 is a regional reference, not the beginning of the whole of Europe.

What is it?

Renaissance usually refers to a cultural and artistic transformation in Europe ' s history immediately following the medieval century, centred on a renewed interest in classical science and values. Britannica describes it as a period that took place in European civilization, accompanied by the exploration of the new continent, the replacement of the Tolmi system by Copernicus astronomy, the decline of feudal systems, commercial growth, and innovative applications such as paper and printing. [1] It's neither a single event nor just a painting style name.

How?

Researchers track this transformation through painting, sculpture, architecture, manuscripts, sponsorship contracts and theoretical writings. Smarthistory summarized the Italian art of Renaissance between about 1400 and 1600, noting that the interest in ancient Greek Rome became more urgent in the fourteenth century, and then extended to the rest of Europe; humanism affected the sphere of life, including visual arts. [2] Linear visualization, anatomy and naturalism have been promoted during this period, with Alberti ' s 1435/36 Theory and Vassari artist biographies becoming important texts of art theory. [2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This entry took the year 1400 A.D. as a point of departure for the cultural transformation of Italian cities. As agreed in the project, the line is approximately 626 years from 1400 to 2026; the upper seconds of the axis are used only for ranking purposes and do not mean that a work or artist appears at that moment. [1][2] A distinction is made between “cultural movements are later named” and “new art techniques” and “printing and geographical expansion to change the conditions for transmission”. These changes overlap, but do not occur on the same day.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that Renaissance is equivalent to Europe's sudden “rebirth” from the darkness of the medieval century, or that it occurs only in Italy. Medieval Europe has been exposed to classical texts on a continuous basis; the effects of Renaissance also vary from region to region, city and sponsors, and the rhythm of northern Europe is markedly different. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is to reduce it to an artistic history, ignoring the interaction between commercial, political, scientific and religious reforms; at the same time, it cannot be understood as simply replicating ancient times.

Relationship to other phases

The Renaissance was preceded by the Middle Ages, followed by the era of great navigation, the spread of printing, the scientific revolution and the Enlightenment Movement. The importance it attaches to classical languages, its interest in experience and technology provide some of the conditions for subsequent scientific discussions, but the scientific revolution is not an automatic result of the Renaissance. [1][2] On the time axis, 1400 was just a regional entry point, with its own cultural transformation and knowledge traditions outside Europe.

Renaissance is not "the sudden rebirth of darkness."

Medieval Europe is not completely divorced from classical traditions; a large number of texts are preserved and disseminated by monasteries, universities and Byzantine scholars. [1][2] Renaissance is intended to be new to the organization of classical resources, to the way nature and human beings are reclaimed, and to be the result of printing and sponsorship networks. To sum up a thousand years ago as “dark” would erase the accumulation of technology, philosophy and art.

Five-axis Contrast

On the time axis, the Renaissance was about 1400-1600; on the space axis, it moved from diffusion in Italian city state to Europe; on the temperature axis, climate and agriculture influenced the city economy; and on the mass axis, the costs of sponsors, paper and printed materials determine the size of the work. Cultural movements are also the result of resources and networks. [1][2]

Renaissance is a later stage.

Renaissance is not the sudden “rebirth” of the Italian city state in a given year; it is a general overview by nineteenth-century historians of the cultural changes of the 14-16 century, covering literature, art, science, religion and politics. The rhythms vary from region to region, and the North Renaissance has other paths. Body text's Renaissance is a phased label, not a single event. [1][S1]
