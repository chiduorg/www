Big evolution is not an instant explosion.

Cold martial law is a rule of geology. In this context, life has evolved significantly, focusing on the expansion of animal forms, modes of activity and ecology relationships over a long period of time, rather than the simultaneous birth of all life. Life and some more complex organisms existed before the Cold War; this history is called an “emergence”, which represents a significant diversity in the Fossil record, not a physical explosion. [2]

What has changed in the seafloor world?

A presentation by the Smithsonian Institute shows the development of more active feeding, cave digging, and body structures such as shells and thorns. Animals are not only host to the environment, but also change the environment by disturbing the environment, feeding and occupying different places of life. What needs to be known here is an interconnected ecology network, which cannot treat a trifle or an alien shape as the entire face of the cold war. [2]

How does Fossil help us know it?

The physical structure, activity marks and sediment deposits left by organisms provide different types of evidence. Fossil communities, such as the Burgis shale, help researchers identify diverse marine organisms and their lifestyles. However, the communities maintained at one location are not complete rosters of the same global day; the presence or absence of a model in scene is no substitute for Fossil evidence. [2]
