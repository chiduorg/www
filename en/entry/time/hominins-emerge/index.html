What is it?

The “early human spectra” refers to subdivisions of apes, such as chimpanzees, which have not yet entered the human family and are normally classified as humans, Ako. The Chadian Shahelanthropus tchadensis lived between 7 and 6 million years ago and was one of the first known candidates. Nine skull materials, including one skull, were found in 2001 in northern Chad without any bones below the skull. It has smaller canine teeth, shorter faces, pre-positioned cortex, hinting that the head may be placed on a straight body, but it cannot be determined by the skull alone. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is to use the earliest Fossil as “the birth day of mankind” or to make a split fork into a global sync event. Fossil only proves the existence of groups with several characteristics at some point in time; earlier, no identifiable material may already exist.
