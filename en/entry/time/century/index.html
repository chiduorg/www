A Century

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

A century is a century of continuous time, calculated on average per year of the calendar at about 3.1556952 × 10⁹ seconds. It shows how long a process lasts, not in what century.

What is it?

A century is a hundred years of continuoustime, about 3.1556952 × 10⁹ seconds. It answers “for how long” rather than “where it happened”. In daily languages, a century is often used to describe the long span of life, the existence of a building or an institution; the “twentieth century” is a historical term, which is not identical.

How do you know or why?

The century is also multiplied by 100 years, without independence. Based on the average calendar year of 365.2425 days, a hundred years is 36 524.25 days, multiplied by 86,400 seconds to receive approximately 3.1556952 × 10⁹ seconds. [1][2] If the regression year is changed to 365.2422 days, the result will be slightly smaller, approximately 2,600 seconds; this means that the average annual use must be indicated first.

Value and Measurement condition

A century consists of approximately 1,200 months, 5,217.75 weeks or 876,582 hours. A particular one may have 24 or 25 years in a hundred consecutive years, so the total number of days is about one day different from the average of 36,524.25 days. [2] So3.1556952 × 10⁹ seconds are the result of an average year of the calendar, not a daily record of a particular century.

Common misunderstandings

It is easy to confuse the length of a century with the number of a century. For example, the “twentieth century” usually refers to a period of history with years of origin, while the “one century” emphasizes only a hundred years; a historical event that occurred in the twentieth century does not amount to a century. Another misconception is that the century has a fixed length in astronomy; it is only a multiple of years. Similarly, the discussion here is about the duration, not the first century after Big Bang.

Relationship with other scale

Ten centuries constitute a thousand years, one century consists of ten decades; upwards can be connected to the millennium scale and down to years, months, weeks, days. [1][2] In the history of human history and civilization, the century is a common narrative; in the history of universe it is almost impossible to show alone. The separation of the words “continuing for a hundred years” from “the historical space of a given century” avoids the use of chronology labels as physical lengths.
