The Middle Ages

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Medieval times usually refer to European history between the demise of Western Rome in the fifth century and the revival of the arts; 476 was a Western European reference and did not cover Byzantine, the Islamic world and China.

What is it?

Medieval times usually refer to the period between the collapse of Roman civilization and the revival of literature in European history. Britannica summed it up as the collapse of Roman civilization in the fifth century A.D. to the beginning of the Renaissance, the beginning of which, depending on the region, could fall in the 13th, 14th or 15th century. [1] This name was proposed by Italian humanists and later became a common segment in European history.

How?

Historic materials include the monastery archives, royal papers, city records, church and castle sites, manuscripts and works of art. Smarthistory points out that the 14th century poet Petraque described the demise of the Roman Empire (about 476) to his own time (about 1330geological age) as “the dark age”, which was later transformed by historians into “middle-century”; broadly speaking, it was a period between the end of ancient times and the revival of the 15th and 16th centuries in Europe. [2] These materials do not record a single event, but rather a long-term change in systems, beliefs, language and convergence.

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This entry took 500 A.D. as a navigational reference near the common year of the demise of Western Rome. As agreed in the project, the time lag between 500 and 2026 is approximately 1526; the upper seconds of the axis are used only for sorting and cannot be used to complete the transformation in the same year on behalf of a village or city. [1][2] A distinction is made between “the end of the Western Roman regime” and “the use of medieval names” and “the phased formation of regions” of three types. The year 476 was only a reference point for Western Europe, not a switch on global history.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is to equate the medieval century with ignorance, stagnation and “darkness”. In fact, universities, written laws, Gothic churches, trade networks and a large number of literary works emerged during this period; neither does religious life amount to the cessation of all intellectual activity. [2] Another misconception is to bring the experience of Western Europe to Byzantine, the Islamic world, China and the Americas, each of which has its own political and cultural rhythm, which cannot be uniformly covered by the term “middle century”.

Relationship to other phases

Before the Middle Ages, following the classical era, the Renaissance, the Great Navigation Age and the spread of printing. Black death, city autonomy, universities and business networks have all accumulated during this period, and later changes have not started from scratch. [1][2] Reorganizing medieval growthtime instead of a history erased by “darkness” makes it easier to understand its continuity and break with modern Europe.
