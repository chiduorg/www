What is it?

Stars III is the first generation of early elements of hydrogen, helium, mass, and short life spans, with heavy elements thrown back into gas at death in the form of supernovaes. [1][2] This article marks “Big Bang approximately 300 million years after” the era of reference, which indicates that the first wave of large massstars concentrated in death and supernova sites began to affect surrounding gases, is not the exact moment of a single outbreak.

How?

The Spitzer note set the first generation of Stars at about 200-400 million years after Big Bang, stating that the Stars III starmass could exceed 100 times the Sun, have a short lifespan and end with bright supernovas. [1] NASA Webb also gives a window of formation for 200-400 million years and points out that the first generation ofstar will explode only millions of years. [2] Both place these eruptions in the same early window, and 300 million years is therefore a reasonable anchor.

Common misunderstandings

Supernovas are also seen as the only pushers of galaxy formation, which in fact contributes mainly to heavy element and disturbance, and galaxy assembly is also controlled by dark matter and gas inhalation.
