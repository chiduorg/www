The Big Bang

What is it?

Hot Big Bangmodel describes the history of universe that used to be hotter, denser and cooled with expansion: from particle plasma toom core, toom, tostar and galaxy. [1] [2] “Big Bang” does not explode in an existing space, but is the overall evolution of the expansion of the space itself and the decrease in material and radiation density. [1][2] The site sets this evolutionary extrapolation to a time zero point as an engagement to compare subsequent events rather than a physical moment that has already been captured. [1][2]

How do you know or why?

Hubble's discovery of Galaxy's retreat was an important clue, but it alone was not enough to build the hot Big Bang; the original light element abundance and the microwave background of universe provided independent evidence of a warmer and more dense past. [1][2] CERN and PDF both emphasized that it was the discovery of the microwave background that brought the hot Big Bang into the mainstream and that the synthesis calculation independently tested the early conditions. [1][2] [3] ESA's Planck information indicates that the CMB is a radiation left over from a complex period, and that temperature rises and falls record the distribution of substances and radiation at that time. [4] These observations support a history of “heateder and more dense”, without filming the zero seconds themselves. [1][3]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

Bentry Quantity takes 0 seconds, and medical convention is the agreed starting point of the history of UNiverse, not the moment of the instrument. [1][3] The commonly used universe age of approximately 13.8 billion years is the result of extrapolating the history of inflation to the past; different data and models give slightly different ages, so the time zero does not represent the annual accuracy of each early stage. [1][2] Writing “t=0” as an event of observation would confuse coordinates with physical evidence; the precision of age as an early event would be similar. [1][3]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misconceptions was to think of Big Bang as an explosion in a space and ask “where is the centre of the explosion”. There are no special centres for flat inflation, and any galaxy sees other galaxy away. [1][2] Another misunderstanding was that Big Bangmode had explained the initial conditions. Both the PDF and the CERN distinguish between a descriptive history of expansion and very early physics that is not known; model can speak of cooling history, not "why exists." [1 [2][3]

Relationship to other phases

After time zero, the stages of burgeoning, reheating, power weakness, quark-strength, lightweight, and nucleination can be discussed in turn, but not at every stage with the same level of evidence. [2][3] Microwave background links the early heat history and subsequent structural growth; light abundance links the first few minutes to today ' s observation. [3] [4] When reading these stages, the “earlyer” coordinates should be separated from “the more direct the evidence” and the adjacent model phase should not be treated as the same event. [1][3]

Another scale to look at it.

13.8 billion years is about three times the age of the Earth and about one million times the history of human civilization. If one year of universe history, the Earth appears in September, dinosaurs disappear at the end of December, and the human text is only a few minutes. This conversion doesn't change physics, but it can change "deep time" from abstract numbers to a senseable order.

The evidence is not the same.

The CMB, the Light Element abundance and the Galaxy retreat all support the hot Big Bang, but they do not have the same type of evidence: the CMB is a direct legacy of radiation, the Light Element abundance is the ratio of nucleination to observation, and the Galaxy exit is the kinetic evidence of an expanding history. [1][2][3][4] The earlier the direct observation becomes less, the more the model is pushed. The separation of “evidence-supported” from “directly seen” is key to reading the early unversime line.

Five-axis Contrast

On the time axis, t= 0 is the agreed starting point; on the temperature axis, the early universe corresponds to the CMB and the earlier high temperature phase; on the space axis, the observation pool changes with the expansion; on the mass axis, the initial nuance determines the abundance of hydrogen, helium and a small amount of lithium. The four axes speak of the same history, but not the same thing. [1][3][4]
