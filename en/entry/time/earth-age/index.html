What is it?

Age of the Earth refers to the time that has formed the Earth to date, which is approximately 4.54 billion years. It's an age, not the coordinates of the absolute point of time on the time axis. The writing of 4.54 billion years as1.4327×10¹⁷ seconds, which is also a different formulation of the same physical mass, should not be added to or subtracted from the time.earth-formation reference coordinates to create a new “time”. In other words, “the Earth has lived for 4.54 billion years” and “the Earth formed 4.54 billion years ago” describe the same age, but in a different direction.

How?

The oldest rock directly measured the Earth does not reach its age, as the early crust of the Earth has been modified by plate motion. Scientists shifted to determine the ratio of meteorite to the lead isotope in the Earth ' s Rock, treating the Earth and meteorite as the same evolutionary system, receiving 4.54 billion years, and error less than 1 per cent [1]. This value is also consistent with the solar system's meteorite age of 4.53 billion to 4.58 billion years [1]. Britannica, on the other hand, summed up the Earth ' s formation about 4.6 billion years ago, as a whole expression [2]. Lead-lead used the lead isotope ratio from uranium decay in fixed years, and raw lead from meteorite provided a starting point reference and thus the entire age of the Earth system.

Value and Measurement condition

4.54 billion years at 31,557,600 seconds per year, equals1.43271504×10¹⁷ seconds, or 143,271,504,000,000 seconds. The “4.54 billion years” here is age, i.e. the range of times from formation to the present; if described in terms of “4.54 billion years from today”, the same age is simply replaced by [1][2]. Of these, 1% of the roror is about 4.5 billion years, which explains why different sources write the results about 4.5 billion years or 4.6 billion years.

Common misunderstandings

The first misperception is to refer to “age of the Earth 4.54 billion years” as “the Earth formed 4.54 billion seconds ago” or “the 4.54 billion-year scale on the Time axis”. The former was wrong, while the latter misdirected continuoustime. The second misconception is that the oldest age of rock is equal to age of the Earth; the oldest is about 4.003 billion years, and the oldest zircon is about 4.4 billion years, all of which are below the threshold [1]. Nor can the Sun's age of 4.6 billion years be directly considered as age of the Earth.

Relationship with other scale

4.54 billion years is 10¹⁷ seconds order of magnitude: it's about 1 billion seconds, 31.43 billion times in 3169, 1 trillion in 317,000, 14.3 times in 13,169 million, and 0.143 times in 10 billion. It is more than half a billion years short of Galaxy crossing Galaxy time, but it is approximately 19.7 times the year of the Milky Way in 230.0 million years [1]. It is about 6.22 million times the human life expectancy of 73 years, and about 90.8 thousand times the millennium record of civilization; these multiples are used only to locate scale.

Error: 4.54 billion years is not a single reading

Lead-lead is given about 4.54 billion years, and error is less than 1 per cent; different meteorite, different minerals and different iisotope systems give slightly different ages, but fall within the range of 45.3-4.58 billion years. [1] This officer is not a "number after decimal" measurement precision, but a model estimation of the Earth's co-evolved system. Write 4.54 billion years into 45.4 trillion years, and create non-existent precision.

Five-axis Contrast

At the time axis, it is an age, not the coordinates of the moment of formation; at the space axis, the current diameter of the Earth is not the same size as it was at the beginning of its formation; at the mass axis, the Earth is about 5.972×10²⁴ kg, which together binds thermal evolution with age; and at the temperature axis, the magma of the Earth at an early stage is at a different stage from the sea surface. The four axes describe the Earth, but they cannot replace each other. [1][2]
