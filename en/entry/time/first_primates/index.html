Early Primate Fossils

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Some 56 million years ago, Teilhardina and other early primates, Fossil, preserved clues to the end of the toe structure. These records help to study the evolution of primates, but they do not amount to a determination of the sole date of origin of the entire primaries.

What is it?

This is about 56 Ma's early primate Teilhardina Brandti. It is of the size of a mouse living on a tree, and the end of a toe preserves bones that support both the combing claw and the nails. [1] These tiny Fossils demonstrate that the primate “claw-and-a” transformation is not complete once, but is more complex than previous assumptions. Fossil also includes fragments of teeth and cheekbones, which are classified by researchers in size and form as Teilhardina and compared with live primates. [1]

How?

Fossil is from sedimentation in the Great Cape Basin in Wyoming, United States, and the research team sifts microbones out of sand particles, then compares the shape of the final fingerbone with a CT scan. [1] The end finger bone of the live primate provides a contrast: narrow but sharp bone supports claws, flat-bone supports nails. On this basis, researchers have identified two structures on different toes of the same animal. [1] CT scans can observe the inner structure and joints of the bone without destroying the specimen and reduce the mechanical damage to the tiny fossil. [1]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

The fossil layer is about 56 Ma. According to the 2024/12 edition of the International Stratigphy Commission, the ancient New World-New World Line is 56.00 Ma, so these Fossils are close to the early days of the ancient era. [3] This is an age reference that includes Fossilstratigraphy and is not the exact moment of origin of the primaries; earlier Fossil or the unsaved subsystems may push forward the division.

Common misunderstandings

One of the misconceptions is that Teilhardina is a direct ancestor of all primates. It belongs to the early age of the new life, and specific spectra positions require systematic developmental support. [1][2] Another misunderstanding is that the first primates have nails; this study precisely shows that the early members have simultaneously kept combustible claws, and that subsequent finger nailing is a number of changes in branches. [1]

Relationship to other phases

The primates expand in a warm forest environment after PETM, with diversification of flower plants, insects and fruit resources. [2] They were later divided into lemurs, eyeglasses, monkeys and apes, and humans were part of this primate spectrum. [1] Early primate tree habitats and visual features gradually emerge in forest expansion after the end of the era, but the evidence of Fossil remains inconsistent. [2]
