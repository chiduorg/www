Early Writing in Mesopotamia

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The two river basins showed early writing at the end of the fourth millennium B.C. The management of city, material count and the linking of symbols to languages interact; this article is not the date on which the world's text was born.

What is it?

Text is an information system that represents the agreed language structure with visible marks; it differs from drawings, count symbols and is based on the ability to stabilize corresponding words or voices. [2] The two river basins were the first to have written records at the end of the fourth millennium B.C., initially for bookkeeping, distribution of materials and registration staff, and later developed wedge text. [1][3]

How?

Archaeological evidence is the slabs, seals, counts and inscriptions. The original wedge of the Uruk site was about 3200 B.C. to 3,000 B.C.; an overview of the Metropolis Museum indicates that the earliest symbols were mostly identifiable physical graphics, followed by voice borrowing, and that the language was recorded more systematically with a sound note about 2,600 B.C. [1][3] Text carriers, writing tools and symbol systems are to be studied together and not just one mud sheet. [2]

Time Coordinates and Measurement condition

This station is located in the middle of 3,400-3200 years B.C., with 3300 B.C. as a point of reference; there are no historical years of 0 B.A., and conversion to 2026 was about 5325 years ago. The upper seconds of the axis are only used for sorting, and do not mean that the slab has a second-scale geologic age. The words “first appear”, the maturity of a symbol system, and the wide use of text in literature are three different times. [3]

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “texts are invented in a given year and then used simultaneously everywhere”. Egyptian holy books, Chinese Oracle and Central American scripts each have their own archaeological sequences that cannot be merged into a global starting point; within the two river basins there has also been a long-term development from counting to pronunciation. [1][3] Another misunderstanding is that all the early symbols are readable; many abstract marks are still unreadable.

Relationship to other phases

The textual record is mutually reinforcing with the countries of the agricultural surplus, the management of the city and the copper age; it brings information beyond the memory of the preachers. [2] [3] Compared to subsequent print, telegraph and digital networks, the early text is still the expertise of a very small number of bookkeepers and cannot be equated with modern universal reading and writing.
