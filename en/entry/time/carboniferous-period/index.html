What is it?

Charcoal is the fifth of the ancient era, after the mudslides and before the diaphragm, lasting about 60 million years. It is well known for its vast coal-bearing swamp forests, large stork trees and diverse amphibians and is one of the earliest times in which reptiles appear. [1] [2] The combination of terrestrial plants and marine invertebrates is significantly different from the mudbasin.

How?

Evidence comes from coal sstratigraphy, which is widely distributed in Europe and North America: the plant Fossil is dominated by stone pine, wedge and seed aluminum, with in situ stakes and roots indicating that forests are growing in swamps. [2] Sea-phased cortex in stratiprophy contains toothy, pyrethroids and gills, which can be used for comparison and yearning; the coal layer itself records large amounts of plant carbon deposits. [1][2] The alternation of the coal layer with the sea phase indicates that the lowland marsh has been repeatedly inundated by sea water, and this rotation also helps to establish internal steps. [2]

Common misunderstandings

One of the misunderstandings was to equate the charade with the “global coverage of the coal forest”. Coal-producing environments are concentrated in low-lying marshes and basins, with different deposits in arid highlands and oceans. [2] There is also a misconception that the high oxygen atmosphere is considered to be a confirmed fixed value; while studies suggest that oxygen concentrations were higher than modern at the time, the specific proportion is still dependent on the model and proxy indicators and cannot be considered as a direct medicality. [2]
