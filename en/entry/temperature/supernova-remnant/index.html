Supernova remnant shock temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Supernova relics are balloon shells formed by the interaction of explosive projectiles with surrounding media. The heat heats the gas up to about 10⁶ K order of magnitude and sends X-rays; young remains can become hotter and reach about ZXXQPH1QZXK. This is the temperature of the post-heat gas, not the light ball of the flash.

The bulges left behind by the explosion.

Supernovae remains are the swelling structure of the interaction of material thrown after the Big Massstar explosion with the surrounding interstellar medium. [1] The explosion injected a large amount of energy into the gas, the density of the blasts and the surge of the temperature, which was subsequently gradually cooled, slowed and incorporated into the interstellar medium.

How do X-rays read hot waves?

Post-heat gas heats up to the energy to emit soft X-rays, and satellites use spectrolines to combine electron temperature and ionizing. [1] The youth remnant is velocityly high, with high post-emperature waves; the older remnant is less velocity and less radiation. Synchronized radiation in the radio band mainly tracks relativity and cannot be counted directly as thermal gas temperature.

Millions of Kelvin's order of magnitude

The remnant heater gas is usually in millions of Kelvinorder of magnitude, and this article takes about 10⁶ K as the anchor. [1] Young relics can be hotter: In the N132D of Chandra observation, the heat heats the gas to about 10 million degree Celsius, about 10⁷ K. [2] Therefore, range is written about 10⁶-ZXXPH3QZXK to accommodate relics of different ages and environments.

It's not the same as the whole group.

The relics contain a forwarding wave, a cleaned shell, a reverse heat wave and an internal ejection, each of which is different from the ionizing state. [1] X-ray images see the overlay of these ingredients. Treating a temperature of a bright ring as a relic of the whole would ignore the internal structure and projection effects.

Comparison with other extreme temperatures

10⁶ K is higher than 10⁴ K in the ionizing hydrogen zone and is much higher than about 10 K in the molecular cloud; ZXXQPH2QZX K in the young relics is close to the temperature zone of the solar flare plasma. [2] But the heat of supernova remains from explosive kinetic energy rather than star stable combustion, which separates it from the planetary nebula and common ionizing hydrogen zones.
