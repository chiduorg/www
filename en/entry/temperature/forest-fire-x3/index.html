Temperature / High temperature

Wildfire flame temperature × 3

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Using 1,073 K of the "forest fires" as a benchmark, x 3 convert to 3,219 K; used to link the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is 3,219 K, obtained by the base reading of the Forest Fire 1,073 K multiplied by 3. The baseline is the representation of forest fires; conversion doesn't change the physical object, it changes the level of the scale we put it on. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review: 1,073 K x 3 = 3,219 K. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect "forest fires" to neighbouring intestry: when the reader asks "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller?", the article can be applied directly without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

When lined with the waste incinerator (1273 K); the crematorium (1073 K), see first whether the unit is uniform and then whether the reference is in diameter, radius or or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The baseline entry summary provides a readable starting point: the visible flame of the forest fires represents approximately 800 °C (1,073 K) and the measured flame is often about 800 – 1,200 K. This is a burning gas temperature from the front of the fire, not the fire field air temperature, the surface soil temperature or the average of the whole fire. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

♪ Bang, bang, bang ♪

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]

Final check: the baseline contains several credible numbers, and the results of the review keep several, without copying the end of the calculator. [1][2]
