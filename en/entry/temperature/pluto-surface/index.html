Pluto surface-temperature range reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Pluto surfaces are changing with regional, seasonal and observation information: the meeting quotes Summary gives about 33-55 K, NASA provides 24-38 K. 44 K on the temperature axis is only the mid-point between 33–55 K, not the single measured global average temperature or permanent upper and lower limit.

What is it?

Pluto's surface is described as a temperature of the near-surface ice of the dwarf planet, rather than a unified temperature within the entire body or above the atmosphere. The surface is covered by nitrogen, methane and carbon monoxide ice, and solar irradiation is weak, with only a few dozen Kelvins. [1] observation is not entirely consistent with the hot zone given by the study: a conference in 2025 wrote 33-55 K, NASA Pluto Fact Sheet 24-38 K. [1][2]

How?

The surface temperature comes from the Flying Infrared and Radiation Measurement, ground cover observation, and ice matter thermal model. The meeting summarized Pluto surface temperature as 33-55 K and discussed nitrogen ice rises, ice volcanoes and underground oceans; the NASA information sheet gave surface temperature 24-38 K, bold temperature 37.5 K. [1] [2] These ranges correspond to different regions, different seasons and different albedo rates and cannot be combined into a “single value”.

Value and Measurement condition

44 K on the temperature axis −229.15 °C, which is the midpoint of 33 and 55, is used only for mapping and positioning, not for adding a new object. [1] 44 K is not equal to the mid-point of 31 K in the NASA region 24-38 K. [2] When the two clusters are grouped together, the respective source and regional meanings shall be retained; the site of Pluto surfaces may be colder or warmer depending on latitude and season.

Common misunderstandings

First, make 44 K as Pluto's global warming; it's only the axis of the midpoint. [1] Second, the surface is mixed with the atmosphere; the thin atmosphere is different at different altitudes. Third, use 33–55 K as a permanent upper and lower limit; different studies and seasons can give a broader range. [2] Fourth, there is no geological activity at all at low levels; nitrogen-ice convection with a possible ice volcano indicates that the surface is still evolving. [1]

Relations with other temperate areas

K well below the average surface of 208 K on Mars and below the nitrogen 77.4 K boiling point and methane 111.7 K boiling point, but above absolute zero. [1][2] At such a low temperature, nitrogen ice remains solid, with only a small amount of volatile substances rising and condensing in the seasonal cycle. It shares the temperature of Mars and Venus with the magnitude of the temperature on the surface of planets in the solar system.
