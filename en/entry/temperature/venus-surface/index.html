Venus mean surface-temperaturee reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The NASA Venus fact sheet lists average surfaces as 737 K, about 464 °C, with surface air pressure about 92 bar. It is the average of the planet scale and does not correspond to the top of the cloud temperaturee, nor does it represent a complete unity of location and moment.

What is it?

The average surface of Venus is about 737 K, or 464 °C, and surface air pressure is about 92 bar, with the atmosphere dominated by CO2. [1][2] This is the planet scale average reference, describing the environment near the surface of Venus rather than the instant reading at the top of the cloud, at the polar altitude or at a landing site. Venus does not have four seasons like the Earth, and the overview shows that the daytime changes are close to zero, but this does not mean that each location is exactly the same temperaturee.

How?

Venus surface temperaturee comes from the lander's in situ measurement, orbital infrared and radar observation, and a planetary fact sheet that integrates these data. [1] Venus Fact Sheet of NASA NISDCA gives overview values in the form of a combination of average temperaturee, surface pressure and atmospheric composition; and Venusentry of Britannica provides the background of planetary size, orbit and dense carbon dioxide atmosphere. [1] [2] Owing to the limited number of landing missions, average surfaces are a combination of various types of management, rather than a map of global parity.

Value and Measurement condition

737 K - 273.15 K = 463.85 °C, with a factual table of 464 °C; if it is inversely calculated from 464 °C, 737.15 K. [1] This review does not add precision to the original overview. 92 bar is approximately 9.2 MPa, more than 90 times the Earth's sea level pressure; when discussing “how hot the surface of Venus” the pressure must be said in conjunction with tymperature, because the carbon dioxide there is at a supercritical level, not the usual pressure gas.

Common misunderstandings

“The planet Venus is nearer to the Sun, so surface heat” only explains part of it; it is the greenhouse effect of the dense carbon dioxide atmosphere that really pushes the surface up because of its high reaction rate, which should reflect more sunlight. [2] The second misunderstanding is that the cloudtop temperaturee is considered to be the surface temperaturee; the high-altitude cloud is distinct from the surface environment. Third, 737 K is considered to be only a little higher than the boiling point of water; it is sufficient to melt lead (lead melting point 600.612 K) and is much higher than the water threshold. Finally, the average temperaturee is not a measured value for each landing site.

Relations with other temperate areas

737 K is about 449 K above the Earth ' s surface average of 288 K and about 527 K above the surface of Mars average of 210 K; it is above the melting point of lead 600.612 K and above the boiling point of water 373.15 K. [1] It is less than the melting point of aluminium 933.473 K and less than about 1,811 K of iron. As a result, the surface of Venus is located in a hot zone where “lead melts and aluminium remains solid” and is an extreme reference for comparing greenhouse effects with material stability in planetary science.
