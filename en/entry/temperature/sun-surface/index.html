Solar effective temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Benentry uses about 5,772 K as an effective tumperature reference for the Sun, based on the nominal solar parameters of IAU 2015. A valid temperature summarises the total radiation, which does not mean that the ball of light happens to be at the same level everywhere.

The surface of the sun is a light ball.

The Sun has no solid shell like the Rock Planet. The surface is commonly referred to as the light ball: the area in which most of the visible light comes out of the solar atmosphere and constitutes the circle we see. It has thickness and internal structure and is not a hard shell that separates itself from the outside. [2]

It's a radiation problem.

Effective temperature links the radiation from the body as a whole to its surface area. It can be understood as an ideal bold one of the same size, and what kind of temperature has the same total radiation power. The IAU description is calculated on the basis of solar radiation photometry, the light ball radius and the Stefan-Bernzmann relationship, rather than a point-by-point temperature measure to the surface of the Sun. [1]

Why does it work together?

The IAU 2015 resolution recommends the notional effectiveness of the sun at 5,772 K for a consistent unit of researchers. Nominal values are agreed to be precise conversation factors, but are selected only in a position close to the real solar parameters; therefore, this page treats them as scalerereference to the sun, not as unlimited precision of the momentous state of the Sun. [1]

A number doesn't cover all regions.

The sun black is colder than the surrounding lightballs, with colour balls, transit zones and corona above. In particular, the corona can be far hotter than the light ball, so the change from the center to the temperature cannot simply be a downward curve. Effective temperature does not require that every substance is as warm as it is. [2]

This explains why the Star Catalogue lists both temperature, radius and light: the two objects that are close to temperature need not have the same total radiation power if they are different in size. Here we are talking about radiation relations, which cannot be further determined by a surface thermometer, either at its core or at its internal nuclear response stage. [1]

How do you understand with spacescale?

When comparing star, temperature needs to be matched with the radius: surface radiation is strong and the total output of the whole body is different. The spherical size, overall validity, and local activity images should be read separately. Textures and arc lines in the picture are used to identify the Sun and cannot be read only in colour or in real spectra. [1][2]

The error book of this value.

The Sun is effective at approximately 5772 K, which is the "temperature in bold" derived from total radiation and radius, not the actual temperature at every point of the light ball. Lightball temperature is about 5,000-6,000 K, darker, hotter corona. Using 5772 K as the balance of the Sun's surface is ignoring the structure of magnetic fields and convection.

Another scale to look at it.

5772 K is about 20 times the temperature of the room and about 3.2 times the melting point of iron 1811 K. The core of the Sun is about 15 million K, 2600 times more than the surface temperature; the corona can reach millions K, but it does not burn hands because of its extremely low density. The temperature of the sun is not a layer, but a gradient from the core to the corona.

Valid temperature is not the actual temperature

5,772 K is the bold equivalent of the total solar radiation to the radius, and is not the temperature reading at every point of the ball. [1] Lightballs have about 5,000-6,000 K distributions, darker, hotter corona. Using effective temperature as a flat surface temperature ignores the structure created by magnetic fields and convection.

Why is the corona hotter than the surface?

The corona is millions of K, but the density is extremely low, so the total is much smaller than the light ball. [2] Its gheat engine system, which involves processes such as magnetic reconnection, wave depletion, etc., remains the subject of research. The high corona is not to say that the surface of the Sun is “heated” or that the corona burns like a stove.

Five-axis Contrast

On the temperature axis, the light ball is about 5,772 K; on the space axis, the light ball is the visible dome; on the mass axis, the solar mass determines the core temperature and lightness; and on the time axis, the surface activity of the Sun changes with the 11-year cycle. Effective temperature, local temperature is not the same as the activity cycle. [1][2]
