Why is the boiling point subject to pressure?

77.34 K corresponds to normal boiling point reduction and cannot be understood to mean that the liquid nitrogen in any container coincides with this temperature. The description of the liquid sample needs to be accompanied by an indication of the pressure and whether it is in a gaseous balance. These requirements have been omitted from the viewing of liquid surfaces, bubbles or white fog as temperature. [1]

How the data sheets organize experimental knowledge

NIST does not list only one number, but also gives a literature book and lists boiling points, melting points, three phase points, etc. It even retained the values of the different studies and some uncertainty explanations. This suggests that when comparing accurately, the same physical volume and proximity should be used, and that the three phase points should not be misconstrued as constant boiling points. [1]

What's the white fog outside the container?

White clouds near cold liquids can easily be assumed to be white. The nitrogen information attached to the US Chemical Safety Committee report indicates that the release at low temperatures results in water condensation in the surrounding air, forming an ice or water fog. The visible clouds therefore contain reactions from the surrounding environment rather than direct photographs of the sample ' s physical state. [3]

For example, when comparing liquid nitrogen with liquid oxygen, it is worth comparing their normal boiling points; if one takes the three-phase point and the other takes the boiling point, the figures, although expressed as K, are changed. The same unit is only the starting point for comparison, and the physical state and pressure make the coordinates truly interpretable. [1]

Safety 77 K

The liquid nitrogen boiling point is about 77 K (−196 °C) and exposure to the skin causes instantaneous burns; evaporation in a closed container creates high pressure and the discharge channel must be left. [2] 77 K is not “ordinary cold”, but a low-temperature liquid that requires a specific dowa, gloves and ventilation. Low is not the same as low risk.

White fog is not the nitrogen itself.

The white fog around the liquid nitrogen is water vapour condensation in the air and is not visible. [3] Nitrogen is colourless and can be replaced with oxygen during leakage, causing asphyxiation. Treating white fog as nitrogen ignores the real oxygen risk.

Read-ment convention and conversion

The quantity used for this article is approximately 77.34 K (−195.81 °C), and the material condition is nitrogen-normal-boiling-temperature. Temperature is the strength, and readings depend on the thermometer, the point and the motheral equilibrium; different parts of the same object can vary at different times. When comparing this reading to the adjacententry, replace it with the same unit and then take the margin; do not reduce it directly with a different value. If there is only one thing to remember: the answer is “the object probably falls on which order of magnitude”, it is not a constant that can replace all samples.
