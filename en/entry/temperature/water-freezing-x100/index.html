27,315 K, based on the "water ice point" baseline; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measration.

What's the value?

The value of this article is 27,315 K, obtained by the base reading of "water ice point" 273.15 K multiplied by 100. The baseline is the constant pressure of ice water balance temperature; conversion does not change the physical object, it changes the level on which scale we put it. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review: 273.15 K x 100 = 27,315 K. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the "water ice point" to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks, "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller?", the article can be directly provided without recalculating. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with the boiling point of water (373.15 K); the three water phase points (273.16 K) to see first whether the unit is uniform, then whether the reference is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The baseline provides a readable starting point: near standard atmospheric pressure, the balance of ice and water is approximately 273.15 K (0 °C). This is a conditioned case changer; precision temperature measurement requires consideration of pressure, composition and dissolved gases, rather than considering the freezing process of all water exactly the same. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

♪ Bang, bang, bang ♪

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]

Boiling water

Water triple-point temperature reference

The three water phase points are assigned a value of 273.16 K, or 0.01 °C, corresponding to pressure 611.657 Pa. It is a state of reference where ice, liquid water and water vapour coexist; since 2019, Kelvin has changed from Bolzmann constant to definition.
