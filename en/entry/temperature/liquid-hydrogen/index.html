Common misunderstandings

First, the liquid hydrogen is considered to be blue; the liquid hydrogen itself is colourless and the blue is often from containers or lighting. Second, consider 20 K as “near absolutely difficult to liquefied”; it is warmer than liquid helium, liquid nitrogen, and industrial liquefaction is mature. The liquid hydrogen has a boiling point below the liquid nitrogen, and leaks in storage cause evaporation more quickly and the insulation requirements are higher. [1]
