Permafrost ground temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The temperature of frozen land for many years is common in successive tundra strips −5 to −15 °C, which is taken from −10 °C (263 K). It refers to the underground soil or rock's temperature, not surface temperature; 0 °C is the upper limit of the temperature.

What is it?

Years of frozen soil are ground, soil, sedition or rock that has been maintained at or below 0 °C for two consecutive years. [1] “Years of freezing tamperature” means the temperature of the ground inside the frozen soil, not the surface temperature. The temperature of the continuous tundra zone may be as low as -15 °C, and the non-serial belt is usually higher than −3 °C; this article takes −10 °C (263 K) as its representative value. [2] 0 °C is the upper limit of the presence of frozen soil, but reaching 0 °C does not mean that the soil must contain large quantities of ice.

How?

The temperature is long-term measurement from the temperature recorder in the drill, usually from the surface to below the upper limit of years of frozen soil. NSIDC emphasizes that the definition of the tundra only looks at temperature and continuoustime, without water, snow or location. [1] USGS, based on information from 350 boreholes, indicates that the temperature of the uncontinuous tundra is generally higher than −3 °C and that the continuous belt may be as low as −15 °C. [2] These are average annual temperatures, not the temperature of one day. The temperature of snow can keep the ground warmer than the temperature, while the activity layer freezes with the season.

Value and Measurement condition

K equals −10.15 °C, approximately −10 °C. From -5 to -15 °C is the common average annual temperature range of continuous thaws, but the layer of activity melts during the summer and freezes in winter; therefore, the thaw must indicate depth and season. [2] Surface temperatures may be much lower or higher than surface temperatures, and snow absorbs temperatures above temperature. 0 °C is the upper limit of the presence of frozen soil, not the only temperature.

Common misunderstandings

First, the tundra is considered temperature; the tundra is an underground medium with high heat inertia and slow seasonal variability. Second, the thaw is considered to be the same temperature throughout the year; the layer of activity will freeze with the season. Third, treating 0 °C as "frozen must be cold"; definition requires only ≤0 °C, which can actually be near -0.1 °C. Fourthly, it is believed that frozen soil exists only in the polar regions; there are also years of frozen soil in high altitudes.

Relationship to other temperature

−10 °C below 0 °C of water-freezing and 4 °C of Fridge, but higher than −18 °C of freezer. It is above absolute-zero and well below human-body-temperature. Both are cold compared to about 2°C in deep-ocean, but one is ice-containing soil and one is liquid sea water. The key to understanding temperature is to separate the temperature from the ice point.
