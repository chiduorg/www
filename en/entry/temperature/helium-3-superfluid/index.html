Temperature / Ultra-cold

Helium-3 superfluid A point on the PLTS-2000

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The BIPM PLTS-2000 lists the overflow A points on the helium-3 melting line as 2.444 mK, 3.43407 MPa. This article replaces 2.5 mK without pressure with this clear temperature fixed point; it is not a uniform transformation of helium-3 under arbitrary pressure.

What is it?

Helium-3 is a fermeteor, which, at very low temperatures, forms a Cooper pair and enters an ultra-current phase. The PLTS-2000 thermometer uses a melting pressure of 0.9 mK to 1 K in the temperature zone of Helium-3, of which point A is the feature point of the conversion of normal liquids from the melting curve to the ultra-current A phase. [1] This point is not a case changeemperature under arbitrary pressure, but a temperature fixed point with clear pressure coordinates. In PLTS-2000, it is a check point for cryogenic markers, together with the A-B shift and the Nair point. [1]

How or how it works?

When achieved, helium-3 is fed into a melted pressure pool, using the in situ pressure sensor measurement pressure, and the pressure is converted to T2000 using the PLTS-2000 formula. The corresponding pressure for point [1][2] A is 3.43407 MPa, temperature is 2.444 mK; the same temperature also lists the A-B transformation 1.896 mK and Nair point 0.902 mK. [1] Melting pressure pools must measure in situ pressure because the low-temperature pressure catheter is clogged with solid helium-3. [2] This is a thermometer grant, not a single temperature reading.

Value and Measurement condition

2.444 mK equals 0.002444 K. The BIPM Guide gives the A point relative to the othermodynamic temperature standard uncertainty is 48 μK (k=1), while the best actual achievement of uncertinty is approximately 0.7 μK; unlike the two, the medical condition condition cannot be used. [2] It is therefore appropriate as an anchor for temperature markers and should not be considered a constant of “helium-3 in any condition at 2.444 mK”. The difference between point A and point A-B is about 0.548 mK and point Nair is about 1.542 mK. [1]

Common misunderstandings

First, the 2.444 mK is applied directly to low-pressure helium-3 or helium-4; the helium-4 is converted to about 2.17 K, three times different. Second, consider point A, point A-B and point Nair as the same transformation; they are different face change features on the melting curve. Third, the resolution of the temperature marker uncertatinty as an experimental device; 48 μK is the value uncertatinty given to the termodynamics, not the minimum scale of the tymperature. [2]

Relations with other temperate areas

2.444 mK is less than 10 mK of diluted refrigeration, 421 mK electron temperature, and more than 170 nK of botanical condensation. It's about 900 times lower than the 2.17 K superflow transformation of helium-4, which suggests that the isotope quantum statistical difference will bring the same element case change temperature away. [1][2]

The othermodynamicsteperature unit of this article is based on BIPM's definition for Kelvin. [3]
