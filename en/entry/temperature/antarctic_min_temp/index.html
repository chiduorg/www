Vostok near-surface air temperature record

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

On 21 July 1983, the Eastern Antarctic Station recorded near-ground temperatures of -89.2 °C, equivalent to 183.95 K. The WMO meteorological observation records are used here to describe the measurement condition of the van, the site and the date, and do not mix the ice sheet of the satellite into the same ranking.

What is it?

The East Antarctic station is located inland on the eastern Antarctic ice sheet, at an altitude of approximately 3420 metres. On 21 July 1983, the lowest temperature form in the van was recorded at -89.2 °C, i.e. 183.95 K. [1][2] This is near-ground air attemperature, not the radiation on the surface of the ice sheet attemperature, or at the same time the entire Antarctica.

How or how it works?

According to the WMO file, the readings were taken from the lowest temperature table in the standard 100-leaf box, at a point of 77°32'S, 106°40'E, and the records were duly reviewed. [1] The van keeps the temperature watch away from sunlight and ground radiation and measures the air at a certain altitude from the ground. Extremely low temperatures occur in winter conditions with no sunlight, clear air, still wind and longtimetimetime lack of vertical mixing.
