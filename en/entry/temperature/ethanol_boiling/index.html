Ethanol normal boiling point

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The normal boiling point for pure ethanol under 1 atm is approximately 351.5 K, or 78.35 °C. The NIST data average is used here; the boiling point of alcohol, disinfectant or ethanol water solution varies with composition and cannot be directly applied to pure ethanol.

What is it?

The normal boiling point for pure ethanol under 1 atm is approximately 351.5 K, or 78.35 °C. [1][2] This is the balance between the pure liquid phase and the gas phase under constant pressure temperature; it is not the boiling point of white wine, disinfectant alcohol or ethanol water solution. The boiling point containing aqueous ethanol changes composition and is subject to a constant boiling mixture when distilled, so "78.35 °C" is only for a state of pure ethanol.

How?

Normal boiling point by boiling or vapour pressure method measurement: Placing pure ethanol in a controlled pressure container, slowly warming, recording continuous boiling of liquid phase and stabilization at 1 atm; it may also be pushed from the vapour pressure curve. [1] NIST Chemistry WebBook's summary of case change data gives 351.5 K and indicates the value of 0.2 K as uncertainty; ethanolentry of Britannica gives 78.5 °C as a round value at encyclopedia level. [1][2] The water content of the sample, pressure control and temperature calibration affect the reading.

Value and Measurement condition

The difference of 351.5 K - 273.15 K = 78.35 °C; 78.5 °C in Britannica is approximately 0.15 K, which is the difference between rounding and data. [1][2] This conversion does not add precision to the original data. ethanol and water form a constant boiling mixture with a maximum direct distillation of approximately 95.6% mass of ethanol at which point the boiling point is no longer equivalent to 78.35 °C of pure ethanol. [2] When comparing “alcohol boiling points”, it must be stated whether it is pure ethanol or a water solution at a certain concentration.

Common misunderstandings

The concentration is ignored in “all alcohol at 78.35 °C boiling”: ethanol water solution usually has a boiling point higher than pure ethanol and changes with composition. [2] The second misunderstanding is that the boiling point is used as a pureness criterion; the boiling point can only describe the condition of ethanol and cannot independently prove ethanol content. Third, the boiling point of alcohol is considered to be close to 100 °C of water; the higher the actual alcohol level, the closer the initial boiling typerature to pure ethanol. Finally, pressure changes move boiling points and values measured at high altitude cannot be directly compared to sea level values.

Relations with other temperate areas

351.5 K is about 21.7 K below the normal boiling point of water (373.15 K) and about 112 K above the boiling point of ammonia (239.8 K); it is well below the boiling point of mercury (629.81 K) and below the boiling point of sodium (1,156 K). [1] It is above the three water phase 273.16 K, and above the room temperature of approximately 293 K. The ethanol boiling point is thus located at the end of the "famation near constant temperature" and is a common reference for distillation, disinfection and fuel volatility discussions.
