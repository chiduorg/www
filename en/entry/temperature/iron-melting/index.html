Iron melting point

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The normal pressure melting point for pure iron is approximately 1,811 K, or 1,538 °C. This value is pure eliment iron and does not directly indicate the complete melting of steel, biomass or high-pressure subterranean steel containing carbon and other element.

What is it?

Here is a discussion of the melting point of pure eliment iron under constant pressure, i.e. the balance of solid iron and liquid iron near 1 atm. Iron is a common metal in the crust and a major component of steel; however, the “iron melting point” is not the same as “temperaturee of steel”. [1][2] Solid iron melts around 1,538 °C and steel is an alloy of iron and carbon and other element.

How?

The melting point of element is usually determined by thermal analysis, electrical resistance, radiation temperaturee detection or static and dynamic compression experiments, with a common pressure approximation given in the standard table. The table of iron content of the Royal Society of Chemistry shows 1,538 °C, 1,811 K; the table of iron properties of Britannica also gives 1,538 °C (2,800 °F). [1][2] Standard table values are multi-wheeled and rounded and suitable for use as a resource material, but the end of the different tables may vary by Kelvin. Thermal differential analysis is judged by heat-sorting peaks, with radiation temperaturee measurements depending on the launch rate model; both are sensitive to the surface state of the sample.

Value and Measurement condition

1,811 K conversion is 1,537.85 °C, which corresponds to a circle of 1,538 °C. [1][2] This value corresponds to conditions of pure iron, constant pressure, and no significant impurities. Steels contain about 0.1% - 2% carbon, and their liquid phase temperaturee changes with composition, usually below the pure iron melting point; high carbon steel, alloy steel and cast iron are less likely to be directly applied to 1,811 K. [1][3] Iron in the core is under pressure of hundreds of gypsies, and the melting point will increase significantly and does not fall under this condition.

Common misunderstandings

First, use the 1,811 K as a melting point for all "iron materials"; the melting zone for raw iron, steel and iron alloys varies. [3] Second, mix the degree Celsius with Kelvin: 1,538 °C is 1,811 K, not 1,538 K. Third, it is considered that the melting point is immediately fully liquid; the alloy is usually partially melted between the liquid phase and the solid phase. Fourth, the high pressure melting point of the sub-nucleus or the temperaturee of iron vapour is compared, ignoring pressure and phase conditions.

Smelting steel is about 1,893 K, slightly above the pure iron melting point, indicating that steel fluids need overheating to flow and water.
