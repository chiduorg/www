Sunspot cool-region reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Darker in visible light because the area is colder than the surrounding lightball. NASA's black chorus uses about 4,000 K to compare the light ball of 6,000 K; Benentry uses this order of magnitudeference, not as the exact temperature of every black, every film or semi-shadow location.

What is it?

The Sun Black is an area with a strong concentration of magnetic fields in the light ball and appears to be darker in visible light than around it; the dark centre is called Ben Shadow, and the brighter ring outside is called Half Shadow. [3] It is not a “no-luminous hole”, but a low-level, less-irradiated lightball area; what is said here, 4,000 K is the location of black shadows, not an effective temperature. [1]

How?

The black temperature can be inferred from visible and infrared light, spectroscopy and model in the solar atmosphere: by comparing the spectral energy distribution of the film, semi-shadow and quiet spherical ball, each can estimate its effectiveness. [3] The presentation for NASA uses about 4,000 K to compare the light ball of about 6,000 K to indicate that the black "dark" is from the temperature differential rather than a mask. [1] NASA's Sun Overview also points out that blacks are just a little colder than the surrounding lightballs. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

Here's about 4,000 K for regional reference, without writing more fine decimals. The black book is usually the coldest, with warmer shadows, and near the outer edge of the area of activity; therefore 4,000 K is not the average of the black or the whole area of activity. [1][3] Observation at heights, magnetic field strength and black age all alters the structure of the temperature, and differences of about hundreds of Kelvin are common; the temperature difference between this film and the semi-image can be hundreds of miles wide, and the larger it becomes more visible.

Common misunderstandings

First, the black thing is considered a completely black hole; it's still glowing, just darker than the surrounding lightball. Second, treat 4,000 K as a single temperature of all blacks; there are differences between this film, half shadow and different twats. [1][3] Third, the presence of blacks is considered to necessarily lead to flares or communication disruptions; the weather effects of space require specific events, magnetic field evolution and corona conditions. [2]

Relations with other temperate areas

About 5,800-6,000 K, which is lower than the quiet light ball, is also lower than the top of the colour ball; it is higher than about 2,400 K at the lower end of the red dwarf, but still far below the million Kelvin of the solar corona. Blacks are “cool spots” inside the light ball, which directly compares them with the nuclear combustion of the core of the Sun or the coronal of timperature, confusing completely different regions and adding heat engine. [1][2]
