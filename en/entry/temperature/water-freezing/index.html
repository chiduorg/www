Ice–water equilibrium

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The balance between ice and water is approximately 273.15 K (0 °C) near the standard atmospheric pressure. This is a conditioned case changer; precision temperature measurement requires consideration of pressure, composition and dissolved gases, rather than considering the freezing process of all water exactly the same.

Ice points describe a balance.

It's a very useful approximation to call zero °C a water ice point. Here is a discussion of the temperature of ice, liquid water and air when the corresponding balance is achieved, and not any glass of water will start to freeze at the same speed in any environment as the first reading. NIST studies incorporate pressure and water dissolved atmospheric gases into the analysis. [1]

Why "about 273.15 K?"

The ice point given under standard atmospheric pressure and its model conditions is approximately 273.1502 K. This page collects approximately 273.15 K for daily scale and does not use more decimal places in the paper to suggest that containers and temperature meters are equally accurate. The study also compared measurement at different altitudes and under air pressure, suggesting that precision results had to be read in conjunction with environmental conditions. [1]

Temperature conversion approximation of accuracy, similar to case changeference

The difference between the values of temperature and Kelvin temperature is 273.15, which is the relationship of the temperature conversion; the temperature difference is the same as the temperature difference of 1°C. So 0 °C corresponds to 273.15 K, not an integer 273 K. Accuracy of the conversion relationship does not transform a true sample under given conditions into a natural constant without conditions. [2]

The ice doesn't mix with the three.

The ice water system that is exposed to air in ice point experiments has different pressure and gas conditions than the three-phase water system used for temperature monitoring in precision. The NIST paper is the test of model by the difference between the two; both pressure effects and dissolved air effects are included in the paper. Thus, the freezing readings of 0 °C, 3 points and any water sample cannot be considered the same simply because the two figures are very close. [1]

How do you understand this?

Ice blocks, water surfaces and containers indicate a link between solid and liquid state, and no crystal rate is calculated or the temperature field at each point inside the container. Reference on the temperature ruler answers, “What kind of phase change condition” and the animation does not answer, “How long does it take a glass of water to freeze?” Continuing to read the boiling point of water allows comparison of the same substance with the other group of pressure and physical conditions. [1]

The error book of this value.

The ice point of water 273.15 K (0 °C) is a definition point under standard atmospheric pressure; pressure, dissolved matter and overcooling alter the actual freezing of the ice. Seawater ice points are about -2 °C, and high salt halogenated water is lower; pure water can be too cold under 0 °C without disturbance. 0 °C is not all water that freezes at the same time.

Another scale to look at it.

0 °C equals 273.15 K, which is 273.15 K higher than absolute Zero, or approximately 93 per cent of room temperature. It differs from the human core at 37°C 37 K; the liquid window of water is very narrow compared to the solar surface 5772 K. Many of the reactions to life on Earth occur between 0-100°C, but this is not the only state of water in the UNiverse.

Too cold and nuclear.

Pure water can be cooled to remain liquid below 0 °C without disturbance or dust, which is called too cold. [1] The freezing of ice requires a nuclear location, and the container wall, impurities or bubbles are reduced to a nuclear base. Seeing that “below 0°C is not frozen” does not mean that the ice point is wrong, but simply that the power condition is required.

Solvents lower the ice.

The ice point of seawater, salt water and sugar-containing solutions is below 0 °C; solubility reduces the gas phase chemistry of water and requires lower temperature to balance with ice. [1][2] It's precisely this effect that is being exploited by the salinization of roads. The ice point is not the only fixed value of pure water and the sample composition and pressure must be specified.

Five-axis Contrast

On the temperature axis, the pure water ice point is approximately 273.15 K; on the space axis, the ice crystal is different from the surface; on the mass axis, the water molecular mass is about 2.99×10⁻²⁶ kg; and on the time axis, the slow ice-freezing depends on the formation of the nuclear and dissipation heat. temperature, composition and time decide whether to freeze.
