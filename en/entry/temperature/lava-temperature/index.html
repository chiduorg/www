Temperature / High temperature

Kilauea lava temperature reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

A specific example of high temperature is given in the lava records of Kirawehe: USGS records the eruption of lava at about 1150-1170°C from 1980 to 1990 in the global age, while in recent years it has landed at about 1144-1191°C. It's a hot zone where all the volcanoes, all the compositions of the lava, can't be identical.

What is it?

Here we are talking about the lava that ejected the surface, not the magma house. Melting rock is the melting rock, and the volcano in Kirawehe is dominated by basalt lava; it is usually at 1,100–1,200 °C order of magnitude, but varies with composition, vent phase, distance from vent and surface cooling. [1][2] The lava flow surface and interior is not the motheral equilibrium state, and much of the temperature measured is the effective temperature at a given location.

How?

The USGS Hawaiian Volcanic Observation Station uses thermoelectric dolls to insert active lava currents, calibration thermal imager and rock studies to measure temperature. Volcano Watch 2013 describes the lava sprayed in the East Rift Valley at approximately 1,150-170 °C from 1980-1990. [1] 2026 Observation gives 1,159–1,165 °C in 2018, 1,144–1,170 °C in 2020–2023, and 1,165–1,191 °C in 2024. [2] USGS also gave examples of approximately 1,250 °C in a lava tube, indicating a wide variety of locations within the same vent system. [3]

Value and Measurement condition

1,150 –1,170 °C conversion is 1,423.15 –1,443.15 K, numeric midpoint 1,433.15 K for positioning purposes only. [1] Colours can be roughly estimated: yellow about 1,000 - 1,200 °C, orange about 800 - 1,000 °C and red about 600 - 800 °C, but not precisiontime. [3] The surface can cool hundreds of degree Celsius in seconds to minutes, so the lava temperature must indicate whether the surface, the interior or the vent are measured. [2] [3]

Common misunderstandings

First, the 1,150–1,170 °C of Kiraweig is considered to be the temperature of all volcanic lava; the flowline lava is more adhesive and cold, and the basalt lava is hotter. [1][3] Second, use lava as a combination of the othermal equilibrium; there is a convection within the lava stream, a crust and a gas escape, with a large temperature difference between the surface and the interior. Third, the temperature is read precisely in the eye color; colour is only a rough indicator. [3] Fourth, the word “temperature” is confused with “temperature”, which is often re-engineered by mineral chemistry and can be dozens more degree Celsius. [1][2]

Relations with other temperate areas

1,433 K above the boiling point of water 373.15 K and the melting point of aluminium 933.15 K below the pure iron melting point 1,811 K. It is lower than approximately 1,893 K of steel or about 2.8×10⁴ K of the lightning channel. Compared to the 6,230 K of pure iron melted from the Earth's core, lava is just a softer melting rock from the surface of the Earth.
