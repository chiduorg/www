Global mean sea surface temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The annual average of the global sea watch is about 16.9-17.0 °C in recent years and this article takes 290 K (approximately 16.9 °C). It is the top-level, area-weighted climate indicator of the oceans, not the temperature of a certain location or of the oceans as a whole.

What is it?

The average temperature of the global sea table is the annual average of the temperature weighted by area from all parts of the ocean surface. It is not a temperature measured by a ship or buoy, but a climate indicator integrated with multiple data sets. [3] This average in recent years is approximately 16.9-17.0 °C, which takes 290 K (approximately 16.9 °C). It describes the top of the ocean, not the average of the ocean as a whole.

How?

Seawatchtime comes from platforms such as satellite infrared and microwave remote sensing, ship observation, buoys and Argo, and is modified by deviation correction and plugs to form grid data sets. NOAA's Climate at a Gloance gives the 2024 global ocean surface at +0.95 °C based on 1901-2000. [2] Conversion with a long-term average of about 16.1 °C can be obtained at about 17.0 °C; differences between different data sets and base periods may be given at about 16.9-17.0 °C. [1][3] This is a weighted statistics, not a single-point measurement.

Value and Measurement condition

290 K equals 1685 °C, approximately 16.9 °C. “Sea surface” here usually refers to surfaces or mixed layers of skins ranging from a few millimeters to a few metres deep, rather than deep-sea temperature; the difference between day and night, the equator and the polar regions is very large. The global average is a weighted result of size and does not represent any particular sea area. [3] 2024 was the warmest year ever recorded, and the global seatime table temperature reached new heights; this makes it necessary to separate “multi-year average” from “a year average”. The data set and the base period shall be indicated.

Common misunderstandings

First, the global average is considered to be 16.9 °C for all seawater; the polar sea surface is close to the ice point and the tropical zone is 30 °C. Second, the sea table is considered temperature; the two are not equal. Third, single-year records are considered long-term climate values; records in 2023, 2024 are higher than long-term averages. Fourth, the differences in data sets are ignored; different institutions, different reference periods and different interpolation methods give a little bit of difference in degree Celsius.

Relationship to other temperature

16.9 °C close to room-temperature, above approximately 2 °C in deep-ocean and above 0 °C in water-freezing. It is lower than the normal core of human-body-temperature and is much lower than 100 °C of boiling-water. Seawatchtime is an important baseary condition for the climate system, but it is only the average state of the top ocean and does not represent the heat content of the ocean as a whole.

Seawatch is not temperature.

Seawatch temperature is the uppermost temperature of seawater and the temperature is the air temperature; the two interact with each other through heat, latent heat and radiation exchange, but with different values. [1] [3] The sea temperature in the same sea area may be lower by day and higher by night. Combining them would misjudge ocean heat waves and weather.

Five-axis Contrast

On the temperature axis, the global sea surface averages about 16.9 °C; on the space axis, the sea surface covers approximately 71 per cent of the Earth; on the mass axis, the upper seawater is high; and on the time axis, sea temperature changes lag behind temperature. temperature, area and weight drive climate.
