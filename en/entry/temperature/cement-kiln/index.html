Temperature / High temperature

Cement rotary kiln burning zone

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Temperature, which is burned into a cement kiln, is usually about 1,450 °C (1,723 K). This is the local temperature of the mobile material bed, not the flame of the kiln head, the preheater, or themperature after the clinker cools.

What is it?

Cement kilns are rotary kilns that burn raw materials such as limestone and clay into cement clinkers. The discussion here is about the burning belt (the highest section of the material) temperature, which usually takes about 1,450 °C, or 1,723 K. [1] It is not the flame of the kiln head temperature, nor is it the temperature when the preheater is exported or the clinker cools. The raw materials are then dry, preheated, decomposition, burning and cooling within the kiln, with the burning belt being only the highest part of the tymperature. [2]

How?

It is difficult to insert thermal voltage directly within the rotary kiln, and industrially it is used more indirectly to measure temperature in kiln shells, flue gas composition, clinker rock phase and infrared temperature to determine the state of the belt. [1] The Danish Technical University's overview has written the burning material temperature to a maximum of about 1,450 °C, while the Portland Cement Association of the United States describes the high temperature in the kiln as “approximately 2,700 °F”, which is approximately 1,482 °C. [1][2]

Value and Measurement condition

1,450 °C conversion is 1,723 K. [1] Materials remain in the burn belt for only a few minutes to ten minutes, and temperature is the local value of the mobile material bed, not the average temperature of the kiln. The kiln head flame may exceed 1,800 °C, but the flame is a gas-air reaction area and cannot be used intermingled with the material. [2] [3] Different kiln types, fuel and raw materials ratio will burn up and down into a band of temperatures about 50-100 K. [3]

Common misunderstandings

First, the temperature of the kiln is treated as a flat number; the gradient within the kiln is hundreds of Kelvin in the pre-tropical to cool zone. Second, the flame is treated as a material; the fire radiation heats the material, but the temperature of the material is lagging. Third, the cement kilns are confused with the cement use temperature; cement hydrolytics are much lower than temperature. [1][2]

Relations with other temperate areas

1,723 K is less than the approximately 1,986 K of quartz melting, higher than the approximately 1,473 K of basalt melting, and close to the approximately 1,773 K of iron water from furnaces. It is in a partially melted zone of silicate, sufficient for clinker minerals to form, but below the melting point of tungsten 3,695 K. [1][3]
