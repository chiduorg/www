Absolute zero

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Absolute Zero is the zero point of the temperature of the termodynamics: 0 K equals − 273.15 °C. Decline to zero is not equivalent to the disappearance of all micromotor movements; quantum zero motion cannot be expressed in total static of classic particles.

Where does the thermometer start?

Absolute Zero is the zero point of the thermometer for thermodynamics. 0 K and −273.15°C represent the same temperature, while zero°C remains 273.15 K. Kelvin does not have the word 'degree'; the temperature difference of 1 K is as large as the temperature difference of 1°C, the difference being that the two thermometers choose different zeros. [1]

Temperature doesn't mean microworld static.

Think of the cooling as all the particles stopping. It's a classic image. Quantum theory allows for zero-point motion at zero temperature, which is not a heat-driven movement. As a result, animated particles in the picture are stopped and can only express a state of display, which does not prove that the position, the momentum and the quantum rise have become zero. [1]

How to create a teleperature reading

Realistic temperature measurements need to link instrument readings to recoverable physical properties. Modern Kelvin uses fixed Bolzmann constants; the laboratory can still use the calibration temperature, etc. The zero-point definition does not require the manufacture of a genuine 0 K sample and the acquisition of all of its equipment. The third law of thermodynamics further indicates that 0 K can only approach indefinitely and cannot be achieved with limited steps. [2]

Energy scale and cool scale

The Bolzmann constant links the temperature to microenergy scale, while the movement of large numbers of particles needs to be described in statistical distribution. The same temperature does not mean that each particle has the same speed or that objects of different sizes have the same total energy. The closer to zero experiments also indicate which part of the substance and its state is the measurement. [3]

For example, when discussing “down to half of the original”, absolute thermometers must be selected; dropping from 20 °C to 10 °C is not the same as reducing by half the amount of thermodynamic system temperature. This simple conversion suggests that the zero-point relationship in the scale chart is more informative than the gradual dilution of all cooling into the same colour. [1]

What do you mean zero on the scale?

From liquid nitrogen to lower temperature systems, readings can be kept close to the starting point of the thermometer; the zero K in the figure is the position of definition, not the experimental record. This precise zero point is retained here in order to understand how far other temperatures are from; the appearance and movement of the device are conceptual indications.

The error book of this value.

Absolute Zero is 0 K, i.e. − 273.15 °C, the theoretical lower limit of thermodynamicsteperature. It cannot be achieved by a limited number of steps, but only by unlimited access; the microwave background of universe is approximately 2.725 K, which is the current low-temperature background of unverse, not absolute zero. Write "no energy" as "no energy" for "no energy": Quantum zero can still exist.

Another scale to look at it.

0 K is 293 K different from room temperature; room temperature is closer to absolute Zero than the Sun surface 5772 K. The liquid helium enters the ultra-current state below 4.2 K and the superconductor produces zero electrical resistance below the threshold temperature. Absolute Zero is not an accessible room, but a line of approach.

Zero-K is a step-by-step, not a accessible room.

According to the third law of thermodynamics, limited steps cannot be taken to cool the system to absolute zero; for each drop in temperature, there is a growing number of heads that need to be removed. [1][2] The lowest possible temperature of laboratories is measured in pikelvin, Nakelvin, but is still not 0 K. Draws 0 K as a solid line at the bottom of the temperature count, only as agreed at coordinates.

Five-axis Contrast

On the tymperature axis, 0 K is definition zero; on the space axis, the cryogenic experimental device isolates the sample from the outside world; on the time axis, the microwave background is approximately 2.7 K, which is the current low temperature background of universe; and on the mass axis, the zero of the helium-4 atom nuclear indicates that 0 K still has quantum motion. Zero, background temperature is not the same concept as the quantum base. [1][3]

Negative is not colder than 0 K

A system that reverses the number of particles can appear as "negative temperature", but its energy is higher than the infinity temperature and still much hotter than 0 K. Negative temperature appears only in the statistical description of a specific isolated quantum system, not in the lower of the refrigerator. Draws negative temperature on the left side of 0 K, misreading the order of the thermometer. [1][S1]
