Global mean sea surface temperature × 0.5

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Based on the "surface average" of 290 K, x 0.5 convert obtained 145 K; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is 145 K, which is obtained by the base reading of "themperature on the surface" 290 K multiplied by 0.5. The baseline is the average annual temperature of the world's seawatch; conversion does not change the physical object, it changes the level of the scale on which we put it. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review: 290 K x 0.5 = 145 K. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the temperature of the surface with the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks “What it feels like to be bigger/smaller”, it can be directly related to this article without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with the deep sea temperature (275 K); the water ice point (273.15 K) to see whether the unit is uniform, then whether the comparator is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The baseline provides a readable starting point: the annual average of the global sea table is about 16.9-17.0 °C in recent years, which takes 290 K (approximately 16.9 °C). It is the top-level, area-weighted climate indicator of the oceans, not the temperature of a certain location or of the oceans as a whole. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.
