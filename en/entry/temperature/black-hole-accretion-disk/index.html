Black-hole accretion disk gas: simulation reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Broadly relativistic magnetic fluid simulations show that non-rotational black holes can draw gases close to the centre to reach about 10 million K, and the corona on the plate is hotter. This article compares only the order of gasnide, and does not treat it as a black hole typerature, nor does it mix it with the corona.

What is it?

A black hole absorber is a flat-tanger structure formed by the gas at the time of its gravitational fall. The gas does not fall directly into the vision, but is transported slowly internally within the disk due to magnetic fluid flow and viscosity, which is compressed, heated and sends low-energy soft X rays. [1] The subject of this discussion is the acoustic gas outside the event vision, not the black hole itself, nor the thinner corona above the plate.

How or how?

The suction disk is very far away from us, and telescopes are usually unable to distinguish their internal details. Researchers combine broadly relativistic magnetic fluid simulations with radiation transport calculations, creating synthetic X-ray spectrums, and comparing them with the observation features of the stellar mass black hole. [2] The simulation for 2013 uses a non-rotated black hole, with approximately 12 million degree Celsius and billions of degrees of corona on the plate as close to the centre. [1] These temperatures are calculated from model calculations and are not direct temperature readings of a black hole, so the conditions of the simulation must be specified.

Value and Measurement condition

This Article uses approximately 10⁷ K as the order of gasnide gas, corresponding to approximately K in the simulation, and retains only one signature figures. It is not a10⁹ order of magnitude, nor is it a Hawkimperature. A disk gas is not the whole motheral equilibrium: temperature varies with radius, altitude and absorption rate, and simulations also fix the premise that a black hole is not rotated and that the disc structure is specific. [1][2] This figure is therefore appropriate for comparison and is not suitable for precise distribution as a facet.

Common misunderstandings

First, use10⁷ K as a temperature of the black hole body; the visual world does not have a surface that can be measured directly as normal objects. Second, we mix the acoustic gas with the corona on the plate; hard X rays come mainly from the hotter corona. [1] Third, consider the simulation of magnitude as the measured peak of a black hole; it only indicates that this type of gas can reach millions of Kelvinorder of magnitude. Fourth, the "soft" of soft X-rays is mistaken for low or visible temperature; it remains high-energy radiation.

Relations with other temperate areas

10⁷ K is about half the core of the Sun K, well above the surface of the Sun 5,772 K and above the example of the neutron surface approximately K. It is lower than the core collapse of supernova10¹¹ K order of magnitude, and much lower than the excrete-gel plasma approximately K. When comparing, it is important to note that these values belong to the plate gas, the Star core, the collapse core and theoretical change, and are not the same object. [1][2]
