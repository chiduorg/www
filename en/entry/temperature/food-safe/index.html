What is it?

The food safety centre means temperature, which is reached within the thickest parts of the food, such as meat, poultry and eggs, and uses the probe to measure, rather than setting the oven or pot. The U.S. federal guide sets the minimum centre for poultry, leftovers and casseroles at 165 °F, or 73.9 °C. [1] 75 °C is also commonly used as a round threshold; 348 K, approximately 74.85 °C, is taken here.

How?

The tables for USDA FSIS give different minimum values by meat: steak and pork steak 145 °F (62.8 °C), meat by hanging 160 °F (71.1 °C), poultry and leftovers 165 °F (73.9 °C). [1] The FDA consumer guide also lists poultry 165 °F, egg dishes 160 °F and emphasizes that colour and texture cannot be a substitute for tymperature. [2] The probe shall be inserted into the thickest place, avoiding bones, fats and grills.

Value and Measurement condition

348 K equals approximately 74.85 °C and is about 1 K higher than the USDA poultry value of 73.9 °C. This difference, which is derived from the rounding and presentation habits of different countries, does not mean that there is an exact physical baseary of food safety thresholds. Security is also related to the time: once the temperature has been reached, there will be enough time to rest and rest in order to reach the centre and kill the pathogens. There are minimum values for different meats, shredding and vegetable thickness. [1][2]

First, the colour determines the level of cormorance; neither the meat nor the juice is reliable. Second, temperature of the Measurement boiler or oven air temperature; food centres must be measured. Third, it is considered that reaching 75 °C is permanently safe; it is influenced by heattime, initial bacteria and subsequent contamination. Fourth, the whole piece of meat is subject to the same threshold as the strangulation, which brings the surface microorganisms into the interior, usually with higher requirements. [1]
