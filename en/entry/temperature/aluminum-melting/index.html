Aluminium melting reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The condensation point for high-purity aluminium is a fixed point for the international operational thermostat ITS-90 with an assigned value of 933.473 K, approximately 660.323 °C. It is suitable for the description of pure metals, and is not a uniform melting of all aluminium alloys.

What is it?

Pure aluminium melts at 1 atm at 933.473 K and conversion at 660.323 °C. [1][2] This is a temperaturee that balances high-purity aluminium solids with liquids and is one of the fixed points of the international operational thermometer ITS-90; it describes pure element, not aluminum, aluminium pot or a single melting of a certain plate of aluminium alloy. [2][3] Industrial aluminium alloys tend to have solid phase lines, liquid phase lines and partial melting within the wider temperaturee zone, which cannot be summarized in a single point.

How?

ITS-90 aluminium fixes are achieved by a controlled condensation process: high-purity aluminium is fed into graphite, slowly cooled after full melting in an inert atmosphere and records its continuity on the condensed platform. The standard platinum-resistant temperaturee measure inserts a fixed-point container, which is then evenly read. [1][2] NIST's thermostat indicates that the calibration paper of Aluminium Condensation Point as 933.473 K, NIST gives the same value; the RSC's element table also lists 660.323 °C for daily reconciliation. [1 [2][3] The purity of the fixed point packaging, the platinum material and the temperaturee measurement position will be introduced into uncertainty.

Value and Measurement condition

933.473 K - 273.15 K = 660.323 °C, in turn 660.323 °C + 273.15 = 933.473 K. This conversion is precise to milligram, but does not mean that each aluminium sample has a melting point to 0.001 K. Temperature values are fixed to the reference value, with physical realization remaining uncertainty; impurities such as iron, silicon, etc., in commercial pure aluminium can cause a slight drop in the melting point, and alloys may cross dozens of K’s melting ranges. [2][3] When comparing materials, purity, state and pressure shall be recorded simultaneously.

Common misunderstandings

Aluminum can be used in the oven, which does not mean that it takes 660 °C to "start softening"; the mechanical strength of the thin beam, the oxidation membrane and the local heat flow are the key. The maximum use of the “melting point” as material is also low: under the “melting point”, the aluminium is clearly softened and creeping. Another misunderstanding is to mix the melting zone of aluminium alloys with the pure aluminium fixes; the solid line of different brands can vary considerably. Finally, the melting point is not a switch “to heat up to the moment when it becomes all liquid”, and melting requires continuous heating and crossing the zone of solids. [2] [3]

Relations with other temperate areas

933.473 K is about 10 K above magnesium 923 K, about 301 K below silver 1234.93 K and about 424 K below copper 1357.77 K. [1][3] It is much higher than the sodium 370.944 K, but less than the iron approximately 1811 K. Placing aluminium on this thermometer can be seen as the threshold between “light metal melting” and “high temperaturee alloy”: ordinary stoves, baking and hot water do not reach it, while metal smelting, welding and casting are carried out around it.
