Electric arc furnace tapping temperature reference × 10

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

18,931.5 K based on x10 conversion; used to connect the adjacent scale anchor, not the new independent measurement.

What's the value?

The value of this article is 18,931.5 K and is obtained by the base reading of "the arc furnace produces steel temperature (approximately 1620°C)" 1,893.15 K multiplied by 10. The baseline entry is a steel arc-fired process; conversion doesn't change the physical object, it just changes which class we put it on. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review: 1,893.15 K x 10 = 18,931.5 K. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect "the arc-fired steel (about 1620°C)" to the adjacententry: when the reader asks "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller?", the article can be applied directly without recalculating. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with the iron melting point (1811 K); Coring 7740 glass work point (1525.15 K) to see whether the unit is unified, then whether the comparator is in diameter, radius or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the different measurement condition. The baseline entry summy provides a readable starting point: the steel from the arc furnace is the target of the temperature when the steel drops into the pack, often about 1620°C, or about 1893 K. It is higher than the liquid phase of the steel and is well below the approximately 3,500°C of the arc inside the furnace; it is not written in this article as a uniform temperature for all steel species or all steel furnaces. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

Aluminium melting reference

The condensation point for high-purity aluminium is a fixed point for the international operational thermostat ITS-90 with an assigned value of 933.473 K, approximately 660.323 °C. It is suitable for the description of pure metals, and is not a uniform melting of all aluminium alloys.
