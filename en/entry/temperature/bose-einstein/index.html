Bose–Einstein condensation

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

In 1995, the thin lymphom gas experiment showed a botanical-Einstein condensation at about 170 nK. (b) Condensation conditions are related to atom type, density and accelerator parameters, which cannot be considered a fixed temperature common to all substances.

What is it?

Botanium – When Einstein condenses with thin botanical gases cooling below the threshold of temperature, a large number of atoms occupy the same minimum quantum state. In 1995, the JILA team achieved this state for the first time with a thatom gas. [2][3] It is not atom synthesis of a new element, but atom loses resolution in space, forming a macroquantitative body that can be described by a single wave function.

How or how it works?

The experiment first captured atom by laser cooling and magnetic traps, then by evaporated cooling to make the hottest atom escape. [2] When temperature fell to about 170 nK, there was a sharp peak of speed distribution separated from the heat background, marking the beginning of condensation; it continued to cool down to about 20 nK and to receive approximately 2,000 atoms of near pure condensation. [1] Here's the temperature, which is the thermodynamic temperature of the gas atom, which is to be combined with flighttime imaging and speed distribution.

Value and Measurement condition

170 nK equals1.7 × 10⁻⁷ K and 0.17 μK. The threshold of condensation is not a generic constant, but depends on the atom type, density and accelerator parameters; in the case of a gas or a change in density, the transformation of a temperature changes. [1][3] As a result, 170 nK can only be labelled as “the beginning of the experiment” and cannot be written as “the condensation point of all substances”.

Common misunderstandings

First, the word “superatom” is used as a literally new atom; condensate is still composed of many atoms, but in quantum. Second, to equate the BEC with the ultra-flow; the ultra-flow emphasizes the non-discretionary flow, and the BEC emphasizes the taking of quantum, which can be relevant but conceptually different. Third, use 170 nK as the bottom limit for temperature; follow-up experiments have cooled the gas to a lower level, and the transformation of the experiment is different.

BEC ' s critical temperature is not a fixed constant, but changes with the atom type, density and accelerator parameters; the higher the density, the higher the transition to temperature. [1] So 170 nK is just a reference of an experiment, not "the condensation of all bovine gases." If you change an atom or change the frequency, the value changes.

BEC is not synonymous with ultra-stream.

BEC emphasizes that a large number of atoms occupy the same quantum pattern; overflows emphasize non-depleted and dispersed flows. The ultra-flow transformation of helium involves strong interactions, and the BEC experiment is thin gas. [2][3] The two may be relevant but not the same concept. Directly referred to as “super-flow gases”, the BEC confuses quantum statistics with fluid behaviour.
