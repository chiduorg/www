Electric arc furnace tapping temperature reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The steel produced from the arc furnace is the target of the steel when it is poured into the pack and is commonly taken at about 1620°C, or about 1893 K. It is higher than the liquid phase of the steel and is well below the approximately 3,500°C of the arc inside the furnace; it is not written in this article as a uniform temperature for all steel species or all steel furnaces.

What is it?

Here is a discussion of the steel output of the arc furnace steel, i.e. the target when the steel fluid from the smelting tank is poured into the steel package. It's not a temperature of an arc in the oven, nor is it a melting point for steel. EAF, with scrap steel as its primary feedstock, uses graphite electrodes to produce arc melting furnaces, which then control the steel temperature by subsequent refining and cavity. [1][3]

How?

Temperature is usually used as a one-time thermomeasure or continuous thermometric probe in a steel package, and the operator sets a target based on steel species, steel package state and downstream waits fortime. The Pittini Group ' s process description gives a typical temperature of the steel phase of about 1,620 °C and indicates that the arc itself may exceed 3,500 °C. [1] EUROFER describes the EAF Melting Pool metal temperature as approximately 1,800 °C, with an arc up to 3,500 °C. [2] There is no contradiction between the two: one indicates the target of the steel, one says the high temperature end of the melting pond or the arc.

Value and Measurement condition

1,620 °C conversion is 1,893.15 K and displays about 1,893 K. [1] This figure is a steel target for particular plants and steel species and is not a uniform standard for all EAFs; steel species, alloy additions, steel pack preheating and even casting rhythms move the target up and down. [1][2] 3,500 °C arc temperature cannot be considered a steel fluid temperature because the arc is only a small volume inside the furnace, and the smelting pool is heated by currents and mixing. [2]

Common misunderstandings

First, the temperature of steel is used as a melting point for steel; the liquid line of steel is approximately 1,500 °C order of magnitude, and it requires overheating to ensure flow and refining. [1] Second, the arc temperature is used as a melting tank; 3,500 °C is a local arc temperature, with an average temperature being much lower. [2] Third, all steel plants are considered to be taking 1,620 °C; different steel species and processes will be adjusted to a wider range. Fourthly, steel is set as the temperature of the furnace; it is also the result of rhythms, durables and impurities control. [3]

Relations with other temperate areas

1,893 K above the pure iron melting point 1,811 K and above the silicon melting point 1,687 K and below the tungsten melting point 3,687 K. It is higher than 1,433 K of the KIlawe lava and lower than the 3,473 K of the oxyacetylene flame. Smelting steel is in the hot zone of “metallic overheating” and temperature is not so high as to melt tungsten, but sufficient to allow the liquid to flow and bet.

Temperature measurement and error

The output of steel is not an average of temperature in the furnace, but a target in the steel bag. One-time thermal dolls, continuous temperature detectors or infrared temperature measurements are commonly used in the field, and readings are influenced by the position of measurement, penetration depth, responsetime and steel sludge cover. [1] [3] 1,620 °C is the process, not the single furnace average; different steel species, alloy additions and steel package preheating state will allow the target to move up and down.

Why not the higher the better?

High steel output increases energy consumption, durable erosion and adhesion, and too low may condensate before a condo. Target temperature is a window that is sufficiently mobile and not hot, and not as high as possible. [1][3]

The use of "temperature 3,500 °C" as a steel fluid is a mixture of local heat sources with the average temperature of the melting pond. [1][2]

It's not hot.

The electric arc furnaces produce steel at about 1,620 °C, which refers to the temperature when the steel water leaves the furnace; the hot spot within the furnace, the furnace wall and the slag layer is likely to be higher and the steel pack will continue to warm or cool. Temperature measurements are based on thermal voltage, infrared thermometry and consumable probes, with different readings given at different locations. 1 620 °C of body text is the steel-measuring condition, not the average of the whole stove.
