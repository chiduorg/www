Dilution refrigerator reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Dilution chillers can support the mkelvinscale experiment. A scanning probe microscope facility, reported by NIST, works at about 10 mK; this specific device is used as an example of a non-declaration that all diluted chillers or all quantum units require the same temperaturee.

What is it?

Diluting chillers use helium-3 and helium-4 blends to be separated from the mkelvin atm for continuous heating. Helium-3 takes the heat from dilution to thick frame, keeping the mixing room cold. [1] It is a commonly used source of cold for quantum devices, ultraconductive circuits and low-temperaturee micro-tests, not as simple as putting samples directly into liquid helium. The cooling power of the mixed rooms is usually labelled as microwarder of magnitude under 100 mK, the lower the temperaturee is available. [2]

How or how it works?

NIST's scanning probe microscope facility uses custom dilution chillers to reach about 10 mK of base temperaturee at a maximum of 15 T magnetic fields, and is marked with electrical resistance temperaturee and experimental stability. [1] The 10 mK here is the base temperaturee of the mixing room or sample table; the actual sample will rise with heat load, wiring and radio frequency heating. Another set of NIST neutron-scattered bits of 60 mK is based on dilution plugins, indicating a wide variation between the devices. [2]

Value and Measurement condition

10 mK equals 0.01 K and 10,000 μK; it is the work of the NIST device under specific loads and is not the theoretical limit of diluting refrigeration technology. [1] For example, another diluted plugin for NIST neutron dispersion is 60 mK, several times different. [2] The cooling power and sample environment under 100 mK is also to be seen in comparison and cannot be copied only by one number.

Common misunderstandings

First, it is believed that diluting chillers can cool samples to absolute zero; it can only approach, and the lower the cooling power of temperaturee. Second, the cold-grade temperaturee is used as a unified temperaturee for the whole instrument; samples, wiring, windows and electrons are in different temperaturees. Third, read millikelvin as “heat is small”; thetemperaturee decides the actual heat stream with the heat guide. [1]
