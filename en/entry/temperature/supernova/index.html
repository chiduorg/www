Core-collapse supernova temperature scale

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Large massstar core collapses with about 100 billion degrees of heat order of magnitude. This compares to the collapse of the core, not to call it the unity of the entire supernova debris or visible light surface, nor to summarize the origin of all heavy element.

What is it?

The core collapse of the supernova took place after the large massstar exhausted its nuclear fuel. The iron nucle cannot continue to release energy through fusion, collapse quickly after loss of support, and then rebound and project material outside, leaving a neutron or black hole. The discussion here is about the core of the collapse, not the swelling gas cloud that we saw later, or the whole of the supernova. The core is compressed to a very high density, with neutrons taking large amounts of energy and the entire process away from the motheral equilibrium.

How or how?

Supernovas can be reached by optical telescopes, X-ray satellites and neutrino detectors, but the collapse core itself is masked by outer matter. The neutron storm of 1987A supernova provides direct evidence of a core collapse, and temperature relies on collapse model, physical equation and neutron transport calculations. [2] Chandra ' s data describe that the substance can rise to billions of degrees when it falls on Neutron, followed by an outbreak. [3] These figures come from a combination of model and indirect observation and are not direct temperature readings of the core. The energy mechanisms of different types of supernovas are different, and the thermonuclear explosion cannot be confused with the core collapse.

Value and Measurement condition

This article uses the order of magnitude coordinates of approximately 10¹¹ K, the core of the collapse, and retains only one signature figure. It is not the temperature of visible lightballs, which usually range from tens to tens of thousands of K; nor is it the flatness of the entire remnant. The core temperature changes with the collapse phase, radius and mass, and neutrinos-launching keeps the heat moving. It should be noted that Chandra and Britannica's public notes are overwritten by “billions of degrees”, corresponding to 10⁹-ZXXQPH2QZX K order of magnitude, while this article follows the10¹¹ K coordinates, which may be high. [1][3]

Common misunderstandings

First, use10¹¹ K as a temperature on the visible surface of supernova; we see bright light balls that are much colder. Secondly, all supernovas are treated as the same mechanism; the Ia type comes from a white dwarf thermonuclear explosion and the core collapse comes from a large massstar. [1] Thirdly, the total energy of the outbreak is directly conversation into temperature; the energy is distributed in heat waves, ejections, neutrons and radiation and cannot simply be summed up in a temperature. Fourthly, all of Element's history is attributed to supernovas; different elements and events require their own synthetic evidence. [2]

Relations with other temperate areas

10¹¹ K is much higher than the Sun core approximately ZXXQPH0QZX K, the neutron surface approximately ZXXQPH1QZX K and the white dwarf surface 25,000 K. It is lower than the quarker-Trust Plasma approximately 1.8×10¹² K, and is much lower than the EMP and Planperature. Compared to about 5,500 K of the thousands of new stars spectra, the difference is more than one order of magnitude because one is the collapse core and the other is the projectile light ball. When comparing these figures, the object and type of evidence are more important than the size itself. [1][3]
