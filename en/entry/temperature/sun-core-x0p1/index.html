Solar core temperature × 0.1

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

1.500×10^06K, based on the1.500×10^07K of the Sun Core, obtained 1.500×10^06 by ×0.1 conversion; used to connect adjacent scale anchors, not a new independent measration.

What's the value?

The value of this article is1.500×10^06K, obtained from the base reading of the "Sun core" 1.500×10^07 multiplied by 0.1. The baseline is the core of the sun temperature; conversion does not change the physical object, it changes the level on which we put it. [1]

How's that?

The calculation is subject to gradual review:1.500×10^07K x 0.1 =1.500×10^06K. If the base value is replaced by another unit and the same multiplier is applied, the result must return to the same order of mass; the unit, the multiplier and the signatureficant figures must be recorded together, and not leave only one number. [2]

Why keep this anchor?

This anchor is used to connect the "sun core" to the adjacent intestry: when the reader asks, "What does it feel like to be bigger/smaller than it?", it can be directly related to this article without having to be recalculated. It is suitable for the contrast position of the scale steps, combinations or object pages. [3]

With the neighbor scale.

Line this article with the Sun (1391.4 million m); the solar surface (5772 K) to see whether the unit is uniform, then whether the comparator is in diameter, radius or or orbital distance; the same number may be twice or more different under the same string. The baseline entry summary provides a readable starting point: the core of the Sun is about 15 million K, much higher than the visible surface of the Sun. Core fusion is turned into solar energy; this value describes the internal area and cannot be confused with the lightball's effectiveness temperature. [1][3]

How does Error pass it?

Error follows the baseline entry: the base value significant figures determine the results of the conversion; the multiplier is a precise integer or simple fraction and does not add to the measurementor. [1][2] If the baseline value itself is a approximation, the limitation of “approximate” should be retained thereafter.

♪ Bang, bang, bang ♪

This article is not a new observation or a substitute for baseline; it does not change ID, slug or canonical, but only adds a recalculated scale reference. Citiation should be accompanied by a reference intestry and multiplier to avoid misreading the conversion value as an independent measurement. [3]

The common misconception is to treat the results of the conversion as an independent measurement of “new discoveries”. It is actually an arithmetical projection of the base value; without a new observation, sample or model, error would not have been reduced by taking a pretty multiple. This article shall be accompanied by a reference, factor and unit. [2] [3]
