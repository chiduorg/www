Planck temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Planck temperature is a natural thermostat obtained from a combination of basic constants. CODATA gives a value of approximately 1.416784 × 10³² K; it is not the highest test test of universe, nor can it be directly considered an absolute maximum.

What is it?

Planck temperature is a natural thermometer obtained by a combination of Planck constants, light speeds, gravity constants and Bolzmann constants. It can be written in T P=(ħc5/G)/k B, order of magnitude about 1.4×10³² K. [1][2] This is not the temperature of a given object or the highest temperature that has been measured in UNiverse, but a unit of scale consisting of a basic constant. It appears in quantum gravitation discussions because the quantum effect of gravity is expected to become important in the vicinity of this indicator and the existing theory is not yet complete.

How or how?

Planck temperature is not the result of the event, but the theoretical value calculated by the constant table. NIST's 2022 CODATA page gives1.416784×10³² K, standard uncertainty is 0.000016×10³² K. [1] This uncertainty reflects only error of the basic constant and does not cover the uncertainty of the quantum gravity theory itself. Humans do not have, nor are they currently able, to produce, in the laboratory, a complete hot bath near Planmperature; whether or not early universe had reached this temperature level was also dependent on bulges, quantum gravity and models of the initial conditions of Big Bang. [2] It therefore belongs to the “mode/early universe inference” natural temperature criterion, rather than to the measurement of reality.

Value and Measurement condition

This article uses1.416784×10³² K as the theoretical value of Planperature, uncertainty approximately 1.6×10²⁷ K. It is the thermometer in the natural unit system, not the flattitude of unverse at any given time, or the highest temperature ceiling. Gravity constants and Planck constants in the context of the definition are pushed out to extremes here, while the quantum gravity theory has not yet been completed; different quantum gravity scenarios may give different descriptions of what happens near the Plankwin standard. [1][2] This value is therefore appropriate for comparison and theoretical rulers of magnitude and not for experimental records.

Common misunderstandings

First, use Planck temperature as the measured maximum of universe; it is a combination of constants, not tymperate readings. Secondly, it is considered to be the absolute cap of temperature; the available physics does not prove that temperature cannot exceed it, but simply that the existing theory lapses when it approaches it. Thirdly, we mix Planck temperature with Planktime and Plank length; they are different natural units. Fourth, consider1.4×10³² K as the real temperature of Big Bang's first moment; early universe's hot history and extrapolation depend on specific models. [2]

Planck temperature is not the "cemperature ceiling"

Existing physics does not function near Planck's marker, but this does not constitute an experimental theory of “temperature cannot be higher”; it simply indicates that known theories cannot be reliably described. [1][2] Using Planck temperature as the constant temperature cap of UNiverse would confuse the “theory sphere of application” with the “natural baseary”. It's more like the edge of a map than a wall.
