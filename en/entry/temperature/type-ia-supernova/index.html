Type Ia supernova temperature requires a model and region

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Thermal explosion studies for Ia supernovas link white dwarf structure, nuclear combustion and ejection. Three-dimensional simulations can track the evolution of the area along with the time, spectrophoto-spectroscopy model; the burning area temperature, the photoball radiation and the model threshold cannot be replaced.

What is it?

The Ia supernova is a thermonuclear explosion by a white dwarf that accumulates in double galaxy and approaches the limits of Chandraseka. The explosion almost completely disintegrates the white dwarf star, rapidly swells the projectile and sends strong light, and is therefore often used as a “standard candlelight” at a distance of unverse. [2] But “standard candlelight” refers to a light experience relationship and does not mean that every event has the same temperature. The subject of discussion here is the nuclear combustion zone, the ejection lightball and the hot zone within the white dwarf, which are not the same in the same outbreak.

How or how?

The remote Ia supernova cannot distinguish the internal structure, and the evidence is mainly derived from spectral, optic curve and element abundance in the projectile. The researchers used a 3D fluid to simulate the recurrence of the explosion-to-explosion process, then calculate the nucleo-synthesis product and compare it with the observation. The 14 group models of Setenzahl et al. changed the ignition structure and initial central density, obtained 56 Ni of the solar mass by 0.32-1.11 and showed the presence of the Iron Nation element in the medium speed crust. [1] These results constrain the history of temperature, density and reaction, but the temperature itself is still from model and is not a direct burning front reading. [3]

Value and Measurement condition

There is no selection of models, regions and moments, so there is no single temperature. The bursting zone inside the white dwarf can be hot, and the ejection lightballs can cool quickly; there is no uniformity of the same event at different times and in different radiuss. The initial central density in model, the number of ignition cores, the probability of drifting and the nuclear response network all change the outcome. Treats a reaction threshold, a light ball temperature or a group of simulated peaks directly as "Ia-type temperature" and removes objects from the list. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

First, the reaction threshold in model is used as the event's measured peak temperature; the threshold indicates whether the combustion can take place and does not equal the real temperature at the time of the outbreak. Second, the colour of the light ball is considered to be internal combustion; the light ball represents only radiation near the optical thickness. Thirdly, all Ia models are treated as the same aforestar and the same light; the Ia model itself still has subtypes that are dispersed from opservation. Fourth, mix the Ia model with the core collapse supernova; the former is a white dwarf thermonuclear explosion and the latter is a large massstar core collapse. [3]

Relations with other temperate areas

The Ia type is linked to white dwarf surfaces, carbon fusion and core collapse supernovas. The white dwarf surface is capable of only about 25,000 K, and the internal combustion zone is much higher; the core collapse of supernovas can reach 10¹¹ K order of magnitude, but it is another energy mechanism. When comparing values at different levels, it is important to ascertain whether the lightball of the Ia type refers to the front line of the burning, the lightball or the whole projectile. [1][3]
