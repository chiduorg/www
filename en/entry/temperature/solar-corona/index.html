Solar corona temperature scale

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The corona is the outermost atmosphere of the sun, and it can reach millions of Kelvinorder of magnitude. NASA's Sun Overview describes its high temperature scale with about 2 million degree Celsius; Bentry takes about 2 × 10⁶ K as a reference and states that it is “attractive/typical” order of magnitude, not the same mean temperature for every corona.

What is it?

The corona is the outermost atmosphere of the Sun, extending from the colour ball and the transition zone, and the thin plasma can be connected to the solar wind source area. [2] The term temperature here is electron temperature and spectroscopy temperature, which is not an effective temperature of the light ball; the coronal has no solid surface and no “temperature to this end”. [1][3] Visible light of the coronal is weak so that it can be seen directly at all times during the eclipse, normally by a coronation or space telescope.

How?

The coronal temperature is mainly extrapolated from ultraviolet and X-ray spectral lines, coronal coronation and white-light coronation observation; the strength of the different ion spectra is more binding than the density of electron temperature. [3] NASA's Sun Overview has written the coronal hot scale to about 2 million °C and layers to about 500,000 K to millions of K. [1][2] These are the different order of magnitude statements, not the same temperature.

Value and Measurement condition

Here about 2 × 10⁶ K is used as a reference, °C and K for a deviation of 273.15 at almost the same order of magnitude, so the “2 million degrees” in the overview can be directly understood as about 2 × 10⁶ K. [1] The coronal is an optical thin, non-othermal equilibrium plasma, and the temperature given by spectral diagnosis may vary according to the ion, the direction of the vision and the model;2 × 10⁶ K cannot be read to mean temperature in the whole layer. [2][3] The formation of different ions is different from the formation of temperature, where the spectra gives an electron temperature range rather than a single temperature reading.

Common misunderstandings

First, the corona is considered to be colder out there; the actual corona is much hotter than the light ball, and the temperature does not drop alone when it extends out. Secondly, the corona temperature as a light ball; the difference is hundreds of times. [1] Third, an interpretation of the coronal heating with a temperature; magnetic reconnection, Alvinbo and wave-diffusion may be mechanisms and are not fully conclusive. [3]

Relations with other temperate areas

2 × 10⁶ K above the colour ball of about 4,000-8,000 K and the upper end of the transitional zone are also well above about 5,800 K on the surface of the Sun; it is lower than the core of the Sun, about ZXXQPH1QZXK, but directly related to the puzzle of coronal heating. In contrast to the Earth's environment, it is several times higher than any industrial flame, and therefore cannot be understood by the daily “hot” instinct. [1][2]
