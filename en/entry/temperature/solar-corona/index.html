What is it?

The corona is the outermost atmosphere of the Sun, extending from the colour ball and the transition zone, and the thin plasma can be connected to the solar wind source area. [2] The term temperaturee here is electrontemperaturee and spectroscopy temperaturee, which is not an effective temperaturee of the light ball; the coronal has no solid surface and no “temperaturee to this end”. [1][3] Visible light of the coronal is weak so that it can be seen directly at all times during the eclipse, normally by a coronation or space telescope.

How?

The coronal temperaturee is mainly extrapolated from ultraviolet and X-ray spectral lines, coronal coronation and white-light coronation observation; the strength of the different ion spectra is more binding than the density of electrontemperaturee. [3] NASA's Sun Overview has written the coronal hot scale to about 2 million °C and layers to about 500,000 K to millions of K. [1][2] These are the different order of magnitude statements, not the same temperaturee.

Value and Measurement condition

Here about 2 × 10⁶ K is used as a reference, °C and K for a deviation of 273.15 at almost the same order of magnitude, so the “2 million degrees” in the overview can be directly understood as about 2 × 10⁶ K. [1] The coronal is an optical thin, non-othermal equilibrium plasma, and the temperaturee given by spectral diagnosis may vary according to the ion, the direction of the vision and the model;2 × 10⁶ K cannot be read to mean temperaturee in the whole layer. [2][3] The formation of different ions is different from the formation of temperaturee, where the spectra gives an electrontemperaturee range rather than a single temperaturee reading.

Common misunderstandings

First, the corona is considered to be colder out there; the actual corona is much hotter than the light ball, and the temperaturee does not drop alone when it extends out. Secondly, the corona temperaturee as a light ball; the difference is hundreds of times.
