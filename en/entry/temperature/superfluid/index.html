Helium-4 superfluid transition reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Helium-4 has an overflow shift near saturated vapour pressure of about 2.17 K. This reference cannot be used directly for helium-3, nor does it mean that helium under any pressure will happen by the same temperature; it's the temperature of the othermal equilibrium.

What is it?

The liquid helium-4 enters the ultra-current phase below approximately 2.17 K, showing a non-glazing flow, reptile and second-sound equivalent quantum phenomenon. [1][2] This shift is called the twilight shift, because the twilight of a bit of a bit of a shift point. Superstreaming is not another element, but the result of the same heliumtom entering macroquantum. Helium-4 atom is a botanical substance, with macro-sized atoms occupying the same quantum when cooled; but the strong interaction in the liquid makes this transformation different from that of the thin gas-Einstein condensed. [2]

How or how it works?

NIST's cryogenic data describe liquid helium at 2.17 K and below as ultra-flow and indicate that it can climb along the wall of the container and drop from the bottom. [1] Conversions are made on an experimental basis by means of more than the peak of physical capacity, second-sound transmission and flow-measurement, which gives a equilibria conversion temperature, not a “zero viscos” reading under any mobility conditions. [1][2] The transformation of temperature also changes slightly with pressure.

Value and Measurement condition

2.17 K equals −270.98 °C, which is usually referred to as λ dots near saturated vapour pressure, which, at higher pressure, drops slightly. [1] The liquid helium boiling point at 1 atm is 4.2 K, and the gill point is about 2 K below the boiling point, so the ultra-current phase appears in the liquid helium bath. [1] It must therefore be subject to the condition of “lique helium-4, near low pressure”. 2.17 K and Helium-3, 2.444 mK are close to three order of magnitude and cannot be interchangeable.

Common misunderstandings

First, the visceral viscosity of the superfluent is considered to be strictly zero; the hyperflux fraction coexists with the normal composition and the overall flow remains affected by the vortex and roundary. Second, the super-flow transformation of liquid helium-4 is considered as a botanical-Einstein synonym for condensing; the two are deeply linked, but the conversion is a multi-body conversion of interactions in liquids, not a BEC of thin gases. Third, it is considered that 2.17 K applies to helium-3; helium-3 is a pheamite, which needs to be paired first.

Relations with other temperate areas

2.17 K is below the boiling point of liquid helium under 1 atm 4.2 K, 2.444 mK above helium-3 ultra-stream A point, and much above the botanical condensation of 170 nK. It is located in a hot zone between diluted cooling and liquid helium baths and is a classic entry point for the study of quantum fluids.
