Oven set air temperaturee

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The usual baker for domestic ovens is set at 180 °C (350 °F), i.e. the round target value of 453 K. It refers to the internal air setting temperaturee, not the food centre temperaturee, or the measured temperaturee at every point in the oven.

What is it?

The oven sets the temperaturee as the target air that the user sets when choosing the baking or baking mode. 180 °C (350 °F) is one of the most common `medium' settings in the recipe; NIST classifies 180-190 °C (350 – 375 °F) as moderate and lists the commonly used 180 °C for cakes, cookies, banana bread, etc. [1] This article takes 180 °C (453 K). It describes the target of internal air control, not the food centre temperaturee.

How?

The oven temperaturee controllers break the heating element according to the temperaturee sensor in the cavity, but show that temperaturee is a fixed value or control target and is not equal to the measured temperaturee at each point in the cavity. NIST's cooking table is similar to the conversion, with clear indications that the tromperature may change and should follow the recipe. [1] The USDA study in the truffle oven of 176.7 °C (350 °F) indicates that 350 °F is a common actual cooking setting. [2] The actual air is checked in an oven temperaturee and the probe temperaturee is used in food centres.

Value and Measurement condition

K equals 17985 °C, about 180 °C. Sets that 180 °C is not equal to 180 °C in a cavity instant: preheating requires times, the opening of the door falls in the instant, and the position of the shelf and the hot wind cycle creates hot and cold spots. [1] There is another amount of temperaturee inside the food, which must be met by a probe, and the oven air is not considered a temperaturee. 180 °C in the baking recipe is usually the air temperaturee, not the dish surface temperaturee or the oven shell temperaturee.

Common misunderstandings

First, settemperaturee as a measurement test; the old ovens may deviate by a dozen degree Celsius. Second, the oven is treated as inside the food; the two objectives are completely different. Third, it is considered that all recipes have an exact equivalent of 180 °C; different ovens, modes and loads change the result. Fourth, the Fahrenheit 350 °F and 180 °C are treated as strictly equivalent; conversion is approximately 176.7 °C, and the recipe is usually rounded to 180 °C.

Relationship to other temperaturee

180 °C is higher than 100 °C of boiling-water and more than 80 °C of sauna, but less than the lava-temperaturee and metal-furnace-type industrial hot zone. It is higher than the 4 °C of Fridge and the −18 °C of the freezer, indicating the presence of a wide-temperaturee zone from frozen to baked in the same kitchen. When you add oven's set value to the surface temperaturee of desert-ground, remember that one is an equipment air target and one is a natural surface test.
