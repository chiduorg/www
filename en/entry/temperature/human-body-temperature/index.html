Human body temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Approximately 37 °C (310.15 K) is a commonly used body temperature reference value rather than a precise temperature that every person has every hour. Temperature readings vary according to factors such as the individual, time period and measurement.

37°C is reference, not the same constant.

The daily body temperature is about 37°C, which is a friendly reference value. MedlinePlus clearly lists the changes caused by the individual, age, time period and mode of delivery; a person does not read the number of times and does not have to always equal this integer. The human body does not have a single, unchanging temperature label. [1]

Where are the instruments?

The readings of the mouth, armpit, ear and forehead are different. Skin temperature is also not directly considered to be internal to the body. When comparing the two sets of data, in addition to numbers, the measurement parts, instruments and the conditions for obtaining readings are considered; source also cautions that an absolute fixed conversion difference cannot be applied mechanically between different methods. [1]

How to handle changes in large samples

Ley et al. analysed the adult outpatient records of the same medical system in 2023 and created the oral temperatemode by algorithms sifting some records related to the abnormal distribution of the temperature. The average of the study analysis was about 36.64°C, showing that the “common values” depended on the sample and the definition, rather than a new constant for all. [3]

What can Kelvin tell us?

37°C conversion is 310.15 K, which changes only the expression of the thermometer and does not suddenly make the pre-like physical reference precise. If reference is made to room at 20°C, the temperature difference is 17 K; the result cannot be explained as a multiple of the thermal or physiological state by direct partitioning to a degree Celsius value. [2]

Statistical averages and individual readings actually answer two questions: the former summarises the selected population, while the latter documents a person ' s state under certain conditions. To connect the two, there is a need to study the design and measuremeasurement condition, and not just to see a few decimals at 37°C. [1][3]

Bookary

This page continues to use approximately 37°C as the daily scale coordinates and does not judge individuals by group averages. The study is limited to data from specific adult outpatient clinics and cannot automatically be extrapolated to all ages and situations; the human coloring is also an explanatory map, not the thermal imaging or diagnostic results of an individual. [1][3]

The error book of this value.

The core of the human body is approximately 37°C, but the normal range changes with the measurement, time and the individual. Americ temperature is usually lower than the core, and the temperature is influenced by the environment and equipment; rectal, cavity and bladder are closer to the core. 37 °C is not a “fever line” and the heat threshold is usually 38 °C or higher, depending on the guide and the population.

Another scale to look at it.

37 °C equals 310.15 K, approximately 1.06 times the room temperature of 293 K; it is insignificant compared to the solar surface of 5772 K. The human body maintains enzymes and cell functions by a narrow window of approximately 37 °C, with a 3-4 °C rise in core typerature that could endanger life. This scale explains that “temperature is not high” does not mean “no impact”.

The core is not the same as the surface.

Temperature, which is commonly rectal, edible or bladder probed, is the heart, brain, and is indirectly estimated. [1] Exterior vascular constriction, sweating and environmental temperature can divert the physiographic reading from the core temperature. Using argon temperature of 36.5 °C as the core temperature may underestimate or overestimate the real state.

Why not an exact constant at 37 °C?

The average oral temperature given in the large sample study is about 36.6 °C, and the individual, age, time period and measurement mode changes the reading. [3] 37 °C is a historical reference value that facilitates communication and is not a target that everyone must hit. The “normal range” of body temperature is more meaningful than an integer.

Five-axis Contrast

At the temperature axis, the core is about 37 °C; at the space axis, the human body scale is about 1.7 m; at the mass axis, adults are about 70 kg; at the time axis, body temperature fluctuates with the day and night rhythm. Body temperature is a physical state, not an environment. [1][3]
