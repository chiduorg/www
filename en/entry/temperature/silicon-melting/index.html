Silicon melting reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The table of pure silicon melting points is approximately 1,687 K, or 1,414 °C; Britannica gives 1,410 °C. This reference belongs to the element silica, and is not a temperature common to silicon dioxide, quartz or all silicon-based materials.

What is it?

This is a discussion of the normal pressure melting point for pure eliment silicon, not the melting of silicon dioxide, quartz or silicate glass. Silicon is the basic material of the semiconductor industry, pure silicon is blue grey solid; it melts around 1,414 °C. [1][2] Silicon has a crystal structure of a diamond-type cube crystal that retains the short-range association of covalent keys when melted, so that the melting point is higher than aluminium and lower than iron. Silicon is a different substance from silica (SiO2) and the word "silica" is used in Chinese, but the chemical composition is different from the case changeemperature. [3]

How?

The melting point of element is determined by methods such as thermal analysis, radiation temperature and electrical resistance, and is included in the standard element table. RSC's Silicon Element table lists 1,414 °C, 1,687 K; Britannica's Silicontry gives 1,410 °C (2,570 °F), with a difference of approximately 4 °C. [1][2] This difference is derived from the circle of watch values, sample purity and measration methods and should not be construed as the presence of different temperatures on the same day. High-purity samples are mostly used in inert atmospheres or vacuums to prevent the oxidation layer from affecting heat-sorting peaks.

Value and Measurement condition

1,687 K conversion is 1,413.85 °C, which corresponds to a circle of 1,414 °C; 1,410 °C is approximately 1,683 K. [1][2] Table values correspond to pure silicon, constant pressure and no conditions for significant mixing. Semiconductor-grade silicon has very low mixing concentrations, and changes in melting points are usually less than differences in apparent values; however, alloys, silicones and silicon dioxide cannot be applied to this number. [3] The crystal grower will be set at a level higher than the melting point to maintain the flow of the melting and the balance of the temperature. Common misunderstandings

First, the Silicon Melting Point is mixed with the softened temperature of quartz or glass; the case change is different from viscosity. [3] Second, treat 1,687 K as a melting point for all silicon materials; silicate alloys, silicates and higher mixing concentrations of samples are subject to deviation. Third, 1,414 °C is considered to be a "semiconductor process" temperature; diffusion in chip manufacture, defunct temperature is well below the silicon melting point. Fourth, mix the degree Celsius with Kelvin: 1,414 °C is 1,687 K, not 1,414 K.

Relations with other temperate areas

The 1,687 K is above the aluminium melting point 933.15 K, the copper melting point 1,357.8 K and the Corning 7740 glass work point is approximately 1,525 K, but below the iron melting point 1,811 K and the tungsten melting point 3,687 K. It belongs to a medium-high-temperature zone in silicon crystal growth and metallurgical, close to about 1,893 K from steel.
