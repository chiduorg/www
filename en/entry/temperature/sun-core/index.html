The core of the Sun is approximately 15 million K, much higher than the visible surface of the Sun. Core fusion is turned into solar energy; this value describes the internal area and cannot be confused with the lightball's effectiveness temperature.

Light and heat get energy from deep.

The core of the Sun is the hottest area in the world, and the page is marked by approximately 15 million K of temperature of magnitude. The hydrogen nuclear reaction in the core eventually produces helium, which provides a sustained energy supply to the Sun. This is the nuclear process of the substance, not the use of the sun as a fireball for burning wood; nor can visible light balls represent the physical conditions of the core. [1]

Neutron provides another observation path

The core nuclear reaction also produces neutrinos. The Borexino team analysed the different components of the proton-proton reaction chain through the dispersion of neutrons and Electron and compared the results with the solar model.
