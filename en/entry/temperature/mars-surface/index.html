Temperature / Low temperature

Mars mean surface temperature reference

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The information on the Earth's near-Earth surface average is approximately 210 K (-63 °C) and the NASA information table gives the average atmospheric average about 214 K (-59 °C). 208.15 K (-65 °C) on the temperature axis is the round value of the overview information; it is neither measured at a particular landing point nor measured by day or by season.

What is it?

The average temperature of the surface of Mars depicts the average state of the global near-Earth, day and night and season rather than a single detection reading. The atmosphere of Mars is thin, with a surface pressure of about 6.36 mb, with a large temperature difference between day and night; the same location can differ by dozens of Kelvins between day and night. [1] Therefore, “Mars temperature” must indicate whether it is global average, typical, highest or lowest.

How?

Mars temperature comes from infrared remote sensing of orbiters, direct temperature measurements of landers and cruisers, and atmospheric and surface thermal models. Mars Fact Sheet of NASA NISDCA gives an average atmospheric average of about 214 K (-59 °C) and lists the day and night range of the Pirate 1 landing site 184-242 K; the Martianentry of Britannica gives an average surface of about 210 K (-63 °C) and describes the characteristics of the lower atmosphere about 200 K. [1][2] These values are derived from different time horizons and weights and cannot be considered as the same.

Value and Measurement condition

208.15 K, or -65 °C, on the temperature axis differs from the average of 210 K surface of Britannica and 214 K atmospheric average of NASA by approximately 2-6 K. [1][2] This difference is mainly due to the average time, altitude selection of the surface and atmosphere, day and night weights and rounding, rather than the short-term change in the number of Kelvins in Mars. 208.15 K is used for comparison only in temperate zones and is not used as a precision measure of global average temperature. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

First, the global average is considered to be the same for each location; the variation in data from Mars by day and by season is much greater than the average. [1] Second, use 250 K in the day or 290 K in the summer peak as a constant average; Britannica clearly distinguishes the average surface from the peak of the summer day. [2] Third, the existence of liquid water is judged separately by mean temperature; liquid water stability also depends on pressure, salinity and persistence oftime. [1][2] Fourthly, the top of the atmosphere is confused with the surface.

Relations with other temperate areas

The 278.15 K ice point below water is also below the typical Earth surface temperature, but above the 24-55 K surface temperature zone of Pluto. [1] [2] In comparison with the temperature zone of the surface of planets in the solar system, Mars is on the cold side; on the same Mars, the low night value of 184 K and the high day value of 242 K are distributed around averages and cannot be compared with only one end point. [1]

The othermodynamicsteperature unit of this article is based on BIPM's definition for Kelvin. [3]
