Mass-energy equivalence: what E=mc² actually says

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

E=mc² is not “mass can turn into energy at will”, but rest mass is a different expression of the same physical amount of energy; mass losses in a nuclear reaction can release huge energy.

Meaning of formula

E=mc² indicates that the rest mass of the object is proportional to the static energy to which it corresponds, and the ratio constant is the light speed square. [1] 1 kg rest mass corresponds to approximately 8.99×10¹⁶ J, equivalent to about 21 megatons of TNT; this is not to say that 1 kg of substances will explode at any time, but that its energy books are large.

Mass losses

The combination of the atom nuclear can be derived from the mass loss: when the atom nuclear is formed, the total is reduced and the balance is released by energy. Both nuclear fission and fusion transform part of the rest mass into kinetic energy and radiation. [2] The sun turns about 4 million tons of rest mass into energy per second, which is the mechanism.

Not Any Conversion

Mass-energy equivalence does not mean that any object can easily become pure energy. conservation laws, reaction cross-sections and interaction conditions limit conversion; mass changes in daily chemical reactions are minimal, about 10⁻⁹ order of magnitude. [1] [3] Only processes such as nuclear reactions and particle extinction can transform significant rest mass into other forms.

Relationship to integration

The greater the combination, the more stable the system, the more rest mass losses. The highest degree of nucleus can be found near Iron Peak, so that fusion to iron does not release any more energy; more heavy element releases energy through fission or neutron capture. Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “E=mc² only applies to nuclear bombs”. It applies to all systems with rest mass, except that the daily reaction is too small. [3] Another misconception is that the term “mass into energy” is "mass away; total energy is constant, rest mass is just another form of energy and momentum.

The books of the sun.

The sun has concentrated about 600 million tons of hydrogen per second into helium, mass losses about 4 million tons, releasing energy by E=mc². [1] This figure determines the solar lifetime with Earth climate.

Convergence Curve

When the nuclear is combined into atom core, mass loses and the combined curve reaches its peak near iron; both light fusion and heavy fission release energy to peak movements. [2] So it's hard to fusion near iron and it's hard to crack.

E=mc² baseary

The formula applies to static objects and total energy in inertia; energy is more complex in a broad relativity and quantum field theory. [3] It's not a permission to "any mass can change energy instantaneously."

The account book for the nuclear power plant.

About 0.1 per cent of mass was converted to energy at the time of the uranium-235 fission; a 1 GW nuclear power plant consumes about a few kilograms of fuel per day and produces large quantities of electricity. [1] This is completely different from the loss ratio of chemical combustion.

Sun and Earth

The Sun loses about 4 million tons of mass per second, and the Earth receives about 10¹⁷ J energy per year; by E=mc², this energy corresponds to about 1 kg of mass. [2] Sun books support the Earth's ecolory.

Mass lost scale

The mass losses of the nuclear reaction were about 1 to 1 per cent of the total mass; the chemical combustion was about10⁻⁹. NIST CODATA's constant and standard model information for CERN gives precise energy-mass-energy conversion. [4] [5]

About 4 million tons of mass losses per second, only 2×10⁻²¹ order of magnitude, are enough to sustain billions of years. Mass losses are not “disappearing of matter”, but rather the transformation of rest mass into radiation and kinetic energy.
