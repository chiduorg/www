Heat and temperature: why 100 °C air and 100 °C water are not the same

Temperature is the mean kinetic energy strength of the molecule, and heat is the process amount of the energy transferred by temperature differentials. Temperature is not the same as heat, but it depends on mass, more than heat capacity and phasy change; when combined, burns, heat island and body temperature are misread.

Temperature is the strength.

Temperature describes the mean kinetic energy of the molecule in the substance, which is the strength: it does not change linearly with the number of samples. When a glass of water and a bucket of water reached their motherl equilibrium in the same room, the temperature was the same, although a bucket of water contained more internal energy. [1][3]

Temperature is not “heat much”. One object can be very high but is small and has little internal power; the other object is low but large and can always be larger. temperature gives the average state, and heat gives the process of energy transfer. [3] [4]

Heat is process amount.

Heat appears only when temperature differentials exist, during energy transfer. To say “how much of this object contains” is not rigid in the context of thermodynamics; more to say “how much of its inner power” is “how much of it it absorbs or releases”. Heat is a path, not a state. [3] [4]

There are three ways in which head transport can be transmitted, transient and radiation. Metallic spoons heat in hot water are conductors; the air cycle in the room is current; the sun sends energy to Earth is radiation. All three approaches can change inner energy, but different mechanisms cannot be summed up by a single “hot”. [3]

The need to raise the same mass water and air to the same temperature varies widely. Water is about 4.18 kJ/(kg open) and air is about 1.0 kJ/(kg open) under constant pressure. Water is much more dense than air, so the same volume can store more heat. [4]

Same 100°C. Why is the burns different?

The water at 100 °C is the same as the vapour at 100 °C but releases different energy when exposed to skin. Steam coagulates on the skin surface, releases carving latent heat and continues to cool; water is high and dense, and shorttimetime exposure can pass a large amount of heat to the skin. Both avoid direct contact and cannot be judged by “temperature”. [3] [4]

The air is another situation. Air at 100 °C is low and dense, and shorttime exposure may not be as severe as hot water burns, but longtime exposure may cause harm. The heat risk is determined by the medium, exposure time, heat transfer coefficient and dermal state, rather than only by tymperature readings. [4] [6]

Urban head island is a typical “same day, different temperature”. Concrete and asphalt absorb solar radiation during the day and slowly heat at night; construction is dense, green is reduced and air-conditioning is deheated, all of which will lead to higher temperatures in neighbourhoods than in the surrounding suburbs. According to EPA, urban head island can cause temperature differentials in the blocks of digital Celsius, which are even greater in extreme cases. [5]

However, “heat island” is not equivalent to a flat warming of the whole city. The shades, the water, the ventilator and the direction of the building may cause the scale to differ. Treating city average temperature as a temperature for every block will mask the population and location most in need of heat protection. [5]

Human temperature

The core of the human body is approximately 37°C, but the normal range changes with the measurement, time and the individual. Americ temperature is usually lower than the core, and the temperature is influenced by the environment and equipment; rectal, cavity and bladder are closer to the core. 37 °C is not a “fever line” and the heat threshold is usually 38 °C or higher, depending on the guide and the population. [6]

The human body maintains enzymes and cell functions through a narrow window of approximately 37 °C. A 3-4 °C rise in the core is likely to endanger life, while the environment can be much higher or lower than body temperature. Body temperature is the result of physiological regulation and is not a direct reproduction of the environment. [6]

Temperature is just an input into the hot environment. [4] [6]

Common misunderstandings

The first misunderstanding is that “temperature is the same, heat is the same”. The internal energy of the thermogenic object also depends on the behaviour of mass, more than nature. The second misunderstanding is that “heat is the object's possession”. Heat is process amount, internal energy is status. [3] [4]

The third misconception is that “100 °C must be more dangerous than 50 °C”. The hazard depends on the medium, exposure and heat transfer efficiency. The fourth misconception is that “urban head island equals global warming”. Heat island is a land-use effect, and global warming is a long-term change in radiation coercion and climate systems, unlike scale. [4] [5]

Reading

When you see temperature, ask what type of temperature it measures: temperature, water temperature, core temperature, surface temperature, or other modynamicsteperature. Seeing heat, ask if it's heat, internal energy, heat flow or heat power. Numbers only make sense when the unit is clear. [1][6]

The relationship between heat and temperature can be remembered in one sentence: the average of temperature depicts the state, the shift of the process. Put that in front, burns, heat island, body temperature and engine cooling are no longer isolated figures. [3][5]
