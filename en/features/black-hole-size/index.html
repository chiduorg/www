The size and mass of a black hole: the event horizon is not a hole

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The black hole has no solid surface. The event vision, the photoring, the shadow and the suction disk are four different “sizes”; the photograph is a shadow, orbital measurement is a mass, and the two cannot be confused.

Four Sizes

Tell me how big the black hole is. The innermost layer is** incident visual**: the light cannot escape one-way baseary. Right on the non-rotation Swahili black hole, radius. [ Chuckles ] I'm sorry.
Ten times the black hole of solar mass, approximately 29.5 km radius of event horizon. [2]

The second layer is** photon rings**: the critical structure of light around the black hole. The third layer is ** shadow**: remote observation sees a combination of the central dark zone and the surrounding lightring, which is about several times larger than the view. The fourth layer is ** Accumulation disk**: luminous gases that fall into material formation in a much greater range than in view; accumulators can reach millions of Kelvin, in units of SI Kelvindefension. [1][6]

What are the pictures?

In 2019, the Event Visual Telescope released an image of M87*: a bright ring surrounded a dark zone. It's not a picture of the “black hole body”, but a picture of the shadow of the black hole and the radiation around it. [7] Mass of M87* is approximately1.29×10⁴⁰ kg, approximately 6.5 billion times the solar mass; according to the Swassi formula, the boundary radius is approximately1.9×10¹⁰ km, close to 130 AU. [5] The distance of M87* is about 16.8 megaparsec and the distance is itself involved in the error budget. [3]

In 2022, EHT released an image of the Silver Mansion A*. Its mass is about 0QX kg, about 4.3 million times the solar mass, and its visual radius is about1.3×10⁷ km. [5][7] EHT ' s long-term multi-band observation and image reconstruction to reduce single-time measurement deviations. [4][8] The shadow angles of the two black holes are similar in diameter, but the physical size is three or four times of magnitude because the distance is about 2,000 times different.

What do you call it, Mass?

For the center of the galaxy, the hardest method is star orbit: follow the Star around Manama A* motion, using the Kepler Law Retrocent Centre mass. [5] It also gives the black hole position and the most direct constraint on mass.

For M87*, methods include gas kinetics, star kinetics and black hole shadow sizes. The size of the shadow is associated with mass, distance, inclination; the more uncertain the distance, the more uncertain the mass is. [7] So, “6.5 billion times solar mass” is not a single weighing, but a combination of observation and model.

The view is not a hole.

The metaphor "black hole is a hole" is misleading. The vision is not a hole in space, but a bundary structure of space and space; after it is crossed, all future cones point to the interior. [2] For remote observations, the clock near the horizon appears to be getting slower; for free fallers, there is no sense of “lamming walls” through the horizon itself (tide forces say otherwise).

The black hole is not "bring in and disappear forever." Mass, angular momentum, charge will remain in an externally measurable gravitational field; where the information goes is another argument, not a “sight-size” answer. [2]

First, whether the mass unit is solar mass or kg; secondly, whether it is visual, shadow or suction; thirdly, whether the distance and inclinations are entering the Uror; and fourthly, whether the photograph is a direct distinction between the shadow or the photoring. [1][7]

To separate them, it is possible to understand why the same M87* can be said as “6.5 billion times solar mass”, or as “shadows about 42 micro-angle seconds”, or as “views about 130 AU”. They are not contradictory, but the same object is of different dimensions. [5] [7]

Tidal Power and Size

The “danger radius” of a black hole is not determined by the public sphere alone. Tide forces change rapidly with distance: a Star class black hole can tear near Star to pieces outside the sight and form a tidal break-up event; the supermass black hole has a large view, but the tidal power in the view is milder. [2][5]

And that's why falling into a black hole is a completely different experience under a different mass. For star class black holes, tidal forces may be pulled out before they reach the horizon; for supermass black holes, free fallers may not immediately feel abnormal when they cross the horizon, but there is no return thereafter. [2]

So, "The size of the black hole" adds: "The geometric dimension, the gravitational impact range, or the tidal damage range." Three answers can be much worse. [1][5]

Which piece is it?

EHT gives direct restraint to the shadow scale, but it relies on thin interference and image reconstruction; different reconstruction methods give slightly different rings. Mass with the distancer, both into the shadow-size interpretation. [7]

The star orbit gives a high accuracy, but only for the immediate neighbourhood, of the silver-heart black hole; the galaxyscale black hole, which relies more on gas motion and light and the system is larger. [5]

The most reliable set of cross-checks is the combination of “directly seen shadow” and “orbitally called mass”. In one way alone, it is easy to treat the model hypothesis as the result of the operation. [1][7]

Space / Stars & interstellar

Stellar-Mass Black Hole Event Horizon

The event radius of the star class black hole is given by the Swazi radius, which is positive for mass; 10 times the solar mass corresponds to approximately 2.95×10⁴ metres, or about 30 kilometres. This number is a radius, not a diameter.
