Why civilization timelines are Eurocentric

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Ages, medievals, modernity and modernity were first used by European historians to organize European experience. It is easy to crush other civilizations as “margins” or “laters” when textbooks and global history follow.

It's not natural.

The “Culture — Medieval — Recent Generations” splits into European events such as Renaissance, Religious Reform, Enlightenment Movement, Industrial Revolution. It cuts Europe ' s history into stages, which are then projected throughout the world. However, the historical rhythms of China, India, the Islamic world, Africa and the Americas do not turn around the same set of events.

How does the textbook go up?

After the creation of the nation-state of the century, history textbooks required a narrative line of “origin of the nation-boom-modern”; the colonial system had written European experience as “universal standards”. [2] The result is that other civilizations appear only in “contact with Europe” or are labelled as “traditional” “suspension”.

Network omitted

Indian Ocean trade, trans-Saharan comptoirs, savannah empires, the Islamic Golden Age and the States of America City have long been at the heart of cross-regional networks. [3] They are not a footnote to European history, but political, commercial and knowledge systems that operate simultaneously; they are simply not given separate chapters in the schedules.

How?

The possible variant is not “elimination of Europe”, but rather: the paralleling of the regional time line to indicate overlap and interaction; the organization of cross-regional chapters with trade, disease, technology and religious networks; and the avoidance of the “first” word “sole”, unless there is clear evidence. [1][3] Shows readers that multiple time lines exist at the same time, rather than a main line with several additions.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “Western civilization is a continuum from ancient times to the present”. The so-called “Western” is itself a later-formulated category that encompasses different traditions such as the Mediterranean, Christianity, Latin, German and Enlightenment. [2] Another misconception is that “the correction of the European centre is the politicization of history”; the phased approach is inherently one of position, which is fundamental to the work of history.

Which European events were bound by the phased approach?

The medieval century is often marked by the extinction of Western Rome and Renaissance/Reform of Religion; the modern era is marked by the discovery of the new continent, religious reform, the scientific revolution and the industrial revolution; and the modern era is linked to the French Revolution, the nation State and the world war. [1] These events are meaningful within Europe and are not a turning point in global synchronization.

How does global history reorder?

Instead of removing Europe from global history, Europe is put back on the web: the Indian Ocean monsoon trade, the Trans-Saharan Business Corps, the steppe empire, the Islamic Knowledge Network, the States of America City and the Pacific Islanders all have their own time lines and interactions. [2] The research unit extends from “the State” to “the sea, commerce, religious circles, imperial frontiers”.

We'll split up.

The “early days” are not proof of “European pioneer modernization”; it is just a descriptivetime label. Interpreting Europe's industrialization as “civil superiority” would ignore the distribution of coal, colonial resources, the slave trade, global markets and institutional competition. [3] Cause and effect require a specific mechanism, rather than a phasing back.

Rewrite the four actions of thetime line

First, the regional time line is paralleled; second, the overlap between events and networks is marked; third, the absolute wording “first/only” is avoided; and fourth, the “Western” is broken down into specific traditions and periods. [1][2][3] So the reader sees multiple time lines, not a single main line.

The Middle Ages are not the dark times of the world.

Europe's medieval century is often described as a “dark age”, but during the same period the Islamic world, the Song generation of China, the Indian Ocean trade and the West African empire enjoyed a sense of prosperity, science and commerce. [1] Using the word "dark" as a global label is externalizing Europe's internal narrative.

Timescale of environmental history

Climate, disease and ecology changes are not divided according to dynasty. Small ice periods, volcanic eruptions, black death and monsoon variability span the “old/near” baseary. [2] Environmental history reminds us that the civilizational time line does not coincide with the time line of the Earth system.

How do you write the lines?

It is a parallel time line, a label of interactive events, a distinction between “within Europe” and “global sync” and recognition of historical deviations. [3] Not to cancel the history of Europe, but to make it one of many time lines.

Key figures

  • The end of Western Rome was often marked as the beginning of the Middle Ages.
  • The fall of Constantinople in 1453 was often marked as the end of the Middle Ages.
  • The transatlantic voyage of 1492, the religious reform of 1517 and the French Revolution of 1789 were often marked as recent transitions.
  • The Renaissance of Arts is about the 14th to 17th century, but the golden age of Islam is about the 8th to 13th century.
  • The Song Dynasty China was about 960-1279, cityization and commercial prosperity.
  • In the 13th to 17th century of the Malian Empire, Timbuktu was an academic centre.
  • The industrial revolution is usually set for the second half of the 18th century in Britain.
  • War I of 1914 was often marked as the starting point for “modern” or “short twentieth century”.

A common reverse example.

To go to the European centre is not to delete the history of Europe, nor is it to say that all civilizations are equal. It calls for the return of European events to specific conditions, while recognizing the existence and achievements of independent time lines in other regions. The removal of comparisons and the removal of prejudices are two things.

Keep checking the directions.

The Indian Ocean trade, the Mongolian Empire, the Malian Empire, the Islamic Golden Age and the history of the global environment can be seen. When making the time line, the distinction between “regional turn” and “cross-regional interaction” is marked.

Self-test question three.

  1. ** “Middle Age” is based on what? ** Often bounded by the demise of Western Rome (476) and the fall of Constantinople (1453) or the revival of literature.
  2. ** Is global history canceling European history? ** No; it put Europe back into the network and increased time lines and interactions in other regions.
  3. ** Does going to the European Center amount to relativism? ** Not equal; still to compare evidence with cause and effect, only to refuse to use European experience as default scale.

If there's only one thing to remember,

Civilization time lines are not facts per se, but means of interpretation. Some events are selected and others are ignored in any instalment; a good time line will indicate the selection criteria.

Put the numbers back in scale.

If 3000 years of civilization were to be reduced to 30 centimetres long, Europe's Middle Ages would be about 10 centimeters; on the same scale, the era of Islamic gold, the Song generation of China and the Malian Empire each had their own scales.

Connect the anchor to a line.

Six anchors pulled the Civilization Line out of Europe: 3500 BC, the Indus River Basin entered maturity in 2600 BC, the Islamic Golden Age in 750 AD, the rise of the Malian Empire in 1235, and the beginning of the Industrial Revolution in northwest Europe 250 years ago. [4][5][6][7][8][9]

The phasing is not a natural fact, but rather the selection of certain events as soundary. Using medieval — Renaissance — Enlightenment as a global framework will set the background for the history of Africa, South Asia and the Americas over the same period. The rewrite was not to delete the European events, but rather to line up the time lines in multiple regions, marking the connection and the lack of synchronization between them. [4][5][6][7][8][9]
