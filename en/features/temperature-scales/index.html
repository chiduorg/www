Thermometers and scales: why 0 °C is not “no heat”

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Temperature translates invisible cold heat into readable numbers, but numbers depend on thermometers. Zero centigrades, Fahrenheit and Kelvin are shared at different intervals; modern Kelvin is definition by Bolzmann constant.

First there's a temperature counter, then there's a thermometer.

The Galileo-era thermostats use air or liquid to expand with the cold and heat, but do not have a uniform scale. In 1714, Warren Hayt set a scale with fixed points such as ice salt blends, ice water and human temperature; in 1742, a degreel split the ice point into 100 grids. [1] These thermometers were first operational tools and later explained by thermodynamics.
