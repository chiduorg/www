How black hole mass is weighed: from orbits to shadows

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The black hole has no known surface. Mass reverses by gravitational effect: Star orbit, gas movement, echo mapping, black hole shadows and gravitational waves, each covering different mass and distance ranges.

No scales, only gravity.

The black hole does not glow and there is no solid surface. Its mass can only be pushed by the dynamics of the surrounding matter or the gravitational wave: how fast a Star turns around it, how fast a gas turns, how big a shadow and how strong a wave. [1][2]

Mass units commonly use solar massM☉. M87* About6.5×10⁹ M☉, A* About 4.3×10⁶ M☉, GW 150914 About two initial black holes 36 with 29M☉ and then about 62 M☉. [2][4][5]

Star orbit: silver heart strongest

The center of the galaxy has a group of Stars around a dark and weak radio source. The third law of Kepler gives the center mass: [ Chuckles ] M = 4pi2^33
Mansama A*'s mass is about 4.3 million times the solar mass, that's what it's called. [2] [4]

This method is highly precise, but only for the most recent and brightest nuclear constellations; other Galaxy centres are too far away to distinguish a single Star. [2]

Gas motion and echo map

For the more distant galaxy, the speed of the gas rotating around the black hole can be measured, or the gas in the vicinity can be heated by the radiation of the central black hole, the late reflection distance of the time of the gas re-irradiation can be combined at mass speed. [1][2]

These methods rely on assumptions of gas geometry, inclination and radiation efficiency. The mass given by different methods may be one order of scale, so the papers usually combine estimates. [1]

Black Hole Shadow

EHT recorded the shadow of M87* and Sgr A*. The angular diameter of the shadow is associated with black hole mass, distance, inclination; at known distance, it can reverse mass. [5]

But shadows are not the event scene itself, and image reconstruction is dependent on methods. EHT mass is estimated to be usually cross-checked with the Star orbit, gastrodynamics, rather than being used alone. [5] [4]

Gravity waves: combined mass

Double black holes and combined gravitational waves. Wave frequency evolves and amplifies, coding two initial mass, and ultimately mass and spin. The wave shape of GW150914 gives an initial black hole of about 36 + 29M☉ and then about 62 ZXXQPH1QZX, about 3 ZXXQPH2QZX radiations in the form of gravity waves. [4] [6]

Massmeasurement in gravitational waves does not rely on electromagnetic radiation and can detect completely invisible black holes; however, it is effective only for concurring events and does not give mass to individual static black holes. [4]

Mass function and selection deviation

Many “black holemass” are statistical estimates rather than single-single weights. Samples are selected for active suction, proximity and bright systems; silent black holes are difficult to detect. [1][2]

The mass function also relies on distance, speed measurement and geometry assumptions. The mass of different methods is placed in the same table, and the method must be marked with the error, otherwise the model difference will be treated as real. [1][3]

Read the black holemass list.

First, it looks at orbits, gases, resonance maps, shadows, gravity waves. Second, look at the distance: the further away, the bigger the roror. Third, look at assumptions: inclination, geometry, radiation efficiency. Fourth, depending on the unit: solar mass or kg. [2][4][5]

It is clear why one black hole has several “mass” and why they can still prove each other. [3] [6]

Masserror with Distance

Black holes are estimated to be often dependent on distance. M87* has a distance of about 16.8 megaparsec, A* about 8 kiloparsec; the distance is direct into Masserror. Shadow size is inversely proportional to distance and orbital velocity is inversely proportional to distance. [4] [5]

The error given by different methods is not the same type of error: orbital measurement has geometry, gas movement has modelerror, shadows have reconstruction error and gravity waves have waveror. They are placed in the same table, and the method must be indicated, not just a central value. [1 [2][4]

From individual to statistical

The mass of a single black hole can be precise, but the sample is small; large sample statistics can bind populations but rely on selective effects. Active galaxy nuclear, X-ray twin stars, gravity wave events, each seeing different slices. [1 [2][6]

When reading the Black Holemass, one asks whether it's a single event or a group average, whether it's electromagnetic or gravitational, whether it's direct kinetic or model reverse. The answer is different and the credibility is different. [3] [4]

Mass is not a single number.

The same black hole, different ways to give a different mass and error. When reading the table, look at the method, then at the distance, then at the assumption; a single number will consider the model difference as real. [1][3][4]

Stellar-Mass Black Hole Event Horizon

The event radius of the star class black hole is given by the Swazi radius, which is positive for mass; 10 times the solar mass corresponds to approximately 2.95×10⁴ metres, or about 30 kilometres. This number is a radius, not a diameter.
