Where does the 37 °C body temperature come from

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

37 °C is the intersection of history communication with group statistics, not the fixed value of each person at every hour. The core temperature, oral temperature, temperature and skin temperature are four different sets of readings.

37 °C is an agreement, not a law.

37 °C is often used as normal body temperature, but this figure comes from 19th century group meeting and clinical engagement and is not a precise point for everyone. Modern studies offer a wider range of oral temperatures, with individual differences, changes in readings in the parts of measurementtime and measurement. [3]

Core temperature usually refers to the tumperature of the chest, abdominal and brain, approximately 36.5-37.5 °C; oral, lower armpit, ear and skin are different parts and readings are low or high. [1][4]

Four readings can't be compared.

Oral temperature is affected by breathing, drinking water and measurementtime; under armpit is affected by sweating and tightness; rectal temperature is closer to the core but less operational; and infrared ear temperature is affected by ear angles and earring. [1]

So "37.2 °C count as fever" does not have a uniform answer. It depends on measurement, time, individual baseline and symptoms; a single reading is not a substitute for continuous monitoring. [3] [4]

The temperature is not constant.

The body has a day and night rhythm: it is the lowest in the morning, high in the evening and can fluctuate by about 0.5 °C. Sports, feeding, menstruation cycles, age and drugs change body temperature. [3]

Fever is the upward movement of the hypothalamus setting point, not the "simply heating of the body". The immune system releases the cell factor at the time of infection and changes the temperature adjustment; this is not the same mechanism as the core temperature rise caused by the sun. [1][3]

Skin

The skin is closer to the environment, influenced by blood flow, sweat and clothing; the core is regulated by internal organs and brain stem. The two can be several degrees different, especially in cold or hot environments. [4] [5]

This is also why “touching on the forehead” can only be subjected to rough screening. The forehead is influenced by the environment and sweat and does not provide reliable representation of the core temperature; clinical judgement needs to combine measurement with symptoms. [1][4]

37 °C with wet ball

Body temperature regulation relies on evaporation and dispersion. When the environmental ball is approaching the skin temperature, the sweat is difficult to evaporate and the core temperature rises. The body temperature of 37 °C and the upper limit of the wet ball of 35 °C are two different sets of physical volumes that cannot be directly converted. [2]

Consider "body temperature 37 °C" as "environment 37 °C is dangerous" or "hazardous". Dry-hot environments can be sweaty; high-humid environments can be more dangerous even if temperatures are below 37°C. [2]

List of reading temperature figures

First, look: core, mouth, armpit, ear temperature, skin. Second, watchtime: morning, evening, after exercise, after dinner. Thirdly, look at the baseline: what is the normal temperature of this person? Fourth, it depends on the symptoms: whether they are infected, dehydration or the effects of drugs. [1][3][4]

It's clear that 37°C is a meaningful reference, not a universal slogan. [3][5]

The physics of body temperature regulation

Human heat is derived from metabolism, which is spread mainly by radiation, convection, transport and evaporation. When the environment is near the skin, radiation and convection are reduced and evaporation becomes the main force; evaporation is slower when humidity is high. [1][2]

That is why the risks of “dry heat” and “wet heat” are different at the same temperature. Body temperature regulation is not a single switch, but a system in which cardiovascular, sweaty, respiratory and behavioural participation takes place. [1][2]

Fever is not the same as heat.

The fever is a set point up, the body takes the initiative to raise the temperature to higher; the heat spread fails in the heat, and the core temperature rises passively. The mechanisms, treatment and risks of both are different and cannot be summarized as “high body temperature”. [1][3]

When reading the text, ask “how many degrees” and then “what parts, what time, what symptoms”. These three additional messages are more clinical than an isolated number. [3] [4]

Distribution in Normal Scope

The “normal” given by modern oral studies is not a point, but a zone; different individuals, different times, can fall at both ends. Using 37 °C as a hard threshold would result in some normal people being miscalculated and some anomalies being missed. [3]

Clinical attention to changes in the “relative baseline” is greater: the person at 36.2 °C rises to 37.5 °C at normal times, probably more noteworthy than the person at 37.0 °C at normal times. The interpretation of single absolute values is limited. [3] [4]

♪ Measurementor ♪

The electron thermometer consists of calibrationeror, infrared ear temperature affected by the environment temperature, and under armpit measurement affected by the degree of sweat and tightening. The difference of 0.3 to 0.5 °C is not rare in clinical terms. [1][4]

So the body temperature data have to be based on methods: what equipment, where, how long it was measured, or whether it was repeated. A “37.5 °C” with no method is difficult to compare with other readings. [1][3]

Distribution, not Switches

Body temperature is a distribution, not a switch. Using 37 °C as an absolute threshold, the individual baseline, the measurement area and the time rhythm are ignored; clinical judgement requires a combination of symptoms and continuous reading. [1][3]

Human body temperature
