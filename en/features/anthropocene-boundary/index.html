The Anthropocene: a stratigraphic boundary debated and rejected

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Anthropocene was once seen as a new geologic unit “human activity becomes the main geological force”. The Working Group proposed that the 1950 Great Action be used as a baseary symbol, marked by the plutonium signal of Crawford Lake; the proposal was rejected by the International Stratigphy Commission in 2024 and therefore remains a concept, not a formal global age.

What's Anthropocene?

Anthropocene is a proposed geological geologic unit used to describe the period when human activity has become the main geological force. It is not an ordinary history break, but rather an attempt to create a formal unit alongside or following Holocene in the international form. [1][2]

This concept was promoted by atmospheric chemist Paul Crutzen around 2000. In 2009, the International Stratigphy Commission, in its Section IV, established the Anthropocene Working Group to assess whether there was enough Stratigphy evidence to classify it as a new unit. The Working Group subsequently collected archives of lakes, corals, peats, ice cores, etc. for many years. [1][4]

1950 Great Action

The Working Group submitted a formal proposal in 2023 to rank Anthropocene as a “world/class” starting around 1950. This point corresponds to the “Great Action” phase of population, energy use, plastic production, radioactivity nuclides, and global trade at the same time. [1]

The year 1950 was not the earliest time when “humans began to affect the Earth”. Agricultural, industrial and colonial expansion has long changed the surface; in 1950, it was chosen because it left a globally comparable signal in the statigraphy that fit as a boundary layer. The proposal sought a statigraphy mark, not a moral starting point. To put this geologic age back on the time axis, the year-to-second conversion takes place by SI second definition. [1][3]

Crawford Lake

The proposal is based on the varve deposition of Crawford Lake, Ontario, Canada, as a reference section. The annual varve formed at the bottom of the lake preserves environmental changes, and the significant increase in plutonium isotope around 1952 became the main indicator. Plutonium comes from atmospheric nuclear tests and is a signal of global synchronization. [1]

The Working Group also presented complementary profiles of Beiwan, Japan, Lake Marr, Four Sealong Bay, China, and the Polish Hinezka peatland. Multiple profiles are selected to test whether signals are globally comparable, rather than relying on the record of only one lake. The status of the reference section needs to be recognized jointly by the science community. [1][2]

Rejected in 2024

In March 2024, the Fourth Session of the Council voted on the proposal, which was rejected by 4 votes in favour, 12 against and 3 abstentions. This result was subsequently confirmed by the Executive Board of the International Stratigphy Committee and IUGS, and Anthropocene did not become the official unit of the International Geogenicage table. [1][2]

This veto is not a judgement that “human beings have not changed the Earth”, but a disagreement as to whether and how it should be written into a formal statigraphy unit”. The data accumulated by the Working Group would continue to be used; “Anthropocene” appeared more as an informal concept in scientific and public discussions. [2] [4]

Why is there a dispute?

Proponents believe that human activity has left global signals in the records of security, ice cores, biota and isotope, sufficient for a new era of definition. Opponents question whether the signal is global synchronous, whether it is too close to the present, and whether it is appropriate to include contemporary political and social issues in the statigraphy table. [1][2]

There is also a technical difficulty: the formal statigraphy unit needs a replicable baseary strattype and global coordination marker. While clear, the plutonium signal of 1950 georgical age is not entirely consistent with the preservation, migration and measurement in different environments. Stratigraphy requires comparable rock records, not just an instinct of “human influence”. [1]

What do you need from sratigraphy?

The unit in the international form is not a name, but a set of agreements: baseary sratorype, global coordination marker, absolute age, upper and lower baseary. To be officially recognized, a unit must pass through the Stratigphy Commission ' s proposals, reviews and votes, and find replicable signs in a global profile. [1][2]

It sounds cumbersome, but it is the basis on which geotimes can be compared across continents. Without common standards, “Anthropocene” will become their own historical narrative and cannot be reconciled with the Rock record. The failure of the Anthony proposal was partly due to the tension between the set of standards and contemporary issues. [2][5]

Anthropone and Holocene

Holocene is the latest officially recognized world, starting from about 11.7 thousand years ago and covering most of the human history since the emergence of agriculture. If accepted, the Anthony proposal would either add a new time unit to Holocene or replace its latest phase. [2] [4]

At present, the Stratigraphy watch is still the latest in the history of Holocene. To say that “we live in Antropocene” can be expressed as a public expression, but in the formal statigraphy context, even more accuracy is expressed as “Antropocene is a proposed, unapproved concept of a global age”. The two statements can coexist, but they cannot be used interchangeably. [1][2]

Common misunderstandings

The first misperception is that “Anthropocene has become the official global age”. It is not currently approved. The second misconception is that “the rejection of Anthropocene is tantamount to a denial of human influence”. It was the Stratigpropy unit proposal that was rejected, not the environmental impact itself. [1][2]

The third misconception is that “1950 was the starting point for human influence”. It is only the baseary level chosen for the proposal, and the early effects already exist. The fourth misconception is that “Anthropocene is just an environmental slogan”. It was originally a strict statigraphy process, and the dispute also occurred at the procedural and evidentiary levels. [1][4]

Reading

Seeing “Anthropocene”, ask first whether it is used in public discussions or in official statigraphy tables. Again, it refers to the status of the proposal, the study by the Working Group, or the regime after its rejection in 2024. Finally, the “human impact” is separated from the “official unit” and not treated as the same. [1][2]

The value of anthropocene may not be that it was approved, but rather that it forced the scientific community to answer the question: Has human activity been sufficiently large to rewrite the Stratigphy record? Whatever the answer, the question itself has changed the way we look at the history of the planet. [2] [4]
