Surface-area-to-volume ratio: why small things lose heat faster

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Area is scaled by square length, volume by cubic scaling; surface area/volume is down by 1/L when the object is bigger. It binds dissipation, diffusion, weight bearing and the size of the cell.

Square - Cubic Law

Change the length of the cube from 1 to 2: surface area from 6 to 24 (x4) and volume from 1 to 8 (x8). The surface area isa/volume ratio fell from 6 to 3, just to halve. [1][5] For any shape, the length is increased by L times and the area is about L2, volume is about L3; the S/V is about to be reduced by 1/L. The shape constant changes, but the scaling trend remains unchanged. [1][5]

Why are the animals spreading so fast?

The small animal unit of volume is large and heat is more vulnerable to dissipation. Metabolism has to be faster in order to maintain body temperature; the elephant unit of volume is small, surface area isa, is slow to disperse and depends on ears, breathing and behaviour. [3][5] This is why insects do not need a lung: the gas is small through the trachea and the body table diffusion, scale, diffusion is short; when it gets bigger, diffusion can't keep up, and it has to evolve out of the circulation and respiratory system. [3][5]

Cell and Construction

The size of the cell is limited by S/V and diffusion: increased volume, relative reduction of nutrition and waste exchange surface area; fragmentation is a way to restore S/V. The specific ceiling is also bound by the cell skeleton, penetration and genome. [2] [3] Construction is also affected by the square-cubic law: pro rata amplification of models, weights increased by L3, cross-section of beams increased by L2 and without re-engineering, the structure will be crushed by its weight. [5] [6]

How?

Unitize first, then calculate the area and volume. Sphere A=4πr2, V=4/3πr3, S/V=3/r; cube S/V=6/a. The smaller the radius, the greater the margin. [1][5] When comparing different shapes, you cannot only look at S/V values but also at heat transfer factors, surface roughness and internal heat production. S/V is a geometric starting point, not a complete hot model. [5] [6]

I'm sorry.

S/V is not “the smaller the better” or “the bigger the better”. The radiator needs to be large and the thermostat small; the organisms need to balance the dispersion with the temperature. [3][5] Considering S/V as the only reason, the material, the flow, the radiation and the behavior are ignored. It explains trends and does not explain all differences. [5] [6]

Read deep: biological scale

Small animals S/V are large, hot and fast, often requiring high-metabolism rates or behaviour to avoid cold; large animals S/V are small, temperature is easy but heat is difficult to disperse. The size is not the only variable, but the fur, the fat, the blood vessels and the behaviour. [3][5]

Read deep: Project scale

Spreaders, catalysts and sensors often pursue large S/Vs; thermostats, tanks and large structures pursue small S/Vs. When scaled up, the heat transfer and weight change at the same time, not only in the outer form. [5] [6]

Read deep: common mistakes

Compare only S/V values without looking at shape and heat transfer coefficients; double the length to consider 2 times the size; treat S/V as the only biological size limit. Geometrics gives trends, materials and environmental decisions. [3] [6]

Continue: square cube

Length amplification L times, area amplification L2, volume amplification L3; S/V is approximately 1/L change. First with simple cubes, then with complex shapes. [1][5]

Continue: biothermal dispersion

Small animals S/V large, heat spread fast; big animals S/V small, warm but hot. Leather, fat, blood vessels and behaviour can be reconciled, and not just body size. [3][5]

Continue: Project heat transfer

The radiator pursues large S/V, the thermostat pursues small S/V; when scaled up, the heat transfer changes at the same time as the load, and cannot only be magnified. [5] [6]

Continuation: calculation method

Sphere S/V = 3/r, cube S/V = 6/a; unit first, then margin. Constants vary in different shapes and trends are similar. [1][5]

Continue: baseary

S/V explains trends, not all of them; material, convection, radiation and behaviour change results. Don't think of it as the only limit to the size of life. [3] [6]

Something to look back on.

Surface area by length square, volume by length cube and S/V by approximately 1/L. The length of the cube is doubled, four times the area, eight times the area of volume, halved S/V; sphere S/V=3/r. Small animals are hot, metabolism is high, large animals are warm, but heat is dissipated; cell is constrained by diffusion, and buildings are severely constrained. The radiator pursues large S/V, the thermostat pursues small S/V. S/V explains trends, not all of them; material, convection, radiation and behaviour change results. [1][3][5][6]
