Orders of magnitude: why a factor of 10 is one step

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Order of magnitude compares scale with 10: 10 times the difference is one step and 100 times two steps. It's appropriate for quick size, not for precision.

The first is ten times.

Order of magnitude writes the numbers in 10, then compares the index. 1 metre is 10⁰ and 1 km is 10³; the difference is 1,000 times. The term “big two order of magnitude” means about 100 times more than the exact 100.0. [1][2] The scientific mode of writing any positive number as a x 10n, order of magnitude is usually n; a close to 10 affects rounding.1.9×10³and 2.1×10³ may be described as "10³order of magnitude". Why 10?

Decimal is the numerical base commonly used by humans, and the SI words are grouped by10³. Use 10 instead of 2 to allow length, mass,time to be placed on the same set of oral scale; the price is that it is not good at describing binary systems. [1][6] Order of magnitude is not the mathematic upper limit. A 3-metre man is a step away from a 30-metre building, three-step away from a 30-metre mountain; an index, then a coefficient, can be quickly located. [2] [4]

Area and volume

10 times length, 100 times area, 1,000 times volume. The application of a "larger order of mass" directly to an area or volume would count less than one or two steps; the squares and cubes in units would have to be indexed together. [1][5] This is also the scale illusion source: the distance on a map is doubled to four times the area covered; the size of an animal is doubled, the volume is about eight times greater, and the dissipation and weight change. I'm sorry.

Order of magnitude is suitable for screening and communication and not for acceptance. Exact values, error and conditions are required for engineering differentials, medical doses and space orbits; the phrase “the same order of space” cannot replace these. [1][3] Negative numbers and zeros do not have the usual meaning of “order of masse”. For 0.001 and 1000, the index is -3 and 3 respectively; for 0, it cannot be said to be less than 1 order of magnitude. [1][5]

The three most common types of errors are to describe “two orders of magnitude” as “twice”, to omit an index of area or volume and to read a negative index as negative. When you check, write down the ten-column before writing the coefficient. [1][5]
