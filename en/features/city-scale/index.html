The scale of a city: area, density and infrastructure are not the same thing

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

City has no natural unity baseary. The city of Shanghai is about 6340 square kilometres and about 100 kilometres between East and West, but treating it as a “city diameter” would mask the true structure of density, commissioning and infrastructure.

City doesn't have a single area.

Ask for the size of this city. The continuous construction-up area is demarcated by government jurisdiction and the interior area is counted by construction and road continuity. The size of the three types can vary several times and the population changes accordingly. [1][2]

The administrative base is often drawn along rivers, roads, mountains or historical boundaries, which may include large fields of agricultural land, mountains and waters; the bilt-up range follows successive buildings with infrastructure; and the functional urban area isa combines surroundings that are closely connected to the central city. All three answered different questions and could not replace one another. [1][2]

Size and extent of Shanghai

The Shanghai Statistical Yearbook gives an area of approximately 6340.5 square kilometres, about 120 kilometres north and south and about 100 kilometres east and west. Xinhua's Shanghai Profile page gives the same order of magnitude. This value is the administrative area, not the bilt-up area. [3] [4]

One hundred kilometres as a "city diameter" is not anaccuracy. The city shape is irregular, and the maximum span is only a envelope value in the direction; a city of the same size can be compact. Nor is the area directly squared as an average width, as there are waters, agricultural land and mountains inside city. [3]

Density and population

With the same size of two city, the population can be much worse. The size of the population is divided by size, but the molecular and qualifier-measurable composition must be consistent: the population of the built-up area is underestimated by the size of the population; the population of the urban area is overestimated by the size of the bilt-up area. [1][2]

According to the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA), the global city population has surpassed the rural population and city is the daily living environment for most people. This “most” is not evenly distributed: large city, small and medium city and peri-urban density, compulsing and public services are very different. Summarizing global city with an average density would remove the most important differences. [6]

The scale of infrestructure

Cityinfrastructure was not spread on an equal scale. The metro lines are extended along the passenger-flow corridor, the bus lines cover the neighbourhoods, the water pipe network is divided by pressure and high range, and the electricity grid is distributed by load centre. A 12-metre bus can carry dozens of people, but its route, station and movement determine its validity. [4] [5]

The “cityscale” of infrastructure is more visible in network connections than in the size of individual components. A subway line can span dozens of kilometres and a bridge can connect the two sides, but the range of their services depends on site, transject and walkability. View the size of the city only as size and ignore the network structure. [1][5]

The difference between the head island and the neighborhood.

Urban head island explains that it's not even inside of city. Concrete and asphalt heat during the day, heat during the night, construction intensity, reduced green areas, and air-conditioning heat, all contribute to higher temperatures in neighbourhoods than in the suburbs. According to the EPA, the urban head island can bring a temperature difference in the neighborhood of digital Celsius. [5]

But heat island is not the whole city evenly warm. The shades, the water, the ventilator and the direction of the building may cause the scale to differ. Treating city average temperature as a temperature for every block will mask the population and location most in need of heat protection. The physics of city does not overlap with the hot environment. [5]

Origin of city

City is not modern scale. Early city, such as Uruk, has brought together population, agricultural surplus, handicraft and religious centres in a limited number of areas to form new social networks. The key to the origin of city is not a certain baseary, but rather the emergence of density, division of labour and exchange. [2] [3]

The population of early city is much smaller than that of modern cities, but they have demonstrated the core contradiction of city scale: concentration brings efficiency and pressure on water supply, sewerage, transport and governance. Modern city simply magnifies these problems and does not eliminate them. [1][2]

City and sustainability

The sustainability of cityscale cannot be seen only in size. The same 1 million people, divided into large-bread suburbs and concentrated in orbital transport corridors, will be completely different in terms of per capita energy consumption and land occupation than in respect of computingtime. Density is not a goal in itself, but the network efficiency, public space and service availability behind density. [1][6]

Breaking a city into the three layers of the “Man-Building-Network” avoids using an area figure: human scale decides to walk and experience on the street, building scale decides on sunlight, ventilation and thermal environment, and network scale decides on computing, water supply and emergency response. Three layers of alignment, and city only works on human scale. city is not a plank, but a process of constant adjustment of the three layers in the time. [1 [2][5]

Common misunderstandings

The first misconception is that “city area is the bilt-up area”. A large number of unconstructed land can be contained. The second misconception is that “population is size”. Population and area are three different indicators. [1][3]

The third misunderstanding is that “city spans can represent city shapes”. Maximum width is given to only one package, without describing shape and connectivity. The fourth misconception is that “urban head island equals global warming”. Heat island is a land-use effect, and global warming is a long-term change in the climate system, different from scale. [2][5]

Reading

When you look at the city data, you ask three questions: what is it, what is it, what is it, whether it's the same population area. Let's see if it's "administrative city," "built-up" or "functional urban". Finally, the size, density, computing and infrastructure are separated and the whole city is not summarized in a single number. [1][2]

City is one of the largest daily scale built by mankind, but not a large room. It is based on a common vision of density, density, network and neighbourhood differences; understanding the city requires both the contours on the map and life on the ground. [3][5]
