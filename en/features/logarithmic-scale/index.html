Logarithmic scales: turning multiplication into addition

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

A logarithmic pressure on the ratio relationship to the margin: 10 times the difference between two numbers and only 1 after the logarithm. Earthquakes, acoustic pressure, pH and stars are used, but zero and negative numbers must be especially careful.

Add Multiplication

If y=log10(x), x from 1 to 10, y from 0 to 1; x to 100, y to 2. Multiplier relationships are pressured to add up, which is where logarithmic scale works best. Common logarithmic feet

Seismic seismic, acoustic, dB, pH, astronomical, etc. and RIAA sound scales are logarithms or approximations. They press the amount across more than one order of magnitude to readable range, but they also allow the ratio of "discretion 1" to change with the bottom. How?

The logarithmic upper equidistance represents the ratio, not the margin. 1 to 10 equals the visual distance from 10 to 100, but 10 times the absolute increment. [1][5] Read the chart by looking for ten points and then see if the scale is marked with an average of 2 or 5. Without a logarithmic axis with a median value, it is easy to read the error of 3 as "almost half of 10. " [5] [6]

Zero and Negative

Log(0) does not exist; logarithms cannot place 0 on the axis. There are no logarithms for negative values; a deviation or a symmetric logarithm is required and a variation is indicated. [1][5] The "0" of the fractions and pH is not absolute zero, but the value of the reference: 0 dB corresponds to an agreed acoustic pressure and pH 7 corresponds to a hydrogen ion activity. Consider the reference point "no" as an error. [2][5]

I'm sorry.

The logarithmic compression hides the absolute difference. Both are large, and a logarithmic number may be a little less; in turn, the two decimals may look much worse. When making a decision, return to the original value. [1][6] Logarithms are only applied to positive non-quantifiable scales or to aggregated amounts. The length of the unit is taken directly as a logarithm, and the result depends on the unit selection; it must be divided by the reference length. [1][5]
