Why cells cannot grow without limit: scale, diffusion and transport

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The size of the cell is limited by the ratio of surface area to volume, diffusion speed, material transport and genome management. Bacterium is at the micrometer level, red blood cell at a few micrometres, egg cell visible, and neuron can be long; these dimensions are not random, but are the result of physical restraints and functional divisions.

Cellscale

Cell isn't just one size. bacterium is usually in one to a few micrometres, the human body is in the range of ten to dozens of micrometres, red blood cell is in the range of seven to eight micrometres in diameter, and the human egg cell is in the range of one millimeter in diameter, which is visible to the naked eye. The neuron cell body is also in the tens of micrometers of magnitude, but its axles can extend to more than one metre. [1][3]

These figures span three order of magnitude and cannot be summed up by the phrase “cells are small”. Cell size is evolutionary, metabolism is shaped with development, not by scaling up the same template. Different cells perform different functions, with different sizes. Micrometres are a millionths of a metre, and the "micro" conversation in the cellscale table is10⁻⁶ miorder of magnitude. [1][5][6]

I'm sorry.

Compare objects to magnification, surface area increases by square, volume increases by cubic. Ten times the length, a hundred times the surface area and a thousand times the volume. For the cell, this means that the corresponding area of membrane, exchange area and dissipation area in volume is decreasing. [1]

Cell absorbs nutrients through surfaces, discharges waste and exchanges gas. The larger the volume, the higher the internal demand for exchange, and the less the apparent exchange capacity available. This geometric constraint is the first threshold at which the cell cannot grow indefinitely. [1][2]

Diffusion Limit

The movement of molecules inside the cell is largely dependent on diffusion. Diffusiontime grew roughly by squares of distance: the distance doubled, and the time that reached the end was about fourfold. The larger the cell, the slower the internal transport, the more difficult it will be to clear the metabolism waste in time. [1][3]

Largecell can be compensated by active transport, the flow of the cell skeletons, the cystal circulation and the vascular system, but these mechanisms require energy consumption and space occupancy. Cell is not unable to become larger, but has to pay more transport costs when it becomes larger. Size is the trade-off between gain and cost. [1][2]

Why isn't there a meter big?

A single cell of a metre size is not geometrically impossible, but it faces a number of challenges: how the internal matter is transported quickly, how the metabolism heat spreads, how the membrane area supports the huge volume, and how the genome and the cell skeleton are managed. There is no “magnification programme” available to the Cell to address these problems simultaneously. [1][2]

The Docell creature has gone another way: tearing large volumes into small cells and moving between the circulatory, respiratory and nervous systems. The human body is not a big cell, but a consortium of about trillions of them. The division of labour has made the whole larger, while individual cells still have a smaller scale. [2] [3]

Size and Metabolism Rate

Cell's metabolism rate does not follow the linear growth of volume. Volume becomes larger, the area of membranes available in units is reduced and transport capacity is reduced, and the costs of metabolism are higher; small cells are more likely to send nutrition quickly inside and waste out. Sizes evolve with the Metabolism policy, not with a single parameter. [1][3]

And that's why it's also a nuclear thing, with yeasts bigger than red blood cell and egg cell bigger than both: they choose different routes of rapid reproduction, oxygen and nutrient storage. Size is the result of function, not the starting point of function. [2] [4]

♪ Red blood cell ♪

Red blood cell approximately 7 to 8 micrometres in diameter, with no cell core and most of the cells, and mainly haemoglobin. Its task is to transport oxygen, shape and membrane elasticity that allows it to squeeze through a fine vein. The loss of the cell nuclear has limited its repair and fragmentation, but replaced by a larger oxygen space. [3] [4]

Bacillus is a bacterium, approximately 2 micrometres long, approximately 0.5 micrometres in diameter, and a negative 15-kg ww. It maintains shapes by cell membrane and cell wall, by lashes, by particles and individual rings of DNA managing genetic information. The size of the acterium matches the rapidly divided, nutritionally absorbed ecology strategy. [3] [4]

Nouron is the exception.

Neuron's cysts are only a few dozen micrometres, but the axles can last up to one metre and transmit the signal from the spinal cord to the toe. This "long" is not a scaling-up of the cyst, but an extension of a long membrane structure. Intra-axis transport depends on molecular motors and micropipes and not on free diffusion. [1][3]

Accordingly, the length of neuron cannot be used to refute the “cell size limited by diffusion”. Its cells are still small and long-range transport is carried out by specialized mechanisms. Consider the axle length as a cell diameter, which combines two different scale. [2] [3]

I don't know.

The human egg cell is about one millimeter in diameter and is one of the largest cells in the human body. It stores the nutrients and molecules needed for early embryo development, and volume is much more functional. The egg cell does not complete all transport by fast diffusion, but by the precision of storage and early development in the cell. [3] [4]

Egg cell also explains that “big” is not costless. It requires more nutrition, more sophisticated polarity and stricter development control. When the size increases, the cell does not simply scale up all the structures, but redistributes space and functionality. [2] [4]

Common misunderstandings

The first misunderstanding is that “all bells are as small”. The sizes of the acterium, red blood cell, egg cell and neuron vary considerably. The second misunderstanding is that “cell is bigger and better”. Bigcell pays more for transport, heat dispersion and membrane exchange. [1][3]

The third misunderstanding is that “surface areavolume only influences shape”. It directly affects the metabolism rate, exchange efficiency and heat dispersion and is part of the physiological function. The fourth misunderstanding is that “neuron is one metre long, so the cell can be one metre large”. The axle is a fine long structure and does not correspond to the cylindrical diameter. [2] [4]

Reading

When you see the size of the cell, you ask whether it's measured in diameter, length, volume or mass, then whether it's an isolated, intra-organised, or a long-deep neuron. Finally, we ask what this size is for: rapid fragmentation, oxygen, storage of nutrients, or long-range transfer. [1][3]

Cellscale is the interface between physical restraints and biological functions. It reminds us that life can build a large whole, but that basic units are usually kept at the micrometer level; rather than magnifying the units, it is adding units, division of labour and transport systems. [2] [3]
