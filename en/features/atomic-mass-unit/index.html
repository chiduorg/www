The atomic mass unit: why carbon-12?

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

1 u definition is one twelfth of carbon-12 atomic mass, approximately 1.6605×10⁻²⁷ kg. It allows atoms to compare easily with molecular mass, but atomic weight is not mass.

Definition

U unit of atomic mass equals one twelfth of a carbon-12 atom in base, rest mass, approximately 1.66053906660×10⁻²⁷ kg. [1] Carbon-12 is selected because it has 6 protons and 6 neutrons, which are stable in abundance and facilitate international harmonization.

Why one twelfth?

Carbon-12 has 12 cores and divides its atomic mass into 12 pieces, each of which corresponds to approximately one nuclear mass. This means that proton, neutron and common nuclides mass can be expressed in numbers close to the integer. [2] Aatom mass, however, also includes Electron and combined energy, and therefore is not a simple nuclear addition.

No, atomic weight and mass

Atomic weight is the weighted average of each natural isotope abundance of the element, often expressed in u or no schematic relative; mass is the number of protons and neutrons for a single nuclide, the integer. [1][2] Tomic weight of chlorine is about 35.45, not 35 or 36, because of the abundance weight of 35Cl and 37Cl.

Bridge with kg

1 u equals approximately 1.6605×10⁻²⁷ kg and approximately 1/6.022×10²³ g. The Avogadero constant connects micromass to macromoremass; 1 mol carbon-12 is about 12 g. [3] This bridge allows chemical metrology to switch with SI kg.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “atomic weight is atomic mass”. Atomic weight is the mean abundance and mass is the integer of a single nuclide. [2] Another misunderstanding is that "u" and "Dalton" are two different units; they have the same values, and u is a non-SI unit accepted by SI and Da is often used in biochemistry.

Moore and Avogardro.

1 mol contains several particles of the Avogadero constant, approximately 6.022×10²³; Mormass, expressed in g/mol, is close to atomic weight. [1] Aatom mass unit connected to Moore through the Avogardro constant.

Iisotope and abundance

Element's atomic weight is the average weighted by natural abundance of each so that it is not integer; mass spectrometers measure iiiiisotopepepic weight. [2] The abundance of different sources of the same environment may vary slightly.

Mass losses

Atom nuclear mass is less than the sum of mass that makes up the nuclear, and the margin corresponds to the combined energy; atomic mass is used in nuclear reaction energy calculations. [3] Mass losses are not “mass disappearing”, but are carried with energy.

Carbon-12 Why Benchmark

The carbon-12 atom core has 6 protons and 6 neutrons, mass is definition to 12 u; other atomic mass are relative to it. [1] The selection of carbon-12 is a historical agreement, not a natural law.

How do you measure the mass spectrometry?

The mass spectrometers decompose the sample ion, separate it by the charge ratio, and measure isotopepmas and abundance; combined with the caliber standard, it gives tomic weight and molecular weight. [2] Massmeasurement of large molecules, such as proteins, requires charge state to accumulate.

Aatom mass Unit and Moore

In 2019, after SI was revised, Moor was still based on carbon-12 in Avogardro constant; the bridge between the two isN_A.1 u is given by CODATA about1.66053906660×10⁻²⁷ kg, updated with measurement. [4]

Standard weight is regularly assessed by IUPAC and CIAAW, giving range or single values; when chemical measurements are made with atomic weight, a distinction is made between “standard value” and “actual sample value”. [5] This is why the same element can differ slightly in the sample of different sources.
