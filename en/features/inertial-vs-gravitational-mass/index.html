Inertial and gravitational mass: why are they equal?

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

One describes “hard to push” and one describes “highly gravity”. They differ, but are accurately equal in the experiment; the principle of equivalence turns this equivalence into a geometric interpretation of gravity.

Two mass, two experiments.

Inertial mass uses F = ma measurement: exert known force, see acceleration. Gravitymass uses scales or freefalls: to see how much force the object receives in the gravity field. [1] The former is concerned with motor response, while the latter is concerned with gravitational coupling; if they are not equal, free fall will change acceleration depending on the composition of the object.

Equivalence

The principle of equivalence is that, on a local scale, the level of gravity field is indistinguishable from acceleration. An observer in a closed elevator is unable to judge whether he or she is still on the surface of the Earth or whether he or she is accelerated upwards in a vacuum. [2] This principle requires inertial mass to be proportional to gravity mass; units are selected to be considered the same amount.

How well does the experiment work?

The accuracy of the test of the principle of equivalence is constantly increasing, from Galileo's slope to modern scales, ground laser ranging and satellite experiments. The comparison of the Moon laser range to the Earth and Moon with the solar gravitational response is extremely restrictive. [2] These experiments did not detect deviations that formed reliance, but still have an error range.

Why is this important?

If inertia is different from gravity, geometric gravity of a broad relativism is absent and free fall is no longer universal. [1][2] Equivalence also means that gravity is not a "force" in the ordinary sense, but rather a manifestation of a space-time bend; mass tells how time-space bends and time-space tells how mass moves.

A common misconception is that “the principle of equivalence proves that the two mass are identical”. More generally, accuracy states that within the precision of existing experiments they are proportional and indistinguishable, and that the two concepts are still physically retained because of the different ways in which ameasurement is conducted. [2] Another misunderstanding is to extend the principle of equivalence to the global context; it is strictly established only in the local freefall system, where tidal power exposes temporal curvature.

Newton's swing experiment.

Newton compares the cycles of different objects on a single scale to test the consistency of the ratio of inertia mass to gravitational mass; the cycles rely only on swing and gravity and not on swing mass. [1] This is an early experimental form of "Equivalence Principles".

Experimental precision

Modern testing limits the difference to less than 10⁻¹⁵ order of magnitude by weighing, atom intervention and satellite experiments. [3] If differences are found, the general relativism needs to be amended.

Why do free fall at the same time?

In a vacuum, objects of different mass are gravitationally proportional to inertia, with the same acceleration; this is the daily expression of the principle of equivalence. [1] When there is air, resistance differs from shape to shape to landtime.

Equivalent Body

Equivalent principles are local: gravitational forces can be eliminated by free faller coordinates in a sufficiently small space-time area; and wide tidal forces can still expose temporal curves. [2] It is not "gravity does not exist" but "gravity can be geometrically ".

Why keep two concepts?

The principle of equivalence is that gravity can be eliminated in the local freefall reference system, but tidal forces are still exposed to spatial and temporal curvature; inertia mass is not the same as gravity mass, in the present case. [4]

Modern experiments limit the relative difference between the two to less than 10⁻¹⁵ order of magnitude, using scales, atom interference and Moon laser ranging. [5] If deviations are detected in the future, the general relativism needs to be amended; the existing evidence still supports the principle of equivalence.

Mass / Planets & small bodies

Earth

The Earth's total mass is approximately 5.972×10²⁴ kg, which contains the atmosphere and oceans. Values are combined by satellite orbit tracking and gravitational field model, relative to uncertainty approximately 1×10⁻³, and are mainly limited by the accuracy of the gravitational constant G.

Mass / Planets & small bodies

Moon

Moon is the only natural satellite of the Earth, with a total mass of about 7.346×10²² kg, or about one-one-first point of the Earth. The laser moon follows the Moon orbiter and gives about 0.01 per cent relative to uncertainty, which is one of the most accurate measurements in the body mass.

Mass / Stars & compact objects

Sun

1.989×10³⁰ kg, or 1 solar mass, of the Sun's total mass, is often used as a conversion unit for the entire mass ladder. The values are proposed by the International Astronomical Union for nominal solarmass parameters GM☉ divided by the gravitational constant G conversion, relative to uncertainty approximately 1×10⁻⁴.
