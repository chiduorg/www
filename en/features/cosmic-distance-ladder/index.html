The cosmic distance ladder: how do we know how far a star is?

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The difference is close, the parent variable is far away from the supernova, and the red move is further away from the universe science model. Each level is used for the previous level of calibration, which is passed by error.

Level I: Disparity

As the Earth moves around the Sun, there is a slight shift in the sky of the nearby Star. View angle p, distance d = 1/p (parsec). [1] The Gaia satellite pushes the difference towards billions of Stars, but the further the difference is smaller, and the perror increases rapidly.

Level 2: Standard candlelight

The pulsation cycle of the parent variable is associated with the light, so that the measuring period can push the light; the light and observation brightness are known and the distance is given by the distance model formula. [2] The parent variable is a key ruler for the proximity of the galaxy, but requires intragalactic vision and constellation data caliberation.

Level III: Supernova

Ia-type supernova eruptions are high and peak light is more consistent and can be observation in very far galaxy. They extend the distance ladder to billions of light-years and are used to detect rapid expansion of universe. [1][3] But the calibration of supernovas still depends on the nearby father-forming star and host galaxy.

Level IV: Red migration

In universe scale, galaxy red migration expands the historical connection through distance rather than simply a straight line of Hubble law. It needs to be assumed that the universe grouping and the acoustic view, using BAO, CMB, is bound by supernovas. [2][3] Red motion is not a "velocity" and the distance of a high red motion object depends on model.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “red migration is further away, so the Hubble constant is speed”. The Hubble Law describes the statistical relationship between the speed of exit and distance, and the red migration also involves the expansion of the universe and the movement rate. [1][3] Another misunderstanding is that “the distance ladder is a continuous ruler”; it is conflated by multiple overlapping methods, each with a systemeror.

What's Standard Candlelight for?

The cycling-photo relationship of the father-made star comes from the physics of the star pulse; the Ia-type supernova comes from a thermonuclear explosion after the white dwarf reaches the critical mass, with a more consistent peak of light. [2] However, the “standards” are not absolutely the same and require caliberation, host galaxy photolysis and metal abundance correction.

How does error pass through the hierarchy?

Near-range vision creates father-to-man, father-to-man, supernova, supernova. Each level of systems is passed down to the lower level and different caliber samples give different H0. [3] The distance ladder is not a continuum, but an interdependent set of measurement.

Red motion is not the speed itself.

The universe learning red migration comes from space expansion, not Galaxy's doppler speed flying in static space; the background speed is superimposed on the red move. [2] [3] In high-red migration, distance must be universe-mode from red, not simple v = cz conversion.

Inverse of distance and light

The brightness decreases with distance squared: the same star is twice the distance and the brightness becomes a quarter. [1] If you know introsinc light, you can ask for distance from the light; if you do not know, you can only use a standard candlelight or visualization.

Dust and polish.

Interstellar dust absorbs and scatters star light, making the bodies darker and reder; the light is different in different bands. [2] The distance ladder must be changed to light, otherwise it will be miscalculated as far or darker.

Future

Gaia provides a billion-degree Star view, JWST pushes standard candlelight calibration further, and gravity waves provide independent distance. [3] The crossover of multiple probes is the main route to addressing Hubble tension.

The distance of unverse is not a ruler, but a set of ladders for each other. Each level is introduced, and eventually H0's uncertainty is a synthesis of the whole chain.

Connect the anchor to a line.

Each step of the 5th step was previously the baseline: the Earth's diameter and the solar-terrestrial distance gave the baseline of the difference, the near-neighbor star was determined depending on the difference, the standard candlelight pushed scale to galaxy, and the supernova to red migration to universe distance. [4][5][6][7][8]

There is an independent error at each level and it passes down. The difference is rapidly weakening over long distances, the standard candlelight depends on calibration, supernovas depend on homogeneity, and red migration depends on universe learning model; therefore “distance” is not a direct reading, but rather the result of a calibration chain. [4][5][6][7][8]

As a result, the history of distance measurement is not constantly “corrected” a number, but rather an ongoing search for independent calibration at the lower level. Each generation of telescopes pushes down the first level and exposes the next level of the system; that is why the distance ladder is still worth repeating. [4][5][6][7][8]

The endpoint of the range is also not an absolute coordinate: on the universe scale, the distance also depends on which universe parameters are used. When writing a single number, the distance must be accompanied by an indication of the one-level method and the set of parameters by which it was obtained. [4][5][6][7][8]
