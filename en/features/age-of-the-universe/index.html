The age of the universe: how 13.8 billion years was calculated

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The universe age is not “how long has the light gone farthest”, but rather time from the universe learning model and observation parameters. Hubble's swelling, microwave background and gravitational acoustic oscillation together give about 13.8 billion years.

From expansion.

In 1929, Huber found that Galaxy's retreat was roughly proportional to distance, and the ratio constant H0 was known as the Hubble constant. [1] If the rate of inflation is constant, 1/H0 gives a timescale, about 14 billion years; but universe's expansion is not even, and matter, radiation and dark energy change history, so it has to be replaced by universe learning model.

Microwave background constraints

Universe microwave background is the bold radiation from the universe complex, temperature approximately 2.725 K, with a small heterogeneity. These ups and downs, scale and acoustic peaks, record the composition, curvature and expansion of the universe. [2] Planck 2018 combines data on CMB, BAO, etc., giving the UNiverse age of approximately 13.8 billion years and the Hubble constant of approximately 67.4 km/s/Mpc.

Hubble.

Some 73 km/s/Mpc, measured at local distance steps, is not fully consistent with Planck ' s 67.4 and has already reached a point of prominence, which is "Haber tension". [3] It may come from distance, early universe physical or statistical methods; if it is maintained, the age is still about 13.8 billion years, but the parameters space will be narrower.

Where does Model depend?

Age calculations rely on: universe components (ordinary matter, dark matter, dark energy), acoustic visual scales, swelling history and initial rises. [2] These are not arbitrary aggregations, but are subject to multiple independent mandates; however, “13.8 billion years” are still the best values within the model, not the direct ones.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “universe age is the observation universe radius divided by the speed of light”. The observation UNiverse radius is about 46.5 billion light-year because space continues to expand during light transmission; age and radius are not simply light speed relationships. [2][3] Another misunderstanding is that “the light just arrived on Earth 13.8 billion years ago”, and that the CMB photons come from a complex period, not from the edge of the universe.

Why is age not "how long is light gone?"

The most distant galaxy light has been gone for about 13.4 billion years, but the universe continues to swell during light transmission, so those galaxys are today about 32 billion light-years; the observation-wide radius is about 46.5 billion light-years. [2][3] Age describes the time of the evolution of universe, not the time of the light from the edge to the Earth.

The oldest spherical clusters are about 12-13 billion years, compatible with the universe age of 13.8 billion; if universe is only 10 billion years, thesestars are “too old”. The radius of observation universe is determined by the distance and inflation that light has been able to walk since Big Bang, about 46.5 billion light-year; it is not the same as the entire unverse. Universe may be infinite or complex, but beyond sight is not causally linked to us. [2] “universe age” means the evolution of time.
