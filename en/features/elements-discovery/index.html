The history of element discovery: from alchemy to spectroscopy

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Element was not once “discovered”. Ancient metals, alchemy, spectroscopy, electrolytics, radioactivity decay and artificial nuclear reactions have expanded their respective periodic tables; each time the meaning of “discovery” has been renewed.

Ancient metals are not found.

Metals such as gold, silver, copper, iron, lead, tin and mercury were used before history. They exist in the form of single or easily reversible minerals and do not need to know that atomic numbers can be smelted and processed. [6] [7]

The term “discovery” is a modern term: knowing it is a fundamental material that can no longer be broken down and can be distinguished from other elements. The ancient craftsmen know the nature of gold, but they don't know that gold is 79% element. [6] [7]

Alchemistry and New Element

The alchemy seeks to convert metals and, while the target has not been achieved, has accumulated a large amount of distillation, dissolving, burning and crystallization operations. The separation of elementes such as phosphorus, arsenic, americium and zinc is associated with these experimental traditions. [3]

The concept of “element” in alchemy is not yet modern. It is more like a vector of nature: sulphur, mercury, salt. Modern element is atomic numberdefinition, which cannot be directly matched. [3]

Spectra: from color to fingerprints

Each element sends a specific wavelength light when it is triggered. Ben and Kirkhov used the spectra as a “fingerprint” for element, and in the 19th century they found new elements such as cataracts and caesium. [1][3]

The spectra found not only new element, but also a measrationstar composition. Helium was first discovered in the solar spectrum and then separated from the Earth; this is a typical example of “seen in heaven and then confirmed on the ground”. [1]

Mendeleev and Periodic Law

Mendeleev ranked element according to the atomic weight and chemical properties, leaving room and predicting the unknown nature of element. The periodic tables are not a classification table, but a testable pattern. [2] [3]

Later, tomic number replaced atomic weight; the electron model explained the physical basis of the cyclical law. The periodic table changes from “experience sorting” to “outcome of quantum structure”. [2][5]

Electrolysis and radioactivity

David used electrolytic separations of live metals such as potassium, sodium, calcium and sodium; the Curry couple separated thallium from the asphalt uranium mine. The former relies on electrochemistry and the latter on radioactivity. [3]

Radioactivity decay also brings a reversal: one can become another. The “conversion” of alchemy has been achieved in nuclear physics, but at the cost of a nuclear, not a chemical, reaction. [3][5]

Synth

After the twentieth century, super-uranium element was synthesized mainly through nuclear reactions. It was found that it was no longer "in ore" but "in a accelerator". New element lives are becoming shorter, recognizing the increasing reliance on the Decay chain and statistics. [2] [3]

This also changes the discovery: a core that exists in a few milliseconds and generates a few atoms, does it count as element? The answer for IUPAC is that it requires repeated independent confirmation and meets the nuclide test. [2]

Read three narratives of history.

First,** material narrative**: humans first use element and then know element. Second,** tool narrative**: spectroscopy, electrolysis, mass spectrometry, accelerator, every tool progress expands the cycle table. Thirdly,** the narrative of the concept**: element has changed from “characteristic vectors” to “proton numbers”. [3][5]

The three narratives were not a substitute for each other, but a mutual interpretation. Looking only at “who first discovered” leaves out the changes in tools and concepts. [2] [3]

Criteria found

IUPAC's requirements for “discovery of new element” include: repetitious independent experiments, clear atomic number, complete decay chain, sufficient statistical visibility. No single event, no confirmed energy spectrum or theoretical prognosis is sufficient to enter the periodic table. [2] [3]

Name is another set of procedures: the discovery team proposal, the IUPAC audit, the public review, and finally the official publication. It was found that the name could be used several years away, and even priority disputes arose. [2]

Tool Change Problem

Spectrums transform “invisible element” into readable spectral lines; electrolysis separates live metals from compounds; mass spectrometry allows isotope and atomic weight; accelerators allow super-uranium element. Each tool progresses not only to discover a new element, but also to change the `element' definition. [1][3][4]

So element finds that history is not a list of names, but a history of tools and concepts. When reading it, you have to look at both “who found what” and “how did you know it at the time”. [3][5]

Empty space for periodic tables

Mendeleev left an empty space not for decoration, but for a testable prophecy. The nature of the subsequent discovery of gills, gills, gills and gills was close to predictions, which led to a shift from a ranking of experience to a predictable structure. [2] [3]

The space also reminds us that the periodic tables are not filled at once. Behind each slot is a set of elements that have not yet been separated or synthesized; the process of filling it up often requires new tools. [2][5]

Empty spaces are filled, so the periodic table changes from a piece of paper to a set of reversible theories. [2] [3]

The periodic table is therefore always a revised worksheet. [2]

Revision is part of the scientific work. [2]

Why the periodic table has this shape

Mendeleev forms element by nature, leaves spaces and predicts unknown element; quantum mechanics later proved that those spaces correspond to the fixed capacity of the electron crust. Periodicity has thus evolved from a pattern of experience to a structural inference.
