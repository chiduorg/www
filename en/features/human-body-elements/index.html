The human body element budget: what is inside 70 kg

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The human body is about 70 kg, with the bulk of oxygen, carbon, hydrogen and nitrogen; calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sulphur, sodium, chlorine, magnesium and trace element are divided. Wet weight, dry weight, and grey fraction are different.

Book of 70 kg

One adult weighs about 70 kg, of which about 43 kg of oxygen, 16 kg of carbon, 7 kg of hydrogen, 1.8 kg of nitrogen; these four elements account for most mass. They consist mainly of water, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids, rather than “micro-element”. [1][2] Next are calcium 1 kg, phosphorus 0.8 kg, potassium 0.14 kg, sulphur 0.1 kg, sodium 0.1 kg, chlorine 0.1 kg, magnesium 0.035 kg; iron only a few grams and iodine only mg order of magnitude. [2] [3]

Constant

Calcium and phosphorus are mainly minerals comprising bones and teeth and are also involved in muscle contraction, neurotransmission and energy metabolism. Potassium is mainly in the cell and sodium is mainly outside the cell; their distribution is balanced by membrane levels and body fluids. [2] [4] Carbon is the skeleton, and hydrogen and oxygen are abundant in water; nitrogen is present in proteins and nucleic acids. Treating "election" as only a piece of ash metal leaves out the largest CHNO component of mass. [1][4]

Micromeasurement

Iron is the core of haemoglobin and transports oxygen; iodine is an essential ingredient of thyroid hormones; zinc, copper, manganese, selenium, etc. are involved in enzymes and antioxidation systems. The need is small and does not mean nothing. [2] [3] There is often only a difference between “necessary” and “toxic”. Selenium, fluorine and copper are beneficial when measured, and excessive quantities are harmful; they cannot be supplemented indefinitely because they are trace amounts. [3] [4]

Dry wetness

About 60 percent of the body is water, wet weight and dry weight elementmass scores very different. The wet weight sample is directly ashized and the moisture and volatile components run off first; when reporting “aerobic 65% in humans”, the wet weight or dry weight must be indicated. [2] [4] Ash is reserved for minerals only and leaves carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen. The difference cannot be combined, let alone directly compare "70 kg of iron in humans 4 g" to "10 mg daily iron intake". [2] [3]

How?

The total amount of element can be used for mass spectrometry, atom absorption, emit spectroscopy and neutron activation analysis; the human body composition can also be extrapolated through body composition, image and autopsy data. For each method, there is a deviation in the sample representation. [1][3] There are significant differences among individuals: age, sex, muscle, fat, diet and disease change books. 70 kg is a reference adult, not an average for all. [2] [4]

I'm sorry.

“The human body consists of 60 species of element” is an analytical list and not every element has a known function. Super trace element may be only environmental exposure or contamination and cannot always be written as necessary. [3] [4] Balance is not a static warehouse. Ingestion, discharge, storage and mobilization occur on a daily basis; a measurement as a fixed value for life can be misread with metabolism and disease. [2] [4]

Read deep: element function

Oxygen and hydrogen make up water, participate in transport, temperature regulate and metabolism; carbon make up organic skeletons; nitrogen is present in proteins and nucleic acids; phosphorus is involved in nucleic acids, cell membrane and energy currency ATP; and sulphur is present in sulphate-containing and acne. Macrolevels are not “fills”, and they assume structures and reactions. [1][2]

In-depth reading: Ingestion and discharge

The human body is not a closed container. Sodium, potassium, water with diet, urine, sweat and breathing; iron, iodine, calcium with storage and recycling mechanisms. A single blood or hairmeasurement reflects only a time window and does not represent a lifetime total. [2] [4]

Read deep: Measurement method

The whole body is composed of body components, images, autopsies and biological samples; trace mass spectrometry, atom absorption and neutron activation. Sample contamination, sampling locations and analysis gaps significantly affect trace results, and test limits must be given in the report. [1][3]

In-depth reading: individual differences

Age, sex, muscle volume, fat volume, pregnancy, disease and medicine all change the element books. 70 kg reference adults are easy-to-comparison models, not standard answers for each individual; clinical judgements depend on trends between regions and dynamics. [2] [4]

Continuation: minerals

Calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and fluorine are found mainly in bones and teeth; sodium, potassium, chlorine maintenance fluids and membrane levels; iron, copper, zinc, selenium enzymes and transport. Mineral eliment is not “impurity”, but structural and signal molecules. [2] [3]

Continue: Micromeasurement

The need for iron, iodine, zinc, copper, manganese, selenium, molybdenum, cobalt, etc., is small, but lack of them leads to specific diseases; overdose may also be toxic. The so-called “micro” is demand, not importance. [3] [4]

Continue: Test limits

Tracemeasurement reports detection limits, gaps and recovery rates; sample contamination (blood-mining devices, dust, reagents) may lift the results. The “election spectrum” without mass control cannot be used for clinical purposes. [1][3]
