Weight and mass: what does a scale measure on the Moon?

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Mass is the attribute of the object and the weight is the effect of gravity on the object. Moon is lightened and mass is constant; the reading of the scales depends on whether it is measured or measured.

One word.

Mass is the object's resistance to acceleration and the source of gravity, in kg; the weight is the gravity the object received in a given gravity field, in Newton. [1] Mass does not change place, weight changes with gravity field. Combining the two would lead to the mistake of “Moon gets lighter, so mass gets smaller”.

What is the scale?

Domestic weight scales are usually measured by human pressure on the scales, then determined by the Earth ' s gravity as kg; on Moon, the same spring scales are smaller. The scales are more unknown as mass with known mass and are scaled in equal gravity fields, so readings still indicate mass. [2] Electron scales and spring scales differ from the “heavy” principle of scales.

Moon and Earth

Moon's surface gravity is approximately 1/6 on Earth. A 60 kg astronaut weighs approximately 588 N on Earth and 98 N on Moon; mass remains 60 kg. [1] [2] If the scales are based on the Earth ' s gravity, the Moon reading will show about 10 kg, but this is not the mass ' s smaller, but the mark is wrong.

Engineering and daily usage

“This steel weighs 5 tons”, usually referred to as mass, not gravity; engineering specifications are indicated as “mass” and “mass”. [2] In space, elevators, bridges and gymnastics, it must be clear whether kg or N, otherwise the thrust, load and inertia calculations can be confused.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “mass is mass times 9.8, so mass is mass”. 9.8 m/s2 is only the near-gravity acceleration near the Earth ' s surface, which changes with latitude, altitude and celestial bodies. [1] Another misconception is to treat “deweight” as “no mass”; in orbit, mass is still, just free falling away from support.

What's the reading of the scales?

Electron scales are pressure or mutation, and gravitational acceleration is mass; readings deviate from real mass if used in a acceleration elevator or tilted ground. [1] The scale is not a “direct reading mass”, but a reading force.

Weightlessness doesn't mean no mass.

Astronauts in the free faller are “weightless” because gravity is offset by acceleration and mass is still present; inertia, physical capacity and chemical reactions remain unchanged. [2] Weightlessness is a local reference phenomenon.

Moon and Mars

Moon is about 1.62 m/s2, Mars is about 3.71 m/s2 and Earth is about 9.81 m/s2; the same object weighs about 1/6 on Moon and about 2/5 on Mars. [3] Mass is unchanged, weight changes.

The scales in the elevator.

When elevators accelerate, the scales are larger than static weight; when the drop is accelerated, the reading is smaller; and when the drop is zero. [1] These three scenarios are the same, with different readings.

How's the scale?

Electron scales map voltage signals to mass using known code calibration; different gravity environments are recalibration or conversion. [2] On Moon's scales, readings are wrong, but mass are the same. [3]

Gravity Fields and Units

The BIPM kg definition and NASA planetary fact sheets provide references to mass and gravitational acceleration; the same object weighs on different bodies by local g conversion. [4] [5]

In project calculations, kg is mass, N is force and cannot be considered the same unit. The weight equipment is read of force or force-related effects and is rated as mass; in exchange for a gravity environment, recaliberation is necessary.

Mass / Planets & small bodies

Moon

Moon is the only natural satellite of the Earth, with a total mass of about 7.346×10²² kg, or about one-one-first point of the Earth. The laser moon follows the Moon orbiter and gives about 0.01 per cent relative to uncertainty, which is one of the most accurate measurements in the body mass.

Mass / Planets & small bodies

Earth

The Earth's total mass is approximately 5.972×10²⁴ kg, which contains the atmosphere and oceans. Values are combined by satellite orbit tracking and gravitational field model, relative to uncertainty approximately 1×10⁻³, and are mainly limited by the accuracy of the gravitational constant G.

Mass / Stars & compact objects

Sun

1.989×10³⁰ kg, or 1 solar mass, of the Sun's total mass, is often used as a conversion unit for the entire mass ladder. The values are proposed by the International Astronomical Union for nominal solarmass parameters GM☉ divided by the gravitational constant G conversion, relative to uncertainty approximately 1×10⁻⁴.
