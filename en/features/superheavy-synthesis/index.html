How superheavy elements are made: writing new elements into the periodic table

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The super-heavy element was not extracted from the ore; rather, it was a heavy ion beam impacting target, integrating the atom nuclear into a very unstable new nuclear, which was recognized by the decay chain. Production is atom and life is often only ms to ms.

Why not add a few protons?

This post is part of our special coverage Global Voices 2011. To turn 113 into 114, we can't rely on a chemical reaction, which only moves Electron, not atom. Another nuclear atom must be brought closer, the cologne must be overcome and the number of protons increased. [1][2]

The heavy ion accelerator accelerates a nucleus (e.g. calcium-48 or thallium-83) to one point near the speed of light, hitting a heavy target (e.g. californium249 or curium248). A very small number of collisions will bring the two nuclear elements together and form a new composite nuclear. [2]

Once you're integrated, you're gonna be fine.

New nuclei are usually in a stimulating state, evaporating several neutrons and then entering a very short-lived hypernuclear. It could be alpha decay or spontaneous fission in milliseconds, seconds or even shorter time. [2] [3]

The nuclear could not be "seed" in the experiment, but could only be pushed back through the decay chain: the probe records the energy of the alpha particles and the time, gradually reducing the number of atomic number and mass of the parent core. A complete decay chain constitutes a single discovery. [3]

Cold integration and heat integration

“Cool integration” uses lighter beams and heavy targets, and composite nuclear stimulation is low, but the integration is small; “thermal integration” uses heavier beams, generating high energy, evaporating more neutrons and not large. The two routes are complementary and cover different element areas. [2]

The cross-section is often described as a “skin target” order of magnitude, meaning that only one reaction per10⁻³⁶ square centimetre is possible. The experiment took months or even years to accumulate several events. [2] [3]

How do you confirm a new element?

The IUPAC recognition requires multiple sets of independent experiments, a clear decay chain, duplicateability and consistency with other nuclides. Single incidents are insufficient; statistical visibility and contextual exclusion are equally important. [1][5]

Upon confirmation, it was found that the team could propose a name and symbol and enter the periodic form only after IUPAC review and public comment. The name is not “discovery”, but an international procedure. [1]

113, 115, 117, 118

Nihonium (113), moscovium (115), Tennessine (117), Oganesson (118) are recent additions. Their chemical properties are mostly derived from cyclical trends and relativistic calculations rather than macro sample experiments. [4][5][6]

The Electron structure calculation for oganesson shows that 7p is very close to the power levels of 7s and 6d and that the relativity effect may make it less than typical rare gases. This type of “situation in a periodic table, which is not typical in nature”, is the value of the overweight element study. [4] [7]

Stable Island is a theory.

The “Stability Island” assumption is that some proton numbers and neutron combinations (e.g., near 114, 120) may have a longer lifespan. It comes from the hull layer model, but has not yet been tested to the extent that it can be called an “island”. [3][5]

Currently synthetic overweights have a much shorter lifespan than seconds; even near the stable areas predicted by theory, yields are so low that chemical experiments are difficult. It is an excessive extrapolation to consider “stabilized islands” as a repository that already exists. [3]

Three differences in reading overweight

First, the distinction** found** and named**: the discovery was confirmed by an experiment and named IUPAC. Second, the distinction between atomic number and mass: proton numbers Third, distinction** experimental data** and relativity**: many chemical properties are still theoretical projections. [1 [2][4]

To separate the three layers, the upper right corner of the periodic table is no longer a “filling grid”, but an extreme set of nuclear physics experiments. [3] [7]

The limits of the detector

The shorter the life of the hypernuclear, the more accidental the signal is. The detector's ability to record both energy, time and location and to link the decay chain; background noise, beam instability and detector efficiency influence the judgement that “show a few events”. [2] [3]

A single experiment may yield only a few candidate events, and statistical visibility is therefore key to detection. It is not enough to “see a chain”, but rather to exclude random matches, confirm the relationship between the parent and the sub-nucleus and repeat them in independent experiments. [1][3]

Why is it worth it?

The study of overweight element tests nucleus model, relativity effects and quantum chemistry. Their presence is extremely short, but it tells us how the atom nuclear is organized under extreme proton numbers. [3] [4]

They also provide an experimental constraint on “the end point of the element cycle table”. For every more proton, the cologne is stronger in exclusion and the stabilization window is narrower; the upper right corner of the periodic table is not an endless blank, but a soundy cut from nuclear stability. [2][5]
