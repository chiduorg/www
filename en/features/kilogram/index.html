The kilogram: from a metal cylinder to a constant

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Kiloton was once a cylinder in Paris, changed from Planc constant 2019 to definition. The new decinition does not change the size of the kg but anchors it at the generic constant.

Old

Since 1889, the international kg prototype is a platinum alloy cylinder, which is kept near Paris. National prototypes are regularly matched to it, but the cylinders may be slightly altered by cleaning, wear or adsorbing. [1] This makes “kg” dependent on a physical rather than a natural constant.

Newdefinition

On 20 May 2019, the SI amendment entered into force. The kg is obtained from the fixed value of the Plank constant h =6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J.s, combined with the definition of seconds and metres, by the Wattsian Equivalence Experiment. [2] This allows the independent re-emergence of kg in national metrology institutes in China, the United States, Germany, etc.

How does the Watt scale work?

Watt scales operate in two modes: one by balancing gravity with electromagnetic force, and the other by allowing the coil to move in the magnetic field to generate induction voltage. Measurement voltage, current, velocity and gravitational acceleration can create equations between mechanical and electric power, which can trace mass back to Planck constants. [2][3] It is not a standard ball, but a physical relationship.

The size hasn't changed.

The new decinition, after multi-year international comparisons, ensures that the size of the kg corresponds to that of the old prototype within the limits of uncertainty; the daily use of the kg does not become heavy or light. [1][2] The change is not the size of the unit.

Common misunderstandings

A common misconception is that “kg is no longer a basic unit”. The kg remains the basic unit of SI, except for the conversion of the base from physical to constant. [3] Another misunderstanding is that “only physicists are affected”; from food labels to aviation loads, all massspaceability is eventually connected to this set of definitions.

The chain.

From the National Metrics Institute ' s original standards to work standards, codes and commercial scales, each level of caliber has uncertainty. [2] Massmeasurement depends on the entire chain, not a single scale.

Why change

International prototypes vary slightly by adsorbing, washing and wear; constants lock kg in natural constants and are theoretically more stable. [3] The cost is complex and costly.

Why doesn't everyday feel the same?

International prototype mass changes are at μgorder of magnitude, where daily scales are much lower in resolution; but precision measurement, space and drug research and development require more stable benchmarks. [1] Constants transfer precision from "object" to "experiment".

From Source to Constant

The SI handbook of BIPM gives kg of definition and actual recovery pathways; national measuring courts use the Watt scales, X-ray crystal density method and Josephsen effects independently. [4]

NIST CODATA gives a recommended value for the Planck and Avogardro constants, which are the basis for the value of the kg definition. [5] The chain traced laboratory mass back to constants, not Paris cylinders.

Revert to Match

After 2019, national metrology courts independently reproduced kg of the Watt scale, X-ray crystal density method and the Josephsen effect, and then validated each other through international critical matching. [4]

Recurrence is not “the creation of a standard kg”, but rather “the detection of a mass by definition”. Different approaches differ from results to determine consistency between national standards. [5] Calibration of daily scales, which ultimately goes back to constants along this chain.

Water molecule

One water molecule consists of two hydrotoms and one oxygen atom, and the molecular mass 18.0153 u, combined with 0QX kg. Its mass is slightly smaller than the sum of three isolated atoms, and the difference is the molecular combination capacity.

Mass / Planets & small bodies

Earth

The Earth's total mass is approximately 5.972×10²⁴ kg, which contains the atmosphere and oceans. Values are combined by satellite orbit tracking and gravitational field model, relative to uncertainty approximately 1×10⁻³, and are mainly limited by the accuracy of the gravitational constant G.
