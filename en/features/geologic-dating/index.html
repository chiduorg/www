How geologic time is measured: from radiometric dating to astronomical tuning

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

The Earth is 4.54 billion years old, not a single figure, but by zircon, meteorite, statigraphy and orbital cycles. Radioactivity is set to be of absolute age, statigraphy learns order, astronomically harmonizes to high resolution rhythms.

First order, then age.

The earliest instrument of geology is not isotope, but ** statigraphy sequence**: younger sediment rock is covered in older sediment rock, cutting points to a later intrusive body than the surrounding rock, and Fossil combination gives relative order. This logic remains today the skeleton of all absolute ages. [3]

There is no relative order, a isotope age is just a number, and it is not known which level or incident it responds to. A combination of statigraphy, fossil, ash and magnetic polarity is needed to put the word “years” back into the “sequence”. [2] [3]

How does radioactivity work?

Uranium-238 decay is lead-206, half-life is about 4.468 billion years; carbon-14 half-life is about 5,700 years. The ratio of the parent to the subbody, plus the decay constant, can be decimated from the mime: [ Chuckles ] It's all right.
Provided that the system is not lost or acquired after formation. [1][3]

Zircon is the ideal object: it absorbs uranium at the time of crystallization and excludes lead, which is then very resistant to erosion; its U-Pb system can give the crystal age. The zircon of Jack Hills, Australia, is about 4.4 billion years old, suggesting that central crust and liquid water may exist at an early age. [4]

What about the Earth?

The Earth itself does not have a piece of the original rock left. The usual method is meteorite: when the solar system is formed, some Meteorite retains its original U-Pb age, giving about 4.54 billion years; Moonrock provides cross-examination with the oldest minerals on Earth. [1][3]

This is not a “direct measurement of the Earth's rocks”, but rather a “starting of matter from the same period of the solar system”. The Earth ' s earliest rock experienced melting, deterioration and plate cycle, and original records were difficult to keep; zircon was a small number of survivors. [4]

Young Sediment and Carbon-14

Carbon-14 for organic and archaeological samples within tens of thousands of years. It is produced in the atmosphere by universe rays that balance living bodies with atmospheric exchange; it stops exchange after death and starts decay. [2]

The limits of carbon-14 are clear: over 50,000 years, the remaining amount is too small; ocean reservir, Fossil fuel contamination, and sample pre-treatment will affect the results. It can't be used to measure dinosaurs, let alone to measure the Earth. [2] [3]

Alteration and high resolution

In the younger sediment sequences, the Milankovic cycle brand can be used as a “symphonograph”: orbital inclinations, age differentials and eccentricity changes in the sun, leaving a cyclical sediment layer. Aligning the age of radioactivity with these cycles can increase resolution to the millennium or even less. [3]

This is the "astronomical harmonization": it's not a telescope that looks directly at the past, it's a cycle in astronomical calculations that goes to caliberstratigraphy's titme axis. It requires continuous sedimentation, reliable ancient climate indicators and a clear phase relationship. [3]

Where did Error come from?

The first is ** closed temperature**: the subbody is not retained until the mineral cools to a certain temperature; the cooling age is measured, not necessarily the crystal age. Second,** inheritance and loss**: the sample may be mixed into older zircon or lead may be lost in later erosion. The third is decay constant: values are small, but over billions of years, Uror is magnified. [1][3]

So a "4.54 billion years" usually takes ± millions of years order of magnitudeer, instead of being precise. Different methods, different samples, and space given by different laboratories are the value of cross-testing. [1][3]

Read the numbers to the time axis.

The geologic age is not an equal-speed straight line. The outbreak of cold martial arts, the extinction of the Second Era, and the impact of the end of the White Age were short events inserted in the long-time calm; in the linear time axis, they were crushed to a stitch. [3][5]

When reading the geological age, look first at which statigraphy it belongs to, which minerals it belongs to, which iisotope system; and then at Error and the conditions of closure. It is easy to mix “order” and “age” into one thing, bearing in mind “many billion years”. [2] [4]

How much does a rock record?

A single rock is not a single event, but a set of “events”: crystallization, cooling, deterioration, erosion, lifting, exposure. Each event may open or shut down a isotope system; different segments of the mineral records differ. [1][3]

Zircon is able to retain U-Pb age because its crystals collect uranium, remove lead and become difficult to modify at a later stage. Other minerals may be more vulnerable to loss of the subbodies and give age closer to cooling or deforming events rather than raw crystals. [4]

So “rock age” is best written as “a mineral, a system, an event age”. Combining the multiple ages of a single rock is one of the most common and easily avoided mistakes of geologic age. [1][3]

Uranium-238 Half-Life

The half-life of uranium-238 is approximately 44.68 billion years. It shows how long half of this nuclide decay takes, is the nuclide's lifetime, is not the moment of a universe incident, and does not mean that uranium-238 will disappear as a whole on a given day.
