Field measurement and error: what to do without a laboratory

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

In the field, Measurement faces the representation of temperature, wind, vibration, electricity and sampling. Calibration, repetition, references and records determine the credibility of results better than an expensive instrument.

There's no lab in the field.

The laboratory can control the temperature, humidity and vibration; not in the field. The wind shakes the tripod, the sun warms the sensors, the rain and snow alters the reflectivity, and electricity fluctuations allow the clock to float. The measurement programme must first list these interferences. [1][2] There are also no “standard samples” in the field. The caliber is to be accompanied by a review, a zero-point and Quantification check and a record of the calibertime; without data recorded in the caliber, it is difficult to judge the drift afterwards. [1][5]

Caliberation and Reference

Caliberation does not transfer instruments to its own satisfaction, but compares readings with a traceability standard. GPS receivers rely on satellite clocks and orbital parameters, altimeters rely on local gravity and base surfaces, temperature meters rely on ice point/triple phase or standard platinum electrical resistance. [2][5] The reference should be clear: in relation to what benchmarks, in what time, in what method. Altitude, air pressure and temperature can all change with time; “accurate to centimetre” without reference is meaningless. [1][4]

Random and System

Repeat measurement can estimate randomerror: readings spread around averages. The systemeror does not disappear because of multiple measurements: the scale of the ruler, the sensor has a fixed deviation, the sampling point is always on the sun, and the overall result is biased. [1][3] The field systems are often derived from “selection points”. Soil moisture is studied, sampled only flat; urban heat island is studied, measured only during the day; these options include space deviations in the conclusions. [3] [4]

Environment and sampling

Temperature, humidity, wind and radiation affect sensors and subjects simultaneously. Temperature must be measured at a high altitude inside and off the ground; wind speed must be measured at an open area and at a standard altitude; precipitation depends on the shielding and wind loss of the rainbox. [3] [4] Sample representation is more critical than the precision of the instrument. One dot does not represent a river valley, and once-in-a-time operation does not represent a season; it indicates sample numbers, space layouts and time windows. [1][4]

Record with uncertancy

The records shall contain original readings, units, times, position, instrument number, caliber status, weather and operator. Without one, data cannot be independently recalculated. [1][5] Uncertancy requires layers: instrument resolution, caliberuncertancy, repetition, sampling deviations and model assumptions. At the time of the final report, uncertability should not be written as “0.1%” without indicating the coverage factor and level of confidence. [1][2]

I'm sorry.

The objective of field-based medical action is not “absolutely correct”, but “repeated and comparable under given conditions”. Precision as anaccuracy or reliability of a success as a method is commonly misinterpreted. [2] [3] If conditions cannot be controlled, conditions are included in the conclusions; if the sample is not representative, the conclusions are reduced. Honesty is more important than pursuing a beautiful number. [1][4]

Read more: Error Budget

All of them are listed and the impact on the final outcome is estimated. Instrument resolution may be only a small fraction, with sampling representativeness or caliberization drift likely to dominate the total uncertainty; without budget, it is easy to misdirect effort. [1][2]

In-depth reading: sample design

There are conditions for random sampling, layer sampling and system grids. In the case of studies of urban head island, points should be placed between different types of land use and day and night; in the case of studies of rivers, upstream, downstream and tributaries should be covered. Where can the conclusions of the sample design be extended? [3] [4]

In-depth reading: data recording

The original records should be recalculated to allow others to recalculate: time, coordinates, instruments, scale, caliberation, environment, operators and anomalies. Aftercares can easily introduce memory deviations; photographs, logs and automatic records can be cross-checked. [1][5]

In-depth reading: reporting modalities

The report should distinguish between precision, accuracy and uncertancy, giving sample numbers and scope of application. If only “measurement succeeds” is written without the condition, the reader cannot judge whether the results can be used in another location or season. [2] [4]

Continue: calibration chain

Caliberation is traced back to national or international standards; at least zero points, Quantities and repeated inspections are performed at the site. The calibration records are not recalculated unless they contain a standard number, environment and date. [1][5]

Continue: sample layout

The sample points cover space and time changes in the target variable; there are differential structures for random, layered and grid design. Select only the points of “accessibility” and the conclusions are dominated by differences in accessibility. [3] [4]

Continue: error synthesis

The randomerror is reduced by the increase in the number of repeats, and not the systemeror. Distinguishing between types is necessary for the synthesis of uncertancy, and it is not possible to average all perrors as random perrror. [1][2]

Continue: Original records

Records shall be independently recalculated: readings, units, times, coordinates, instruments, scales, caliberation, environment, operators and anomalies. Data for missing items cannot be judged later on. [1][5]

Continue: report soundary

The report is to write applicable locations, seasons, sample numbers and uncertainty; and to describe a field event as a “universal pattern” would cross the sample baseary. [2] [4]

Something to look back on.

The credibility of field-based measurement is determined by the caliberization chain, sampling design, error budget and records, and not only by the price of the instrument. The calibration must be retroactive, with at least zero points, Quantities and repeated checks performed at the site; sampling must cover space and Time changes, not just convenience points. The randomerror is reliable, and the system is not; the report distinguishes between precision, accuracy and uncertancy. The original records must be recalculated and the conclusions should indicate the applicable location, season and sample numbers. When conditions are uncontrollable, they are included in the conclusions, rather than pretending that they do not exist. [1 [2][3][4]
