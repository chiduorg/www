The hottest and coldest in the universe: the two ends of temperature

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

UNiverse microwave background is about 2.725 K, which is the cold base of the universe; Planck temperature is about 1.4×10^32 K, which is the upper limit of the available physics. Between the two ends, supernova and neutron cores are only local extremes.

Cold bottom is not zero king.

Universe microwave background is a legacy of early universe radiation, which today is about 2.725 K. It's full of the sky, and it's the most even hot bath of the UNiverse. [1][4]

2.725 K is not the “minimum temperature of unevere”. Laboratories can be even lower in milligrams; some of the insulated areas in unverse may be colder. But as a big scale background, CMB gives a cold bottom that exists almost everywhere. [1]

The hot top is Plankteperature.

Planck temperature about1.417×10³² K, made up of Plank constants, light speeds, gravity constants and Boltzmann constants. It is not a measured temperature, but rather of magnitude of existing physical frameworks. [5] [6]

Under this temperature, the heat energy of particles is the same as that of Planck, and the quantum effect of gravity cannot be ignored. Universe may have experienced this at the very early post-Big Bang stage, but no direct observation can return to that moment. [7] [8]

Local extreme: supernova and neutron

10⁹ order of magnitude can be created by a10⁹ order of magnitude;10⁹-ZXXXQPH2QZXK, depending on cooling model and physical equations, may reach within the neutron. [2] [3]

These temperatures are several times higher than the core of the Sun, but still far below Plankteperature. They are “local hot spots”, not “universe as a whole”. Considering local extremes as the average universe temperature is the most common error in reading such numbers. [2]

It's radiation, not a temperature.

CMB temperature is derived from the radiation spectrum: COBE/FIRAS finds almost perfect bold print, temperature approximately 2.725 K. [1] Star and supernova temperature, inverted by spectrometry, spectrality and model. [2] [3]

Plankteperature has no teleperature. It is a combination of theoretical quantum, bound by high-energy physics and universe science model; to say that “universe once reached Planperature” is a statement within model, not a direct reading. [5] [7]

Symmetry and asymmetries at both ends

The cold end has a clear observation anchor: CMB. The hot end does not have a direct access anchor for observation: Planck temperature appears only at an extremely early stage in unevere and requires quantum gravity theory to describe it. [1][5]

This is the asymmetry between the two ends: we can see the cold end's residual, but we can only extrapolate the heat threshold. One is passive reception of light, and one is the limit of theory. [1][7]

Read extreme temperature

First, whether it is ** background or ** partial ; second, whether it is ** reality or theory**; and third, what model assumptions it relies on. CMB is the real context, supernovas are models, Plankteperature is theorder of theory. [1][3][5]

To mark these three points, the "universe is the hottest" is not a record that can be measured with temperature. [2][8]

Early universe temperature

In the first few minutes after Big Bang, universsetemperature was so high that a nuclear reaction could occur that it formed light element; in the future, temperature was higher, neutral atoms were non-existent and photons collided frequently with matter. [2] [7]

Temperature declines withtime, not so simple as “universe cooling”, but rather space expansion stretches the radiation wavelength. The CMB 2.725 K is the result of the expansion of the same radiation field until today. [1][4]

Why does the cold end have 2.7 K?

Even with the removal of galaxy, Star and dust, universe is filled with CMB photons. Any object exchanges radiation with this background in universe; to be reduced to a lower temperature than CMB, active refrigeration or insulation is required. [1]

So the "universe is the coldest" depends on whether you ask about the background, the lab or the object. CMB gives a cold base that is almost everywhere, but not all local areas can get colder. [1][7]

Unit of temperature

2.725 K is approximately equal to−270.4 °C; ZXXQPH1QZX K is almost unchanged because the addition of 273.15 is negligible on this order of magnitude. [5] [8]

The difference between kelvin and oct is important when reading extreme temperature only at the low-temperature end; what really matters at the high-temperature end is the order of magnitude, not the number of decimal places. A number of10⁹ K, Error may be a source of magnitude, written as "billion degrees". [2][5]

Order of magnitude

The number of extremes, first of all, must be kept of precision. The CMB 2.725 K can be written to mkelvin, and Planck temperature can only be written to order of magnitude; both must be placed in the same table, and must be marked as “actual” and “theory”. [1][5]
