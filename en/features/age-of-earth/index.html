The age of the Earth: how 4.6 billion years was measured

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

It's not from the literature, but it's about 4.54 billion years from the radioactiveisop clock, meteorite and the oldest minerals. It has a history of methods ranging from “sea salinity” “cooling rate” to modern isotope measurements.

Four-six billion years is not a guess.

Age of the Earth is usually about 4.54 billion years, often rounded to 4.6 billion years. This figure is not derived separately from “how long it takes to cool the Earth” or “the rate of accumulation of sea salinity”, but rather from radioactiveiisotope, meteorite age and the oldest earth minerals interacting with each other. [1][2]

It's not exactly a birthday. Earth formation is a cumulative process lasting millions to tens of millions of years, with no single “day zero”. The term "age of the Earth " is usually used to refer to the moment when raw materials of the solar system condensate with the Earth ' s body, rather than to a specific rock crystal. [2] [3]

Radioactivity clock

Radioactivity uses the parent iisotope as a pattern of fixed half-liftecay. It is possible to calculate the size of the time since the mineral crystal is crystallized, provided that the initial submersible ratio, the Matrix and the submersible content are known. Systems such as uranium - lead, thorium - and thorium - each have a temperature zone and a time range. [1][4]

The “fixed half-life” is the basis for the reliability of the clock. half-life is not significantly affected by ordinary temperature, pressure or chemical state, so once minerals are formed and remain closed, the parent-child ratio is like a timer that continues to move. High-temperature condensation or lateral fluid modification will reset the clock and the results of the measurements must be interpreted in conjunction with the mineral and geological background. [4] [5]

Zircon and Moon

The Earth ' s surface is constantly eroded, dived and melted, and the oldest complete rock is difficult to preserve. The oldest zircon gave about 4.4 billion years of age, indicating that there was an environment at that time in which the water was involved with liquid water. zircon is resistant to weather and variability and is a few minerals that can retain early Earth information. [2][5]

Moon sample provides another anchor. The moon rock brought back by the Apollo mission was measured around 4.5 billion years in multiple isotope systems, close to the Earth's main body's time. Moon is considered to have been formed by the accumulation of debris after a major collision, so its age can help to limit the early history of the Earth ' s collision. [3] [6]

Why not 6000 years?

Reading of the Earth for thousands of years usually comes from considering religious texts, historical records or the length of human civilization as the entire history of the planet. The geological records give a ratio of rock, meteorite to isotope, not a literature year. The answers to the questions are different and cannot be directly subtracted. [1][2]

Another misread is to refer to “the oldest rock age” as “age of the Earth”. The Earth was formed earlier than the oldest rock because the early crust had been remelted or subdued. Meteorite and Moon samples provide the missing section of the Earth's Rock, while the oldest zircon pushes forward the emergence of the time. [2][3][5]

I'm sorry.

4.54 billion years usually carry about 1 per cent of uncertainty, or tens of millions of years. This range is derived from the Meteoriteiisotope system, the initial iiiisotopepeope hypothesis forming a time joint binding with the solar system, not a single pointer for the Measurement instrument. The values given in different studies will fluctuate between hundreds of thousands and millions of years. [1][3]

Read this figure to distinguish between the “Earth formation age” “the oldest mineral age” and the “Moon formation age”. They each have different events and different year targets. Considering one of them as Earth's birthday would crush the complex formation process into a non-existent moment. [2][6]

Stratigraphy and Fossil

It's an absolutetime framework for statigraphy. Radioactivity measures the absolute geologic age of volcanic ash, intrusion rock and meteorite impact layers in statigraphy; biotitigraphy provides relative order to magnetic statigraphy. In combination, there is the expression “how many million years ago a certain era began”. [1][5]

Fossil itself is rarely directly measured as it does not contain suitable radioactivity minerals. It is more common to measure the age of volcanic ash up and down in Fossil, and to define the relative position of Fossilgeological age. This “frossil” method is more reliable than a direct test, and explains why a statigraphygeological age is often accompanied by a partition. [4] [5]

You know, age of the Earth and UNiverse

Earth is about 4.54 billion years, universe about 13.8 billion years, and the sun about 4.6 billion years. The age of the Earth is close to that of the Sun, because both form the collapse of the same molecular cloud; the age of the Earth is about three times that of the Earth. Put these numbers on the same time axis to see that “the history of the Earth” is only one third of the history of universe. [2] [3]

But the numbers on this axis are different in accuracy. Universe age is dependent on universe science model, age of the Earth is dependent on meteorite and isotope systems, and sun age is dependent on star evolution model. They can't be treated as being of the same precision as "billion years". [1][3]

Common misunderstandings

The first misperception is that “age of the Earth is the oldest rock age”. The oldest rock has only a partial record of over 4 billion years, and Earth's body forms earlier. The second misperception is that “radioactivity can be measured by any stone”. The sample must remain closed, otherwise the clock will be reset. [4] [5]

The third misconception is that “4.54 billion years is the exact value”. It's still an estimate with uncertainty. The fourth misconception is that “all meteorites are older than Earth”. Meteorite comes from different Matrixs of the solar system and has experienced some later heating and collisions, not all of which are equal to the moment when the solar system is formed. [1][6]

A way to read the geologic form.

See how many billions of years it has been measured: meteorite, zircon, moon rock, ash, or model inference. And ask what clock it is, how closed it is, how big it is. Finally, it returns it to the position of Stratigraphy, confirming whether it is absolute age or relative order. [1][4][5]

The power of age of the Earth lies not in giving an integer number, but in linking Rock, Meteorite, Moon and the Sootope system into a verifiable chain of evidence. Each link has a error, but the chain is the same: the Earth's history is much longer than human civilization and much longer than the oldest rock. [2] [3]
