From cell to body: how ten orders of magnitude make a person

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

One cell is about 10-30 micrometres, one man is about 1.7 metres, close to five times of magnitude. The human body is not a simple pile of cells, but a multilayered structure that divides, cell external matrices and cycles.

Cell's scale.

The size of the human cell varies widely: red blood cell about 7-8 micrometres, common body cell about 10-30 micrometres, neuron can extend a few metres, egg cell about 0.1 mm. [1] Cell does not have a hard basey, and the diameter under the microscope depends on the direction of fixed, dyeing and measurement; it cannot be represented by a single number for allcells.

Number and division

The human body is made up of about 30 trillion golls, an estimate that changes with the method. They are not exactly the same: the same genome expresses different genes in different tissues, forming neural, muscular, upper skin, immune, etc. [2] Distinction is not “to become another species”, but a difference in genetic regulation procedures.

Multi-level organizations

Cell forms tissues, tissues form organs, organs form systems. Cell external matrix, blood vessels and nerves connect the cell; without these “noncell parts”, the cell cannot maintain the body alone. [1][3] The body mass therefore does not amount to a simple combination of cellmas, but also includes water, glue, minerals and cell external fluid.

If you zoom in 20 micrometres to 1 millimetre, the human body becomes 1.7 m; conversely, you shrink to 1 millimetre, and the cell is only about 12 micrometres. [1] This type of conversion helps to understand why Cell needs microscopes, and the human body can use a ruler.

Common misunderstandings

The common misconception is that “the body is all the same, just different”. Cell type, size, lifetime and genetic expression vary widely; neuron and red blood cell structures are almost non-interchangeable. [2] Another misconception is that “cells are bigger and better”; surface area and volume trade with restricted substances, most of which remain small scale.

Why can't the cell grow?

Cell exchanges oxygen, nutrients and waste by surface. Volume increases three times in length, surface area increases in a double; the larger the cell, the smaller the surface available in the unit of volume. [1] Most cells maintain a micrometer class in order to make diffusion fast enough; neuron and muscular cells break through through a long shape or transport system.

How do you get 30 trillion?

There is no accurate census of the number of human cells. Common estimates divide human volume by cell average volume, or DNA total mass by individual cell DNA mass, with 30 trillion in order of magnitude. [2] Different methods give a difference of several trillions of dollars; non-bacterium and cell genres are not included in “human cell”.

Division is not a simple pile of cell.

Cell external matrices, blood vessels, lymphs and neurons form the framework of the organization; it cannot be maintained without vascular oxygen and nutrients. An organ is a functional combination of various tissues, while a system is a collaboration of organs. [3] The human mass therefore includes water, glue, minerals and cell external fluid, which cannot be explained by the quantity of cell alone.

From micrometres to metres.

A cell of 20 μm, magnified 50 times to 1 mm; a man 1.7 μm, 85,000 μm. If the cell is reduced to 1 cm, the body will be up to 8.5 km. [1][3] This type of conversion indicates that the human body is not “a big cell”, but a multilayer scale organizational system.

Cell type and lifetime

Red blood cell has no cell core and has a lifetime of approximately 120 days; neuron can survive for decades; and intestinal cell has been updated several days. [1] The size of the cell, the ability to divide and the metabolism ratio vary greatly, and “human quell” is not a homogeneous category.

Drycell and Update

Blood dry cell, intestinal dry cell and skin base bottom cell maintain tissue renewal; dry cell split needs to balance self-renewal and fragmentation. [2] Ageing is often associated with cancer associated with drycell regulation, DNA injury accumulation.

Unhuman.

The human intestinal, skin and mouth carry a large number of micro-organisms in quantities comparable to the human cyll order of magnitude; they are involved in digestion, immunization and metabolism. [3] The “human body” is a host-microbiology community, and the cell count must indicate whether microorganisms are included.

A common reverse example.

"The body has 30 trillion dollars" is not an exact constant. The number of cell units varies according to age, weight, fat and microbes; different methods of counting give different results. It is used as an estimate of risk of magnitude rather than as an indicator of medical examination.

If there's only one thing to remember,

The human body is a nested system of ten order of magnitude: molecules, cells, tissues, organs, individuals. Each layer has new constraints and cannot be attributed directly to a single cell.

Put the numbers back in scale.

One cell 20 micrometre, 1.7 metres, 85,000 times the difference; zoom in the cell to 1 centimetre, and the body will be 8.5 kilometres high, above Everest.

Cell is subject to diffusion restrictions, and the increase in volume has led to a decrease in the ratio of surface area to volume, which is not kept up with the exchange of materials; and Docell's body cycle, breathing and division of labour. 30 trillion golls do not zoom in on a big goll, but organize different bell types.
