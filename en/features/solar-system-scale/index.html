How large is the solar system: a scale ledger from the Sun to the Oort Cloud

Machine-assisted draft · native review pending

Neptune's orbit, the Kuiper Belt, the top of the heliosphere and the Oort Cloud gave four different “sun system bubbles”. From 30 AU to 100,000 AU, the same sentence can be more than 3,000 times different.

Set a ruler.

None of the solar system is painted on paper. The ruler most commonly used in astronomy is astronomical unit AU, which is equivalent to the average distance from the Earth to the Sun, approximately1.496×10⁸ metres. The inside planets, the outer planets, the Kuiper Belt and the Oort Cloud were placed in the same table in the AU mass. [4]

Neptune's average orbital radius is about 30 AU and a turn around about 165 years. [1] If the “solar system” is understood to be the face of the planet's orbit, it has a diameter of about 60 AU; the9×10¹² metres in knowledge, is exactly the sort of planetary orbit. [4]

Four boundaries.

The first one is** planetary area**: eight planets are within about 30 AU, and Neptune has ice bodies like Pluto. The second boundary is** the Kuiper Belt**: roughly from 30 AU to 50 AU, the main source of short-term comets. [2]

The third roundary is ** the heliosphere **: the solar wind and the interstellar medium reach pressure balance, and the Traveler 1 crosses around 121 AU. [3] This is not gravitational, but the sun's gravity continues to extend even further; it is just the edge of the “sun wind bubble”.

The fourth boundary is Ortwyn: a hypothetical spherical comet bank with a potential for thousands of UUs and an outer edge of 10⁵ AU order of magnitude. [2] It is very difficult to be pushed back directly by the orbit of the long-cycle comet. If the Ort cloud is included, the diameter of the solar system is more than 3,000 times larger than that of the planetary area.

Why is size so different?

The difference comes from a security error, not from a medical error. The planetary orbit is a kinetic structure, the heliosphere top is a plasma structure, and the Altair cloud is a statistical extrapolation of comets. And the three of them paint a different physical size, but they don't overlap. [2] [3]

There is also the question of scale: the gravitational effects of the Sun are not suddenly zero at a precise distance. Other stars and galactic tidal fields are involved; the so-called “margin of the solar system” is a zone of gradual transition in gravity, not a one-size-fits-all sphere.

Put the solar system in universe.

The solar system is a small unit in the galaxy. Earth to the Sun 1 AU, Sun to the nearest Star around 2.7×10⁵ AU; Galaxy around10⁹ order of magnitude; and cobservation UNiverse is again about 10⁵ more in diameter than Galaxy about. [5]

And that's why Flying out of the solar system sounds like the last stop, actually leaving a thin planetary disc. At the rate of Traveler 1, it will take thousands of years to reach the inner edge of the Altair cloud; to the nearest Star, it will be tens of thousands of years.

The way to read this book.

Let's ask, "Who are you talking about? If the other side says “60 AU in the diameter of the solar system”, it's planetary orbit; if it's “100,000 AU in the radius of the solar system”, it's the upper limit estimate for counting the Alt cloud. [2] [4] Both numbers are not erroneous, but cannot be placed in the same comparative table without reference to medical condition.

Finally, remember that the presence and scope of Altyun still depend on the model. It is not a object that has been photographed by a spacecraft, but a structure that is co-constrained by comet orbits, star disturbances and the solar system. [2] For its "size", it is preferable to keep a range rather than give an exact number to place.

Draw the four boundary on the same logarithmic map.

By linear scale, the planetary zone shrinks to a point, and the Ortwyn is far from visible. A logarithmic axis will only appear in four instances: 30 AU, 50 AU, 121 AU,10⁴-10⁵ AU. [2] [3]

This figure also explains why the Traveler flew out of the solar system as news headlines. It passes through the heliosphere, not the gravitational base; from the planetary zone, it's gone very far, and from the Alt cloud, it just left. [3]

When reading the news, look for “bundary's” definition and then look at the unit of “distance”. It is possible to refer to 121 AU as “the edge of the solar system”, but to indicate that it is the top of the heliosphere; it is also possible to refer to 100,000 AU as “the radius of the solar system”, but to indicate that it is an external estimate of the Ortwyn. [2] [4]
